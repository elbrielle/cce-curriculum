You are the cybersecurity analyst. Check a flag each time you spot it. A message can still be suspicious even when it looks polished. No visible red flags prove that a message is safe; verify through the official app or site, or through a contact you already know.

## Five Red Flags

- [ ] **Strange sender address.** Letters are swapped or extra words are added.
- [ ] **Urgent language.** The message pushes you to act before you can think.
- [ ] **Private-information request.** It asks for a password, ID, birth date, card number, or other private data.
- [ ] **Suspicious link or attachment.** The real address does not match the organization it claims to be.
- [ ] **Spelling or grammar problems.** Errors add to the concern, but one typo alone does not prove a message is fake.

## Practice Safely

If your teacher asks you to draft a practice message:

- use a fictional organization and fictional address;
- do not use a real student's, teacher's, or family member's name;
- do not include a working link, QR code, attachment, password request, or login form;
- keep the draft on paper or in the assigned document; and
- do not send or post it.
