## Three ways people improve plant and food systems

All pay figures are **May 2024 U.S. median annual wages**. They are not starting pay and not DFW-local wages. Outlook figures are national projections for **2024-2034**.

| Career | What the worker does | Typical entry route | 2024 U.S. median | 2024-34 outlook |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Agricultural and Food Science Technician | Helps scientists test plants, soil, food, and production methods; records samples and data | Usually an associate degree; entry routes vary and may include related experience | $48,480 | 5% growth |
| Soil and Plant Scientist | Studies soil, crops, pests, water, and growing methods to improve plant production and protect resources | At least a bachelor's degree in plant biology, agriculture, or a related field | $71,410 | 5% growth |
| Agricultural Engineer | Designs and tests equipment, structures, environmental controls, irrigation, and automated farm systems | Bachelor's degree in agricultural, biological, or another engineering field | $84,630 | 6% growth |

### Read the measures correctly

- **Median** means half of workers earn more and half earn less. It does not predict a new worker's first salary.
- **Outlook** is a national projection, not a promise that every local employer will hire.
- A specialty such as hydroponics, precision agriculture, or controlled-environment growing may sit inside a broader occupation. Job titles vary.
- More education can expand responsibility and pay, but it also requires more time, cost, and preparation.
