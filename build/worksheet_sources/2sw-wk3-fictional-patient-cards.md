## Training boundary

These cards are fictional classroom data. Students observe, compare, document, and hand off. They do not diagnose or practice on classmates.

## Card A: Recovery after activity

**Scene:** Community fun run medical tent.

**First check:** Heart rate 104; temperature 98.4°F; oxygen level 98%. The participant is awake, talking, and says, "I am tired from the run."

**After 3 minutes seated:** Heart rate 82; temperature 98.4°F; oxygen level 98%. The participant says they feel better.

**Your job:** Record what changed, what stayed the same, and what you would report.

## Card B: Symptoms need prompt handoff

**Scene:** Community fun run medical tent.

**Check:** Heart rate 132; temperature 98.5°F; oxygen level 97%. The participant is awake and says, "My chest hurts and I feel short of breath."

**Your job:** Separate the numbers from the reported symptoms. Identify the prompt adult/emergency handoff required by the classroom scenario. Do not write a diagnosis.

## Card C: The person matters with the numbers

**Scene:** School health office training scenario.

**Check:** Heart rate 78; temperature 98.2°F; oxygen level 99%. The student says, "I feel dizzy and I might faint."

**Your job:** Explain why a normal-looking number does not erase what the person reports. Record the symptom and identify the next supervised handoff.

## Card D: Question the device

**Scene:** Community clinic training station.

**Three readings in 20 seconds:** Heart rate 75, then 180, then 42. The fictional patient is sitting still, talking normally, and reports no symptoms.

**Your job:** Identify the data-quality problem. Explain how you would recheck the device or measurement process and when you would report the concern. Do not ignore the person or claim the device is correct.
