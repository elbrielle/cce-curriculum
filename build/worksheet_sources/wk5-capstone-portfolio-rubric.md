Score the three-part portfolio students submit at the end of 1SW Week 5. Score each part 1 to 4 and add the three parts for a score out of 12. The four levels use the same names the workbook uses for its year-end Capstone (FYF p. 280: "Capstone Project Requirements"), so students are scored on a scale they have already seen on the Day 3 peer-feedback slip. The "Sounds like" column is a real student response at that level, not a required wording.

**The three pieces.** (1) Cybersecurity Bootcamp plan and sign-up flyer from Day 3. (2) The college or career logo from Day 4, plus the postsecondary goal it stands for. (3) The updated My Career Journey reflection from Day 5, submitted with the original Week 0 page.

## Part 1: Bootcamp Plan and Flyer — d(4)(F)

| Level | What the submission shows | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **4 Excellent** | All six planning questions answered. The audience is one named group, and the goals, the format, the location, and the community benefit all match that audience. The benefit ties back to one of the four research findings. The flyer names a real sign-up method. | Audience: "families at my apartment complex." Benefit: "60 out of 100 adults get a scam text every week, and my parents get them in Spanish, so I am running it in both languages." |
| **3 Good** | All six questions answered and the flyer has a sign-up method, but one row is general or does not follow from the audience. | Goals: "teach people about scams." Audience: "middle school students." |
| **2 Okay** | Four or five questions answered, or the audience is "everyone," which leaves the goals and the flyer vague. The flyer has no sign-up method. | "My program is for everyone who uses the internet." |
| **1 Needs Improvement** | Three or fewer questions answered, or there is no flyer. | Program name only. |

## Part 2: Postsecondary Logo and Goal — d(3)(A)

Score the **goal and the connection**, not the fabrication. A hand-cut card-stock logo and a laser-cut logo score the same. What the criterion measures is whether the student can name a specific destination after high school and the Irving ISD pathway that leads there.

| Level | What the submission shows | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **4 Excellent** | The logo stands for one specific destination, and the student names the Irving ISD pathway or high school course that prepares for it. The goal is reachable from Irving ISD. | "My logo is the Dallas College mark with a lock in it. I want the Cybersecurity pathway at Singley, then Dallas College for an associate degree." |
| **3 Good** | The logo stands for a specific destination, and the student explains the connection, but no pathway or course is named. | "UT Dallas, because they have computer science." |
| **2 Okay** | The logo names a general category rather than a destination, or the student cannot say why this one. | "College." / "The Army, I guess it looked cool." |
| **1 Needs Improvement** | The logo has no stated postsecondary goal behind it, or no artifact was submitted. | (art with no goal) |

## Part 3: Updated My Career Journey Reflection — d(1)(D), d(1)(C)

Read the update against the student's own Week 0 page. Growth is the thing being measured, so a student who wrote little in Week 0 and a lot now is showing exactly what this part is for.

| Level | What the submission shows | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **4 Excellent** | All seven items answered. Six or more favorited Hats are listed by name. ## Scoring Notes

- Record the total out of 12 as the six-weeks summative. Parts are equally weighted because each carries a different TEKS strand.
- A student who is missing Part 2 because a machine was down, a file did not export, or a design tool was not provisioned still submits a logo. The fallback logo is printed or cut by hand from card stock and scores normally. Never enter a zero for a missing machine.
- Return the scored rubric with the reflection. Both go back into the persistent portfolio folder, because 4SW Week 1 and 6SW Week 6 both ask students to measure how far their thinking moved.

## Reteach Triggers

- More than a third of students score 1 or 2 on **Part 1**: the audience row is the usual cause. Model one plan aloud with a named audience before the next Powerskill activity.
- More than a third score 1 or 2 on **Part 2**: students picked a logo before they picked a goal. In the next planning lesson, hold the design tool closed until the goal sentence is finished.
- More than a third score 1 or 2 on **Part 3**: the Week 0 pages were probably not in front of them. Reissue both pages side by side and give the reflection its own period.
- Compare these scores against the 1SW CFA (`docs/1sw/cfa.md`). A student strong on the CFA and weak on Part 3 is a writing-stamina issue, not a knowledge gap, and the bilingual or stem version of the update sheet is the fix.
