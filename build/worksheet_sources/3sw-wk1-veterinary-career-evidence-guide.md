## Three members of a veterinary team

All pay figures are **May 2024 U.S. median annual wages**. They are not starting pay and not DFW-local wages. Outlook and openings are national projections for **2024-2034**.

| Career | What the worker does | Typical entry route | 2024 U.S. median | 2024-34 outlook | Openings each year |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Veterinary Assistant or Laboratory Animal Caretaker | Provides routine animal care, prepares equipment, cleans work areas, and supports the veterinary team | High school diploma or equivalent; short-term on-the-job training | $37,320 | 9% growth | About 22,200 |
| Veterinary Technician | Performs medical tests, records results, prepares animals and equipment, and assists with treatment under a veterinarian's direction | Usually a 2-year associate degree in veterinary technology; credentialing requirements vary by state | $45,980 | 9% growth | About 14,300 |
| Veterinarian | Examines animals, diagnoses illness or injury, treats patients, performs surgery, and protects animal and public health | Doctor of Veterinary Medicine or equivalent professional degree; state license | $125,510 | 10% growth | About 3,000 |

### Read the measures correctly

- **Median** means half of workers earn more and half earn less. It does not predict a new worker's first salary.
- **Growth rate** describes the expected percentage change in the occupation.
- **Annual openings** includes new jobs and openings created when workers leave the occupation.
- More education can expand responsibility and pay, but it also requires more time, cost, and preparation.

## District workbook pathway evidence

*Find Your Future* pp. 100-101 name **Animal Science** and **Plant Science** at Nimitz High School. The Animal Science section describes a mentorship with Irving Animal Services, pet-care work, animal-science projects, Elanco Fundamentals of Animal Science certification, FFA, and CTE Completer opportunities. Use the workbook wording when you record a local program or experience.

**Important boundary:** A high-school pathway builds knowledge and experience. It does not make a student a veterinarian or replace the college, professional degree, licensing, or credentialing route listed above.
