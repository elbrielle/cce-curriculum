Score the week's two collected pieces together: the IT Salary Comparison worksheet from Day 3 and the four sentence Hour of Code reflection from Day 5. Four criteria, 5 points each, 20 points total. Circle the descriptor that fits what the student actually submitted.

Student: _______________   Period: ______   Total: ______ / 20   Teacher initials: __________   Date: __________

| Criterion | 1 Beginning (2 pts or fewer) | 2 Developing (3 pts) | 3 Proficient (4 pts) | 4 Mastery (5 pts) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Data Completeness** (3 careers across salary, education, growth, daily tasks, and a DFW employer) | Fewer than 3 careers, or the front table is mostly blank. | 3 careers named, but several boxes are blank or the whole back side is missing. | 3 careers with the front table complete. One or two back-side boxes are blank. | 3 careers with every box filled on both sides, including the one sentence on what each career does and a DFW company for each. |
| **Source Accuracy** (H&L for the DFW range, BLS for the national rows) | Numbers appear with no source behind them, or the same number is copied into every column. | Most numbers are present, but the DFW range and the national median are used as if they were the same figure. | Numbers match the two sources, with one field off, such as an hourly rate copied where a yearly figure belongs. | Every number traces to the right source. The DFW range comes from the app, the median, education, growth, and job count come from Quick Facts, and the pay figure is yearly. |
| **Comparison Reasoning** (the back-side questions) | No answer, or an answer that names a career with no number attached. | Names the highest paying career or the fastest growing one, but not both, and gives no evidence. | Answers both questions correctly and cites one number. Does not say whether the two are the same career. | Answers both questions with numbers from the student's own table, states whether the highest paying career is also the fastest growing, and revisits the prediction from the top of page 1. |
| **Hour of Code Reflection** (four sentences from Day 5) | One or two sentences, or the tutorial is not named. | The tutorial and a concept are named, but the concept is a label with no explanation of what it did in the tutorial. | All four sentences present. One is thin, most often the last one about seeing themselves in IT. | All four present and specific: the tutorial by name, a concept explained by what it did in the code, a favorited IT career with a reason, and an honest yes, no, or not sure about IT with something from the week behind it. |

**Scoring notes.** Record the total as the week's summative score. A student who scores 1 or 2 on Source Accuracy reopens the BLS page and corrects the worksheet in pen before the score is entered, because that criterion is the one the labor-market TEKS actually measures. A student who did not finish the tutorial can still earn full credit on the reflection by writing about what they tried, what failed, and what they would change.

**Reteach triggers**

- More than a third of students score 1 or 2 on **Source Accuracy**: reteach the Quick Facts box on the projector before the next BLS week, using the extraction guide as the walkthrough.
- More than a third score 1 or 2 on **Comparison Reasoning**: the class is reading the table but not comparing it. Model one out-loud comparison with two numbers before the next salary activity.
