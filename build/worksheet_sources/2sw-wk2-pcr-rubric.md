Score the student's written report and complication plan as one 16-point evidence set. Physical technique, public speaking, a medical diagnosis, and willingness to be touched are not graded.

| Criterion | 4 - Masters | 3 - Meets | 2 - Approaches | 1 - Needs Improvement | 0 - No evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accurate observation | Clearly separates scenario observations from inference and includes specific scene/condition details | Records accurate scene and condition details without inventing a diagnosis | Mostly accurate but vague or mixes one inference with observation | Major details are missing or inaccurate | Blank/off-topic |
| Simulated action and reasoning | Explains represented actions, safety checks, communication, and why each matters | Explains represented actions and gives sound reasoning | Names actions but reasoning or safety checks are incomplete | Lists an action with little explanation | Blank/off-topic |
| Handoff documentation | Gives a concise, organized handoff with the facts the next responder needs | Includes the important scene, condition, action, and safety facts | Handoff is understandable but misses an important category | Handoff is difficult to follow or mostly incomplete | Blank/off-topic |
| Complication decision | Chooses a safety-first response, explains patient/team protection, communication, reassessment, and an alternative | Chooses a safe response and explains the main reasoning | Response is partly safe but misses communication, reassessment, or an alternative | Response introduces avoidable risk or is mostly unsupported | Blank/off-topic |

## Scoring Notes

- Score each student's written evidence even when the simulation work was collaborative.
- An observer/documenter may earn the full score.
- Do not reward medical-sounding vocabulary that is unsupported by the fictional facts.
- Do not lower a score for English mechanics unless meaning is unclear.
- An absent student uses the embedded scenario images and completes the report independently.
