## Three changes affecting sustainable engineering work

Use the evidence as written. Do not turn a national median into local starting pay or a projection into a promise.

| Trend | What is changing? | Work and careers affected | Evidence and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Precision agriculture and drones | Farms can use sensors and drones to scout crops, monitor conditions, and support in-season decisions | Agricultural engineers, plant scientists, drone technicians, farmers | USDA research says these systems can support scouting and monitoring. Cost, calibration, accuracy, and profit still limit use. |
| More wind and solar installation work | Energy systems need workers who install, inspect, maintain, and repair equipment | Wind turbine service technicians; solar photovoltaic installers; engineers | BLS projects 50% wind-tech growth and 42% solar-installer growth from 2024-34. These are national projections for occupations with different job counts. |
| Water systems need specialized workers | Communities must operate and improve drinking-water and wastewater systems while technology changes the work | Environmental engineers, water and wastewater operators, technicians | EPA says advanced technology increases the need for specialized skills. BLS lists Environmental Engineers at 4% growth and about 3,000 openings each year for 2024-34. |

### Source/date key

- USDA Agricultural Research Service, “Unmanned Aerial Systems for Agriculture and Natural Resources.”
- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook; May 2024 pay and 2024-34 outlook, accessed August 2026.
- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, Water Infrastructure Sector Workforce.

## Read across sources

### What the guide can support

- A technology can change what workers monitor, repair, install, or decide.
- A high growth percentage and a large number of annual openings are different measures.
- Preparation routes differ. Some roles commonly require a bachelor's degree; others use technical training and on-the-job learning.
- Sustainable engineering includes tradeoffs. A tool may save time or improve information while adding cost, training, maintenance, or accuracy concerns.

### What the guide cannot prove

- that every listed occupation is available near one student's home;
- that a student will earn the median pay when beginning;
- that one pathway guarantees a credential, job, or college admission;
- that technology will replace every older work method; or
- that the occupation with the fastest growth is automatically the best fit.

### Vocabulary

**Societal trend:** a broad change in how people live, work, use resources, or solve shared problems.

**Emerging work:** a new specialty or a changing set of tasks inside an occupation.

**Projection:** an estimate based on current evidence, not a guarantee.

**Tradeoff:** gaining one benefit while accepting a cost, risk, or limit.

**Parent occupation:** the broader BLS occupation used when an exact new specialty does not have its own data page.
