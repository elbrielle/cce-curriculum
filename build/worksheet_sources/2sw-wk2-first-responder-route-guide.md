## Read the Numbers Correctly

The pay figures below are **U.S. median annual wages from May 2024**. A median is the midpoint for workers at many experience levels. It is **not** a starting salary or a DFW guarantee. Xello may be used to add a current local figure when the geography, date, and salary type are visible.

| Career | What the work includes | Common route after high school | 2024 U.S. median |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Police and sheriff's patrol officer | Respond to calls, patrol, collect evidence, and write detailed reports | Requirements vary by agency; education ranges from high school to a degree, followed by an agency academy and on-the-job training | **$76,290** |
| Detective / criminal investigator | Gather facts and evidence, interview people, examine records, and investigate serious crimes | Detectives typically begin as police officers and gain law-enforcement experience | **$93,580** |
| Firefighter | Respond to fires and other emergencies, rescue people, provide medical help, and write reports | High school diploma is typical; written/physical tests, fire academy, and locally required credentials such as EMT may follow | **$59,530** |
| Emergency medical technician (EMT) | Assess patients, provide emergency care, transport safely, and document observations and care | Postsecondary EMT program; all states require licensure and requirements vary | **$41,340** |
| Public safety telecommunicator | Answer emergency calls, collect location/details, dispatch responders, and track units | High school diploma is typical, followed by agency training; certification rules vary by state/locality | **$50,730** |

**Sources checked August 6, 2026:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook pages for Police and Detectives, Firefighters, EMTs and Paramedics, and Public Safety Telecommunicators. The Singley Academy page currently lists **Law Enforcement** and **Emergency Medical - EMT** pathways.
