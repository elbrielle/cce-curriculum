FYF p. 24

Five clues deserve a closer look.

  1. Strange sender address
  2. Urgent or emotional language
  3. Request for private information
  4. Suspicious link or attachment
  5. Spelling or grammar problems
Weigh the evidence

A typo may be a clue. A mismatched domain is stronger evidence.

Weak by itself

One awkward sentence or misspelled word

Stronger

A company name that does not match the sender or destination domain

Look for a pattern of evidence. Do not make one typo do all the work.

Important boundary

Safe-looking is not the same as proven safe.

A polished message can still be fake. A normal-looking message can still deserve verification.

If you are unsure, open the official app or site yourself—or contact someone you already know.

Do not use

  • a phone number inside the message;
  • a reply button inside the message;
  • a link inside the message.

Safe response protocol

When a message is suspicious, use a route you control.

01

Pause

Do not click, reply, or forward.

02

Verify

Use the official app, site, or known contact.

03

Report

Use the approved school or workplace route.

04

Delete

Remove it after reporting.

Optional safe-practice sketch • 2 minutes

Build a fictional example without creating a real threat.

Include

  • fictional person and organization;
  • an example.com address;
  • two written red flags;
  • a paper or assigned-document draft.

Never include

  • working links or QR codes;
  • real names or credentials;
  • attachments or password requests;
  • Irving ISD impersonation.
The message is never sent or posted.
