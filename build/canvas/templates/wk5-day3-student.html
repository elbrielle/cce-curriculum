How can a flyer help our community avoid a scam?

Plan in your workbook. Build one clear, trustworthy flyer in Canva.

Write in: your Find Your Future workbook, p. 35. Create in: Canva. Your final flyer goes to the Canva flyer assignment.

Today you will

  • choose an audience and safety topic from the workbook;
  • plan the event on FYF p. 35; and
  • start a one-page Canva flyer with accurate advice.

Do Now

What is one thing you would check before teaching someone how to spot a scam?

Para empezar: “Comprobaría ___ para asegurarme de que ___.”

1. Choose the need

Open your Find Your Future workbook to p. 34. Choose one audience and one safety topic: suspicious messages, stronger passwords, safer public Wi-Fi, or another teacher-approved topic. Treat the printed percentages as part of the story, not facts to repeat on your flyer.

2. Plan in the workbook

Open your workbook to p. 35. Answer its six planning questions there. Make the two learning goals actions your audience could actually practice.

Need more help planning? The bootcamp planning and flyer checklist packet gives extra prompts. If your teacher gives you the packet, write your plan there instead of in the workbook.

Everyone can use the packet's flyer checklist to check a design.

3. Build the Canva flyer

Open Canva with your school account. Search for a portrait flyer design. Replace the sample words and images with your own event information; make the title easy to see and the smaller text easy to read.

  • Show the program name, audience, topic, time/place, and a teacher-approved way to sign up.
  • Teach two specific online-safety actions. Do not promise that a tip stops every scam.
  • Use one relevant visual. Write your own words and credit any reused material.
  • Keep personal phone numbers, personal email addresses, and home locations off the flyer.

Open the Canva flyer assignment

Save your Canva draft. You will have time to improve and upload the final flyer on Day 5.

You are done when

  • your plan is in FYF p. 35 or the packet your teacher gave you;

  • you have one idea to improve the flyer after a peer check.
