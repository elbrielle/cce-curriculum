Today you will

  • choose ten items for an emergency scenario;
  • design the kit and defend the three most important choices; and
  • connect the task to an emergency-management career.

Get ready

Open the Emergency Kit Decision Plan. You may draw on paper or use an approved digital tool. The thinking and labels are what count.

1. Choose the scenario and ten items

Choose earthquake, fire evacuation, or flood. Select exactly ten items from the workbook list. For each item, write the problem it solves.

2. Design and defend

Label all ten items so another person can understand the kit quickly. Explain why your top three fit this specific emergency. After feedback, record one change you would make.

3. Connect the task to a career

Complete this thought: “Firefighters or emergency-management workers plan before an emergency by ___ so that ___.”

You are done when: one scenario is named, exactly ten items are labeled, three choices have reasons, one revision is recorded, and the career connection names a planning task.
Working alone or absent?

Complete the same plan. Use the final checklist as your feedback partner: Is every item useful for the scenario? Is each label clear? Do the top-three reasons explain a real need?
