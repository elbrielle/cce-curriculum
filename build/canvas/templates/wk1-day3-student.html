Week 1 · Day 3

Design, build, and test a bike rack

Make the engineering choices a welder makes before a product reaches the factory floor.

Response Home: Find Your Future workbook pp. 204–206 (Bike Rack Sketch & Decisions) & Pair Prototype.

Today you will

  • draw top and side views of your bike rack;
  • choose a metal and welding methods, explaining the trade-off;
  • build or dry-fit one prototype with a partner; and
  • test it, name a weak point, and plan one improvement.
Hot-glue safety: Glue guns stay on heat-safe mats. Wear craft gloves. Never touch fresh glue. At the five-minute warning, unplug the gun and leave it to cool.

1. Sketch the Design

Your rack must hold several bikes, survive outdoor weather, and fit beside a sidewalk. Draw a top view and a side view on FYF p. 204.

2. Choose Material and Welds

Compare aluminum, stainless steel, and carbon steel on FYF p. 205. Choose one and explain the trade-off. Then select welding methods and label every planned joint on your sketch.

Need sentence frames or Spanish vocabulary?

Spanish terms: Alloy / Metal = Aleación / Metal · Joint = Unión o junta · Weld = Soldadura · Trade-off = Ventaja y limitación.

“I chose [metal] because it is [strength/cost benefit], even though [trade-off/limitation].”

3. Build One Prototype per Pair

  1. Compare both sketches and choose the clearer design.
  2. Dry-fit craft sticks and straws before gluing.
  3. Build for seven minutes. Focus on secure joints.
  4. Press gently to test balance and stability.
  5. Identify one weak spot and the first engineering improvement.
No glue station available?

Dry-fit the prototype and use masking tape at each joint. Run the same balance test and label the weld that would replace each taped connection.

4. Exit Ticket

Make your copy: Super Sports Manufacturing — Design, Build, Test

Open your CCE Work OneNote tab to rank the three metals and complete your exit reflection.

You are done when

  • your sketch has top and side views with labeled welds, plus a material choice with a clear trade-off reason;
  • your pair tested one prototype and recorded an improvement; and
  • your exit ticket in OneNote is complete.
Absent or unable to build?

Complete the full design sequence: top and side views, material and weld decisions, and a test prediction. Use the supplied prototype image or teacher demonstration to identify a likely weak point and explain one improvement.
