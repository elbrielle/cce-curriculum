    Week 1: Animal Planet — Veterinary Science Careers

    3rd Six Weeks | Agriculture, Food & Natural Resources Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

    Lesson Objective

    Students explore the Agriculture, Food & Natural Resources cluster through Hats & Ladders and the Find Your Future workbook, investigate veterinary careers from Vet Tech to Veterinarian, compare education pathways, and work the triage desk in the workbook's "Powerskill: Critical Thinking" activity, weighing two emergency patients against normal-range references to decide who is seen first.

    Learning Target: I can name at least three veterinary careers, tell how much school a Vet Tech and a Veterinarian each need, compare a sick animal's vitals to the normal range for its species, and decide which patient the vet sees first.

    Success Criteria: I will know I am successful when my career research worksheet lists the job, the training, the pay, and the tools for one Animal Systems career, when my comparison worksheet fills in all six rows for both the Vet Tech and the Veterinarian, when my triage notes name the critical information for both patients and my decision cites two pieces of evidence, and when my Veterinary Pathway Brief has all four parts.

    Demonstration of Learning

    "I can describe at least three veterinary careers, explain the difference between a Vet Tech and a Veterinarian's education pathway, use normal-range evidence to decide which emergency patient is seen first, and connect the Nimitz HS Veterinary Science pathway to a real career goal."

    TEKS Alignment

    • d(1)(A): Analyze and discuss assessment results (Day 4 Xello Life Experiences reflection).
    • d(1)(B): Explore and describe the CTE career clusters (Day 1).
    • d(1)(C): Identify various career opportunities within the Ag, Food & Natural Resources cluster (Days 1-5).
    • d(2)(A): Research and describe applicable academic, technical, certification, and training requirements (Days 2, 5).
    • d(2)(B): Use available resources to research and evaluate educational and training options (Days 2, 5).
    • d(4)(E): Community service and volunteerism (Day 4 Xello Volunteer Hours).

    Materials Needed

    • Chromebooks with internet access (1 per student)
    • Hats & Ladders student accounts + Find Your Future workbook, Agriculture, Food, and Natural Resources chapter (printed pp. 87-102)
    • BLS, Veterinarians: bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/veterinarians.htm
    • BLS, Vet Technologists & Technicians: bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/veterinary-technologists-and-technicians.htm
    • Xello student accounts (Life Experiences activity + Volunteer Hours)
    • Printed Vet Career Comparison worksheet (Vet Tech vs. Veterinarian)
    • Printed Animal Systems career research worksheet (CCE artifact; the research format is taught in Wk0)
    • Optional printed copies of the two patient charts and the normal-range references for Day 3 (FYF pp. 96-99: "Powerskill: Critical Thinking")
    • Projector

    Career Connection

    Vet Techs assist with surgery, run lab tests, and may use 3D printing for prosthetics and surgical guides. Veterinarians diagnose illness, perform surgery, and make treatment decisions after about eight years of college and veterinary school. This week lets students compare the daily work, pay, and training instead of treating every animal-care job as the same career.

    What is Happening at Irving ISD? Veterinary Science at Nimitz High School leads to the Elanco Veterinary Medical Applications certification. The workbook's district pages describe a greenhouse, an orchard, more than 40 chickens, and a daily petcare program where students care for, handle, and train dogs (FYF pp. 100-101: "What is Happening at My District?"). The pages name Plant Science and Animal Science as the programs of study, Elanco Veterinary Medical Applications as the industry certification, and Future Farmers of America (FFA) as the student organization. Day 5 uses the pages' mentorship prompt to discuss how students learn this work from someone with experience.

    Vocabulary

    • Veterinary Technician: A professional who provides support to veterinarians in medical and surgical procedures. Requires a 2-year associate degree and state credentialing.
    • Veterinarian: A doctor of veterinary medicine (DVM). Requires 4 years of undergraduate study + 4 years of veterinary school + state license.
    • Animal Systems: The CTE pathway focused on the care, management, and health of animals.
    • Diagnostic Testing: Medical tests used to identify diseases or conditions in animals.
    • Triage: Sorting patients by how urgently they need care. It is the first step when a sick or injured animal arrives at an emergency clinic.
    • Powerskill: In Hats & Ladders, a skill that carries across careers. This week's Powerskill is critical thinking: analyzing information and using evidence to make a decision.
    • Climber Profile: The H&L app feature where students save favorite Hats and track exploration.
    • Hat: In H&L, a "Hat" is a career profile with information about education, daily tasks, salary, and demand.

    Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

    The Find Your Future workbook gives Agriculture, Food, and Natural Resources printed pages 87-102. The chapter opener (FYF p. 87: "Exploring the World of AGRICULTURE, FOOD, AND NATURAL RESOURCES") frames the cluster as growing food, caring for animals, and managing the land and resources people depend on, hands students a "Be the Decision Maker" future-farm design problem, and prints three Hats on the page: Feedlot Manager, Agricultural Engineer, and Veterinarian. The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet the Agriculture pathways in the Hats & Ladders app instead, through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 102: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's own programs of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 100-101).

    One chapter activity carries this week. Powerskill: Critical Thinking (Day 3) puts students at the triage desk of a busy emergency animal hospital (FYF pp. 96-99: "Powerskill: Critical Thinking"). Two patients arrive at the same second: Leo, a ball python whose owner reports cloudy eyes, dull skin, and refused food, and Barnaby, a Golden Retriever retching with a swollen abdomen an hour after eating. Students compare each chart against the workbook's printed normal-range references, identify the critical information, decide who is seen first, and compare their evidence in a small group. The reversal the activity is built around, that Leo's alarming symptoms are normal shedding while Barnaby's are not, is the same reasoning a Vet Tech runs at a real triage desk, and it replaces the old food-science teamwork activity with content that sits inside this week's own career field.

    The chapter's three Career Climb activities belong to the other two weeks of this six weeks: Grow System Rescue (FYF pp. 88-90) and Farm to Table (FYF pp. 91-92) run in Week 2 Plant Science, and Ag-Tech Pest Patrol (FYF pp. 93-95) runs in Week 3 Sustainable Engineering. Leave them for those weeks.

    IISD Instructional Strategies

    • Think-Pair-Share: Day 1, Activity 1, the "Be the Decision Maker" future-farm prompt on the chapter opener (FYF p. 87), where students design a farm that has to feed a busy city. Distinguished: pose it to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "I would build it _ because " and "A farm inside a city has to solve __, so I would _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who names a constraint (space, water, light, delivery) rather than just a location, and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
    • Stop and Jot: Day 2, Activity 2, the two analysis questions on the back of the Vet Career Comparison worksheet. Distinguished: give a stated time box, keep the BLS tabs open, and post two stems so the salary and training vocabulary is in front of every student: "A Vet Tech earns $_ after years of school. A Veterinarian earns $ after _ years." and " fits me better right now because ." Read jots over shoulders while students write and track who is answering the personal-fit question with opinion instead of a number. If most jots skip the data, stop the room and model one sentence that cites a BLS figure before students move to the exit ticket, since the ticket demands a specific number.
    • Active Monitoring: Day 3, Activity 2, the normal-range check and triage decision. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times, one target per lap (lap 1, every student has compared Barnaby's 150 bpm against the 60 to 100 canine reference; lap 2, every student has found the shedding note and marked Leo's cloudy eyes as expected; lap 3, every decision sentence carries two pieces of evidence). Have the feedback ready before class for the two known misconceptions: "the snake looks worse" and "the dog is not bleeding so he can wait." If more than a handful of students miss the lap 2 target, pivot and reread the shedding note with the whole room instead of fixing it desk by desk.
    • Chunking: Day 1, Activity 1, the App Exploration sequence (FYF p. 102). Distinguished: project the page and release one step at a time rather than handing students the whole list (Cluster Tour video, then Game Time and the one-thing-I-learned line, then the fit Hat, then the non-fit Hat), say what each step is for before students run it, and check the room between steps before releasing the next one. Note which step stalls. If the non-fit Hat is the one students cannot finish, that becomes a 3 minute reteach at the top of Activity 2, because naming why a career does not fit is exactly what the Day 1 exit ticket asks for.
    • Time, Voice, Body (TVB): Day 5, Activities 2 and 3, the Climber Profile screenshot and the Veterinary Pathway Brief assembly. Distinguished: post the three blocks before the brief starts, Voice 0 for the reflection paragraph, Voice 1 for checking one missing component with a neighbor, a visible countdown on the screen, and one stated place on each table where the Day 1 and Day 2 worksheets go so no component gets lost mid-assembly. Narrate the students who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If several students are still hunting for a worksheet when the countdown starts, pause it and run a 60 second component check before the writing time begins.

    Formative Assessment

    • Career research worksheet + Mini-Case exit ticket (Sofia allergic to cats/dogs + loves outdoors; pick Animal Systems career). Day 1, d(1)(B), d(1)(C)
    • Vet Career Comparison + Comparison Matrix exit ticket (Vet Tech vs. Veterinarian x education/pay/task). Day 2, d(2)(A), d(2)(B)
    • Triage notes and decision + Decision Tree exit ticket (new patient, normal-range check, and who the clinic tells first). Day 3, d(1)(C)
    • Xello Life Experiences + Volunteer Hours + SCR exit ticket (experience + specific skill + vet career + 2-week volunteer plan). Day 4, d(1)(A), d(1)(C), d(4)(E)
    • Nimitz pathway brief + Concept Map exit ticket (favorited career + Nimitz pathway fit + life experience + core personality type). Day 5, d(2)(A), d(2)(B), d(1)(C)

    Summative Assessment

    Veterinary Pathway Brief (Day 5): Students submit a one-page brief that includes (1) the career research worksheet for one veterinary career, (2) the comparison worksheet showing Vet Tech vs. Veterinarian education and salary, (3) a paragraph explaining whether the Nimitz Vet Science pathway interests them and why, and (4) a screenshot of their updated H&L Climber Profile. Scored on accuracy of education/training data (d(2)(A)), depth of comparison (d(2)(B)), and connection to a personal career goal (d(1)(C)).

    Differentiation

    Scaffolded Learning

    • Pre-fill the career research worksheet with the career name and one section completed as a model
    • Provide a simplified comparison worksheet with one row pre-filled
    • Sentence stems for the pathway reflection paragraph
    • Triage note-catcher for Day 3 with the four reference rows pre-printed and a NORMAL / ABNORMAL box beside each finding

    Extensions

    • Research the difference between a DVM and a VMD degree
    • Compare US vet education to UK vet education (length, cost, license)
    • Read a real Vet Tech job posting on Indeed and identify the credentials required
    • Add a fourth Hat to the Climber Profile from a different Ag pathway (Plant Science, Food Science) for cross-cluster comparison

    ELL Language Support

    • Pre-teach: Veterinarian = Veterinario/a, Animal = Animal, License = Licencia, Diagnosis = Diagnóstico, Volunteer = Voluntario/a, Triage = Triaje
    • Bilingual Vet Career Comparison worksheet
    • The workbook reads well above grade level, so read the Day 3 patient charts aloud while students underline, and read the chapter opener aloud on Day 1 rather than assigning it as silent reading
    • Let students write their Day 3 triage notes in Spanish or bilingually, and pair ELL students with bilingual peers for the share-and-compare group
