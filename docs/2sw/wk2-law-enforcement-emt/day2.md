## Before Class

Open all six Canvas evidence images. Keep the teacher guidance below out of the student page. Students need readable individual images or text descriptions; a single projected montage is not sufficient.

## Warm-Up (5 min)

**A lake is polluted. Which question must investigators answer before naming a cause?** Sort responses into harm, timing, source, and missing test.

## Activity 1: Evidence Routine (7 min)

Teach four questions: What does the file directly show? Who produced it? What can it not prove? How strongly does it address the case question?

## Activity 2: Six-File Review (23 min)

Students review each Canvas image and complete one tracker row at a time. Pause after Files 2 and 4 so students do not treat an inspection note or public statement as independent proof.

## Activity 3: Careful Conclusion (10 min)

Students choose the two strongest harm files, one source-related file, and one evidence gap. Their conclusion may support a landfill cause, an outside cause, mixed causes, or insufficient evidence.

## Teacher Answer Guidance

- Files 1 and 6 strongly show serious pollution/ecological harm.
- File 3 documents a major storm; File 5 raises possible illegal dumping. Both support plausible outside or mixed causes, not certainty.
- File 2 reports routine landfill conditions but is limited and partly relies on an older major check.
- File 4 is the City's own statement. Its lack of confirmed containment testing weakens confidence but does not prove leakage.
- No file directly documents a containment failure. A defensible conclusion must preserve that gap.

Integrity here means reporting inconvenient evidence and source limits. Do not tell students that one classroom rule settles every real legal disclosure duty.

## Exit Ticket (5 min)

**EXIT TICKET** (Mini-Case) · [Printable PDF](../../resources/exit-tickets/2sw-wk2-day2-clinton-lake-weigh-the-evidence.pdf):

An investigator omits File 5 because it complicates the team's theory. Identify the integrity problem and the professional next step.

## Supports and Fallbacks

- Pre-teach evidence/evidencia, claim/afirmación, source/fuente, reliable/confiable, uncertain/incierto.
- Read each file aloud and let students highlight before paraphrasing.
- A private written conclusion is equal to discussion. An absent student uses all six embedded files independently.
