# Week 3: Nursing Science - Routes, Simulation, and Handoff

**2nd Six Weeks** | Health Science Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students compare preparation, responsibility, and pay across four nursing-related careers; build and test a micro:bit vital-signs simulator; and use fictional patient information to write an accurate supervised handoff. The week ends with the required Xello **Save careers** task.

**Learning Target:** I can compare nursing routes, explain what a training simulator can and cannot do, and hand off fictional patient information without diagnosing.

**Success Criteria:**

- My route comparison uses at least three careers and labels every pay figure correctly.
- My MakeCode program displays fictional values on Buttons A and B and passes its test plan.
- My handoff separates supplied values, reported symptoms, and inference.
- My Xello account shows at least three saved careers.

## Demonstration of Learning

Students submit the Nursing Route Comparison and one Observation and Clinical Handoff Record. The MakeCode build may be completed on a physical micro:bit, in the browser simulator, or through a paper block trace. Day 5 completion is verified in the Xello Completion Standards report.

## Safety, Privacy, and Scope Boundary

The micro:bit program is a training simulator. It does not measure a person or function as medical equipment. Students do not take classmates' temperatures, blood pressure, oxygen levels, or medical histories. Day 4 uses fictional cards so students can practice observation, data-quality checks, and handoff without diagnosis or collection of health information.

## Vocabulary

- **Median wage:** The middle wage in a group of workers, not a guaranteed starting salary.
- **Licensure:** Legal permission from a state board to practice a regulated profession.
- **Scope of practice:** Work a licensed role is legally prepared and allowed to perform.
- **Simulation:** A model used to practice a process without claiming to be the real system.
- **Observation:** A supplied value or directly reported fact.
- **Inference:** An interpretation that goes beyond the direct evidence.
- **Handoff:** An organized transfer of information to the next responsible person.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, students use the FYF health fair prompt to connect a booth to the worker who would staff it. Hold the silent think before both partners speak, then select share-outs from what you heard.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 2, students write one advantage and one trade-off for the ADN and BSN routes before discussion. Check whether they named cost, time, admission, licensure, or long-term goals instead of repeating "more school equals more money."
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 3, use three fixed laps: Button A displays a fictional heart-rate value; Button B displays the temperature code; the student can explain why neither value came from a person.
- **Chunking:** Day 4, separate situation, observations, comparison, handoff, and reasoning. Do not release the next section until students have removed diagnoses from the observation section.
- **Time, Voice, Body:** Day 5, post a 30-minute Xello block with Voice 0, screens facing the aisle, and a visible checkpoint at 15 minutes. Students with access problems move to the paper reflection and supervised catch-up list.

## Assessment and Grading

- Daily exit tickets remain formative.
- The Day 4 route-and-handoff evidence set is the recommended **minor checkpoint**, scored with the 16-point rubric. Device access, platform access, public speaking, and physical measurement are not graded.
- The Canvas practice quiz is ungraded and may be retried. It checks misconceptions before the handoff is submitted.
- Xello completion may be recorded as part of a later profile checkpoint. Do not create a separate grade for every click.

## Differentiation

- Keep required directions visible in Canvas; use disclosures only for examples, glossary help, and optional extensions.
- Offer the physical micro:bit, browser simulator, and paper block trace as equal evidence routes.
- Provide bilingual labels and sentence frames for route, median, observation, symptom, inference, and handoff.
- Allow speech-to-text or oral rehearsal before individual writing when documented supports permit it.
- An absent student completes the same fictional case and simulator route. No peer measurement or live presentation is required.
