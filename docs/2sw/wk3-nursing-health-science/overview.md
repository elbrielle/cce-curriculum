# Week 3: Healing Hands — Nursing Careers

**2nd Six Weeks** | Health Science Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore the Health Science career cluster through the Hats & Ladders app and the *Find Your Future* workbook, investigate the nursing career ladder from CNA to Nurse Practitioner, work the "Vitals in Motion" travel-nurse activity by taking and recording a partner's vital signs, build a micro:bit vital signs monitor prototype, and compare education pathways, costs, and salaries across nursing levels using BLS and H&L data.

**Learning Target:** I can name the four steps of the nursing career ladder, tell how much school and money each step takes, take and record a partner's vital signs the way a travel nurse does, and program a micro:bit to show a heart rate and a temperature.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my Nursing Career Ladder worksheet has all four rows filled in with education, time, DFW salary, and one daily task, when my Vital Signs Chart shows a reading after activity and a reading after rest, when my nursing report names the scene, the patient's condition, the care I gave, and the next step, and when my micro:bit shows a heart rate on Button A and a temperature on Button B.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe the nursing career ladder, explain the education required at each level, compare salaries from CNA to Nurse Practitioner, take and record a partner's vital signs and write them up in a nursing report, and build a micro:bit vital signs monitor that simulates how nurses use technology in patient care."

## Career Connection

Nursing is one of the most in-demand and rewarding career fields in America. The DFW metroplex has a documented nursing shortage, meaning job opportunities are abundant for qualified candidates at every level. What makes nursing unique is the career LADDER: students can enter as a CNA with just 4-12 weeks of training, then climb through LVN, RN, and Nurse Practitioner. Each step up means more education, more responsibility, and more salary. This visible, climbable structure makes nursing especially appealing to students who want a clear pathway with options to grow.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** The workbook's district pages carry the local detail students respond to most (FYF pp. 84-85: "What is Happening at My District?"). Irving ISD runs five Health Science programs of study, and the nursing week's home is **Patient Care Technician**, described there as preparing students for direct patient care, monitoring vital signs, assisting with daily living activities, and supporting medical staff through clinical experiences. Health Science students at **Singley Academy** job-shadow at local hospitals and dental offices. The related industry certifications include Patient Care Tech and Certified Phlebotomy, and the student organization is HOSA, the Health Occupations Students Association. The district pages also describe the labs: programmable patient simulators whose manikins display changing vital signs to replicate critical emergencies, which is the professional version of the micro:bit monitor students build on Days 3 and 4. Students who finish the program in high school can enter the workforce immediately or use the credential as a stepping stone toward LVN or RN programs.

## Vocabulary

- **CNA (Certified Nursing Assistant):** An entry-level healthcare worker who assists patients with daily activities. Requires a 4-12 week certification program.
- **LVN (Licensed Vocational Nurse):** A nurse who provides basic nursing care under the supervision of an RN or doctor. Requires about 1 year of training.
- **RN (Registered Nurse):** A nurse who provides and coordinates patient care. Requires an associate degree (ADN, ~2 years) or bachelor's degree (BSN, ~4 years) in nursing.
- **Nurse Practitioner (NP):** An advanced practice nurse who can diagnose conditions, prescribe medications, and manage patient care independently. Requires a master's or doctoral degree (~6-8 years total after high school).
- **Career Ladder:** A progression of jobs within a field where each step requires more education and offers higher pay and responsibility.
- **Vital Signs:** Measurements of the body's most basic functions, heart rate, body temperature, respiration rate, and blood pressure, that nurses monitor to assess patient health.
- **Travel Nurse:** A trained nurse who works temporarily in different locations, such as hospitals, clinics, or community events, stepping in wherever care is needed most. This is the role students take in the "Vitals in Motion" activity.
- **Pulse Oximeter:** A clip-on device that measures the oxygen level in the blood and the pulse rate. One of the four tools students research in "Vitals in Motion."

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Health Science printed pages 59 to 86. The chapter opener (FYF p. 59: "Exploring the World of HEALTH SCIENCE") frames the cluster as helping people stay healthy, recover from illness, and improve their quality of life, puts a "Be the Decision Maker" community health fair prompt in front of students, and prints three Hats on the page: MRI Technologist, Pediatrician, and Dentist. The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet the Health Science pathways in the Hats & Ladders app through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 86: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's own five programs of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 84-85), which name Patient Care Technician, Medical Billing & Coding, Dental Assistant, EMT, and Biomedical Sciences. Those five names line up closely with the CCE week topics in this six-weeks block.

The chapter activity that carries this week is **Vitals in Motion** (Days 3 and 4). It is a Career Climb activity set at a community summer fun run, where students are the travel nurse on duty in the medical tent. They research the tools in pairs, take and record a partner's vital signs after activity and after rest, switch roles, and write a five-part nursing report (FYF pp. 60-61: "Vitals in Motion"). The activity needs the teacher's Climber Notes deck for the tool reference table and the fever, blood pressure, and pulse oximeter charts (Climber Notes: "Vitals in Motion", slides 2-4). It is the on-ramp to the micro:bit vital signs monitor, because students hold real readings in their hands before they program a device to display simulated ones.

The chapter's second Career Climb activity, **Ultrasound Detectives** (FYF pp. 64-68), puts partners in the role of a diagnostic medical sonographer: learn three common ultrasound types, practice reading a scan, study a symptom and condition reference table, take a patient, label the sonogram, complete a Scan Report, and exchange partner feedback. It runs as this week's enrichment rather than as a daily anchor, because Days 2 through 4 are committed to the career ladder and the micro:bit build. Note for the teacher: the printed steps skip a number. The symptom reference table on page 65 is Step 3 even though it is printed without a number, so the real order runs Step 1, Step 2, the symptom table, Step 4, Step 5, Step 6. The activity needs the Climber Notes scan images (Climber Notes: "Ultrasound Detectives", slides 2-4).

The H&L app supplies the localized DFW salary and demand data that powers the salary comparison and the career-ladder analysis. The district pages supply the cost half of that conversation through their Career Exploration Connection on financial resources, which explains grants, scholarships, and loans (FYF p. 84).

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 1, the "Be the Decision Maker" prompt on the chapter opener (FYF p. 59), where students plan a community health fair that teaches families about healthy habits, exercise, nutrition, or mental wellness. Distinguished: pose it to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "One booth I would set up is _____ because _____" and "Families would remember _____ better than a poster because _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who names a health worker rather than only an activity, and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 2, the two pauses during the Health Science cluster tour video. Distinguished: name both stop points before the video starts, give 45 seconds per jot with the prompt on screen, and offer two stems: "One healthcare career I did not expect in this cluster is _____" and "One thing I still want to know about _____ is _____." Read jots over shoulders during both pauses and track which careers students name. If nearly every jot says Nurse or Doctor, steer the Hat browse that follows toward MRI Technologist, Phlebotomist, and Surgical Technologist so the career research worksheets are not twenty copies of the same career.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 3, Activity 3, the MakeCode build time, using the three-checkpoint clipboard already written into the plan (Button A displays a heart rate number, Button A shows an LED animation, Button B displays a temperature number). Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway with one target per lap rather than drifting toward raised hands, and have the feedback ready before class for the two known misconceptions: a variable that was never initialized, so the display stays blank, and an LED animation with no pause between frames, so nothing appears to move. If more than a handful of teams miss the animation target, pivot and reproject the pause block to the whole room instead of fixing it team by team.
- **Chunking:** Day 3, Activity 2, the MakeCode demo. Distinguished: teach one block at a time on the projector, say what each block is for before demonstrating it (the variable holds the reading, show number puts it on the LEDs, show LEDs draws the heart, pause makes the beat visible), and close each chunk with a thumbs check before releasing the next one. Note which block draws the most thumbs down; that block becomes a 3 minute re-teach at the start of Day 4 Activity 1, and teams that cleared every chunk start Button B early instead of waiting. The same chunking runs at week scale on the career ladder: CNA and LVN on Day 1, RN then NP on Day 2, and the salary comparison only after all four rows are filled.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 4, Activity 2, "Vitals in Motion" Steps 2 and 3, where partners take readings on each other. Distinguished: post the three blocks before any student stands up, Voice 1 for nurse and patient talk and Voice 0 during the counting so pulses can be counted accurately, a visible countdown for the 60 seconds of activity and again for the 2 minutes of rest, and a stated rule that the shared thermometer and pulse oximeter return to the station tray between teams. Narrate the pairs who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If the room gets loud enough that counts are drifting, pause the countdown, reset to Voice 0, and restart the count rather than accepting numbers the pair cannot trust.

### Extensions

- **"Ultrasound Detectives" enrichment** (FYF pp. 64-68): partners work the chapter's second Career Climb activity as diagnostic medical sonographers. They learn the three common ultrasound types and match each to its purpose, practice reading a scan and say what clues led to their guess, study the symptom and condition reference table, each take one of the two patients (Raul Lewis, abdominal, and Taylor Brown, vascular), label the sonogram, complete the Scan Report with at least three observed structures plus a possible condition and the evidence for it, then compare diagnoses and record whether they agree and why. Project the Climber Notes scan images (Climber Notes: "Ultrasound Detectives", slides 2-4). Runs in about 45 minutes as a flex-day activity, or split across two early-finisher blocks. The printed steps skip a number: the symptom table on page 65 is Step 3.
- Research specialized nursing careers (flight nurse, nurse anesthetist, forensic nurse, oncology nurse) and add them to the career ladder as a 5th and 6th level
- Add a third vital sign to the micro:bit monitor (respiration rate using the accelerometer)
- Calculate the total cost of education for each nursing level (tuition × years) and find the "break-even point" where extra education starts paying off, then use the district page's financial resources note (FYF p. 84) to say which costs a grant or scholarship could cover
- Build a 4-year plan for the Patient Care Technician program of study at Singley Academy
