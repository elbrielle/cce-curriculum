## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Name every type of healthcare worker you can think of in 60 seconds. How many did you get?**

Take a quick whip-around and count the distinct careers students name. Most classes stop around 5-10, while Health Science contains more than 200. Today students begin sorting that larger field into pathways and job types.

## Activity 1: Exploring the World of Health Science (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 59: "Exploring the World of HEALTH SCIENCE")

Open the workbook to page 59. Read the chapter opener together: Health Science is about helping people stay healthy, recover from illness, and improve their quality of life, and the people who do it work in hospitals, laboratories, clinics, and community spaces. Some of them give direct patient care while others study diseases, develop treatments, or help people build healthier habits. Point to the three Hats printed on the opener page: **MRI Technologist**, **Pediatrician**, and **Dentist**, then ask the page's closing question: what strengths or values do you think people in these roles might need?

Tell students this is the start of a four-week run through Health Science (Wk 3 nursing, Wk 4 dental and medical billing, Wk 5 powerskills, Wk 6 biomedical).

The chapter does not print a pathway list. Walk through the **five Health Science programs of study Irving ISD actually runs**, from the district pages (FYF pp. 84-85: "What is Happening at My District?"):

1. **Patient Care Technician**: direct patient care, monitoring vital signs, assisting with daily living activities, and supporting medical staff through clinical experiences. This is the nursing week's home.
2. **Medical Billing & Coding**: health care administration, medical coding systems, insurance processes, documentation, and reimbursement.
3. **Dental Assistant**: chairside assisting, sterilization techniques, and patient care in clinical settings.
4. **Emergency Medical Technician (EMT)**: emergency response, patient assessment, trauma care, and life-saving techniques.
5. **Biomedical Sciences**: human body systems, disease, medical research and treatment, and laboratory procedures.

Point out that Health Science students at Singley Academy job-shadow at local hospitals and dental offices, and that the labs there use programmable patient simulators whose manikins display changing vital signs. That is the professional version of the monitor students build on Day 3.

**Be the Decision Maker (FYF p. 59):** Read the opener's boxed prompt aloud. Students are helping create a health fair for families in their community. What would they include to help people learn about healthy habits, exercise, nutrition, or mental wellness in a way that is fun and memorable?

**Think-Pair-Share:** 30 seconds of silent Think, then Partner A for 30 seconds, then Partner B for 30 seconds. Post two stems so every pair has a way in: "One booth I would set up is _____ because _____" and "Families would remember _____ better than a poster because _____." Call on two pairs to share, chosen from what you heard while circulating rather than from raised hands. Listen for pairs who name the health worker who would staff the booth, not just the activity.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    The health fair prompt is the bridge to Day 3's micro:bit project. When a pair proposes a blood pressure check or a temperature station, highlight that those tools are electronic and someone PROGRAMMED them to work. That is the connection to the vital signs monitor build.

## Activity 2: Health Science App Exploration + Career Research (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 86: "App Exploration") + CCE career research worksheet

Nursing is not one career. It is a ladder. A CNA certification takes 4 to 12 weeks, and the same field climbs all the way to Nurse Practitioner with a doctoral degree. Each step up means more education, more responsibility, and more money. Frame the app browse with that idea before students open their Chromebooks.

> [H&L PLATFORM] The App Exploration page walks students through the cluster in order. Today they do the first half: open the Hats & Ladders app, go to Clusters, click the Health Science Cluster, watch the "Cluster Tour" video, play "Game Time" and write down one thing they learned, then open "Pathway Possibilities," answer its questions, and rate at least 1 pathway. Students record their answers on workbook page 86. The rest of the page, the fit and non-fit Hats and the 3 Hat ratings, finishes on Day 5.

**Stop and Jot procedure:** While watching the cluster tour video, students pause TWICE in their engineering notebook (dated entry, labeled "2SW Wk3 Health Science") to write (1) one healthcare career that surprised them and (2) one question they still have about the cluster.

Then students use the Hat Finder to look at the four nursing Hats specifically: **CNA, LVN, RN, Nurse Practitioner**, noting the education path and DFW salary for each. Each student picks ONE nursing career and completes the printed **career research worksheet**. This is the same six-field format students learned in Wk0, so name the connection rather than reteaching it:

- Name of Career
- What Interests You?
- Brief Job Description
- Education/Training Needed
- Average Salary
- What Tools, Equipment, or Skills are Needed for This Career?

Students pull each field from the Hat's profile in the app, then confirm the salary and training figures against at least one additional source, such as the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook.

## Activity 3: Begin the Nursing Career Ladder Worksheet (12 min)

Distribute the Nursing Career Ladder worksheet. The worksheet has 4 rows (one per level) and 5 columns:

| Level | Education | Time to Complete | DFW Salary | One Daily Task |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Walk students through the **CNA row** as a worked example on the projector:

- **Level:** CNA (Certified Nursing Assistant)
- **Education:** State-approved CNA certification program
- **Time to Complete:** 4-12 weeks
- **DFW Salary:** ~$30K-$35K (BLS)
- **One Daily Task:** Help patients with bathing, feeding, mobility

Students then complete the **LVN row** independently using H&L Hat Finder data and BLS supplements.

**DOK 2:** How would you describe the difference between a CNA and an LVN in terms of what they do each day?

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Some students will get stuck on the difference between LVN and LPN. Tell them: "LVN = Licensed Vocational Nurse (Texas term). LPN = Licensed Practical Nurse (other states). Same job, different name." This is a Texas-specific vocabulary item.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a CNA-modeled career research worksheet so students see the level of detail expected. Allow voice-to-text for students who struggle with writing. Provide a Stop and Jot template with sentence stems: "One healthcare career I did not expect is _____. One question I have is _____."
- **Extension:** Students complete TWO career research worksheets and write a 1-sentence comparison: "Career A starts you working faster, but Career B has higher pay long-term."
- **ELL:** Bilingual nursing career glossary (CNA = Asistente de Enfermería Certificado, LVN = Enfermera Vocacional Licenciada, RN = Enfermera Registrada). Pre-teach: Patient = Paciente, Care = Cuidado, Hospital = Hospital.
