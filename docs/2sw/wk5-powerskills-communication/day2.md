## Before class

- Provide **one FYF workbook, one pencil, and one internet-connected device per student**, plus **one teacher display/device** with the written account, three practice cards, and quiz open.
- Open the locked FYF p. 62 account and p. 63 response page.
- Post the written Maria account as well as the image. Students do not have to hold the account in memory.
- Students use FYF pp. 62-63 by default. Print the two-page lab only for assigned no-workbook/no-device students: **one double-sided copy per assigned student; default copies: 0**.
- Keep the fictional-case boundary visible. Real chest pain with shortness of breath needs immediate adult or emergency help.

## Lesson flow

### 1. Warm-up (4 min)

Ask: “What can a listener do when a story has too many details?” Students name one action, such as paraphrase, jot key words, or ask a new question.

### 2. First and second read (7 min)

Read the FYF p. 62 account once with pencils down. Read it again while students mark details. Their job is to notice and record, not diagnose.

### 3. Essential or background? (10 min)

Students complete four rows independently. Accept as essential: persistent chest tightness, time of onset, shortness of breath, pain spreading to shoulder, high-blood-pressure medication/history, and family heart history. Paperwork, a busy week, and carrying a bag or coffee are usually background unless the student gives a sound reason.

### 4. Ask and compare (9 min)

Students write two questions that ask for new information. Then they compare one detail and one question with a partner or complete the written compare route.

Do not reward diagnosis. Reward a clear question that closes an information gap.

### 5. Safe workplace listening practice (12 min)

Students choose one fictional supply, scheduling, or equipment scenario embedded in the Student Guide. They paraphrase, ask one clarifying question, and route the issue to a safe next step. Acting, observer notes, and written analysis are equal routes.

Use **Notice + Question + Next Step** for feedback. Do not use a praise-criticism-praise sandwich.

### 6. Canvas evidence check (5 min)

Students complete the ungraded, retryable Canvas practice quiz. It checks essential detail, a new question, role boundary, paraphrase, and transfer. This quiz is the transfer check; do not assign a separate exit sheet.

### 7. Review, submit, and reset (3 min)

Students review one feedback message, confirm FYF p. 63, and close the quiz. **Trim:** if the class is behind at minute 32, use one practice card instead of multiple role rotations. Do not cut the role-boundary question or close.

## Active monitoring move

- **Second-read CFU:** students point to one exact detail without naming a diagnosis.
- **Lap 1:** check two essential and two background details with reasons. If more than one in four students sorts by “interesting,” model relevance to the listener's task.
- **Lap 2:** check that questions request new information. If students repeat known details, use “What gap is still open?” and revise one question.

## Supports and fallbacks

- Keep the transcript visible and allow read-aloud, text-to-speech, or enlarged print.
- Place these frames beside the task: **“The detail ____ is essential/background because ____.”** and **“I heard you say ____. What ____?”** Pre-teach essential, background, paraphrase, and clarify.
- No student must perform publicly or disclose a health experience.
- If Canvas is unavailable, the two-page lab contains the complete route.
