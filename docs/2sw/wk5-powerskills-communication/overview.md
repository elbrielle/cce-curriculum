# Week 5: Power Up — Communication Skills for Any Career

**2nd Six Weeks** | Health Science Cluster (Skills Focus) | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students work four Powerskill lessons from the *Find Your Future* workbook (Conflict Resolution, Active Listening, Advocacy, Written Communication), practice each through teacher-led healthcare workplace role-plays, set a SMART goal for career exploration, and complete the Xello Time Management Lesson.

**Learning Target:** I can plan a way through three team conflicts, pick out the details that matter when a patient talks, explain what a food desert is and who a nutritionist speaks up for, write a clear message that tells readers what to do, and set a SMART goal for my own career exploration.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my conflict plan fills all three columns for all three conflicts, when my detail sort names two essential details and two background details plus one clarifying question, when my Little Library post has a clear status, a call to action, and two hashtags, and when my SMART goal names what I will do, how it is measured, and the date it is due.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can work a team through three real disagreements and write a plan for each, listen to a patient's account and separate the essential details from the background, explain what a food desert is and who a nutritionist speaks up for, write a clear message that tells the reader exactly what to do, and set a SMART goal for my career exploration."

## Career Connection

Communication is consistently ranked the #1 skill employers want across ALL industries. In healthcare, poor communication can be life-threatening, a nurse who does not listen carefully to a patient's symptoms could miss a critical diagnosis. A medical biller who does not write clearly could cost a hospital thousands of dollars. After two content-intense weeks (Law cluster + Health Science cluster), Week 5 shifts gears from career exploration to the Powerskills that transfer across every career. The mode changes, less reading and discussion of clusters, more hands-on practice of skills, and each workbook Powerskill lesson is paired with a teacher-led healthcare role-play so students see the skill applied in context.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** This week serves every Irving ISD Health Science pathway rather than one program. The workbook's district pages for the cluster (FYF pp. 84-85: "What is Happening at My District?") name the five programs of study students are heading toward: Patient Care Technician, Medical Billing & Coding, Dental Assistant, Emergency Medical Technician, and Biomedical Sciences. They also name the certifications students can earn (Patient Care Tech, Registered Dental Assistant, Phlebotomy, Certified Billing and Coding Specialist, Biotechnology Aptitude and Competency Exam), HOSA as the student organization, and the job-shadowing and ride-along experiences Singley Academy students get at local hospitals and dental offices. Point at that page when students ask why a communication week sits in the middle of the Health Science block. Job shadowing, a ride-along, and a patient simulator lab all put a student in front of a person before they put a student in front of a chart. Communication and goal-setting then transfer past Health Science to every cluster and every job a student will ever have.

## Vocabulary

- **Conflict Resolution:** Working through disagreements in a way that helps everyone feel heard and keeps the project moving forward.
- **Active Listening:** Paying close attention to what someone says, noticing the details that matter most, and responding in a way that shows you understood.
- **Triage:** The first step when patients arrive at an emergency room. A nurse gathers key information so the team can decide who needs care first.
- **Feedback Sandwich:** A CCE protocol for giving feedback, start with a positive comment, share an area for improvement with specific language, end with another positive remark.
- **Advocacy:** The act of supporting or standing up for someone or something, a person, a cause, or an idea.
- **Food Desert:** An area where people have limited access to affordable, nutritious food, usually because grocery stores are far away and transportation is limited.
- **SMART Goal:** A goal that is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.
- **Transferable Skill:** An ability that is valuable across many different careers and industries. Communication, problem-solving, and teamwork are common examples.
- **Time Management:** The ability to plan and control how you spend your time to effectively accomplish your goals.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook does not park its Powerskills in one supplement. It teaches Powerskill lessons inside the cluster chapters and gathers five more into a chapter of their own, Powerskills in Action (printed pp. 139-148). That chapter is this week's home base. Its opener (FYF p. 139: "Exploring the World of POWERSKILLS IN ACTION") puts three unrelated Hats on one page, Electrician, 3D Printing Technician, and Fashion Designer, and asks why two people in very different careers need the same skills. The ten-row reference chart at the front of the book (FYF pp. 12-14: "Introduction to Powerskills") gives the shared vocabulary: Communication, Teamwork, Problem-Solving, Adaptability, Leadership, Time Management, Creativity, Work Ethic, Empathy, and Confidence.

Four Powerskill lessons carry this week:

- **Conflict Resolution** (Day 1): Students work at a smoothie company launching a new flavor while the team argues about the name, the launch date, and who does which marketing job. They plan all three conflicts in a Conflict / Why It Matters / How We Can Solve It table, then design an advertisement poster for the flavor they agreed on (FYF pp. 144-145: "Powerskill: Conflict Resolution").
- **Active Listening** (Day 2): Students are the nurse at an emergency room triage desk. They read a 52-year-old patient's account of chest pain, sort her details into essential and background, write two clarifying questions, and compare what they caught against what a partner caught (FYF pp. 62-63: "Powerskill: Active Listening").
- **Advocacy** (Day 3): Students are a nutritionist proposing a mobile farmers' market for a food desert. They work the need, including the workbook's food desert definition and three community voices, before turning the same skill inward toward their own SMART goal (FYF pp. 134-135: "Powerskill: Advocacy").
- **Written Communication** (Day 4): Students run the social media presence for their neighborhood's Little Libraries. They brainstorm the box's status and write a post with a clear status, a call to action, and at least two hashtags (FYF pp. 147-148: "Powerskill: Written Communication").

Each lesson is paired with a teacher-led healthcare workplace role-play that puts the same skill in a Health Science context.

One thing the workbook does not supply: it asks for constructive feedback at several points but never prints a structure for giving it. The **feedback sandwich** taught on Day 2 and reused on Days 3 and 4 is CCE-authored scaffolding, not a workbook page. Do not send students looking for it in the book.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 1, the "Be the Decision Maker" prompt on the chapter opener (FYF p. 139), where a thunderstorm clears an outdoor concert stage and the event needs a new plan fast. Distinguished: pose it to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "I would use _____ because _____" and "The hardest part is _____, so the team needs someone who can _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who names a Powerskill beyond "communication," and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands. If nearly every pair lands on communication only, name Adaptability and Leadership yourself before the Conflict Resolution setup so the week does not collapse into one word.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 2, Activity 2, the essential-versus-background detail sort on the triage patient (FYF p. 63). Distinguished: give a stated 6 minutes with the table on screen, require reasoning not just a copied phrase, and post two stems: "This detail is essential because it changes _____" and "This detail is background because the team can act without _____." Read the tables over shoulders while students write. If most students list the shoulder pain but nobody lists the shortness of breath or the blood-pressure medication, reread just those two sentences to the whole room before releasing Step 3, rather than fixing tables one desk at a time.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 2, Activity 4, and Day 4, Activity 3, the healthcare role-plays. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway with one target per lap, carrying the clipboard rubric already built for this week (paraphrases before responding, asks a clarifying question, respectful tone, stays in role). Have feedback ready before class for the two known failures: the pair that turns the card into a comedy bit, and the pair where one partner talks for the full three minutes. Immediate and specific beats general praise, so name the move you heard rather than saying "good job." If more than a handful of pairs never paraphrase, pivot and model one 30 second exchange for the whole room instead of coaching pair by pair.
- **Chunking:** Day 1, Activity 3, the three steps of the smoothie conflict activity (FYF pp. 144-145). Distinguished: release one step at a time and say what each step is for before groups start it (Step 1 is reading the conflict, Step 2 is planning it, Step 3 is proving the team is back together), with a check between each: after Step 1 every group states which conflict they think is hardest, after Step 2 every group reads one How We Can Solve It cell aloud. Note which conflict draws the weakest solutions. That conflict becomes the example you reopen during the Day 1 class discussion, and groups that cleared all three rows early start the advertisement poster instead of waiting.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 3, Activity 3, the twenty minutes of SMART goal writing and partner checking. Distinguished: post the three blocks before worksheets come out, Voice 0 for the first ten minutes of independent drafting and Voice 1 for the partner check, a visible countdown for each half, and a stated rule that the Day 1 conflict plans and the SMART worksheet do not share desk space so nothing gets lost. Narrate the students who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If half the room is still stuck on the Measurable box when the countdown hits ten minutes, stop the drafting, put one measurable example on the board, and give the time back rather than letting the partner check start with blank worksheets.

## Summative Assessment

**SMART Goal + Communication Reflection (Day 5):** Students submit (1) the polished SMART goal worksheet from Day 3 and (2) a 4-sentence reflection paragraph: "The transferable skill I improved most this week is _____. It connects to the career of _____ because _____. I will practice this skill in [Wk 6 / outside class] by _____." Scored on: SMART goal quality (d(4)(A)), transferable skill identification (d(4)(B)), and whether the named career genuinely uses the named skill (d(4)(B)).

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning

- SMART goal examples and non-examples to clarify each component
- Pre-built role-play scripts with dialogue suggestions
- Sentence stems for the reflection paragraph
- Pair work for all role-plays (no solo performance required)

### Extensions

- Create a Communication Skills Guide for one chosen career pathway listing the top 5 communication skills needed
- Finish the workbook's advocacy project (FYF p. 135, Steps 2-4): a mobile farmers' market flyer with a mission statement, at least 10 nutritious offerings, and a reason to use it
- Compare CareerOneStop Skills Matcher results to the core personality type recorded in Week 0 from the H&L "Discover Your Core" task, what is the overlap?
- Lead a small-group review of one Powerskill lesson for peers who missed a day

### ELL Language Support

- Pre-teach: Conflict = Conflicto, Listen = Escuchar, Feedback = Retroalimentación, Advocacy = Defensa, Goal = Meta, Skill = Habilidad, Transferable = Transferible
- Bilingual role-play cards with key phrases in both languages
- Bilingual SMART goal worksheet
- Pair ESL students with bilingual peers for role-plays
- The workbook reads well above grade level. Read the scenario paragraphs aloud (FYF pp. 62, 134, 144, 147) and allow browser translation for any independent reading
