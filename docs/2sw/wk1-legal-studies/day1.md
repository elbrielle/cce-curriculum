## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: If you were accused of something you did not do, who would you want on your side, and what skills would they need?**

Take 2-3 student responses, then add the roles students usually miss: paralegals, court reporters, judges, and investigators. Each has a different job and education path within the legal system.

---

## Activity 1: Exploring the World of Law and Public Safety (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 39: "Exploring the World of LAW AND PUBLIC SAFETY")

Open the lesson by connecting the 1st Six Weeks (IT, Manufacturing) to today's pivot into the Law cluster. Open the workbook to page 39 and read the chapter opener together: this cluster brings together fast action and careful decision-making. Some roles respond in the moment during an emergency, while others investigate, analyze, and piece together what happened. Together they keep order, solve problems, and protect communities.

Three Hats appear on the opener page. Read each one aloud and ask students whether it fits more closely with fast action or with investigation and analysis, and why. Do not settle the category for them; a Judge or Warden can reasonably draw both answers.

- **Judge**
- **Firefighter**
- **Fish and Game Warden**

**Be the Decision Maker (FYF p. 39):** You are the city safety inspector for a music festival on opening night. During a final equipment test, part of the main stage begins shaking. Some engineers say the festival can continue safely, while others think the structure could become dangerous once the crowd arrives. Thousands of fans are already waiting outside. Do you allow the festival to continue, delay the event, or shut it down completely? What information would help you make the final decision?

**Think-Pair-Share:** 30 seconds of silent Think, then Partner A talks for 30 seconds, then Partner B for 30 seconds. Two pairs share with the whole class. Listen for students who name the information they would go get before deciding, not just the decision itself.

Then map the cluster the way Irving ISD maps it. The district pages (FYF p. 56: "What is Happening at My District?") name **two programs of study**:

1. **Legal Studies:** legal research, legal writing, rules of procedure, case management, law office technology, and legal ethics, with concentrations in criminal, family, and workplace law.
2. **Criminal Justice:** the history, organization, and functions of local, state, and federal law enforcement, plus criminal law, investigation, and appropriate legal response to violations.

This week takes Legal Studies. Wk 2 takes the first-responder side of the cluster.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    The Law and Public Safety cluster is broad. Students often think only of lawyers, and the opener's three Hats are deliberately not what they expect. Use the Fish and Game Warden card to open the range, then point at the two Irving programs of study on page 56 so the cluster has a local shape before students browse the app.

## Activity 2: Law Cluster Exploration + Legal Career Research (25 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 58: "App Exploration"), Steps 1-3 and the Hat rating step

Use the supplied **Legal Career Evidence Cards** as the dependable research route. They use dated BLS national medians on one consistent basis. H&L or Xello may supplement the lesson when available, especially for the district-provided local view, but students must label the geography, year, and salary type rather than mixing unlike figures. A working vendor login is not required evidence.

**Stop and Jot procedure:** While watching the Law cluster tour video, students pause TWICE in their workbook margin to write:

- **Stop 1:** One legal or public safety career you learned about that you had never heard of before
- **Stop 2:** One question you have about legal careers

Students compare profiles for several legal careers, such as **Lawyer, Paralegal, Judge, Court Reporter, and Legal Secretary**. For each source, they label the salary type, geography, and data year instead of treating every figure as a DFW starting salary.

Each student then selects ONE legal career and completes the **legal career research worksheet**, using the research format taught in Wk0. The fields are:

- Name of Career
- What Interests You?
- Brief Job Description
- Education/Training Needed
- Average Salary
- What Tools, Equipment, or Skills are Needed for This Career?

**DOK 2:** Compare the common preparation and May 2024 U.S. median pay for a paralegal and a lawyer. Which tradeoff matters more to you right now, and what evidence supports that choice?

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Students often overlook Court Reporter and Paralegal. Use them to compare different legal-career routes, but verify current preparation and pay facts in the dated source instead of teaching a fixed salary or program length from memory.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pre-fill the career research worksheet with the "Court Reporter" career as a model so students see the level of detail expected. Allow students to dictate answers if writing is a barrier.
- **Extension:** Students complete TWO career research worksheets and write a one-sentence comparison: "Career A is a better fit for me than Career B because _____." Students who finish early open the H&L app's "From the Field" interview videos for this cluster and note one piece of advice a professional gave.
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Lawyer = Abogado, Paralegal = Asistente Legal, Judge = Juez, Court Reporter = Taquígrafo Judicial. Provide the career research worksheet with bilingual field labels. The workbook opener reads above grade level, so read page 39 aloud.
