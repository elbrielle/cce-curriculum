# Week 4: Dental Science Careers + Health Informatics

**2nd Six Weeks** | Health Science Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students work the two dental Career Climb activities in the *Find Your Future* workbook, "Smile Squad" and "Perfect Toothbrush," research dental careers and the training each one takes, connect Xello Education Experience and School Subjects at Work to healthcare careers, and run a focused medical coding simulation that introduces Health Informatics as a short-credential entry into healthcare. Dental is the primary focus, anchored on the Dental Assistant program of study named on the workbook's Irving ISD district pages; medical billing and coding appears on a single day as career-exploration content.

**Learning Target:** I can read a patient's clues to rate cavity risk, design a toothbrush that helps oral health, tell how much training four dental careers take, and sort a health career as high-skill, high-wage, or high-demand.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my Cavity Risk Assessment marks all five clue rows and names the evidence behind my recommendation, when my toothbrush sketch labels each part and says how it helps, when my Career Comparison worksheet lists education time, certification, DFW salary, and job growth for three careers, and when my recommendation paragraph names a career, its training time, its DFW salary, and the Irving ISD program behind it.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe careers in medical billing and dental science, rate a patient's cavity risk from evidence and recommend prevention, design a toothbrush using my knowledge of dental health, classify a healthcare career as high-skill/high-wage/high-demand using BLS data, and explain how my current school subjects connect to specific health careers."

## Career Connection

Medical billing and coding specialists translate medical procedures into standardized codes for insurance billing, while dental assistants and hygienists work directly with dentists and patients. Both fields have relatively short training timelines and high demand in DFW.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** The workbook's district pages name five Health Science programs of study: Patient Care Technician, Medical Billing & Coding, Dental Assistant, Emergency Medical Technician, and Biomedical Sciences (FYF pp. 84-85: "What is Happening at My District?"). This week uses two. **Dental Assistant**, the focus of Days 1 and 2, covers chairside assisting, dental procedures, sterilization, and patient care; at **Singley Academy** it leads to the Registered Dental Assistant certification. **Medical Billing & Coding**, the focus of Day 4, covers health care administration, insurance processing, coding systems, and reimbursement. Page 85 also names job shadowing at local hospitals and dental offices, HOSA, and labs where students practice with patient simulators and dentistry and phlebotomy tools. Its Career Exploration Connection explains that grants and scholarships usually do not have to be repaid and loans do, which supplies the distinction used in the Day 3 college-credit discussion.

> [VERIFY] Irving ISD's public CTE offerings page lists Singley Academy at the School of Health Sciences level and does not name Medical Billing & Coding as a separate campus pathway, while the 2026 district workbook lists it as a Health Science program of study. Confirm the campus and course sequence with your CTE coordinator before advising a student toward it.

## Vocabulary

- **Medical Billing and Coding:** Translating healthcare services into standardized codes (ICD-10, CPT) for insurance billing. Requires specialized certification.
- **Pediatric Dentist:** A dentist who specializes in babies, children, and teens, from first teeth through adult smiles. The "Smile Squad" activity puts students on a pediatric dental team.
- **Cavity Risk Assessment:** A chart a dental team uses to rate how likely a patient is to get cavities, based on clues such as brushing habits, sugar intake, past cavities, family history, and X-ray results.
- **Dental Assistant:** A healthcare professional who works alongside dentists, preparing patients, sterilizing instruments, taking X-rays, and assisting during procedures.
- **Dental Hygienist:** A dental professional who cleans teeth, screens for oral disease, and educates patients. Requires an associate degree.
- **High-Demand Occupation:** A career with many job openings relative to qualified workers. Indicated by low unemployment, strong job growth (5%+), and active hiring.
- **College Credit in High School:** Opportunities like dual credit, AP exams, or articulated CTE credit that allow students to earn college credits before graduation.
- **Health Informatics:** The intersection of healthcare and information technology, medical records, billing systems, health data analysis.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Health Science printed pages 59-86. This week uses two Career Climb activities from the dental half of the chapter:

- **Smile Squad** (Day 1): Students join a pediatric dental team. They read the chart of Mia, a 4-year-old patient, examine her five X-rays from the teacher's Climber Notes deck, complete a five-clue Cavity Risk Assessment, choose a prevention recommendation, and defend the evidence behind it in a team check-in (FYF pp. 69-71: "Smile Squad"). The printed steps jump from Step 3 to Step 5; the team check-in is the final step and no page is missing.
- **Perfect Toothbrush** (Day 2): Students are the dental expert on a design panel. They research bristle type, handle, brush head, and features, sketch and label a toothbrush, explain how each element helps oral health, and compare designs with a partner (FYF pp. 72-73: "Perfect Toothbrush"). The work requires both dental knowledge and design decisions.

The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet the Health Science pathways in the Hats & Ladders app through the Pathway Possibilities and Hat rating steps on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 86), and they meet Irving ISD's own programs of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 84-85). The career research worksheet students fill in on Day 1 is the CCE format taught in Wk0 and carries no workbook page.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 1, the "Smile Squad" Team Check-In (FYF p. 71). Distinguished: pose "what is your strongest single piece of evidence for Mia's risk level," hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds. Post two stems at different levels: "I rated Mia _____ because the _____ row showed _____" and "My partner and I disagree about _____, and the clue that would settle it is _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who cites a chart row rather than the sugar story alone, and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard. If most pairs are arguing from sugar only, reproject the X-ray slides before Activity 2 so the evidence base widens.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 2, Activity 3, the classification sentence on the Career Comparison worksheet. Distinguished: give a 3 minute box, put one exemplar on the board ("I classify Dental Hygienist as high-wage because BLS lists a DFW median near $82K on a 2-year associate degree"), and post two stems: "I classify _____ as _____ because the BLS data shows _____" and "Career A requires _____ education and pays _____, so I classify it as _____ because _____." Read jots over shoulders as students write. If most classify on salary alone, pull the room back to the job-growth column before the exit ticket, because the ticket's check marks depend on all three dimensions.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 5, Activity 1, the ten minutes of H&L favorites, and Activity 3, the recommendation paragraph. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway with one target per lap. Lap 1 target is that the Climber Profile actually shows 4-5 Health Science favorites, not that a heart was clicked. Lap 2 target is that the paragraph names a career, a training time, a DFW salary, and an Irving ISD program. Have feedback ready before class for the two known misconceptions: a heart that never saved because the student left the tile too fast, and a recommendation with no salary figure attached. Talk to 10-12 students, prioritizing the ones whose favorites mix differs from their neighbors'. If more than a handful of paragraphs are missing the Irving ISD program line, pause the room and reproject the district Programs of Study page rather than repeating it desk by desk.
- **Chunking:** Day 4, the three coding rounds. Distinguished: say what each round is for before releasing it. Round 1 is three simple charts after one worked example, self-graded against a revealed key, with 100 percent as the target before anyone moves on. Round 2 opens with the 30 second specificity scaffold and a second worked example, then five ambiguous charts. Round 3 is the optional speed round. Use the Round 1 reveal as the gate: if a chunk of the room missed the worked example's code, reteach the reference sheet before Round 2 rather than after, and cut Round 3 to buy back the time.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 3, Activity 3, the 4-3-3 college credit block. Distinguished: post the three blocks before the Chromebooks close, Voice 0 for the two Xello activities, Voice 1 for the 3 minute turn-and-talk on which credit option fits the student, Voice 3 for the whole-class share, and run a visible countdown on each of the three segments. Say where Chromebooks go, closed and pushed to the top of the desk, so the turn-and-talk is not conducted over a screen. Narrate the pairs meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If the room is still finishing Xello when the intro is due, cut the whole-class share to two volunteers and protect the turn-and-talk, which is where the processing happens.

## Summative Assessment

**Health Science Career Comparison + Recommendation Paragraph (Day 5):** Students submit (1) the completed Career Comparison worksheet (3 careers, 5 data points each) and (2) a 4-sentence recommendation paragraph: "I would recommend [career] to a friend who wants to work in healthcare quickly because [education time] + [salary] + [job outlook] + [Irving ISD pathway connection]." Scored on: data accuracy (d(5)(B)), classification reasoning (d(5)(B)), education pathway knowledge (d(2)(A)), and college credit awareness (d(3)(B)).

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning

- Cavity Risk Assessment with the brushing-habits row marked as a worked model before students start the chart
- Pre-filled Career Comparison worksheet with the Medical Biller row modeled
- Toothbrush design template with bristle, handle, and head sections pre-labeled
- Simplified ICD-10 reference sheet (5 codes instead of 10) for the coding simulation
- Sentence stems for the recommendation paragraph

### Extensions

- Compare ICD-10 and CPT coding systems, when is each used?
- Build a 4-year plan for the Singley Dental pathway including dual credit and AP options
- Calculate the return on investment for each health science certification (training cost ÷ first-year salary)
- Design a SECOND toothbrush for a different population (toddler, senior, person with arthritis)

### ELL Language Support

- Pre-teach: Dental = Dental, Toothbrush = Cepillo de Dientes, Bristle = Cerda, Medical Billing = Facturación Médica, Code = Código, Certification = Certificación
- Bilingual ICD-10 reference sheet with Spanish procedure descriptions
- Visual toothbrush design template with picture prompts
- Pair ESL students with bilingual peers for the medical coding simulation
