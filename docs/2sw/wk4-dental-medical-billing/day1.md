## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Think about the last time you visited a dentist's office. How many DIFFERENT people worked there? What did each one do?**

Take 3-4 responses. Students typically name the dentist and maybe a "dental assistant." Bridge: "A working dental office runs on 4-6 career roles. Today you join one of those teams, and your team has to decide what a 4-year-old patient needs."

## Activity 1: Smile Squad — Mia's Cavity Risk (20 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 69-71: "Smile Squad"), a Career Climb activity

Open the workbook to page 69 and read the opener together: pediatric dentists care for babies, kids, and teens, check for cavities, clean teeth, teach brushing and flossing, and use tools like X-rays to see what is happening beneath the surface so problems get caught early. Students spend the activity on a pediatric dental team. Their job is to review patient information, assess risk, examine X-rays, and report what they notice so the team can decide on treatment and prevention.

The printed activity runs Step 1, Step 2, Step 3, and then a step labeled Step 5. There is no Step 4 and no missing page. Name that for the room before students open the book so nobody spends the period hunting for it.

**Step 1: Meet Your Patient (3 min).** Students read Mia's chart on page 69. Mia is 4 years old. She sometimes brushes at night, and her parents do not always help. She drinks juice and milk through the day and snacks on crackers, cookies, and fruit. She had one cavity filled last year, and an older sibling has had several. Her X-rays show a small dark spot on one lower molar and plaque on some front teeth. All of her baby teeth are present and developing normally.

**Step 2: Look at Mia's X-Rays (7 min).** The workbook sends students to the teacher for Climber Notes here. Project the deck: slide 2 is the observation chart students use to guide what they are looking for, and slides 3 through 7 are Mia's five X-rays (Climber Notes: "Smile Squad", slides 2-7). Hold each X-ray on screen long enough for students to write on the Observations lines on page 69.

**Stop and Jot:** 45 seconds per X-ray, with the slide 2 observation chart left on the board the whole time. Two stems for students who stall: "On X-ray #___ I see _____" and "This one looks different from a healthy tooth because _____."

**Step 3: Fill Out the Chart on Mia's Cavity Risk (7 min).** Students complete the Cavity Risk Assessment on page 70. Five clue rows, brushing habits, sugary foods and drinks, past cavities, family history, and X-ray results, each rated Low, Medium, or High Risk. Students mark each row from the case, circle Mia's overall risk level, then check what the dental team should recommend from the printed options: brush twice a day with help from parents, fluoride treatment, drink fewer sugary drinks, more dental visits, healthier snacks, or one of their own. The page closes by asking which clues drove BOTH the risk level and the recommendation, so students have to name evidence, not just a rating.

**Team Check-In (3 min).** Students share their answers with a partner or small group and work the workbook's three questions on page 71: did you choose the same risk level, what evidence did each person use, and do you agree on the best recommendation. Each student owes their partner one positive comment and one helpful suggestion, which is the page's own requirement.

**Think-Pair-Share:** 30 seconds of silent Think on "what is my strongest single piece of evidence," then Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds. Two pairs share with the whole class, chosen from what you heard while circulating rather than from raised hands.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Most students rate Mia high risk on the sugar row alone. Push for the whole chart: several of her clues sit in the medium band and only some sit high, so a defensible answer names which rows moved the rating. The rating itself matters less than whether the student can point to the rows behind it.

**DOK 3:** Two students rate Mia's risk differently but recommend the same prevention plan. What does that tell you about how a dental team uses evidence to make decisions?

## Activity 2: Dental Career Comparison (8 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 84-85: "What is Happening at My District?")

Project a quick comparison of the four dental roles using BLS and H&L data:

- **Dental Assistant:** 9-12 month certificate. DFW salary ~$42K. Supports the dentist chairside.
- **Dental Hygienist:** 2-year associate degree. DFW salary ~$82K. Cleans teeth, screens for oral disease, educates patients.
- **General Dentist:** 4-year bachelor's + 4-year DDS/DMD. DFW salary ~$170K. Diagnoses and treats oral disease.
- **Orthodontist:** Dentist + 2-3 years residency. DFW salary ~$250K+. Specializes in teeth/jaw alignment.

The key value proposition: students can enter the dental field in under a year (Dental Assistant) or invest in 2 years (Hygienist) for a career with strong DFW demand and above-median pay.

Then turn to the district pages and read the **Dental Assistant** entry out of the Programs of Study sidebar (FYF p. 84): it prepares students for careers in dental care by focusing on chairside assisting, dental procedures, sterilization techniques, and patient care in clinical settings. Page 85 adds what the program looks like day to day, including job shadowing at local hospitals and dental offices, the Registered Dental Assistant certification, HOSA as the student organization, and labs stocked with tools students use to practice dentistry and phlebotomy skills.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Many students assume "only the dentist makes real money." Dental Hygienist is a counterexample: 2-year associate, ~$82K DFW salary, strong growth, no dental school. Show the BLS job outlook for Hygienists (9% growth, "much faster than average"). This is the dental-industry analog to the Medical Billing trade-off on Day 4.

## Activity 3: Career Research + Begin Career Comparison (15 min)

Students complete the **career research worksheet** for ONE dental career of their choice (most will pick Dental Hygienist or Dentist). The fields are the same ones taught in Wk0 and used in Wk 3: Name of Career, What Interests You?, Brief Job Description, Education/Training Needed, Average Salary, Tools/Equipment/Skills.

> [H&L PLATFORM] Open the Hats & Ladders app, go to Clusters, and click the Health Science Cluster. Students use Hat Finder to pull up the dental Hats, then open "Pathway Possibilities" to see where those Hats sit inside the cluster. Dental Assistant is the fastest entry (9-12 month cert), Dental Hygienist requires an associate (2 years), and Dentist requires 8 years of school (bachelor's plus DDS/DMD). Students record their cluster notes and Hat ratings on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 86) as they browse.

Then they fill in the **first row of the Career Comparison worksheet** (Dental Hygienist). The worksheet has 3 rows (Dental Hygienist, RN, Medical Biller) and 5 columns:

| Career | Education Time | Certification | DFW Salary | Job Growth (BLS) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The Dental Hygienist row example:

- **Education Time:** 2-year associate degree
- **Certification:** State dental hygiene license (Texas State Board of Dental Examiners)
- **DFW Salary:** ~$82K (BLS)
- **Job Growth:** 9% (BLS, "much faster than average")

Students will complete the RN row on Day 2 and the Medical Biller row on Day 4 (when the Health Informatics career appears).

**DOK 2:** How would you describe the difference between a Dental Assistant and a Dental Hygienist to someone who thinks they are the same job?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Mark the brushing-habits row of the Cavity Risk Assessment as a worked model before students start Step 3. Pre-fill the career research worksheet with the Dental Hygienist career modeled, and highlight the BLS sections students need to read for the salary and growth fields.
- **Extension:** Research the Singley Academy Dental pathway course sequence. Which specific classes would a 9th grader take? Which certifications does the pathway confer by graduation?
- **ELL:** Bilingual career research worksheet. Pre-teach: Dental = Dental, Hygienist = Higienista, Assistant = Asistente, Cavity = Caries, X-ray = Radiografía, Certification = Certificación, Associate degree = Grado asociado.
