# Day 4: Outbreak Investigators — Build the Response Plan

## Lesson Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 50 minutes |
| **Objectives** | Choose the tests that confirm the outbreak source; estimate the impact of an uncontrolled outbreak; write immediate actions and a prevention plan; present the response plan to another team |
| **TEKS** | d(1)(C) |
| **5E Phases** | Engage: Warm-Up · Explore: Confirming tests and estimated impact · Extend: Immediate actions and prevention plan · Explain: Response-plan presentations · Evaluate: Exit Ticket |
| **Deliverable** | Completed Response Plan (investigation steps, estimated impact, immediate actions, prevention plan, each with written reasoning) + 3-minute pair presentation |

---

## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: The city announces that your tap water may be unsafe. What is the FIRST thing your family should do TODAY? What should the city do so this never happens again?**

Take 3-4 responses. Bridge: those are two different jobs. One keeps people safe right now, the other keeps the town safe next year. A response plan has to do both, and today the class writes one.

---

## Activity 1: Confirm the Source and Estimate the Impact (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 77: "Outbreak Investigators", Step 4)

Pairs return to yesterday's Investigation Report and open the workbook's response-plan page. Start with the workbook's Helpful Info for Investigators box and read the five facts aloud, because the whole plan is built on them:

- Waterborne illnesses often spread through contaminated drinking water
- Common symptoms include stomach pain, vomiting, diarrhea, and fever
- Boiling water can kill many harmful germs
- Scientists test water by collecting samples and checking for bacteria
- Flooding can cause dirty water to mix with clean water sources

**Investigation Steps.** Pairs check every step they would take to confirm the source: test water from the well, test river water, ask people about their symptoms, compare who got sick and who did not, or write their own step in the Other line. Then they answer the workbook's follow-up in writing: why are these steps important?

**Estimated Impact.** Pairs choose how many people could be affected if the outbreak is not controlled, a few dozen, hundreds, or thousands, and explain the reasoning. The town has about 2,000 residents and nearly everyone drinks from the same system, so the estimate has to square with the population number from the Day 3 case file.

**Active Monitoring:** Walk one lap per part. Lap 1 target: does the pair's set of investigation steps include at least one test of a water source AND one comparison of sick to healthy residents? Lap 2 target: does the estimated-impact reasoning cite the 2,000-resident population or the shared water system rather than a feeling? Have the response ready before class for the two predictable misses: pairs who check every box without explaining any, and pairs who estimate thousands in a town of 2,000. If more than a handful miss the population check, pause the room and reproject the Fairview Edge background paragraph instead of correcting pair by pair.

---

## Activity 2: Immediate Actions and Prevention Plan (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 78: "Outbreak Investigators", Step 4)

**Immediate Actions.** Pairs check what people should do right away to stay safe: boil water before drinking, avoid drinking tap water, drink bottled water, close the water source, or their own action in the Other line. They then explain their choices in writing. Push on the trade-offs: closing the town's water source protects people but leaves 2,000 residents with no tap water at all, so the plan has to say what replaces it.

**Prevention Plan.** Pairs check how this outbreak could be prevented in the future: regularly test water quality, protect the water source from flooding, improve water treatment systems, educate the community about safe water, or their own idea. They explain those choices too.

Name the difference out loud, because it is the day's core idea. Immediate actions treat the emergency that already happened. Prevention steps change the system so the next flood does not become the next outbreak. Public health careers are built around the second one.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Pairs that check all four prevention boxes with one shared sentence have not made a plan, they have made a list. Ask which single prevention step they would fund first if the town could only afford one, and make them defend it with a clue from the case file. That question turns the checklist back into reasoning.

---

## Activity 3: Present the Response Plan (12 min)

Each pair joins another pair and presents its plan in 3 minutes. Every presentation covers four things:

1. **The source we identified** and the evidence that points to it
2. **The tests we would run** to confirm it, and why those tests
3. **Two immediate actions** and what each one protects people from
4. **One prevention step** and why we would fund that one first

The listening pair responds with one strength they heard and one question they still have. Then the pairs switch roles. This is a check on reasoning, not a performance, so keep the feedback to those two items and move on.

Close with the workbook's class discussion questions (p. 78): what clues helped you figure out the source of the illness, what other ways could epidemiologists help the community, and why do epidemiologists need to know what activity people were doing before they got sick?

**DOK 4:** Fairview Edge can afford exactly one of these: a new water treatment system, yearly water testing for the whole town, or a flood barrier around the well. Which one would prevent the most future illness? Defend the choice with evidence from the case file.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the response plan with the Helpful Info facts printed at the top of the page so students do not have to flip back. Provide sentence stems for the written explanations: "We would test _____ first because _____" and "People should _____ right away because _____."
- **Extension:** Write the one-paragraph public notice the town would post at the grocery store and the school. It must say what happened, what people should do today, and when the town will test again. Keep it short enough for someone to read while walking past.
- **ELL:** Bilingual response-plan glossary (Prevention = Prevención, Treatment = Tratamiento, Boil = Hervir, Safe = Seguro, Test = Analizar, Water source = Fuente de agua). The plan is built from checkboxes, so ELL students can complete the choices independently and use the stems for the written explanations. Pair ELL students with a bilingual partner as co-presenter.
