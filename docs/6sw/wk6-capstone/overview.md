# Week 6: The Grand Finale — My Career Ladder Capstone

**6th Six Weeks** | Cross-Cluster Capstone | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students complete and present the *Find Your Future* Capstone Project, an eight-rung Career Ladder built across the whole year. This week they debrief the Rung 4 Strengths Interview assigned as a take-home two weeks earlier, audit all eight rungs against the workbook's own completion check, transfer the ladder onto a written Career Plan, choose one of the workbook's seven presentation formats, deliver that presentation to the class against the workbook's 32-point rubric, download the finalized H&L Career Plan, write the Rung 8 motivation plan, and close the course with the workbook's Final Reflection.

**Learning Target:** I can build all eight rungs of my Career Ladder, present my ladder in the format I choose, and explain what I learned about myself this year.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when all eight rungs in my workbook are filled in, when my written Career Plan names my career goal, my training and pay research, my three goals, and my Irving ISD pathway and campus, when my presentation shows all eight rungs in the format I chose, and when my Final Reflection names the rung that mattered most to me and one real step I will take next.

## Career Connection

Over 36 weeks students have explored every CTE cluster: Manufacturing, IT, Law & Public Service, Health Science, Agriculture, Hospitality, Human Services, Business / Marketing / Finance, Aviation, Automotive, Skilled Trades, Architecture, Civil Engineering, Construction, Real Estate, Education, Arts & A/V, and Sales. By this week they have a resume, a cover letter, a thank-you letter, a mock interview, references identified, a personal budget, and dozens of researched career profiles. The capstone chapter opener puts three more Hats in front of them, Air Traffic Controller, Dentist, and Cement Mason, and asks the question the whole project answers: how can you prepare for your future career (FYF p. 277)?

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** Students select and present the SPECIFIC Irving ISD CTE pathway they want to begin in 9th grade. Any pathway from any Irving ISD high school explored during the year is valid. The Capstone chapter prints no district page of its own; the local detail lands in Rung 7's "What's Available to Me?" prompt (FYF p. 296), which asks students what their own high school offers that connects to their goals.

## Vocabulary

- **Career Ladder:** The workbook's name for the capstone project. Eight rungs, each one a part of the student's career journey, presented as one project.
- **Rung:** One numbered part of the Career Ladder. Rung 1 is who you are. Rung 8 is how you stay motivated.
- **Capstone:** A culminating project demonstrating everything learned throughout a course.
- **Career Plan:** The H&L app tool and the written document that hold the career goal, the pathway, the high school course sequence, the postsecondary requirements, and the goals.
- **Postsecondary Goal:** Plan for after high school: 4-year university, 2-year community college, trade school, military service, apprenticeship, or direct employment with certification.
- **SMART Goal:** A goal that is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-Bound. Rung 6 asks for three of them.
- **Affirmation:** A short, positive statement students tell themselves when the work gets hard. Written in Rung 8.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* Capstone chapter runs printed pages 277 through 300. It is one project rather than a set of separate activities: students build a Career Ladder with eight rungs and then present it. The chapter opener frames a career as a ladder that may be straight, may twist and turn, and may have a few broken steps to fix along the way (FYF p. 277).

- **Capstone Project Requirements** (FYF p. 278): a one-page preview of all eight rungs, with a two-sentence description of each. Rung 1 Who Are You, Rung 2 Career Goal, Rung 3 Career Deep Dive, Rung 4 Strengths Interview, Rung 5 Local Connection, Rung 6 Goal Setting, Rung 7 High School Plan, Rung 8 Stay Motivated.
- **Presentation Options** (FYF p. 279): seven named formats plus an open option. Comic or Storyboard, "Unboxing My Future" Video, TED Talk-Style Speech, Podcast Interviewing Your Future Self, Essay, Slideshow, Website, and "have another idea, discuss with your teacher." Whatever the format, all eight rungs must be included, the ideas must be clear and complete, and the work must show effort and creativity.
- **Rubric** (FYF p. 280): eight categories scored at four levels for 32 total points. All 8 Rungs Completed, Career Research, Personal Connection, Goal Setting, Interview Reflection, Creativity and Design, Presenter Delivery, and Participation and Collaboration. This is the published rubric students hold in their own books, so it is the scoring standard for the week.
- **Rung 4: Strengths Interview** (FYF pp. 287-289): the interview itself is a take-home assigned two weeks earlier. The "Make Meaning of What You Heard" page (FYF p. 289) is the classroom half and it lands on Day 1.
- **Rung 8: Stay Motivated** (FYF pp. 297-298): an affirmation, two to four hashtags, three Strategies to Stay on Track including who to go to for support, and a Picture Your Future prompt.
- **Prepare & Present** (FYF p. 299): a three-question completion check across the whole ladder plus the list of what the presentation has to show and four delivery tips.
- **Final Reflection** (FYF p. 300): four closing questions that end the book and the course.
- **The Career Iceberg** (FYF pp. 6-8): a World of Work activity that opens the workbook rather than closing it. The printed example is a sports trophy, and pages 7 and 8 are blank iceberg frames labeled Electrician and Nurse for students to research and fill in.

This week does not rebuild every rung. Rungs 1-3 (FYF pp. 281-286) return from mid-year, Rung 5 (FYF pp. 290-291) from last week, and Rungs 6-7 (FYF pp. 292-296) from the course-mapping week. This week covers the requirements page, the rubric, the Rung 4 debrief, Rung 8, Prepare & Present, and the Final Reflection.

The Career Plan students download from H&L this week updates the d(8) artifact produced in 4SW Wk1-Wk2. Students can share it with families and Irving ISD high school counselors during the 8th-to-9th grade transition.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 1, the printed trophy iceberg the workbook asks partners to read together (FYF p. 6). Distinguished: pose the book's own question to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "On top I see _____, underneath I see _____" and "Winning first place is really about _____ because _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who can name something underneath that is not practice time. If most pairs only name the visible prize, add three items from your own career to the projected iceberg before releasing Step 2, so the personal icebergs do not come back with two words under the water line.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 5, Activity 1, the written warm-up sentence and the Final Reflection sections (FYF p. 300). Distinguished: give 90 seconds for the warm-up with the prompt on screen, name the time box for each reflection section before students start it, and offer two stems for the hardest section: "The rung that mattered most to me was _____ because _____" and "My next step in real life is _____, and I will do it by _____." Read the "what I learned about myself" answers over shoulders while students write, mark three that are specific, and ask those three students first during the celebration share-out so the room hears strong models rather than one-word answers.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 1, Activity 2, the Rung 4 debrief and the ladder check. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times with one target per lap, carrying a second device with the H&L app dashboard open so Career Plan completion is visible as you go. Lap 1, interview notes are actually on page 288. Lap 2, all five Make Meaning prompts on page 289 are answered. Lap 3, Rungs 1-3 and 5-7 are filled in and the blanks are flagged for Day 2. Have feedback ready before class for the two predictable problems: a student who copied the adult's words without answering whether they agree, and a student whose Rung 5 job posting is a national listing rather than a local one. If more than a handful arrive with no interview at all, pull that group to one table and run the interview questions with an available staff adult during Activity 3 instead of coaching them one desk at a time.
- **Chunking:** Day 2, Activity 1, the eight sections of the written Career Plan. Distinguished: release the plan in three chunks rather than all eight sections at once, and say what each chunk is for before students start it. Chunk 1 is Rungs 1-3, who you are and the career you chose. Chunk 2 is Rungs 4-5, what a trusted adult sees in you and what an employer actually asks for. Chunk 3 is Rungs 6-7, the three goals and the high school plan. Model Section 2 out loud with a career nobody in the class chose before releasing Chunk 1, so students hear the level of detail the rubric's Career Research row asks for, and close each chunk with a thumbs check before releasing the next. Note which chunk stalls the most, because that chunk becomes a three minute re-teach at the start of Activity 2, and students who cleared all three chunks start planning their presentation format early instead of waiting.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Days 3 and 4, Activity 1, the capstone presentations. Distinguished: post the three blocks before the first presenter, Voice 0 while a slideshow or video loads, Voice 3 for the presenter, Voice 1 for the two stars and a wish talk, and run a visible countdown for every presenter set to whichever slot length you chose. Define where presenters stand and where feedback slips go so transitions do not eat presentation time. Narrate the students who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If the rotation is running long by the third presenter, trim the peer talk to one star only and announce the change before the next presenter begins.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning

- Career Plan template with sentence starters for every rung section
- Allow note cards (or a printed slide deck) during the capstone presentation
- Option to present 1-on-1 to the teacher instead of the whole class for very anxious students
- Simplified Final Reflection with guided questions instead of open prompts
- Pre-built iceberg with example items in each zone, or the workbook's printed Electrician frame (FYF p. 7) as a lower-lift alternative to a personal iceberg

### Extensions

- Create a visual career roadmap poster from 8th grade to age 25 with milestones marked
- Write a letter to your future self (to open in 12th grade)
- Build the presentation in one of the workbook's higher-production formats: the "Unboxing My Future" video, the podcast interview with your future self, or the website (FYF p. 279)
- Present TWO career plans (Plan A and a backup Plan B) and explain why having a backup matters

### ELL Language Support

- Bilingual Final Reflection with Spanish prompts
- Capstone presentation can be delivered in English, Spanish, or bilingual format
- Bilingual Career Plan template with Spanish rung headers
- Bilingual Certificates of Completion
- The Rung 4 Strengths Interview can be conducted in Spanish and the notes kept in Spanish. Bilingual ESL students should name bilingual capability as a Career Plan strength, because it is one of the most marketable skills in DFW.
