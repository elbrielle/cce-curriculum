## Warm-Up (3 min)

**WARM-UP: In ONE sentence, what is your career goal? In ONE more sentence, what is your first step toward it?**

Take 4-5 student responses. This forces clarity. If a student can't answer in 2 sentences, they need the work time today. Their career goal is Rung 2 and their first step is the short-term goal in Rung 6, so both sentences are already somewhere in their book.

---

## Activity 1: Check Your Ladder and Write the Career Plan (25 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 299: "Prepare & Present") + (FYF p. 278: "Capstone Project Requirements")

**Step 1: Check Your Ladder (5 min).** The workbook opens Prepare & Present with a three-question audit of the whole project (FYF p. 299). Students answer it honestly:

1. Did you complete all 8 rungs?
2. Did you fully answer each section with effort and detail?
3. Is your work neat, organized, and easy to understand?

Anything marked "not yet" is the first thing that gets fixed today. Rung 8 is the one rung students should expect to be blank, because it is written on Day 4. The eight rungs and what each one holds are previewed on page 278: Rung 1 Who Are You, Rung 2 Career Goal, Rung 3 Career Deep Dive, Rung 4 Strengths Interview, Rung 5 Local Connection, Rung 6 Goal Setting, Rung 7 High School Plan, Rung 8 Stay Motivated.

**Step 2: Write the Career Plan (20 min).** Distribute the printed Career Plan template (or share a Google Doc version). This is the one-document version of the ladder, the copy that goes home and to the 9th-grade counselor. Its eight sections follow the eight rungs, so every answer already exists in the workbook and students are transferring and tightening rather than starting over.

**Chunking:** release the template in three chunks and say what each chunk is for before students start it. Model Section 2 out loud first, using a career nobody in the room chose, so students hear how much detail a rung section is supposed to carry. Chunk 1 is Sections 1-3, who you are and the career you chose. Chunk 2 is Sections 4-5, what a trusted adult sees in you and what an employer actually asks for. Chunk 3 is Sections 6-7, your goals and your high school plan. Close each chunk with a thumbs check before releasing the next.

**Section 1: Who You Are (Rung 1, FYF pp. 281-282)**

- My top core personality type, my top 2 work values, 3 Building Blocks
- Two strengths I am confident about

**Section 2: Career Goal (Rung 2, FYF pp. 283-284)**

- *"My career goal is to become a _____ in the _____ cluster."*
- Where someone in this career works, and whether the work is mostly with people, ideas, data, or tools
- One perk and one quirk of the job

**Section 3: Career Deep Dive (Rung 3, FYF pp. 285-286)**

- Education or training required, plus any certification or license
- Estimated starting salary and the average in our area
- Growing, stable, or declining

**Section 4: What Others See in Me (Rung 4, FYF pp. 287-289)**

- One strength my interview adult named that I agree with
- Whether the interview changed my plan, and why

**Section 5: Local Connection (Rung 5, FYF pp. 290-291)**

- One real job posting: the title, where I found it, and two things the employer is asking for

**Section 6: My Three Goals (Rung 6, FYF pp. 292-293)**

- **Short-term (now to the end of 8th grade):** _____
- **Medium-term (high school):** _____
- **Long-term (after high school):** _____
- Each goal SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-Bound), each with the support I need to reach it
- My after-high-school path, pick ONE: 4-year university, 2-year community college (Dallas College / TCC), trade school, military, apprenticeship, or direct employment with certification

**Section 7: High School Plan (Rung 7, FYF pp. 294-296)**

- *"I will pursue this career through the _____ pathway at _____ High School."*
- One club, CTSO, or special program at that campus, and how it helps

| Year | Required Courses | CTE / Pathway Course | Electives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9th | English I, Algebra I, Biology, World Geography, PE/Health | | |
| 10th | English II, Geometry, Chemistry, World History | | |
| 11th | English III, Algebra II, Physics, US History | | |
| 12th | English IV, Math elective, Science elective, Government/Econ | | |

**Section 8: Stay Motivated (Rung 8, FYF pp. 297-298)**

- Left blank today. Students write it on Day 4 and add it to the plan before it goes home.

The Career Plan should be 1-2 pages. Students who finish early start planning their presentation format.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Walk around with the template visible. Students get stuck on the Time-Bound part of the Rung 6 goals because they have never attached a date to an intention. Coach them: "When exactly? Before winter break? Before registration in the spring? Write that date down." A goal without a date is a wish, and the rubric's Goal Setting row is looking for specific and realistic.

**DOK 4:** Look at your three goals. If you actually reach all three, how will your life be different by the end of 9th grade?

## Activity 2: Choose Your Format and Read the Rubric (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 279: "Presentation Options") + (FYF p. 280: "Rubric") + (FYF p. 299: "Prepare & Present")

Students turn to page 279 and read the seven presentation formats the workbook offers:

| Format | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Comic or Storyboard | Draw yourself climbing the ladder, with captions and speech bubbles |
| "Unboxing My Future" Video | Unbox an object for each rung and explain what it means |
| TED Talk-Style Speech | An inspiring talk about your career path, delivered on your feet |
| Podcast: Interview Your Future Self | An audio interview where you ask and answer your own questions |
| Essay | A clear, organized written version with paragraphs and details |
| Slideshow | Google Slides, PowerPoint, or Canva, presented live or recorded |
| Website | A simple site with a page or section for each rung |

The page also invites a format the workbook did not list, discussed with the teacher first. Whatever the format, the workbook requires the same three things: all 8 rungs included, ideas clear and complete, and effort and creativity shown.

!!! warning "Match the format to the days you actually have"
    Students present on Day 3 or Day 4, so the format has to be buildable in the time left. The TED Talk-Style Speech, the Slideshow, the Comic or Storyboard, and the Essay are the in-class-feasible defaults and should be offered as such. The video, the podcast, and the website are worth offering to students who will finish them at home tonight or who already have the pieces. Say this out loud before students choose, not after one of them commits to a website.

**Read the rubric together (FYF p. 280).** The workbook prints its own eight-category rubric, scored at four levels for 32 total points: All 8 Rungs Completed, Career Research, Personal Connection, Goal Setting, Interview Reflection, Creativity and Design, Presenter Delivery, and Participation and Collaboration. Students already hold this page, so it is the scoring standard for the week. Do not hand out a second rubric.

**Plan the presentation (FYF p. 299).** The workbook lists what the presentation has to show, and it is simply the ladder in order: who you are (Rung 1), your career goal (Rung 2), what it takes (Rung 3), what others see in you (Rung 4), real-world connections (Rung 5), your goals (Rung 6), your high school plan (Rung 7), and your motivation plan (Rung 8). Students write one line per rung on the presentation planner, which becomes their notes on presentation day. Bullet points only, no scripts. The workbook's own delivery tips close the activity: speak clearly and at a steady pace, look up and engage the audience, show confidence even when nervous, and use the work to guide you instead of reading it word for word.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Project a timer for any student who wants a 30-second test run during the last 5 minutes. Hearing yourself out loud once is better than reading silently 10 times.

**DOK 3:** What is the difference between a career goal and a goal from Rung 6? Why does a finished ladder need both?

**DELIVERABLE:** Written Career Plan + chosen presentation format + presentation planner with one line per rung.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pre-fill the 4-year course plan grid with the standard required courses. Students only fill in the CTE pathway courses and electives. Provide sentence starters for every rung section ("My career goal is _____. This fits me because _____."). For students whose Rung 5 job posting is missing, print one local posting per common career goal so the section can be completed today.
- **Extension:** Students build the presentation artifact itself during the work time rather than only planning it, or they draft a Plan A and a Plan B ladder and write a paragraph on why a backup matters.
- **ELL:** Bilingual Career Plan template with Spanish rung headers and sentence starters. Allow the written plan and the presentation to be in Spanish or bilingual. Pair ESL students with bilingual peers for the Rung 6 goal-writing section, which carries the most abstract vocabulary on the page.
