## Warm-Up (3 min)

**WARM-UP: Yesterday you watched the first half of the class present. What was the BEST ladder presentation you saw, and what made it strong?**

Take 2-3 student responses. This primes today's presenters to bring the same energy. Naming what worked yesterday makes it more likely to happen again today.

## Activity 1: Capstone Presentations Day 2 (30 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 299: "Prepare & Present") + (FYF p. 280: "Rubric")

The SECOND HALF of the class presents their eight-rung ladder. Same format and the same eight required rungs as Day 3.

!!! warning "Presentation timing: the math is tighter today than Day 3"
    Half a class of 25 = 12-13 students. At 5 min each, that is 60-65 min. DOUBLE the 30-min Activity 1 budget. Apply the same compression you picked on Day 3:

    - **2.5 min/student:** 12-13 × 2.5 min = 30-32 min presentations. Fits with no margin. Exit ticket runs tight.
    - **2 min/student:** 12-13 × 2 min = 24-26 min. Leaves ~4 min for Two Stars and a Wish batching.
    - **Corner rotation:** 2 corners of 6-7. Teacher circulates. Each student presents to a smaller audience at 3 min each.

    Day 4 has less runway than Day 3 because Activities 2 and 3 (Career Plan download + Rung 8) together eat 17 min. Protect them. The download is the artifact that goes home, and Rung 8 is the last rung on the ladder.

- 5-minute time limit per student (visible timer; see warning above for realistic slot)
- Teacher scores on the workbook's rubric (FYF p. 280), the same eight categories used yesterday
- Class gives Two Stars and a Wish after each
- Applaud every speaker
- Move quickly to keep momentum

The room should feel celebratory by now. Students who watched yesterday's presentations come in calmer because they know the format. Students who present today benefit from yesterday's models.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Take quick photos (with permission) of presenters. These can be used for end-of-year slideshows, parent communications, and yearbook. The capstone presentation IS the most important moment of the course, capture it.

**DOK 4:** Looking at all the presentations across both days, what patterns do you see in the strongest ladders? What do they have in common?

## Activity 2: Download H&L Career Plan (7 min)

**Source:** H&L app Career Plan, the app step Rung 2 sends students to (FYF p. 283)

After all presentations are done, direct students to open H&L and download or export their finalized Career Plan.

> [H&L PLATFORM] Students open Career Plan in the Hats & Ladders app and download or print it. The downloaded Plan is the app-side copy of the ladder they just presented. Confirm the export process with the H&L district admin; most H&L installations support a PDF export of the Career Plan or at least a print view.

> [VERIFY IN H&L] Confirm the specific download/export workflow in the current H&L app version. If the app does not support direct PDF export, use Print → Save as PDF as a workaround. **Day-of backup plan:** if simultaneous PDF export fails for 24 students (most likely failure mode), have students print the H&L Career Plan directly to PDF via the Chromebook browser Print dialog, OR take full-page screenshots and email them to themselves for home and counselor sharing. Every student leaves with an artifact, paper, PDF, or screenshot.

Students save the Plan to:

1. Their school Google Drive (digital backup)
2. Print one copy to take home (for parents/guardians and the 9th-grade course registration meeting)

Every student leaves with a copy of the Career Plan to show a parent or guardian and bring to the 9th-grade course-registration meeting. Pair it with the written Career Plan from Day 2.

## Activity 3: Rung 8 — Stay Motivated (10 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 297-298: "Rung 8: Stay Motivated")

The last rung is about what happens after the presentation is over. The workbook's framing is plain: reaching your goals will not always be easy, and staying motivated is what keeps you climbing. Students build their own motivation plan on pages 297-298.

**Your Affirmation.** A short, powerful statement students can tell themselves when things get hard. It should sound positive, confident, and personal. The workbook's two examples are about growing through challenges and being capable of reaching goals. Push students past copying those: the affirmation should sound like them.

**Your Hashtags.** Two to four short, catchy hashtags that represent the student's mindset and goals. The workbook models the length with #KeepClimbing and #FutureReady. This is the fastest part of the page and it is worth protecting, because it is the part reluctant writers finish first.

**Strategies to Stay on Track.** Three prompts, and this is where the old Support System work now lives:

1. What is one thing you can do when you feel stuck or discouraged?
2. Who is someone you can go to for support?
3. What is one habit you can build to stay consistent?

For question 2, students go back to the three-person career community they named in Week 0 (FYF p. 22: "Building a Career Community"). Nine months have passed. Some of those people are still right, some have been replaced by a teacher or a coach the student did not know in September, and some students now need a high school name they have not met yet. Students update the list to three current, real names.

| Name | How this person can help me | How I will stay connected |
|---|---|---|
| | | |
| | | |
| | | |

Real names, not "a teacher" but "Mrs. Garcia, my science teacher." Real names mean they actually know who to ask.

**Picture Your Future.** Two closing prompts on page 298: what does success look like for you, and how will you feel when you get there. The page ends with a one-word answer to how the student feels about the future now.

Students add the finished Rung 8 to Section 8 of the written Career Plan before it goes home.

**DOK 3:** Why is having someone to go to for support more important than having the perfect plan? What does a person give you that a plan alone cannot?

**DELIVERABLE:** Capstone presentation delivered + downloaded Career Plan + completed Rung 8 with an affirmation, hashtags, three strategies, and three named people.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Help students navigate the H&L download/export process at the device level. Provide a printed Career Plan summary as a backup if the app export fails. Pre-fill the support table with example role categories (Parent, Teacher, Mentor) so students fill in just the names, and offer two affirmation sentence frames ("I can _____ even when _____." / "I am the kind of person who _____.").
- **Extension:** Students answer the workbook's "Being a Career Thinker and Doer" discussion question in writing (FYF p. 22): what is the difference between thinking about a goal and taking action toward it? They tie the answer to one of the three strategies they just wrote. Students who chose the Comic or Storyboard presentation format (FYF p. 279) can add a final panel showing where the affirmation gets used.
- **ELL:** Bilingual support table with Spanish column headers. Allow the affirmation, hashtags, and "how they help" descriptions to be in Spanish. Many ESL students have strong family support systems, encourage them to list family members who have wisdom even if they speak limited English.
