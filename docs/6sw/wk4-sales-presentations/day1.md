## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Have you ever tried to convince someone to give you something, buy you something, lend you something, agree to a plan? What strategy did you use?**

Take 4-5 responses. Pull out the words students use to convince someone: a reason, a benefit, or a direct request. Sales turns those same moves into a planned message for a buyer.

---

## Activity 1: H&L Sales Pathway Tour (10 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 254: "App Exploration") + (FYF pp. 252-253: "What is Happening at My District?")

Direct students into the Business, Marketing, and Finance cluster in the app and let them browse the Hats inside it. The workbook's district pages describe the Marketing and Sales program of study as preparation for careers in market research, advertising, branding, product promotion, and data-driven decision making, which is a wider job than the word "sales" suggests to most students.

> [H&L PLATFORM] Open the Hats & Ladders app, go to Clusters, and click the Business, Marketing, and Finance cluster. Students browse Sales-specific Hats: Sales Manager, Account Executive, Retail Manager, Sales Representative, Inside Sales Specialist. Open "Pathway Possibilities" and rate at least one pathway.

**Quick salary check (5 min):** Students look up Sales Manager on bls.gov/ooh/management/sales-managers.htm. The median pay nationally is around $135K, with the top 10% over $200K. Sales is one of the highest-paying business careers WITHOUT requiring a graduate degree.

The big idea: many people think sales is "annoying phone calls." Real sales is more like consulting, understanding what customers need and helping them solve a problem. The best salespeople are great listeners.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Students often write off sales as a career because of the stereotype. Counter it directly: "Real sales is helping people solve problems they have. The 'annoying salesperson' stereotype is bad sales, and bad salespeople don't last. Good salespeople build long-term relationships." Repeat this until at least one student rethinks their assumption.

## Activity 2: 30 Seconds to Sell — Steps 1-3 (30 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 241-242: "30 Seconds to Sell", Steps 1-3)

Open the workbook to page 241 and set the frame the activity uses: students are an entrepreneur who has started their own business, and one of their main jobs is selling what they built. In sales you rarely get a long explanation. Sometimes you get a few seconds. That is why sales professionals build a 30-second pitch, a message short enough to deliver in about half a minute and strong enough to make someone act.

**Step 1: What Is a Sales Pitch? (10 min)**

Walk the four parts of a strong pitch on the projector, one at a time, and say what each part is doing before moving to the next:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Hook | Grabs attention right away |
| What it is | Explains clearly what you are selling |
| Benefits | Says how it helps the customer |
| Call to action | Tells the customer what to do next |

Then read the workbook's SparkClean Toothbrush example on p. 241 aloud once at normal speed, and a second time stopping after each sentence so students can name which of the four parts they just heard. The point the workbook makes with that example: a good pitch skips the detail list and keeps only what matters most.

**Step 2: Choose What You're Selling (5 min)**

Each student picks ONE and writes it in the workbook:

- A product (game, app, snack, clothing item)
- A service (landscaping, tutoring, pet care, cleaning)
- Their own idea

**Step 3: Know Your Audience (15 min)**

Students choose the audience they are trying to convince from the workbook's list, students, parents, gamers, pet owners, busy families, or their own, and then answer the workbook's question: what does that audience care about most?

**Stop and Jot:** Give 90 seconds on the workbook prompt "What does your audience care about most?" The expectation is three lines, not three words. Post two stems: "My audience cares most about _____ because _____" and "If I said _____, my audience would stop listening because _____." Read jots over shoulders while students write. If most of the room names price and nothing else, put time, effort, and how the product makes the buyer look on the board before students move on.

With the remaining minutes, pairs trade audience profiles and each partner names one benefit that would land with the OTHER person's audience. Students write it in the margin of their own page. This is raw material for the pitch they write tomorrow.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Walk around with one prompt: "Who is this for, and what do they want?" Students who cannot answer the second half will write a pitch that lists features instead of benefits. Push them to finish the sentence "This matters to my audience because _____" before they leave.

**DOK 3:** Two students are selling the exact same product but to different audiences. Why would the benefits they name be different? Use your own audience to explain.

**DELIVERABLE:** Product or service chosen + written audience profile naming what that audience cares about most.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give students a shortlist of three products and three audiences to choose from instead of an open choice. Provide the SparkClean example with the four parts already labeled in the margin so students can match their own pitch against a model. Allow the audience profile to be two lines instead of three.
- **Extension:** Students write a second audience profile for the SAME product aimed at a different group, then list the two benefits that would have to change.
- **ELL:** Pair ESL students with bilingual peers for the audience trade. Allow the audience profile to be written in Spanish and translated later. Pre-teach: Pitch = Presentación de venta, Audience = Audiencia, Benefit = Beneficio, Customer = Cliente.
