# Week 4: Pitch Perfect — Sales & Career Presentations

**6th Six Weeks** | Marketing / Business Management Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore Sales and Marketing careers through the Hats & Ladders app and the *Find Your Future* workbook, build and deliver a 30-second sales pitch, work the "Powerskill: Problem Solving" campaign rescue as a team, prepare and deliver a 3-minute oral presentation on a career they explored this year, and describe appropriate professional appearance for an interview.

**Learning Target:** I can name at least three Sales and Marketing careers, write and deliver a 30-second sales pitch with a hook and a call to action, help my team redesign a marketing campaign that did not work, describe what to wear to three kinds of interviews, and give a 3-minute presentation on a career I explored this year.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my pitch plan names my audience and what they care about, when my written pitch is 4 to 6 sentences and ends with a call to action, when my team's redesign lists three chosen solutions and a new campaign message, when my Interview Appearance Guide describes a top, a bottom, and shoes for all three interview types plus five things to avoid, and when my career presentation names the education path, the DFW salary, and the daily tasks inside the 3-minute limit.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can deliver a 3-minute oral presentation on a career I explored this year, write and deliver a 30-second sales pitch, work with a team to fix a marketing campaign that underperformed, give and receive constructive feedback, and describe appropriate professional appearance for an interview."

## Vocabulary

- **Oral Presentation:** A formal speech delivered to an audience on a specific topic. Students present on a career they have researched.
- **Professional Appearance:** The way you dress, groom, and carry yourself in a workplace or interview setting.
- **Sales Management:** The process of leading a sales team, setting targets, developing strategies, and building client relationships.
- **Sales Pitch:** A short, clear message that explains what you are offering and why it matters.
- **Hook:** The opening of a pitch. It grabs attention in the first few seconds.
- **Call to Action:** The close of a pitch. It tells the listener exactly what to do next.
- **Target Audience:** The group of people you are trying to reach. What they care about decides what you say.
- **Problem Statement:** One sentence naming why something is not working. Marketing teams write one before they try to fix a campaign.
- **Constructive Feedback:** Specific, actionable suggestions that help someone improve, given respectfully.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Business, Marketing, and Finance printed pages 221 through 254. Two of its sections carry this week.

- **30 Seconds to Sell** (Days 1-2): Students are the entrepreneur who has to sell what they built. The workbook walks seven steps: what a sales pitch is, choose a product or a service, know your audience and what that audience cares about, plan the four parts (hook, what it is, benefits, call to action), write the pitch in 4 to 6 sentences, practice it out loud against a 30-second check, and deliver it to a partner and record two pieces of feedback (FYF pp. 241-243: "30 Seconds to Sell"). The workbook prints a worked example, the SparkClean Toothbrush pitch on p. 241, which is the exemplar the teacher reads before students write.
- **Powerskill: Problem Solving** (Day 3): A team activity. A campaign for BrainBoost Bars reached exactly the right audience, students aged 14 to 18, and still underperformed. Teams review the email, the social post, and the in-store display, read three lines of customer feedback, write a one-sentence problem statement, brainstorm at least ten fixes on sticky notes, choose their best three, build a mini campaign plan, and present the redesign to another group (FYF pp. 244-247: "Powerskill: Problem Solving").

Two Capstone pages support the week's presentations. The workbook's own presentation tips, speak clearly at a steady pace, look up and engage the audience, show confidence, and use your work to guide you rather than reading it word for word, come from **Prepare & Present** (FYF p. 299), and the **Presenter Delivery** row of the Capstone rubric supplies the four levels the Career Presentation Rubric uses for delivery (FYF p. 280). Students will meet both pages again when they build the Capstone itself.

The week also assigns **Rung 4: Strengths Interview** (FYF pp. 287-289) as take-home work on Day 5. It requires a conversation with a trusted adult outside class, so it cannot be a same-day task. Students choose the adult, add at least two questions of their own to the workbook's five, conduct the interview, and complete the "Make Meaning of What You Heard" reflection. It is collected and debriefed in the Capstone week.

One clarification for the teacher: the peer-feedback protocols this week uses, the **feedback sandwich from the communication week** and **Two Stars and a Wish**, are CCE-authored scaffolding. They are not workbook pages, so do not send students hunting for them in the book.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 2, Activity 2, the workbook's Step 7 partner delivery of the 30-second pitch. Distinguished: hold a silent 30 second Think while each student reads their own pitch once, then time Partner A for 30 seconds of delivery and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two feedback stems at different levels: "Your hook worked because _____" and "I would change _____ so the benefit is clearer, because right now it sounds like _____." Ask two pairs to deliver again for the whole class, not one. While pairs work, listen for whose pitch ends without a call to action, and pick your two share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 2, the workbook's Step 3 prompt "What does your audience care about most?" Distinguished: give 90 seconds with the prompt on screen, name the expectation as three lines and not three words, and offer two stems: "My audience cares most about _____ because _____" and "If I said _____, my audience would stop listening because _____." Read jots over shoulders while students write. If most jots name price only, pull the next segment toward time, effort, and how the product makes the buyer look, so the pitches students write on Day 2 have more than one benefit to sell.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 4, Activity 2, the three paired practice rounds. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times, one lap per round, each lap with a single target (round 1 all three body facts present, round 2 the fix from partner feedback actually applied, round 3 eyes up and off the note card), marking the Career Presentation Rubric on a clipboard as you go. Have feedback ready before class for the two predictable problems: a presenter who reads the outline word for word, and a conclusion that stops at "and that is my career" with no personal fit statement. If more than a handful miss the round 1 target, pivot and reproject the three-fact structure to the whole room instead of repeating it pair by pair.
- **Chunking:** Day 3, Activity 2, the three-part career presentation structure. Distinguished: teach one part at a time, say what each part is for before modeling it (the introduction earns attention, the three facts earn trust, the conclusion earns the audience's own decision), deliver a 30 second teacher model of each part on the projector, and close each part with a check before releasing the next one. Note which part draws the most blank outlines. That part becomes a 3 minute re-teach at the start of Day 4, and students who cleared all three parts start rehearsing their opening line instead of waiting.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 5, Activity 1, the career presentations. Distinguished: post the three blocks before the first presenter, Voice 0 while the next presenter sets up, Voice 3 for the presenter, Voice 1 for the star and wish response, and run a visible 3 minute countdown for every speaker, the same cap students practiced against on Day 4. Define where presenters stand and where note cards go so transitions do not eat speaking time. Narrate the students who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If the rotation is running long by the fourth presenter, cut the class response to one star only and announce the change before the next presenter begins.

## Summative Assessment

**Career Oral Presentation (Day 5):** 3-minute individual presentation on a career explored this year. Structure: introduction (career name and why chosen), body (education path, DFW salary, daily tasks), conclusion (connection to personal career plan). Delivery is coached against the workbook's presentation tips (FYF p. 299: "Prepare & Present"): speak clearly at a steady pace, look up and engage the audience, show confidence even when nervous, and use your notes to guide you rather than reading them word for word. Scored using the Career Presentation Rubric on content accuracy, organization, delivery, and career connection, with the delivery criterion using the four levels of the workbook's **Presenter Delivery** row: engaging and confident, clear delivery, some difficulty, hard to follow (FYF p. 280). Primary d(4)(C) artifact for the year.

**Assigned Day 5, due in the Capstone week:** "Rung 4: Strengths Interview" (FYF pp. 287-289). Students interview a trusted adult about the strengths that adult sees in them, using the workbook's five questions plus at least two of their own, then complete the "Make Meaning of What You Heard" reflection. It is collected and debriefed when the Capstone ladder is assembled.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning

- Provide a presentation outline template with fill-in-the-blank structure
- Allow note cards during the 3-minute presentation
- Offer small-group presentation option (present to 4-5 peers instead of full class) for anxious students
- Provide a partially-filled pitch planner with the hook and the call to action modeled on the workbook's SparkClean example

### Extensions

- Students create a professional slide deck (Google Slides or Canva) to accompany the 3-minute career presentation
- Present WITHOUT notes for bonus
- Record the career presentation and self-evaluate using the rubric
- Rewrite the 30-second pitch for a completely different audience and explain in two sentences what had to change

### ELL Language Support

- Bilingual presentation outline template with Spanish sentence starters
- Allow ESL students to present to a small group if full-class presentation is too much
- Pair ESL students with bilingual peers for the team campaign redesign on Day 3
- Pre-teach: Pitch = Presentación, Audience = Audiencia, Feedback = Retroalimentación, Benefit = Beneficio
