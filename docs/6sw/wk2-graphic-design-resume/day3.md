## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: If you were hiring someone for your dream job, what TWO things would you look for first when you read their resume?**

Take 4-5 responses. Then put the same problems into a resume: a typo in the phone number, a missing activity, or a claim so vague it tells the employer nothing. Today's Powerskill activity is practice finding those gaps before the resume is sent.

---

## Activity 1: Powerskill — Attention to Detail (20 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 272-273: "Powerskill: Attention to Detail")

Introduce the activity using the workbook framing: *"Attention to detail means noticing small things, making sure everything is correct, and including important details from the start. It helps you catch mistakes before they happen and ensures your work is complete and accurate."*

The workbook scenario: students are the lead sound designer for a short movie called "Kitchen Mess!" The video is finished and it is time to add the sound effects, but the Audio Cue Script, the list that tells the sound team what to find or record, is missing most of what the team needs.

**Step 1: Review the Audio Cue Script (5 min)**

Students read the script on p. 272. Chef Tony runs into a kitchen carrying a large mixing bowl [AUDIO CUE: Running sound]. He trips and drops the bowl on the floor [AUDIO CUE: Drop sound]. Flour goes flying. A cat jumps onto the counter and knocks over the sugar [AUDIO CUE: Animal noise]. Tony yells at the cat, and the scene closes [AUDIO CUE: Background music starts].

Four cues, and not one of them tells the sound team what to actually record.

**Step 2: Identify the Missing Details (5 min)**

Students list what is missing, using the workbook's three prompts (p. 273):

- **Materials:** what are things made of? A heavy metal object sounds nothing like a feather when it hits the ground.
- **Environment:** what are the surfaces? What kind of floor is Tony running on?
- **Mood:** how should the background music make the audience feel?

Students work individually or in pairs and write 4-6 missing details.

**Step 3: Rewrite the Audio Cues (10 min)**

Students rewrite all four cues so the sound team knows exactly what to record. Each rewritten cue names the material, the surface, and the feeling it should create, and it is specific enough that another person could produce the sound without asking a follow-up question.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Rewrite the first cue live on the board before students start. "[AUDIO CUE: Running sound]" becomes "[AUDIO CUE: fast footsteps in rubber kitchen clogs on wet tile, about six steps, getting closer and slightly panicked]". The shift from vague to specific is the whole skill, and students will copy the format once they see it once.

**DOK 2:** What could happen to the movie if the sound team just guessed at the sounds instead of asking for details? How would that change the movie for the audience?

**Connection to resumes:** This same skill applies to the resume work students are doing this week. A resume that says "good with computers" is the cue that says "Drop sound." A resume that says "Used Canva and Adobe Express to design 5 school posters" is the cue that names the bowl, the tile, and the flour. Specific = stronger.

## Activity 2: Resume Peer Review (15 min)

**Source:** Xello Resume Builder + Two Stars and a Wish strategy

Pair students up. They trade Chromebooks (or pull each other's resume up on Xello). Each student reads their partner's resume like an employer would and writes feedback using the Two Stars and a Wish format:

**Two Stars (specific strengths):**

- "Your skills section is strong because you listed specific software (Canva, Google Slides) instead of just 'computers.'"
- "Your career objective is clear and specific."

**One Wish (specific improvement):**

- "Your activities section is empty. I know you do TSA. Add it!"
- "Your career objective says 'I want a job.' Make it specific to a type of job."

The teacher distributes a printed peer review form OR students write feedback directly on a sticky note. Specific feedback only, vague comments ("looks good") are sent back.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Walk around with one question: "Show me ONE specific change your partner suggested that you actually made." Hold students accountable to acting on the feedback, not just receiving it.

---

## Activity 3: Resume Revision (8 min)

Students apply the peer feedback to their Xello resume. They strengthen weak sections, add missing items, and fix any vague language.

**Goal:** Every student leaves Day 3 with a noticeably stronger resume than when they walked in.

**DOK 3:** What is the connection between the audio cue rewrite and your resume revision today? Both required you to look for what was MISSING and add specifics. Why does this skill matter for any job?

**DELIVERABLE:** Revised resume v2 saved in Xello + rewritten audio cue script (in the workbook or digital).

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a "Resume Revision Checklist", 5 yes/no questions ("Is your career objective specific? Did you list at least 3 skills? Did you list at least 2 activities? Did you list any community service? Did you spell-check?"). Students self-check before peer review.
- **Extension (from workbook):** The workbook's "Extra Time?" box (FYF p. 273) pairs early finishers up. One partner reads the revised audio cue script aloud and the other tries to produce each sound effect using only their voice or objects in the room. Cues that cannot be reproduced still need more detail.
- **ELL:** The audio cue rewrite is strong ELL practice, it explicitly trains students to add concrete nouns and adjectives. Pair ESL students with bilingual peers for the resume peer review. Allow the rewritten cues to be in Spanish or bilingual.
