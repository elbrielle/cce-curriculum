## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Open your phone's camera roll (or just think about it). Who took most of the photos and videos on there? You are already creating visual content. Could that become a career?**

Take 3-4 student responses. Most students will say "me", and that is the bridge. Bridge to today: every visual thing you see (movies, ads, social media, video games) is created by someone whose job is making visual content.

## Activity 1: H&L Arts, A/V Technology, and Communications Cluster Tour (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 255: "Exploring the World of ARTS, AUDIO/VISUAL TECHNOLOGY AND COMMUNICATIONS") + (FYF p. 276: "App Exploration")

Open by framing this as the second-to-last six weeks block, but the FIRST week of intense job-readiness work (resumes start on Day 2). Today is the creative-careers warm-up.

Read the chapter opener together. This cluster is about creating the things people watch, hear, and share every day, and the work splits three ways: some careers shape how something sounds, some shape how it looks, and some shape how it makes people feel. The opener page puts three Hats in front of students right away: Photographer, Audio Engineer, and AV Technician.

**Think-Pair-Share:** Pose the opener's "Be the Decision Maker" scenario (FYF p. 255): *"You are creating all the sound design for a major animated movie using only everyday household objects. Do you try to make things sound realistic or go completely weird and unexpected? Do you start with the biggest most important scenes or work through it beginning to end?"* Hold a silent 30 second Think, then Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds. Two pairs share out.

Then direct students into the app.

> [H&L PLATFORM] (FYF p. 276: "App Exploration") Open the Hats & Ladders app and go to Clusters. Click on the Arts, Audio/Visual Technology and Communications Cluster. Watch the "Cluster Tour" video, click "Game Time" and play the game, find 1 Hat that matches your interests and 1 Hat that does not, open "Pathway Possibilities" and rate at least 1 pathway, then rate at least 3 Hats.

Hats worth steering students toward while they browse: Graphic Designer, Animator, Video Editor, UX Designer, Photographer, Social Media Manager, Sound Engineer, Art Director. Irving ISD's own programs in this cluster, Digital Communications and Graphic Design & Illustration, appear on the district pages students meet on Day 5 (FYF pp. 274-275).

**Stop and Jot:** Pause the cluster tour twice, 45 seconds each. Prompt 1: one Arts/AV career that surprised you. Prompt 2: one question you still have about this cluster.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Students often think "art = poor career." Push back with concrete data: UX Designer median salary is $85K+ in DFW. Animation lead at game studios can clear $100K. Show them the BLS page for Graphic Designers ($57K median nationally, higher in DFW) to anchor expectations.

## Activity 2: Behind the Microphone (25 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 270-271: "Behind the Microphone"), a Career Climb activity

Introduce the activity using the workbook framing: *"Did you know that podcasting can mean huge business in today's world? There are some people who make podcasts just for fun while there are others who make podcasts as their full-time job and get paid to do it! Popular podcasts can make thousands, even millions, of dollars through ads, sponsorships, and subscriptions."*

Today students plan their own podcast in groups of 3-4. They will not record, that is an extension. Today is about the creative planning process that podcast producers use.

**Step 1: Brainstorm Ideas (4 min)**

Groups of 3-4 brainstorm on sticky notes and settle the workbook's three questions (p. 270):

- What is your podcast going to be about? (Sports, music, gaming, movies, true crime, school life, etc.)
- Who is your target audience? (Kids, teens, adults, gamers, parents, etc.)
- What makes your podcast stand out? Why would people want to listen?

**Step 2: Plan an Episode (6 min)**

Using the workbook table (p. 270), the group settles the key details of ONE specific episode:

- **Podcast Name:** A catchy name for the show
- **Episode Title:** What is this episode about?
- **Episode Length:** How long will the episode run?
- **Hosts & Guests:** Who is talking? One host, two, a group, a special guest?
- **Key Topics:** Three main points, stories, or questions the episode will cover
- **Promotion Plan:** How will people find out about the podcast? Social media, ads, something else?

**Step 3: Create an Episode Outline (12 min)**

Groups write their outline on chart paper and work the workbook checklist (p. 271) item by item:

- **Introduction:** Welcome listeners, introduce host and guest
- **Main Topic:** Provide an overview of what this episode is focused on
- **Engaging Material:** Background information so listeners can follow along + interesting details that keep them engaged
- **Interview Questions:** At least 3 engaging questions to ask the guest
- **Closing:** Tie the episode together, thank listeners and guests, give a sneak peek of a new episode coming soon

**Step 4: Podcast Pitch (3 min)**

Each group pairs with another group and pitches: what the podcast is, how the episode is laid out, who the hosts are, and why someone would listen to this show instead of the thousands of others available. The listening group owes the presenters one thing they loved and one thing they would like to see improved.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    The workbook checklist IS the rubric. Walk around during work time with the checklist visible. Ask each group "Show me your three interview questions." If they only have one, redirect them. The checklist makes accountability visible.

**DOK 2:** How would you compare the work a podcast host does to the work a graphic designer does? Both are Arts/AV careers, what skills do they share? What skills are different?

**DELIVERABLE:** Podcast episode outline on chart paper (one per group of 3-4), photographed and submitted to Google Classroom or kept in the class binder.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a podcast outline template with sample interview questions ("What inspired you to _____?", "What is your favorite _____?", "What advice would you give _____?"). Allow students to use a podcast they already love as the basis for their planning rather than inventing from scratch.
- **Extension (from workbook):** The workbook's own "Extra Time?" box (FYF p. 271) sends early finishers to RECORD their podcast episode, using a Chromebook microphone and free recording software (Audacity, Vocaroo, or Google Recorder).
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Podcast = Pódcast, Episode = Episodio, Audience = Audiencia, Host = Anfitrión, Interview = Entrevista. The podcast can be planned in Spanish or be a bilingual show. Many DFW podcasts are bilingual, so this is an authentic format choice.
