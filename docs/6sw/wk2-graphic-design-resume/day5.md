## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Name a brand whose logo you would recognize anywhere, even without the name on it. What makes the brand stick in your memory?**

Take 4-5 student responses. Apple, Nike, McDonald's, Target, students name brands instantly. Bridge to today: a graphic designer chose every one of those marks on purpose, and the choice had about three seconds to land. Today students take that job for a band about to go on its first tour.

## Activity 1: Merch Mode (30 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 256-258: "Merch Mode"), an Arts chapter activity

Introduce the activity using the workbook framing: *"Graphic designers are the creative minds behind the visuals you see every day like posters, logos, ads, and even the packaging on your favorite products. Their job is to make ideas look eye-catching... But graphic design isn't just about making things 'look cool', it's about solving problems and figuring out how to grab people's attention."*

The scenario: a brand-new band is going on their first major music tour and needs a t-shirt that defines their look and gets their fans excited. Each student is the graphic designer on that job.

**Time, Voice, Body:** Post the three blocks before students open Canva. Voice 1 for the brainstorm and the sketch, Voice 3 for the presenter in Step 5, and a visible countdown for each step. Sticky notes go on the table edge, not in laptop keyboards, and finished sketches go face-up in the middle of the table so partners can see them during the share.

**Step 1: Define Your Band's Style (5 min)**

Students fill in the workbook page (p. 256): Band Name, Music Style or Genre, Visual Style (what people see when they think of the band, colors, mood, vibe), Stage Look (what the band wears or has on stage), Audience (what kind of fans connect with this band and what they wear), and Overall Band Identity (the feeling or message of the band in a few words).

**Step 2: Review Design Principles (7 min)**

The workbook's design-principles table (p. 257) is the teaching asset of this activity. Walk the five principles, then have students write their own plan in the third column:

| Design Principle | What It Means | Your Plan |
|---|---|---|
| The 3-Second Rule | People only glance at a shirt for a few seconds. It has to be understood quickly. | What is the ONE main idea your shirt should communicate instantly? |
| Visual Hierarchy (Make It Pop!) | The most important part should stand out the most: biggest, boldest, or brightest. | What will people notice first on your design? |
| Font Choice Matters | Fonts affect readability and style. Bold and simple fonts usually work best on shirts. | What kind of lettering fits your band's vibe? |
| Memorable Design | Strong designs often repeat or highlight a key symbol, shape, or style. | What repeating element or symbol could you use? |
| Does It Fit the Vibe? | Everything should feel like it belongs together as one design. | What feeling or style do you want your shirt to give off? |

**Step 3: Brainstorm Ideas (5 min)**

Students jot phrases, list colors, and try out fonts on sticky notes. Every choice has to trace back to one of the five principles.

**Step 4: Sketch Your Design (9 min)**

Students sketch the shirt in Canva or on paper. One design, front of the shirt, with the type and the key symbol both visible.

**Step 5: Share Your Ideas (4 min)**

Students present to a partner, a small group, or the class, and explain what the band is like and how the design shows that style, why they chose those colors, fonts, and images, and how the design would help promote the band. Close with the workbook's discussion question (p. 258): what are the top 2 Powerskills that would matter in a graphic design career?

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Run the 3-Second Rule on the class before Step 3. Project one well-known band or team shirt for exactly three seconds, hide it, then ask what students remember. Whatever they name is the visual hierarchy working. Whatever nobody remembers is the part that would have to be cut from their own design.

**DOK 4:** Two bands could pick the same colors and the same font and still end up with shirts that say completely different things. Using at least two of the five design principles, explain what actually makes a design belong to one band and not the other.

**DELIVERABLE:** Band t-shirt design (Canva or paper) + the completed "Your Plan" column of the design-principles table.

## Activity 2: Finalize Xello Resume + H&L Hat Rating (10 min)

**Source:** Xello Resume Builder + (FYF p. 276: "App Exploration")

Direct students to open Xello and make ANY final changes to their resume based on the week's work. The resume is the official deliverable for d(7)(A) and should be in good shape by the end of today.

Then direct students to open H&L and complete two tasks:

1. **Rate at least 3 Arts/AV Hats and favorite the ones that fit** (Graphic Designer, Animator, UX Designer, Video Editor, Photographer, Sound Engineer, Social Media Manager, Art Director).
2. **Update Building Blocks** with the design work from this week: the podcast episode outline, the rewritten audio cues, the Game Design Document, the band t-shirt design, and the Xello resume itself.

> [H&L PLATFORM] (FYF p. 276: "App Exploration") In the Hats & Ladders app, go to Clusters, open the Arts, Audio/Visual Technology and Communications Cluster, rate at least 3 Hats, and click the heart icon on the ones worth keeping. Ratings and favorites save to the Climber Profile.

Everything students record here comes back at the end of the year, when they check every rung of the career ladder before presenting it (FYF p. 299: "Prepare & Present").

While students work, project the workbook's district pages (FYF pp. 274-275: "What is Happening at My District?"). They name Irving ISD's two programs of study in this cluster, Digital Communications and Graphic Design & Illustration, the Adobe Certified Professional certification, TSA and SkillsUSA as the student organizations, the daily school-wide news program Digital Communications students produce and broadcast, and the annual Irving ISD Film Festival, a red carpet event that screens student work. Students need this page in front of them for the exit ticket.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide pre-made Canva t-shirt templates students can customize. Hand out the design-principles table with the first row already filled in as a model. Allow students to design only the key symbol instead of the full shirt.
- **Extension:** Students who finish the sketch early take the TrailSip logo brief (FYF pp. 262-263: "Powerskill: Time Management"), a company that sells eco-friendly reusable water bottles to hikers and bikers, and use the workbook's 10 minute timeboxing plan to redesign its logo under a hard clock.
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Brand = Marca, Logo = Logotipo, Slogan = Eslogan, Font = Tipografía, Color Palette = Paleta de colores, Sketch = Bosquejo. The design work is HIGHLY visual and accessible across language levels. Allow the Step 5 share and the design-principle plan to be written or spoken in Spanish or bilingual format.
