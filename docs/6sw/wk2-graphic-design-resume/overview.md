# Week 2: Design Your Brand — Graphic Design + First Resume

**6th Six Weeks** | Arts, A/V Technology & Communications Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore the Arts, Audio/Visual Technology and Communications career cluster through the Hats & Ladders app and the *Find Your Future* workbook, plan and outline a podcast episode in "Behind the Microphone," write their first professional resume in Xello, practice attention to detail by rewriting an incomplete audio cue script, build and pitch a Game Design Document in a studio team in "Game On!", design a touring band's t-shirt in "Merch Mode," and identify the steps of an effective job search to bridge into the job-readiness weeks ahead.

**Learning Target:** I can name at least three Arts/AV careers and say what each one does, write a complete resume in Xello, name the steps of an effective job search, and design a band t-shirt that follows the five design principles.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my podcast outline has all five checklist parts, when my Xello resume has specific content in the header, career objective, education, skills, activities, and community service, when my rewritten audio cues name the material, the surface, and the mood, when my team's Game Design Document is complete and pitched, and when my band t-shirt design has one main idea, one repeating symbol, and a font that fits the band.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe at least three Arts/AV careers, write a complete resume in Xello including education, skills, activities, and career objective, identify the four steps of an effective job search, and design a band's brand identity on a t-shirt using graphic-design principles."

## Career Connection

Graphic design and digital communication careers are everywhere in our visual world, every website, app, advertisement, social media post, and brand logo was created by a designer. In DFW, companies of all sizes hire graphic designers, animators, video editors, UX designers, and social media managers. This is also THE resume week of the year, students write their first professional resume in Xello, which becomes a living document they refine through the rest of the six weeks.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** The district runs two programs of study in this cluster, **Digital Communications** and **Graphic Design & Illustration**, offered at Irving High School, MacArthur High School, and Nimitz High School, a multi-campus pathway students can pursue regardless of their home school. The workbook's district pages add the local detail students respond to most (FYF pp. 274-275: "What is Happening at My District?"): Digital Communications students produce and broadcast a daily school-wide news program on professional equipment, Graphic Design & Illustration builds visual communication, branding, layout, and creative problem solving, the industry certification is Adobe Certified Professional, TSA and SkillsUSA are the student organizations, and the annual Irving ISD Film Festival is a red carpet event that screens student-produced work with an awards ceremony. The page frames the whole cluster around Transitioning to High School, which is the right frame for a week that ends with students holding a resume.

## Vocabulary

- **Graphic Designer:** A professional who creates visual content for print, digital, and social media using tools like Adobe Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign.
- **Resume:** A one-page document summarizing your education, skills, activities, and career objective. Used when applying for jobs, internships, or college programs.
- **Portfolio:** A collection of work samples that demonstrates a designer's skills and style. Essential for creative careers, often more important than the resume.
- **UX Designer:** A User Experience Designer who focuses on how people interact with websites, apps, and digital products. One of the fastest-growing design careers.
- **Branding:** How a business shows who they are through logos, colors, fonts, and messaging to make people remember them.
- **Visual Hierarchy:** Making the most important part of a design stand out the most, through size, boldness, or brightness. One of the five design principles students apply in "Merch Mode."
- **Career Objective:** A 1-2 sentence statement at the top of a resume that summarizes the kind of role you are seeking.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Arts, Audio/Visual Technology and Communications printed pages 255-276. The chapter opener (FYF p. 255: "Exploring the World of ARTS, AUDIO/VISUAL TECHNOLOGY AND COMMUNICATIONS") frames the cluster as creating the things people watch, hear, and share every day, splits the work into how something sounds, how it looks, and how it makes people feel, and puts three Hats on the page: Photographer, Audio Engineer, and AV Technician. Its "Be the Decision Maker" scenario hands students the sound design for an animated movie built entirely from household objects. The chapter prints no pathway list. Students meet the cluster's pathways in the app through the Pathway Possibilities step (FYF p. 276: "App Exploration") and meet Irving ISD's own programs on the district pages (FYF pp. 274-275).

Four chapter sections carry this week:

- **Behind the Microphone** (Day 1): Career Climb activity. Groups of 3-4 brainstorm a podcast, settle the name, episode title, length, hosts and guests, three key topics, and a promotion plan, then write a full episode outline on chart paper against a five-part checklist and pitch it to another group (FYF pp. 270-271).
- **Powerskill: Attention to Detail** (Day 3): Students are the lead sound designer on a short movie called "Kitchen Mess!" whose Audio Cue Script is missing the material, surface, and mood information the sound team needs. They name what is missing and rewrite all four cues (FYF pp. 272-273).
- **Game On!** (Day 4): Career Climb activity. Teams of 3-5 name a studio, take one of four roles (Game Designer, Artist, Writer, Sound Designer), build a Game Design Document, and pitch the game to another team for feedback (FYF pp. 268-269).
- **Merch Mode** (Day 5): Students design a touring band's t-shirt from the chapter's five-principle design table (The 3-Second Rule, Visual Hierarchy, Font Choice Matters, Memorable Design, Does It Fit the Vibe?) and present the choices they made (FYF pp. 256-258).

Arts is the best-supplied chapter in the workbook relative to the weeks assigned to it, and four sections go unused: **The Visual Hook** (FYF pp. 259-261), product packaging design with a target market, sales channels, and structured peer feedback; **Powerskill: Time Management** (FYF pp. 262-263), a logo redesign for an outdoor water-bottle company under a 10 minute timeboxing plan; **Safety on the Set** (FYF pp. 264-265), stunt coordination where partners each research one film safety method and teach the other; and **Setting the Stage** (FYF pp. 266-267), set design with labeled set elements, a blueprint, and a 3D prototype. All four are available as enrichment.

**The resume half of this week is CCE-original.** The *Find Your Future* workbook carries no resume, cover letter, or job-application activity anywhere in its 300 printed pages. Days 2 and 3 run on the Xello Resume Builder, the Xello Resume lesson from the 7th-grade task list, and the CareerOneStop Resume Guide, and the four job-search steps on Day 4 are a CCE framework paired with eDynamic Unit 8.2. The workbook's nearest relative is Rung 5 of the capstone (FYF pp. 290-291: "Rung 5: Local Connections"), where students search real local job postings and record what employers ask for.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 1, the chapter opener's "Be the Decision Maker" sound-design scenario (FYF p. 255). Distinguished: pose it to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "I would make it sound _____ because _____" and "A realistic sound tells the audience _____, but an unexpected sound tells them _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who names what the audience feels rather than what the object is, and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 1, the two pauses during the Arts/AV cluster tour video. Distinguished: name both stop points before the video starts, give 45 seconds per jot with the prompt on screen, and offer two stems: "One Arts/AV career I did not expect is _____" and "One thing I still want to know about _____ is _____." Read jots over shoulders during both pauses and track which careers get named. If most jots say Graphic Designer only, steer the Hat browse toward Sound Engineer, UX Designer, and Art Director so Day 5's exit tickets are not thirty copies of the same career.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 2, Activity 2, the Xello Resume Builder work time, and Day 3, Activity 2, the Two Stars and a Wish peer review. Distinguished: on Day 2, walk three laps with one target each (lap 1 header and career objective filled in, lap 2 at least three specific skills, lap 3 activities and community service not blank), carrying the Resume Revision Checklist as the clipboard, with feedback ready before class for the two predictable misconceptions, "I have nothing to put down" and a career objective that reads "I want a job." On Day 3 the lap target is the feedback itself: post the two star stems ("Your skills section is strong because _____", "Your career objective is clear because _____") and the one wish stem ("Your _____ section would be stronger if you added _____"), and ask each pair to show you one specific change they actually made. If a lap turns up more than a handful of blank Skills sections, reproject the skills list to the whole room instead of prompting student by student.
- **Chunking:** Day 2, Activity 1, the six resume sections modeled live in Xello. Distinguished: build one section at a time, say what each section proves before you fill it (Skills proves you can do something, Community Service proves you show up), and close each section with the thumbs check already written into the plan before starting the next. Note which section drew the most thumbs down; that section becomes lap 1's target in Activity 2, and students who cleared every chunk start on CareerOneStop action verbs instead of waiting. The same live-build move runs on Day 3, where the first audio cue gets rewritten on the board before students touch the other three.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 5, Activity 1, the Merch Mode sketch and share. Distinguished: post the three blocks before Canva opens, Voice 1 for the brainstorm and the sketch, Voice 3 for the presenter in Step 5, and a visible countdown on every one of the five steps. State where sticky notes go and where finished sketches sit so the share does not eat sketch time. Narrate the tables that are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones that are not. If the room is still sketching when the Step 4 countdown ends, cut the share to partners only and announce the change before the timer runs out.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- Provide a printed resume template with section headers and sentence starters for students who struggle with Xello
- Offer pre-made Canva t-shirt templates students can customize rather than designing from scratch
- Allow students to complete the resume in bullet-point format if full sentences are too challenging
- Pre-built podcast episode outline template with the workbook checklist (FYF p. 271) already laid out

### Extensions
- Students create a complete personal brand package: logo, resume header, and social media banner all in a consistent visual style
- Research the Adobe Certified Professional exam topics and create a 4-year course plan to earn it through Digital Communications or Graphic Design & Illustration at Irving High, MacArthur, or Nimitz
- Compare the career trajectories of a Graphic Designer vs. UX Designer over 10 years using BLS data
- The chapter's four unused sections are the enrichment bank. Send students who need a clock to Time Management (FYF pp. 262-263), students who want a second design brief to The Visual Hook (FYF pp. 259-261), students coming off the 5th six weeks architecture and TinkerCAD work to Setting the Stage (FYF pp. 266-267), and students drawn to film production to Safety on the Set (FYF pp. 264-265)

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Resume = Currículum vitae, Portfolio = Portafolio, Graphic Design = Diseño gráfico, Branding = Identidad de marca, Job Search = Búsqueda de empleo
- Bilingual resume template with Spanish section headers and career objective sentence stems
- Canva and Adobe Express have Spanish interface options, show ESL students how to switch
- Pair ESL students with bilingual peers during resume peer review to ensure feedback is understood
