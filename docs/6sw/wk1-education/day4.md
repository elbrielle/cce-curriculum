# Day 4: Teach Through Play + Why Service Matters

## Target and Evidence

**TEKS:** d(1)(C), d(4)(E)
**Evidence:** seven-page Teach Through Play and Service Reflection

Students design and revise a classroom-safe activity that practices gross and fine motor skills, then explain how service benefits a community and develops transferable skills.

## Before Class

- Open licensed FYF pp. 216-217 and the student packet.
- If using physical testing, provide teacher-approved scissors, scrap paper, and clear movement space. A tabletop walk-through or written simulation is equal.
- Do not require disclosure of personal volunteer history.

## 50-Minute Flow

1. **Warm-up and model — 5 min.** Demonstrate gross versus fine motor without grading physical ability.
2. **Read the brief — 7 min.** Establish safety, access alternatives, and child-friendly language.
3. **Plan and map — 18 min.** Students write steps and draw the activity.
4. **Test and revise — 10 min.** Partner, tabletop, teacher-conference, or individual simulation route.
5. **Service analysis — 7 min.** Real, planned, or supplied tutoring scenario; connect one skill across two careers.
6. **Exit — 3 min.** One revision and one reason service matters.

## Monitor and Key

Evidence must show both target skills or an access-equivalent fine-motor route, directions a child can follow, two specific revisions, a community benefit, a volunteer skill, and transfer to two careers. Do not score balance, cutting performance, disclosure, artistry, or partner availability.

## Access, Absence, and Platform Route

Students can complete the full task through the packet and Canvas. Speech-to-text, enlarged print, pre-cut materials, adapted scissors, a seated/supported movement route, and teacher scribing are valid supports.
