# Week 1: Teaching Tomorrow — Education & Training Careers

**6th Six Weeks** | Education & Training Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore the Education and Training career cluster through the Hats & Ladders app and the *Find Your Future* workbook, design a learning space and a persuasive poster in the "Community Classroom" activity, compare traditional and alternative certification pathways for Texas teachers, rank four leaders against workplace situations in the "Powerskill: Leadership" lesson, read real local job postings to see what employers ask for, and design an early childhood play activity that builds gross and fine motor skills in "Teach Through Play." Students connect the cluster to Irving ISD's Education and Training and Early Childhood Education programs of study.

**Learning Target:** I can name at least three Education and Training careers, tell what training each one needs, design a learning space and a play activity that meet stated goals, and read a real job posting to say what an employer wants.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my Community Classroom poster explains the space, persuades a teacher to book it, and names the learning goals it meets, when my Pathway Comparison worksheet has every cell filled for both certification routes, when my two job posting records list the responsibilities and the education each employer asks for, and when my Teach Through Play activity gets a partner to balance on one foot for 5 seconds and cut along a straight line.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe at least three Education & Training careers, explain the difference between a traditional teaching degree and alternative certification, design a learning space and an early childhood play activity, and name two leader strengths that would make me a great educator."

## Career Connection

Education professionals shape every other career field, every doctor, engineer, lawyer, and entrepreneur had a teacher who helped them get there. In Texas, there are two main pathways into teaching: the traditional route (4-year Education degree + student teaching) and the alternative certification route (any bachelor's degree + alt-cert program like iTeach or Texas Teachers). Both pathways lead to the same TEA teaching certificate. Education is also uniquely connected to community service, a core value of the profession and the natural anchor for d(4)(E).

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** The workbook's district pages (FYF pp. 218-219: "What is Happening at My District?") open with the **"I Am Next" pinning ceremony**, which celebrates students committing to a career in teaching, and they name two programs of study. **Education and Training** builds foundational skills in instruction, communication, and classroom management, and during their junior and senior years its students visit elementary schools daily to work with and support younger students. It is offered at **Irving High School, MacArthur High School, and Nimitz High School**, a multi-campus pathway students can pursue regardless of their home school. **Early Childhood Education** builds skills in child development, learning activities, health and safety, and classroom support, and it is offered at **Cardwell Career Preparatory Center** as a travel-to career center pathway. The same pages name **Educational Aide I** as the cluster's industry certification and the **Texas Association of Future Educators (TAFE)** as its student organization, and they close by asking students whether an endorsement should be chosen for current interests, future goals, or both. Day 5 carries a `[VERIFY with CTE coordinator]` note on the Early Childhood credential, which the workbook's district pages do not name.

## Vocabulary

- **Alternative Certification:** A pathway to becoming a teacher without a traditional Education degree. Requires a bachelor's degree in any field plus completion of an approved certification program.
- **Student Teaching:** A semester-long supervised classroom experience required as part of a traditional Education degree.
- **Community Service:** Unpaid work done to benefit the community. Community service builds career skills like leadership, communication, and empathy.
- **Educational Aide:** A paraprofessional who assists teachers in the classroom. Irving ISD's Education and Training program of study names Educational Aide I as its industry certification.
- **TEA:** Texas Education Agency, the state organization that oversees public education and teacher certification in Texas.
- **Instructional Designer:** A professional who designs curriculum, courses, and learning experiences (often for online or corporate training).
- **Gross Motor Skills:** Big movements like balancing, jumping, and running. They build strength and coordination, and early childhood teachers plan play that practices them.
- **Fine Motor Skills:** Small hand and finger movements like cutting and drawing. They build the control a child needs for writing.
- **Endorsement:** A group of related high school courses focused on one area such as business, arts, technology, or public service.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Education and Training printed pages 213-220, the thinnest cluster chapter in the book. The opener (FYF p. 213: "Exploring the World of EDUCATION AND TRAINING") frames the cluster as helping people learn, grow, and build skills for the future, in classrooms and workplaces both. It puts a "Be the Decision Maker" prompt in front of students, who are in charge of curriculum at a school and must invent a brand new class every middle schooler has to take, and it prints three Hats on the page: Curator, Early Childhood Teacher, and Middle School Teacher. The chapter prints no pathway list. Students meet the cluster's pathways in the Hats & Ladders app instead, through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 220), and they meet Irving ISD's own programs of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 218-219).

Two chapter activities carry this week:

- **Community Classroom** (Day 1): Career Climb activity. Students are in charge of curriculum at a school and design an exciting new learning space for third-grade science. They learn the requirements, brainstorm with a partner on sticky notes, build a poster that explains the concept and persuades teachers to book the space, and present it to a second pair playing third-grade teachers (FYF pp. 214-215: "Community Classroom").
- **Teach Through Play** (Day 4): Career Climb activity. Students step into an early childhood teacher's role and design a themed play activity that gets young children to balance on one foot for 5 seconds and cut along a straight line. They build it on chart paper, test it with a partner taking turns as child and teacher, and write two improvements (FYF pp. 216-217: "Teach Through Play").

Two activities cannot carry five days, so the week borrows from two other chapters. **Powerskill: Leadership** (FYF pp. 17-20) sits in the World of Work chapter. Students meet four leaders with named strengths and challenges, rank them best fit to worst fit against workplace situations, and defend each ranking with evidence from the profiles. It runs on Day 2. **Rung 5: Local Connections** (FYF pp. 290-291) belongs to the Capstone. Students search real local job postings and record the responsibilities, skills, qualifications, and education each employer asks for. They practice it on Day 3 inside the Education cluster on a practice handout, and the graded rung pages are completed later this six weeks in the job-skills week, so the workbook pages stay blank. The rest of the week runs on the Hats & Ladders app, Xello, eDynamic, and CCE-authored worksheets.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 1, the "Be the Decision Maker" prompt on the chapter opener (FYF p. 213), where students invent a brand new class every middle schooler must take. Distinguished: pose it to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "My class is called _____ and students would learn _____" and "Middle schoolers need this class because _____, so the teacher would have to be good at _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. While pairs talk, listen for who gets past naming the class to naming what its teacher would have to be able to do, and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 1, the two pauses during the Education and Training cluster tour video. Distinguished: name both stop points before the video starts, give 45 seconds per jot with the prompt on screen, and offer two stems: "One education career I did not know existed is _____" and "One thing I still want to know about _____ is _____." Read jots over shoulders during both pauses and track which careers students name. If nearly every jot says Teacher, steer the Hat browse that follows toward Curator, Instructional Designer, School Counselor, and Corporate Trainer so Day 2's career research worksheets are not all the same career.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 3, Activity 2, the search for two local job postings. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times with one target per lap, lap 1 that a real current local posting is open rather than a career article, lap 2 that the responsibilities are copied from the posting's own list, lap 3 that the education and experience fields are filled in even when the posting buries them. Have the feedback ready before class for the two known errors: a job board career profile recorded instead of an actual opening, and "college degree" written where the posting says a diploma plus 48 college credit hours. If more than a handful of students miss the lap 1 target, pivot and reproject the search string and the district careers page to the whole room instead of fixing it screen by screen.
- **Chunking:** Day 2, Activity 1, the two certification pathways, and Day 3, Activity 1, the four job posting field groups. Distinguished: teach one pathway and one field group at a time, say what each chunk is for before walking it, and close each chunk with a check a student can visibly fail, restating what the traditional route costs in time on Day 2 and pointing to the answer on the projected posting on Day 3. Whichever chunk students cannot restate is the one to reproject before independent work starts, and students who clear all four posting chunks early begin their first search instead of waiting.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 4, Activity 1, the Teach Through Play build and partner test. Distinguished: post the three blocks before any scissors come out, Voice 1 for build talk in Step 4 and Voice 2 in Step 5 where one partner gives a young child instructions out loud, a visible countdown for the build and another for the partner test, a named place where scissors, tape, and scrap paper go when a pair finishes, and a marked floor space for each pair's balancing test. Narrate the pairs meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If a pair is losing test time to hunting for supplies, move that pair's materials to the table before the next countdown starts.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- Provide sentence starters for the Community Classroom poster: "My space is a _____. Teachers should sign up because _____. Students will get to _____."
- Offer a simplified Pathway Comparison with one row pre-filled as an example.
- Provide a printed list of Education hats so students who struggle to navigate the app still get exposure.
- Provide one completed Job Posting record as a model before students search on Day 3.
- Allow students to describe hypothetical community service if they have limited real experience.

### Extensions
- Students research the Texas teacher shortage in a specific subject (math, science, special ed, bilingual) and propose how alternative certification could help.
- Interview a teacher at your school about their pathway and present findings.
- Compare Texas teacher salaries to three other states and analyze whether pay affects teacher supply.
- Run the workbook's Leadership Twist (FYF p. 20): write a new challenge situation, trade it with another group, and rank the leaders for the situation you receive.

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Education = Educación, Certification = Certificación, Community Service = Servicio comunitario, Volunteer = Voluntario, Learning Space = Espacio de aprendizaje, Balance = Equilibrio
- Bilingual Community Classroom poster template with Spanish sentence stems
- Pair ESL students with bilingual peers during pathway comparison research
- Education careers have strong visual context for ESL students, they interact with teachers daily. The workbook reads well above grade level, so read the scenario paragraphs aloud and encourage browser translation for any independent reading.
