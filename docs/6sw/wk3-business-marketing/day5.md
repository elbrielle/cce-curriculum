## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: If you could work at any company's marketing department in DFW, which company would you choose and why? AT&T, Toyota, Mary Kay, American Airlines, Frito-Lay, Topgolf, the Cowboys?**

Take 4-5 student responses. This builds personal connection to the local marketing industry. DFW has one of the largest concentrations of corporate marketing teams in the country. Students should know they can have this career at home.

## Activity 1: Mini Marketing Plan Pitches (25 min)

**Source:** Compilation of the week's work ("Click Factor" + "Expert Edge" + the Family Fun Pass data work + the career research worksheet)

Today students pull together everything from this week into a 1-minute Mini Marketing Plan pitch. Most will pitch the consulting service they built in "Expert Edge" on Day 3, and any student who would rather pitch a different product or service may.

**Pitch structure (1 minute, no more):**

1. **Hook (10 seconds):** One sentence that grabs attention. Example: *"You have three days to learn a song for the talent show, and I am the person who gets you there."*
2. **Product/Service (15 seconds):** Name + what it is + what the client gets
3. **Target Audience (10 seconds):** Who buys it and why
4. **One Marketing Channel (15 seconds):** How will you reach that audience? Use the same six options the Family Fun Pass teams worked from on Day 4: social media ads, an influencer partnership, in-store promotion, an email campaign, video ads, or one of your own.
5. **Why it will sell (10 seconds):** One sentence connecting to economic conditions: *"This will sell because even in a tough economy, people still pay for something that saves them time."*

**Student work time (10 min):** Students draft their 1-minute pitch on a notecard or in a Google Doc. They practice silently or with a partner.

**Pitches (15 min):** Students take turns presenting to a small group of 4-5 (NOT the whole class, there isn't time). Each student presents in 1 minute. Group members give Two Stars and a Wish feedback.

For larger classes, run pitches in parallel groups simultaneously. Each group selects ONE "best pitch" to present to the whole class at the end.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    **Time, Voice, Body:** post the three blocks before the first pitch. Project a 60 second visible timer for every pitch, hold Voice 0 while a group member is presenting, Voice 2 for the Two Stars and a Wish feedback, and say where presenters stand and where notecards go. Because pitches run in parallel small groups, not whole-class, students get a low-stakes first try at a timed delivery. Announce the 60 second limit before pitches start. If a student is still wrapping at 60 seconds, let them finish the sentence and move on.

**DOK 4:** What makes a pitch convincing? Is it the product, the target audience research, or the way the person delivers it? Which matters MORE in real marketing, and why?

**DELIVERABLE:** 1-minute Marketing Plan pitch (delivered orally to small group with feedback).

## Activity 2: eDynamic Unit 4.1 — Enterprise and Money Matters (12 min)

**Source:** eDynamic Learning Unit 4.1 (scope-and-sequence column 9)

Direct students to log into eDynamic Learning and open **Unit 4.1: Enterprise and Money Matters**. This unit covers Business Management & Administration, Finance, and Marketing/Sales/Service clusters together. Students focus specifically on the Marketing content.

## Activity 3: H&L Marketing Favorites + Career Plan Update (5 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 254: "App Exploration")

Direct students to open H&L and complete two tasks:

1. **Favorite at least 2 Marketing careers** using the Hat Finder. Save them to the Climber Profile.
2. **Update Building Blocks:** Add any marketing or design experiences from this week (built an ad mock-up, opened a consulting business, designed a logo, chose a campaign strategy from data, gave a pitch).

> [H&L PLATFORM] Students click the heart icon on Hats they want to save. Favorites feed into the H&L Career Plan and the District Course Planner. Anyone still missing an App Exploration step from earlier in the week finishes it now: rate at least 1 pathway and at least 3 Hats.

The Career Plan is cumulative. Students should NOT start over. It now reflects every cluster they've explored this year.

Tell students where this week's saved careers go next. They already wrote a career goal and ran a deep dive on it at mid-year (FYF pp. 283-286: "Rung 2: Career Goal" and "Rung 3: Career Deep Dive"). A marketing career favorited this week that beats the one written on those pages is a reason to revise the goal before the Capstone presentation at the end of the six weeks (FYF p. 299: "Prepare & Present").

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Allow students to read their pitch from a notecard (not memorized). Provide a fill-in-the-blank pitch template ("Our product is _____. It's for _____. We will reach them through _____. People will buy it because _____.").
- **Extension:** Students who finish their pitch early write a 2-paragraph "marketing resume" describing themselves as a marketing freelancer (skills, experience, services offered, target clients).
- **ELL:** Allow pitch in Spanish or bilingual format. DFW has a huge Spanish-language marketing industry, bilingual marketers are highly paid. Pre-teach: Pitch = Presentación corta, Audience = Audiencia, Channel = Canal.
