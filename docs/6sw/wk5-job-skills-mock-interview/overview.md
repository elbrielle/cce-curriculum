# Week 5: Hired! — Job Skills & Mock Interview

**6th Six Weeks** | Business Management & Administration Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students complete the full job-seeking skill set: identify the steps of an effective job search and record what employers actually ask for in two real local job postings, write a cover letter and thank-you letter, complete a sample job application, learn the protocol for selecting and using references, and participate in a mock interview demonstrating professional appearance and communication.

**Learning Target:** I can name the steps of a job search, read what an employer asks for in a real job posting, write a cover letter and a thank-you letter, fill out a job application, pick three references and ask one for permission, and interview for a job.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when both of my job posting pages list the job title, the skills, and the education the employer wants, when my cover letter has a header, a greeting, three paragraphs, and a closing, when every line of my practice application is filled in and easy to read, when my reference list names three people and says why each one, when I have drafted a permission email to one of them, and when my mock interview is scored on the rubric and my thank-you letter is written.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can complete a job application, write a cover letter and thank-you letter, select appropriate references, and participate in a mock interview with professional appearance and communication."

## Vocabulary

- **Job Posting:** An employer's public listing for an open job. It names the title, the duties, the skills required, and the education or experience wanted.
- **Cover Letter:** A one-page letter sent with a resume introducing yourself and explaining why you want a specific position.
- **Job Application:** A formal document providing an employer with your personal information, work history, education, and references.
- **References:** People who can speak positively about your character, skills, and work ethic to a potential employer.
- **Mock Interview:** A practice interview that simulates a real job interview, used for rehearsal and feedback.
- **Thank-You Letter:** A brief letter sent within 24 hours of an interview thanking the interviewer and reaffirming your interest.
- **Greeting / Salutation:** The opening line of a business letter, usually "Dear [Name]:", addressed to a specific person if possible.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook has no job interview activity, interview question set, dress-code page, application form, cover letter, or thank-you letter. Those parts of this week are **CCE-authored** and carry no workbook citation. Use the printed templates as the source; there is no workbook page for students to find.

The workbook supplies three pieces that feed the CCE templates:

- **Rung 5: Local Connections** (FYF pp. 290-291): students search actual job postings in their community for the career they chose on Rung 2, using searches like "[career name] jobs near me," then record for each of two postings the job title, the location, where they found it, two or three listed responsibilities, the required skills and qualifications, and the education and experience the employer wants. Students practiced this form earlier in the six weeks. **This week is where Rung 5 is completed for credit**, and it becomes the employer-expectation input for the cover letter on Days 1-2 and the practice application on Day 3.
- **Rung 4: Strengths Interview** (FYF pp. 287-289): the out-of-class interview students were assigned earlier in the six weeks and carry into this week. Part 1 has them name a trusted adult to interview, plan questions from a provided list, and add at least two questions of their own. Part 2 is interview notes, and "Make Meaning of What You Heard" is the reflection. It is an informational interview with a trusted adult, not a job interview, so this week uses it for interview conduct only: what a prepared interviewer does, and what the asking side of the table feels like. The Rung 4 pages themselves are debriefed in the capstone week.
- **Make the Call: CEO Edition** (FYF pp. 248-251): the Business chapter's Career Climb activity and the closest match in the book to this week's Office Manager, Executive Assistant, and Human Resources Specialist Hats. Partners run Summit Gear, review a six-situation Situation Board, sort each situation into Act Today, Plan This Week, or Monitor and Decide Later on a CEO Priority Map, justify one placement from each category, align with another pair, then absorb a CEO Curveball that cuts the budget 20 percent and removes a department leader. It runs as the Day 1 extension because every minute of the five days is already carrying a job-skills standard.
- **What is Happening at My District?** (FYF pp. 252-253) and **App Exploration** (FYF p. 254) carry the Business, Marketing, and Finance cluster's district content and the app path used on Day 1.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 4, Activity 1, the partner read of the Interview Readiness Guide and the one-tip share-out. Distinguished: assign the read as partner work, hold a silent 30 second Think on "which of these would I forget on the day," then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "The tip I would most likely forget is _____ because _____" and "Arriving 15 minutes early tells an employer _____ before I say a word." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who names appearance and who names timing, then pick your two share-out pairs so both show up before Day 5 rather than taking raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 1, the notes students take as the seven steps go up on the board. Distinguished: name the jot before the first step goes up, give 45 seconds after Step 3 and again after Step 7 with the prompt on screen, and offer two stems: "Step _____ is one I have already done this year, because _____" and "The step I would most likely skip is _____, and skipping it would cost me _____." Read jots over shoulders during both pauses. If most students name Step 4 as the one they would skip, use the live posting search that follows to show that a posting tells you exactly what an application is going to ask for.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 5, Activity 1, the two paired mock interview rounds. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times, one target per lap (lap 1 both partners have their question sheet out and the timer running, lap 2 the interviewee is holding eye contact and answering in more than one sentence, lap 3 the interviewer is scoring the rubric instead of watching), and score two or three students directly on the same rubric the students are using. Have feedback ready before class for the two known failures: a "tell me about yourself" answer that turns into a life story, and an interviewer who reads all eight questions without waiting for the answers. If more than a handful of pairs stall on the problem-solving question, pause the room, model one 30 second problem story, and restart the round.
- **Chunking:** Day 3, Activity 1, the practice application walked section by section. Distinguished: project one section at a time (Personal Information, Education, Employment History, Skills, Availability, References, Signature), say what the employer is checking in that section before students write anything in it, and close each section with a check that every line in it is filled and legible before releasing the next. Note which section produces the most blanks. Employment History is the usual one, and if it stalls the room, stop and reteach that babysitting, lawn care, and family business help all count before students move on to Skills.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 5, Activity 1, the two interview rounds. Distinguished: post the three blocks before the first round, Voice 0 while partners read their prepared answers, Voice 2 for the interviews so two pairs can work side by side without shouting, and a visible countdown for every round with a short feedback window between. Define where the interviewer sits, where the interviewee sits, and where phones go for the whole period. Narrate the pairs who start on time rather than correcting the ones who do not. If the first round runs past the countdown, cut the feedback window to one star and one wish and announce the change before the switch.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- Sentence-by-sentence cover letter template with blanks for students to fill in
- Simplified job application with fewer required fields
- Allow students to use their notes as a reference during the mock interview
- Mock interview question cards with suggested answer frameworks on the back

### Extensions
- "Make the Call: CEO Edition" (FYF pp. 248-251) as the Day 1 extension: partners sort the six-situation Situation Board into a CEO Priority Map, justify one placement per category, align with a second pair, then rework the map after the CEO Curveball
- Second mock interview with a different partner for an improvement comparison
- Write a cover letter for a REAL summer job or volunteer position the student wants
- Research video interview best practices and prepare answers for a video format
- Practice with the Xello Job Interviews lesson as additional reps

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Cover Letter = Carta de presentación, Job Application = Solicitud de empleo, Mock Interview = Entrevista de práctica, References = Referencias, Thank-You Letter = Carta de agradecimiento
- Bilingual cover letter template with Spanish sentence starters
- Mock interview question cards with Spanish translations on back
- Allow ESL students to mock interview with a bilingual partner
- Bilingual interviewers are highly valued in DFW, make this an asset, not a barrier
