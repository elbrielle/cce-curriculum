## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Imagine you are 18, just graduated from high school, and looking for your first real job. Write down, in order, the first 5 things you would do.**

Take 4-5 responses. Most students will have part of the process and leave gaps. Keep their list visible, then compare it with the seven job-search steps used in today's lesson.

## Activity 1: The Seven Steps of a Job Search (15 min)

**Source:** CCE-authored framework (scope and sequence d(6)(A)) + (FYF pp. 290-291: "Rung 5: Local Connections") for Step 2

The seven steps below are CCE-authored. The *Find Your Future* workbook does not print a job search process, so the numbered list is the source, not a workbook page. Walk the seven steps on the projector in about seven minutes, then hand the room over to Step 2 for the rest of the block.

1. **Self-Assessment:** Know what you want. Use your Hats & Ladders Climber Profile, your core personality types, your Building Blocks, and the careers you have favorited all year.
2. **Research Openings:** Search job boards (Indeed, ZipRecruiter, LinkedIn, Handshake), company websites, and people you know. Use specific keywords.
3. **Prepare Materials:** Resume, cover letter (Day 2), references (Day 3). Have these ready BEFORE you find a job posting.
4. **Apply:** Submit application + cover letter + resume. Follow directions exactly. Sloppy applications get ignored.
5. **Prepare for Interview:** Research the company. Practice common questions. Plan your outfit. Bring copies of your resume.
6. **Interview:** Eye contact, firm handshake, clear answers, smile. Show interest in the company.
7. **Follow Up:** Send a thank-you email within 24 hours. This step alone makes you stand out.

**Stop and Jot:** After Step 3 and again after Step 7, give 45 seconds with the prompt on screen. Stems: "Step _____ is one I have already done this year, because _____" and "The step I would most likely skip is _____, and skipping it would cost me _____." Students keep these notes with their seven-step organizer.

**Step 2, live (about 7 min).** Students open the Capstone Rung 5 pages (FYF pp. 290-291) and run the workbook's own search on Chromebooks: "[career name] jobs near me" or "[career name] jobs in Texas," using the career they chose on Rung 2. They fill **Job Posting #1** completely: job title, location, where they found the posting, two or three responsibilities the posting lists, the skills and qualifications required, and the education and experience the employer wants. Students practiced this form earlier in the six weeks, so today is the graded run.

**Job Posting #2** is finished outside class. Both pages are collected at the start of Day 3 and scored.

**Connection to the year:** Step 1 is everything students have done since the first week of the course. Steps 2 and 3 are what this week covers. Steps 4 through 7 are Days 3, 4, and 5. Students are already further along than they realize.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Number each step on the board big and visible. Refer back to them all week. ("We did Step 3 yesterday. Today we're working on Step 5.") Repetition cements the steps in students' memories, and the steps are what students need to recall by name on today's exit ticket. During the live search, the fastest fix for a student with zero results is to widen the location from Irving to Dallas-Fort Worth before widening the job title.

## Activity 2: Business and Administrative Management Hats (10 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 254: "App Exploration") + (FYF pp. 252-253: "What is Happening at My District?")

The district pages describe **Business Management** as preparing students for careers in business by focusing on leadership, organizational operations, and decision making while building workplace communication skills, and **Administrative Management** as preparing students for careers in office management and business support by focusing on administrative procedures, professional communication, and organizational skills. Read both aloud and put them next to the app.

> [H&L PLATFORM] Open the Hats & Ladders app. Go to Clusters and click the Business, Marketing, and Finance Cluster. Click "Pathway Possibilities," answer the questions, then explore and rate at least 1 pathway. Rate at least 3 Hats before leaving the cluster.

Inside the cluster, students browse five specific Hats: Office Manager, Executive Assistant, Human Resources Specialist, Operations Manager, and Administrative Assistant.

Quick salary check on bls.gov: Administrative Services Manager median is around $100K nationally. Office workers are the backbone of every company, and these jobs are everywhere in DFW.

**Why this connects to today:** Administrative careers REQUIRE strong job skills (cover letters, resumes, references, interviews) because they are the people who often handle the hiring documents at companies. They know what good looks like.

## Activity 3: Cover Letter Outline Setup (15 min)

**Source:** Cover Letter Template + sample job posting (CCE artifacts; the workbook has no cover letter page)

Distribute the printed sample job posting. Use a real-sounding posting like:

> **Position:** Part-Time Office Assistant, Pecan Creek Veterinary Clinic
>
> **Description:** Pecan Creek Vet Clinic is hiring a part-time Office Assistant for after-school hours. Duties include greeting patients (and their pets), answering phones, filing paperwork, and helping the office manager with daily tasks. Looking for friendly, organized, dependable students. No experience required, we will train.
>
> **Qualifications:** Friendly attitude, basic computer skills, ability to work 3-5 hours after school, comfortable around dogs and cats.
>
> **To Apply:** Send cover letter and resume to pecancreekvet@example.com.

Students will write a cover letter for THIS posting starting Day 2. Today they only need to outline what they will say. Before they outline, have them put the vet clinic's qualifications line next to the "skills and qualifications required" box they just filled on Job Posting #1. Every posting answers the same question, and the cover letter's job is to answer it back.

Distribute the Cover Letter Template. Walk through the six parts:

1. **Header:** Your name, contact info, date, employer name + address
2. **Greeting:** "Dear [Hiring Manager / Employer Name]:" (use a real name if possible)
3. **Body Paragraph 1, Why I'm Writing:** Name the job, where you saw it, one sentence about why you're interested
4. **Body Paragraph 2, Why I'm Qualified:** Connect YOUR skills to THIS job. Use specific examples.
5. **Body Paragraph 3, Call to Action:** Thank the reader, ask for an interview, share your contact info
6. **Closing:** "Sincerely," + your name

Students fill in JUST the outline today, bullet points for each section. They will write the full sentences on Day 2.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    The most common cover letter mistake is making it about YOU instead of the job. Push students: "What does the vet clinic NEED? What do YOU have that matches?" Cover letters work when they answer the employer's question, not when they list the student's life story.

**DOK 2:** Why does a cover letter need to be different for each job? Why can't you write one cover letter and send it to everyone?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a printed seven-step job search reference card with simple icons next to each step. For the live search, pre-load two Irving-area postings so a student with no results still has a real posting to read. Provide a partially-filled cover letter outline for the vet clinic posting with the header pre-completed.
- **Extension:** "Make the Call: CEO Edition" (FYF pp. 248-251). Partners become the CEO of Summit Gear, an outdoor equipment company, and read the six-situation Situation Board: a delayed backpack shipment, a conflict between the Head of Marketing and the Head of Product, spending over projection, a competitor's price drop on a tent, a national retail partnership offer with a one-week deadline, and rising complaints about late deliveries and damaged packaging. They sort all six into Act Today, Plan This Week, or Monitor and Decide Later on a CEO Priority Map, pick one situation from each category and explain what happens if they wait too long and what happens if they act too soon, compare maps with another pair, then rework the map after the CEO Curveball cuts the budget by 20 percent and a department leader resigns. Also available: students outline their cover letter for the posting they recorded on Job Posting #1 instead of the vet clinic sample.
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Job Search = Búsqueda de empleo, Application = Solicitud, Interview = Entrevista, Follow Up = Seguimiento. Many DFW employers value bilingual workers, encourage students to mention bilingual abilities in their cover letter.
