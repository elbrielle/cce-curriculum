## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Who in your family or neighborhood is most likely to fall for a fake email or a scam text? What is the ONE thing from yesterday you wish you could teach them?**

Quick share. Listen for specific people and specific red flags, not "everybody" and "be careful." Bridge: "Today you build the program that teaches them. Nobody pays you for it and nobody checks up on you, which is exactly where work ethic shows up."

---

## Activity 1: Plan Your Cybersecurity Bootcamp (20 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 34-35: "Powerskill: Work Ethic")

Open the workbook to page 34. Read the Powerskill definition together: work ethic is working hard and doing it well. It means showing up on time, doing your best, being responsible, and not giving up when things get hard. People with a strong work ethic take pride in their work and do their best even when nobody is watching.

Then read the activity scenario: students volunteer in their community by creating and leading a **Cybersecurity Bootcamp** to teach other people how to protect themselves online.

**Step 1: Understand the Need (7 min).** The workbook supplies the research that justifies the program. Put the four findings on the screen and have students underline the one that surprises them most:

- 73% of adults have experienced an online scam or attack, such as credit card fraud or a fake shopping site
- more than 60% of adults get a scam phone call or text every week
- many students do not know how to build a strong password and put personal information like a birthday or a name into it
- many students do not know the red flags of a fake email, text, or social media message

Students then pick a bootcamp topic from the workbook's menu: identifying phishing emails, texts, and social posts; password security basics, including the passphrase method of stringing random words together; the risks of public Wi-Fi at coffee shops, airports, and parks; or their own idea. Point out that the first topic is yesterday's red-flag table, so every student already owns enough content to teach it.

**Step 2: Plan Your Program (13 min).** Students fill the six-row planning table on page 35. Students who need the questions spread out with a stem under each use the printed planning template instead ([Printable PDF](../../resources/worksheets/wk5-bootcamp-planning-template.pdf)):

| Question | What the answer has to do |
|---|---|
| What is the name of your program? | Be catchy and say what it is. "Scam Slam" beats "Cyber Program." |
| What are the main learning goals? | Name what a person can DO when they walk out, not what they will hear about. |
| Who are you trying to reach? | Pick one group: kids, middle school students, or families. The rest of the plan depends on this. |
| What will you do during the program? | A workshop, a hands-on activity, a game. Name the actual thing people do. |
| Where will it take place? | School, library, park, or online. |
| How could your program help your community? | Tie it back to one of the four findings from Step 1. |

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    The audience row is the one that decides the plan's quality. A student who writes "everyone" ends up with vague goals and a vague flyer. Send them back to the warm-up: they already named a real person. Build the bootcamp for that person.

## Activity 2: Step 3 — Design the Flyer + Peer Feedback (10 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 35: "Powerskill: Work Ethic", Step 3)

**Flyer (7 min).** Students design a colorful flyer that shares information about their program. The workbook's one hard requirement is that the flyer must include a way for people to sign up. Students work in Canva, Adobe Express, or on paper.

**Peer feedback (3 min).** Pairs swap flyers and score on the printed peer feedback slip, one per student ([Printable PDF](../../resources/worksheets/wk5-flyer-peer-feedback.pdf)). The workbook prints no rubric for this activity, so the slip uses the four-level scale the workbook uses for the year-end Capstone (FYF p. 280: "Capstone Project Requirements"): **4 Excellent, 3 Good, 2 Okay, 1 Needs Improvement**. The same four levels come back on the six-weeks capstone rubric, so students see this scale three times.

| Criteria | Score 1-4 | One specific note |
|---|---|---|
| The flyer shows the reviewer understands what problem this bootcamp solves | | |
| The flyer gives the right information for the audience it names | | |
| The flyer fits that audience's age and reading level | | |
| The flyer tells people how to sign up | | |

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Watch for students who write 4 / 4 / 4 / 4 without reading. A score with no note in the third column is not feedback. Ask for the note first and the number second, and the numbers get honest fast.

## Activity 3: Work Ethic and Integrity in the Cybersecurity Workplace (12 min)

Move from the bootcamp to the workplace. The bootcamp is volunteer work: nobody pays for it and nobody checks it, so the only thing keeping the quality up is the student's own standard. That is the workbook's definition of work ethic doing real work. In cybersecurity the same standard has a second name, integrity, because the professionals in this field hold access that nobody is watching them use.

Three real-world examples anchor the point:

1. **A hospital cybersecurity analyst** has access to patient medical records: diagnoses, prescriptions, test results. Integrity means never looking unless the job requires it.
2. **A bank security engineer** has access to every customer's account data. Integrity means never using that access for personal gain or to satisfy curiosity.
3. **An ethical hacker** is hired to find weaknesses in a company's website. Integrity means reporting what they find to the company immediately and never using the weakness themselves or passing it to anyone else.

**Think-Pair-Share:** Run the workbook's sponsor prompt (FYF p. 35). A local company wants to sponsor next year's bootcamp because it heard the crew has an incredible work ethic. What specific things would that company have noticed the crew doing behind the scenes? Hold a silent 30 second Think, then Partner A for 30 seconds, then Partner B for 30 seconds. Two stems on the board: "They would notice us _____" and "The part nobody sees is _____, and that matters because _____." Two pairs share, picked from what you heard while circulating. The second workbook prompt is the follow-up if the room is warm: what other Powerskills does it take to launch a bootcamp?

Students then write a brief reflection (4-5 sentences) on the back of their bootcamp plan page answering:

- Why is integrity especially important in cybersecurity compared to other careers?
- What could happen if a cybersecurity professional lacked integrity?

**DOK 3:** What conclusions can you draw about why integrity and work ethic are MORE important in cybersecurity than in some other careers?

## Exit Ticket (3 min)

**EXIT TICKET** (Trade-off / Dilemma Analysis) · [Printable PDF](../../resources/exit-tickets/1sw-wk5-day3-powerskill-work-ethic-design-a-cybersecurity-bootcamp.pdf):

You are a NEW cybersecurity analyst at a DFW hospital. You have access to every patient's medical records. On your second day, a coworker says: "Hey, can you quickly look up my ex's diagnosis? Nobody will know."

- **(A) Look it up.** Pros: helps a coworker, feels like a small favor, easy.
- **(B) Say no and report it.** Pros: follows rules, protects patient privacy, keeps your job.

Pros of picking A: _______________________________________________________

Pros of picking B: _______________________________________________________

My choice (A or B): ________

Quality list: honest / trustworthy / careful / brave / private / professional. (Or write your own.)

Which quality fits THIS choice best? ________________________

In one sentence, why is THIS quality the right one in cybersecurity, where NO ONE would find out if you looked? *(d(4)(F))*

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Hand out the printed bootcamp planning template ([Printable PDF](../../resources/worksheets/wk5-bootcamp-planning-template.pdf)), which writes the six questions out with a sentence stem under each ("My program is for _____ because _____") and reprints the four research findings so the community-benefit answer has something to point at. For Activity 3, hand out the integrity reflection stem sheet ([Printable PDF](../../resources/worksheets/wk5-integrity-reflection-stems.pdf)): "Integrity means _____. In cybersecurity, integrity matters because _____. Without integrity, a cybersecurity professional could _____."
- **Extension:** Students research the 2017 Equifax data breach (school-appropriate version) and identify what failures of integrity or cybersecurity practice contributed to it. Students who finish the flyer early add a second bootcamp session on a different topic from the workbook's menu and say how the two sessions build on each other.
- **ELL:** Bilingual integrity vocabulary: Integridad = Integrity, Honestidad = Honesty, Confianza = Trust, Ética = Ethics, Ética de trabajo = Work ethic, Voluntario = Volunteer. Allow ELL students to write the flyer text and the integrity reflection in Spanish.
