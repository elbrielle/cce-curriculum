# Day 2: Safe or Spoofed?
<!-- CCE DAILY CONTRACT START -->
## Daily Learning Contract

- **Topic:** Phishing and Integrity
- **Objective:** Students will define integrity and identify how it appears in cybersecurity work by analyzing message evidence and choosing a safe professional response.
- **TEKS:** d(4)(F)
- **Demonstration of Learning:** Students classify seven messages, explain the hardest decision with visible evidence, state the safe response, and name one integrity behavior a cybersecurity worker demonstrates.

## Before Students Arrive

1. Open all seven locked email images in Canvas and confirm they are readable from the back of the room.
2. Post or print the [Phishing Red-Flag Checklist](../../resources/worksheets/wk5-red-flag-checklist.pdf).
3. Keep the answer guidance on the unpublished teacher page. The Climber Notes deck has no speaker-note key.
4. Post the response sequence: **Pause. Verify independently. Report. Delete.**

## Bellringer — Evidence Before a Decision (5 min)

Project Email 1 without asking students to click anything.

**Prompt:** Name one thing you can see that should slow you down. Do not decide yet.

Take two observations, then separate **observation** from **decision**: “The sender is `freerewards@prize-center123.net`” is visible evidence. “It is spoofed” is the decision supported by that evidence.

## Activity 1 — Five Red Flags (10 min)

Use FYF p. 24 and teach one clue at a time:

1. strange sender address;
2. urgent or emotionally loaded language;
3. request for private information;
4. suspicious link or attachment; and
5. spelling or grammar problems.

Two boundaries matter:

- one typo does not automatically make a message fake; and
- a polished message with no obvious flags is not automatically safe.

Hovering may preview a link on a desktop, but it does not verify the sender. On touch devices, students use the visible domain and verify through a trusted route.

## Activity 2 — Seven-Email Investigation (23 min)

Open one email at a time. Students first mark visible clues, then choose **Safe-looking** or **Spoofed**. Use **Safe-looking**, not “proven safe,” for Emails 3 and 7.

After Email 4, pause for a Think-Pair-Share:

> “The strongest clue is ___ because ___. The safe next action is ___.”

Both partners record their own evidence. Students do not need to circle directly on a projected image; the checklist supplies accessible labeled fields.

## Activity 3 — Safe Practice Draft (7 min)

Preserve the FYF practice extension with firm boundaries. Students draft on paper or in the assigned private document only. They use a fictional organization, fictional person, and an `example.com` address. They may include two written red flags, but they may not include a working link, QR code, attachment, password request, real credential, real person's name, or Irving ISD impersonation. The message is never sent or posted.

If time is tight, the draft becomes a two-line subject/sender/message sketch instead of a full email.

## Exit — Professional Response and Integrity (5 min)

Students finish three lines:

1. The hardest email was ___ because ___.
2. Instead of clicking or replying, I would ___.
3. A cybersecurity worker shows integrity by ___ because ___.

## Teacher Key

| 5 | Spoofed | Account-suspension pressure, one-hour deadline, unrelated fix domain. Contact known IT support. |

## Supports

- Read sender and domain strings aloud and enlarge the image when needed.
- Use the frame: “I marked ___ because I can see ___.”
- Let pairs discuss before each individual decision.
- Provide a text description of visible evidence for low-vision access without announcing the answer.
- Score evidence and revision, not whether the student's first call matched the key.

## If a Student Is Absent or a Device Is Unavailable

The seven locked images and checklist are the complete independent route. No live email account, link, or partner is required.
