## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Name one college, trade school, military branch, or career certification program you have heard of. What do you know about it?**

Quick share. Listen for: nearby Texas universities (UNT, UT, A&M, TWU), trade schools (Lincoln Tech, Universal Technical Institute), military branches, and certifications (CompTIA, FANUC, AWS Welding). Bridge: "Today you make a physical object that represents YOUR future plan. It is the first time this year you commit to a specific direction."

## Activity 1: MS to HS to Postsecondary Transition Discussion (10 min)

Project the four Irving ISD Information Technology pathways and walk them one by one:

- **Computer Science (District Wide):** Every IISD high school. Leads to Python certification.
- **Programming & Digital Tech (Singley Academy):** Specialized programming track. Leads to Python certification.
- **Cybersecurity (Singley Academy):** Cybersecurity Fundamentals certification.
- **Technology Support Services (Singley Academy):** Computer Repair Technology certification.

Have students open the workbook's district pages alongside the projected list (FYF pp. 36-37: "What is Happening at My District?"). The same four Information Technology programs of study are described there in the district's own words, with two industry-based certifications named (Certified Entry-Level Python Programmer and Cybersecurity Fundamentals), two student organizations (Technology Student Association and CyberPatriot), and the Artificial Intelligence program planned for the district's new Career and Technical Education Center. The page also explains what an industry-based certification is: proof that a person has learned a specific job skill, which schools, training programs, and employers all read as evidence of readiness.

Tell students: "Next year or the year after, you will be in HIGH SCHOOL. The choices you make about which courses to take in 9th grade directly affect which careers you can pursue. The good news is, all of these IT pathways are RIGHT HERE in Irving ISD. You don't need to leave your district to start any of these careers."

Then show 3-4 example postsecondary destinations:

1. **A 4-year college** (e.g., UT Dallas, strong CS program)
2. **A community college** (e.g., Dallas College, affordable, transfers to 4-year)
3. **Direct-to-work with certification** (e.g., earning CompTIA A+ at age 19, working as Help Desk Tech)
4. **Military training** (e.g., Air Force Cyber career field, Army Cyber Command, Space Force). Students curious about this path can start in **JROTC** at their Irving ISD high school, which introduces military careers and leadership without committing to enlistment.

**DOK 2:** How would you describe the academic requirements for getting into one of these IT pathways at your future high school?

## Activity 2: Logo Design (25 min)

Students design a logo representing their postsecondary goal. The logo MUST connect to a SPECIFIC postsecondary destination (not random art). The default is an icon the student draws themselves:

- **A career icon of the student's own design** (the default): a shield and lock for cybersecurity, a stethoscope for nursing, a wrench for automotive, a wing for aviation
- **A symbol for the trade school, certification, or training program** the student is aiming at
- **A student-drawn mark for a college or a military branch**, built from the student's own shapes rather than copied from the institution

Official university logos and military-branch insignia are trademarked, so a class set of laser-cut copies is a rights question no campus needs to take on. Say it once and move: a designer makes their own mark. A student aiming at UT Dallas designs what UT Dallas means to them, and that logo is stronger portfolio evidence than a traced one.

**Group by destination.** Machine time is the constraint tomorrow, so students whose destination matches sit together in pairs or trios and design ONE logo the team will cut. Every student still writes their own goal sentence, their own exit ticket, and their own reflection on Day 5. A student whose destination is unique either joins the nearest match or goes on the individual cut list for later in the week.

**Design tools.** Pick ONE and provision it before this week:

- **Canva for Education** ([canva.com](https://www.canva.com)): easiest, has logo templates and a free SVG export. Free for teachers, but it requires teacher verification and a class join code, and both take days to clear.
- **Adobe Express:** also has logo templates. K-12 use runs on a district Adobe entitlement, so check whether Irving ISD has one before planning around it.
- **Inkscape** (advanced): free desktop vector tool for students who already know SVG editing. It cannot go on a managed Chromebook without a district app push.
- **Paper and markers:** the stated alternative, the same allowance Day 3 already gives for the flyer. If no tool is live by Monday, students design on paper and the design is a finished deliverable on its own.

**Glowforge file requirements:**

- Black and white (no gradients)
- Vector format (SVG export)
- Lines for cutting (red), shapes for engraving (black)
- Simple shapes work best, avoid tiny details under 1mm

**Active Monitoring:** Walk the room with one target for every lap: the student can name the specific postsecondary goal behind the logo. Carry a clipboard printed from the [Active Monitoring Clipboard Roster](../../resources/worksheets/clipboard-roster-grid.pdf) with that target written into the headers. Keep the feedback line ready as a stem rather than a question, since it is faster to finish than to answer: "My logo represents _____ because I want to _____ after high school. The Irving ISD pathway that prepares me is _____." A student who cannot finish that sentence picks the goal before touching the design tool. If a whole table is stuck on the third clause, pivot and reproject the pathway list from Activity 1.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Students often pick a famous college (Harvard, Stanford) without knowing why. Push them to think locally and realistically. UT Dallas, UT Arlington, UNT, Texas A&M, and Dallas College are all in driving distance and have strong programs. The best postsecondary plan is one the student can actually afford and access.

!!! warning "Before Monday: Glowforge, Material, and Venting"
    Confirm three things weeks ahead, not the morning of. **One, the machine.** Does the lab have a working Glowforge, and has anyone run a cut on it this school year? **Two, the venting.** A Glowforge needs exterior venting or a working filter unit, and both are facilities questions. Ask your campus facilities contact where the exhaust goes and whether the filter cartridge is current, because a machine that cannot vent cannot run. **Three, the material.** Order cutting stock sized to the machine bed, roughly 12 by 20 inches, and budget about 12 to 15 pieces per section, not per class, since it is a consumable and every section cuts. Corrugated cardboard is the cheapest and by far the fastest to cut; 1/8" plywood and acrylic look better and cost you minutes per piece. Run one test cut yourself on the material you actually bought before students design against it, and time it, because that number is what Activity 3's queue is planned against.

## Activity 3: First Batch of Glowforge Cuts (10 min)

**How the queue actually runs.** The machine cuts one file at a time, and one logo takes 5 to 15 minutes on plywood or 2 to 5 minutes on cardboard. That is machine time, not class time, so thirty individual designs do not fit in a 10 minute window and do not fit in two of them either. The queue is one file per team, and the teacher runs it across the week rather than across this window. Ten team files on cardboard is roughly 40 minutes of machine time, which is one planning period, not a 10 minute block. In these 10 minutes you send the first one or two files with the class watching, so students see the workflow and hear the machine work. Every other team saves its file into a folder for the period, named by team, and those get cut between sections, during conference, or before school. Day 5's window is for picking pieces up, not for starting fresh cuts.

**Time, Voice, Body:** Post the three blocks before the first file is sent. Voice 1 at the design stations, Voice 0 at the machine while a cut is running so the head can be heard, only the team whose file is running stands at the Glowforge, and finished pieces go straight into a labeled tray rather than back to a desk.

Walk the whole class through the workflow once while the first file runs:

1. Open the Glowforge software with the SVG file.
2. Place the cutting material on the Glowforge bed.
3. Position the design on the material using the camera view.
4. Set the cut/engrave settings (preset for cardboard, 1/8" plywood, or acrylic).
5. Press the print button on the Glowforge to start.
6. Wait, watch through the lid, then remove when done.

Teams whose design is not ready keep working while the first file cuts. Anything not saved to the period folder by the bell goes on the individual list for later in the week or takes the card-stock route below.

!!! tip "No Glowforge? Cut the Logo by Hand"
    The deliverable is the postsecondary goal and the artifact that stands for it, not the laser. If the machine is down, unvented, out of material, or the lab never had one, students print the design on card stock and cut it out with scissors, or draw and cut it by hand. That piece hangs in the Gallery Walk, goes in the capstone portfolio, and scores exactly the same on the [1st Six Weeks Capstone Portfolio Rubric](../../resources/worksheets/wk5-capstone-portfolio-rubric.pdf), which scores the goal and the connection rather than the fabrication. Run the same TVB blocks: the cutting table replaces the machine, and finished pieces still go into the labeled tray.

Step 2: After high school, which path fits FIRST?

Circle ONE: **4-year college** / **community college** / **trade school / cert program** / **military** / **direct to work**

Step 3: Branch on cost.

   IF my family CAN afford the first-choice path above, my next step after graduation is: ___________________________________________________________________

   IF my family CANNOT afford it, my BACKUP first step after graduation is: ___________________________________________________________________

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Put a shape menu on the board rather than handing out finished marks: shield, lock, gear, wrench, stethoscope, wing, hard hat, book, circuit line. A student who is stuck picks two shapes and combines them, which is what a real logo is. Students who need the goal settled before they design finish the Active Monitoring stem out loud with you first, then draw. Do not supply copies of official college or military marks, since those are trademarked and the point of the exercise is the student's own destination.
- **Extension:** Advanced students design a logo with TWO elements, their own mark for the school or program AND a career icon (a UT Dallas mark of their own design plus a microscope for a biology major).
- **ELL:** Allow ELL students to label their logo with text in Spanish (Universidad de Texas, Cybersecurity = Ciberseguridad). Pair ELL students with bilingual peers when navigating the Canva or Adobe Express interface.
