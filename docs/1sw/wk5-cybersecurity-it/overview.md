# Week 5: Cybersecurity and Community Safety

**1st Six Weeks** | Information Technology Cluster | 5 class periods (50 minutes each)

## Week Purpose

Students begin with the phishing clues and seven-message investigation in *Find Your Future*, examine one emerging cybersecurity occupation, plan a community safety lesson in the workbook, and design a Canva flyer for a fictional event. They compare IT roles with their interests and describe two academic transition requirements. The flyer is the week's single major-grade product.

## Week Learning Target

I can use evidence to judge a suspicious message, explain one cybersecurity career, plan accurate safety teaching for an audience, and describe next steps toward a flexible school and career goal.

## Week Demonstration of Learning

Students submit one **Canva Cybersecurity Bootcamp Flyer** through the [Major 2 Canvas assignment](https://learn.irvingisd.net/courses/98060/assignments/3094826). The [16-point flyer rubric](../../resources/worksheets/wk5-capstone-portfolio-rubric.pdf) scores the final flyer for audience and event information, accurate safety actions, readable design, and integrity/privacy. FYF p. 35 is the default planning surface. The existing [bootcamp packet](../../resources/worksheets/wk5-bootcamp-planning-template.pdf) supplies a flyer checklist for everyone and optional alternative planning prompts for students who need them. There is no four-part portfolio, goal-symbol submission, duplicate plan upload, or Evidence Log requirement this week.

## TEKS Alignment

- **d(1)(D):** On Day 1, students use a dated, teacher-modeled BLS snapshot to evaluate Information Security Analyst growth and one limit of the source.
- **d(1)(C):** On Day 4, students compare three IT roles with their interests and explain a possible fit in FYF p. 38.
- **d(4)(F):** On Days 2-3, students identify integrity in phishing responses and work ethic in fact-checking, planning, revising, and protecting participants' information. FYF pp. 24-25 and 34-35 hold the core activities.
- **d(3)(A):** On Day 5, students use the Personal Graduation Plan explanation on FYF p. 36 and the Day 1 Career Route Guide to describe a high-school graduation course requirement and a typical career preparation requirement. This is formative evidence, separate from the flyer score.

## Source Grounding

- **FYF pp. 24-25:** `Safe or Spoofed?` clues, seven email decisions, safe practice, and discussion.
- **FYF pp. 34-35:** `Powerskill: Work Ethic` scenario, six planning questions, flyer direction, and discussion. The statistics on p. 34 are part of the scenario, not current verified statistics for the flyer.
- **FYF pp. 36-37:** Irving ISD Information Technology programs, Personal Graduation Plan context, certifications, and CTSOs. Use the district-customized workbook as the classroom source for these printed details.
- **FYF p. 38:** Information Technology App Exploration and the cluster-thought response space. H&L use is optional; no grade depends on favorites or live Hat titles.
- **BLS Information Security Analysts:** The Day 1 guide supplies the May 2025 U.S. median and 2025-35 projection with their limits. Day 4 does not reassign those figures.
- **CISA Secure Our World:** A suspicious message is paused, checked through a known channel, reported, and deleted. The flyer teaches specific safe actions without promising complete protection.

## Instructional Moves and Access

- Teach one phishing clue at a time on Day 2, then reveal one email at a time. Students decide from visible evidence before hearing the key.
- On Day 3, model one accurate safety action and one privacy-safe sign-up method. Ask what a sponsor would notice the team doing behind the scenes, and use student answers to identify work ethic and integrity.
- For the Day 3 work-ethic discussion, use the oral stem “They would notice us ___ because ___.” Listen for checking facts, protecting privacy, revising, or following through. Do not collect another written plan.
- On Day 5, show Canva **Share → Download** and a Canvas file upload. Check the final submission status. A downloaded file alone has not been submitted.
- Use a focused word bank, read-aloud, oral rehearsal, and optional sentence stem where language could hide the student's reasoning. Personal career interests and contact information remain private.
- Offer enlarged print, speech-to-text, and a private response when needed. Score safety reasoning and design evidence; do not score accent or require a personal cybersecurity story.
- If Canva is inaccessible for one student, the teacher selects an accessible design format and submission process. A paper or alternative digital flyer replaces the Canva file for that student; it is not an extra product.
