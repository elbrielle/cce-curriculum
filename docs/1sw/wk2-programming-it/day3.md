# Day 3: Resilience and Reading Career Data
<!-- CCE DAILY CONTRACT START -->
## Daily Learning Contract

- **Topic:** Resilience and labor market information
- **Objective:** Students will plan how a cybersecurity team responds to a failed test, then compare three programming careers using one dated national data set.
- **TEKS:** d(1)(C), d(5)(A), d(5)(E)
- **Demonstration of Learning:** FYF p. 27 Flip the Failure chart and page 4 of the existing IT Salary Comparison packet, with an accurate three-career salary comparison, a growth comparison, and one data limit.

## Lesson Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 50 minutes |

| **Materials** | FYF pp. 26-27, students' Day 2 packets, projector, optional [BLS reading guide](../../resources/worksheets/wk2-bls-data-guide.pdf) |

## Before Students Arrive

1. Open FYF pp. 26-27 and return each student's Day 2 packet. Students keep their app career records on pp. 1-3 as written.
2. Project packet p. 4 with the three BLS rows. Check the printed source date and labels before class.
3. Have one completed model ready: Software Developers, $135,980 May 2025 U.S. median annual pay, 10% projected growth for 2025-2035. Say that a national median is neither a Dallas starting wage nor a job offer.
4. Keep the [BLS reading guide](../../resources/worksheets/wk2-bls-data-guide.pdf) for students who need the labels beside them. No student needs to find three separate BLS pages today.

## Do Now: What failed? (5 min)

Ask, "A cybersecurity team tested a system and the warning arrived too late. What should the team do next?" Take two answers. Look for a specific repair, such as making the alert faster and testing it again, rather than blaming one teammate.

## Activity 1: Flip the Failure in FYF (13 min)

Open FYF pp. 26-27. Read the four failures aloud. Point out that the frozen laptop, phone notifications, and noisy printer are distractions in the story; the four failures in the test are the work students must repair.

Students complete the four rows of **Flip the Failure** on FYF p. 27. For each, they write what went wrong, one specific change the team could test, and one way to stay focused while repairing it. Model the slow alert row: "Shorten the delay and run another test; discuss the result without blaming the person who noticed it." Give partners one minute to compare a row. Ask, "Which fix can the team actually test?"

## Activity 2: Teach the BLS labels with one row (10 min)

Tell students that BLS is the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and that p. 4 gives them a dated national snapshot. Point to the **Software Developers** row. Read the title, **May 2025 U.S. median annual pay**, and **projected growth for 2025-2035** separately. Ask:

- "Does $135,980 tell us what a new worker in Irving will earn?" Expected: no; it is a U.S. median for workers at different experience levels.
- "Does 10% growth promise that I will get this job?" Expected: no; it is a projection for the occupation, not one applicant.
- "Why can we compare the three pay numbers on this page?" Expected: they use the same source, place, year, and annual-median measure.

Students underline those labels on p. 4. Then point to one app pay figure on pp. 1-3 and ask what would have to match before comparing it directly with the national median. Leave both figures in their original places; no copying between sections.

## Activity 3: Compare and decide on packet p. 4 (17 min)

Students answer the four questions on p. 4 using the printed three-career table. They identify the highest national median, the fastest projected growth, one app figure that uses a different place or measure, and a career they would investigate next. They may choose a career outside the three printed examples for their personal interest decision on Day 5.

At minute 34, check two sample papers. If students name a salary without "U.S. median annual" or call growth a guarantee, stop for a two-minute repair using the modeled row. At minute 42, ask partners to compare their fourth answers. Each partner must point to two facts and name one question the table cannot answer.

## Close: one accurate claim (5 min)

Ask students to read their p. 4 choice and its supporting facts. Collect the same packet for Day 5. The p. 4 response is today's exit evidence; do not assign a second comparison sheet. FYF p. 27 remains in the workbook.

## Teacher Key and Misconceptions

- Software Developers have the highest May 2025 U.S. median of the three at **$135,980** and the highest projected 2025-2035 growth at **10%**. Software Quality Assurance Analysts and Testers are **$104,300** and **6%**; Web Developers are **$92,650** and **4%**.
- A median is the middle wage, not the starting wage. The growth percentages estimate a change in jobs over ten years, not an individual's chance of being hired.
- A student's app career may use a different title or pay measure. Do not force a direct comparison with the BLS rows. The student can identify that difference as the data limit.
- A student can reject all three careers and still meet the learning target by making an accurate comparison and explaining a current preference.

## Supports and Recovery

- Read the FYF scenario aloud, then allow partners to rehearse one repair before writing. Use the optional prefilled [Flip the Failure scaffold](../../resources/worksheets/wk2-flip-the-failure-scaffold.pdf) instead of the workbook page only when needed; do not assign both.
- Put **median = middle wage**, **projected = estimated future change**, and **national = U.S., not Irving** beside the p. 4 table. The [BLS reading guide](../../resources/worksheets/wk2-bls-data-guide.pdf) stays optional.
- If a student needs a digital writing surface separate from the packet, assign the [digital three-career comparison](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1jua53MPweEUjs4uBfVEoS71fyhtK3swtvcJNxj90k-8/copy) **instead of** packet p. 4. It has the same dated data. Do not collect both.
- If a student missed Day 2, provide the same p. 4 table and a supervised route to complete the app records later. Do not require invented app numbers. No live BLS site is needed for today's comparison.
