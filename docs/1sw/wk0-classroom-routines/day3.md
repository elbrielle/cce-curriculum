## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Would you rather have a job that pays a lot but you work alone all day, or a job that pays less but you work with a fun team? Write one sentence explaining your choice.**

Collect 2-3 verbal responses. Bridge to the lesson: "What you just answered is a clue about your Work Values. Today we figure out what really matters to you in a job, not just what type of person you are."

---

## Activity 1: Exploring Your Work Values (20 min)

**Source:** (Climber Notes: "Exploring Your Work Values", slides 2-4). The *Find Your Future* workbook has no work values page, so the deck carries this content and results go in the engineering notebook.

Project slide 2 and read the hook together: everyone needs different things from a job to feel good about doing it. Work values are not about being good at something or getting along with coworkers, because those matter to almost everyone. Work values are the things that genuinely differ from person to person, and knowing yours helps you spot careers that fit your life and not just your skills.

Before students open the app, give them the vocabulary. The six starter words below are a CCE scaffold, not a workbook or deck list, and the app may return different words:

- **Achievement**: You enjoy seeing the results of your hard work. You set goals and reach them.
- **Independence**: You like to make decisions and work in your own way.
- **Recognition**: You want to be noticed and rewarded for doing a good job.
- **Relationships**: You like jobs where you can work closely with others and help people.
- **Support**: You want to feel supported by your boss and workplace. You prefer clear rules and guidance.
- **Working Conditions**: You care about job security, daily environment, and the type of tasks you do.

Ask students to write a one-sentence prediction in the engineering notebook before they open the app: which of these matters most to me right now, and why?

> [H&L PLATFORM] Students open the Hats & Ladders app, click **Profile**, then go to **Discover Your Work Values** and complete the activity (Climber Notes: "Exploring Your Work Values", slide 3). They then answer the deck's two questions in the engineering notebook: what work values did you get, and was it what you expected or did something surprise you? Students write their top 2 results in the notebook, dated and labeled "Wk0 Core Day B."

If time remains, run the deck's discussion slide (Climber Notes: "Exploring Your Work Values", slide 4): how can knowing your work values help you find a career you are excited about, and do you think your work values will stay the same for life or change as you get older?

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Some students will rank Independence first because it sounds cool ("I want to be my own boss"). Push them: "Independence means you handle EVERYTHING (taxes, your own health insurance, no paychecks if you have a bad month). Are you sure?" This builds critical reflection rather than aspirational picking.

## Activity 2: My Building Blocks (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 9-11: "My Building Blocks")

Open the workbook to page 9. Read the introduction together: experience does not just mean having a job. Everything students have done so far counts, including responsibilities they carry, things they do for fun, and moments they stepped up without anyone asking. Cooking dinner teaches planning and time management. Taking care of a younger sibling teaches responsibility and patience.

**Step 1: Take Inventory (pp. 9-10).** Students list everything that applies to them in four categories: **At School** (classes, clubs, teams, performances, leadership roles, helping classmates, competitions), **At Home** (chores, caring for family, cooking, managing schedules, yard work), **In Your Community** (volunteering, cultural or faith activities, neighborhood involvement), and **Hobbies and Interests** (gaming, drawing, writing, building, coding, music).

Seed the inventory with a fast whole-class brainstorm before students write. This brainstorm is a CCE addition, not a workbook step. Call out one example and ask students to raise a hand if they do it:

- "Who has cooked dinner for your family in the last month?"
- "Who plays a sport, school or rec?"
- "Who babysits a younger sibling or cousin?"
- "Who has a hobby like drawing, gaming, building things, music?"
- "Who helps with chores at home (dishes, laundry, yard work)?"

Tell students: every single one of those is a Building Block. None of them are "too small." Every one teaches a future-career skill.

**Step 2: Name the Skills (p. 10).** In the workbook table, students write at least one skill each Building Block is teaching them. Push them past "responsibility" toward planning, patience, persistence, and problem-solving.

**Step 3: Talk It Out (p. 10).** **Think-Pair-Share:** 30 seconds of silent Think while each student picks two Building Blocks, one they already know has taught them something and one they do regularly but are not sure what it is building. Then Partner A for 30 seconds, Partner B for 30 seconds. The listening partner's only job is to name the skills they hear. Post two stems: "One thing I do a lot is _____. I think it teaches me _____" and "I am not sure what _____ is teaching me. What skill do you hear in it?" Two pairs share out.

> [H&L PLATFORM] Students then open the Hats & Ladders app and create at least 3 Building Blocks in the Building Blocks section of the Climber Profile. Examples: "I babysit my little brother," "I play soccer on Saturdays," "I draw anime characters." The workbook's Building Blocks pages are paper only, so moving them into the app is a CCE step, not a workbook instruction. The app uses these entries to refine cluster recommendations, so at least 3 must be saved before Activity 3.

**Active Monitoring:** walk the room with one target, that every student has at least 3 Building Blocks visible on their Climber Profile. Building Blocks are foundational data for the rest of the year. The pivot: if a cluster of students has nothing entered, run Activity 3 as a projected walkthrough while you finish those entries with them.

!!! note "Checkpoint Guidance"
    If 1-2 students are still typing, you may move to Activity 3 while they finish. Have them submit Building Blocks by end of class. Do not hold the whole class waiting, but do follow up individually to ensure all students have at least 3 entries before Core Day C (Day 4) runs. Step 4 Reflect (FYF p. 11) does not fit inside these 15 minutes. Give its three questions to fast finishers and pull them back into the Day 4 reflection.

**DOK 3:** What conclusions can you draw about how your core personality type, your work values, AND your Building Blocks all connect? Pick ONE Building Block and explain how it connects to a possible career cluster.

## Activity 3: Initial Cluster Recommendations (8 min)

After completing all three inputs (core personality type, work values, and Building Blocks), the H&L app generates initial cluster recommendations. Direct students to:

1. Open their Climber Profile.
2. Find the recommended clusters section.
3. Note which clusters appear and which surprise them.
4. Write the top 3 recommended clusters in the engineering notebook under the Core Day B entry.

> [H&L PLATFORM] The H&L app uses the core personality result, work values, and Building Blocks to generate cluster recommendations. Students should now see a personalized list. These recommendations will refine over the year as students explore and favorite Hats. Frame them as starting suggestions rather than a final answer, and tell students they will use the list tomorrow on Core Day C in the My Career Journey reflection.

## Exit Ticket (2 min)

- **(A) Repair shop.** You fix things with your hands all day. You work alone in a quiet bay. Nobody checks your work until you are done.
- **(B) Team shop.** You fix the same things, but with three coworkers. You talk all day. A lead checks each repair with you.

Pros of picking A: _______________________________________________________

Pros of picking B: _______________________________________________________

My choice (A or B): ________

Work value list (pick ONE, or write your own):

- Achievement
- Independence
- Recognition
- Relationships
- Support
- Working Conditions

Which work value made you pick that job? ________________________

In one sentence, why does THIS work value matter more to you than the others right now?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pre-print a Building Blocks word bank (sports, music, video games, cooking, babysitting, fixing things, drawing, helping at church, etc.). Students circle 3-5 from the bank rather than generating from scratch.
- **Extension:** Students explore one of their recommended clusters in the H&L app and find a Hat (career) that matches both their core personality type AND their top work value. They write the Hat name in the engineering notebook.
- **ELL:** Bilingual word bank for Building Blocks (Cocinar = Cooking, Cuidar hermanos = Babysitting siblings, Deportes = Sports, Dibujar = Drawing). Pair ELL students with bilingual peers to brainstorm aloud.
