## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Name a bridge, highway, or water system in Irving that you use regularly. Someone ENGINEERED that, designed it, calculated how much weight it could hold, figured out where to put it, and made sure it would last 50+ years. What kind of education do you think that takes?**

Collect 2-3 responses. Last week students designed buildings; this week they test what happens when a design has to carry weight, move water, or hold up a road.

---

## Activity 1: Engineering Cluster Tour + Be the Decision Maker (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 103: "Exploring the World of ENGINEERING") + (FYF p. 110: "App Exploration")

Open the workbook to the chapter opener and read it together. Engineering is about designing, building, and improving the world around us, and engineers, technicians, and systems designers work together to solve problems and develop new ideas, whether that is a stronger bridge, an advanced aircraft, or a more powerful machine. Three Hats sit on the page: Aerospace Engineer, Biomedical Engineer, and CAD Technician. The chapter prints no pathway list, so the pathways come from the app.

**Think-Pair-Share:** Read the page's "Be the Decision Maker" prompt: a company has asked students to help design the airplane of the future, and they have to say which features they would include and why their design would improve the trip. Hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds. Two pairs share with the whole class.

> [H&L PLATFORM] Open the Hats & Ladders app, go to Clusters, and click the Engineering Cluster, which is the path the App Exploration page uses (FYF p. 110). Students watch the "Cluster Tour" video, play "Game Time," and write one thing they learned in the box on page 110. They then use the Hat Finder to browse engineering Hats, prioritizing Civil Engineer, Structural Engineer, and Environmental Engineer this week because those are the Architecture and Construction overlap careers.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Engineering appears in both the Architecture & Construction cluster (Construction Management and Inspection) and the Engineering cluster (Civil, Structural, Environmental). Show students that careers overlap across clusters. This is how the real world works.

## Activity 2: Engineering Career Research (15 min)

**Source:** CCE career research worksheet (the research format is taught in Wk0); app steps follow (FYF p. 110: "App Exploration")

Students select one engineering career (Civil Engineer, Structural Engineer, or Environmental Engineer is recommended this week) and complete every field on the research worksheet:

- **Name of Career**
- **What Interests You?:** why this career caught their attention
- **Brief Job Description:** what this professional does daily
- **Education/Training Needed:** degrees, certifications, licensing (PE license)
- **Average Salary:** DFW data from the Hat profile, confirmed against BLS
- **Tools, Equipment, or Skills Needed:** AutoCAD, Revit, math, physics, problem-solving

> [H&L PLATFORM] Students pull each worksheet field from the Hat profile in the app, then confirm the salary and training figures against at least one additional source, such as the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook page for civil engineers. Before they close the app, they finish two steps from the App Exploration page (FYF p. 110): find 1 Hat that matches their personality or interests and 1 Hat that does not feel like a fit, and write why for each.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Civil engineers need a Bachelor's degree plus a Professional Engineer (PE) license, which takes 4 more years of experience plus two exams. This is a long pathway, point it out as a contrast to trade careers students saw in 4SW.

## Activity 3: Powerskill: Systems Thinking — Kitchen Layout (10 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 174-175: "Powerskill: Systems Thinking")

Introduce the idea the rest of the week runs on. Systems thinking means seeing how all the pieces of a project fit together, thinking ahead, and noticing how a change in one area affects other areas. Civil engineers live in that problem: widen a road and the water has somewhere new to go; raise a bridge and the approach ramps have to move with it.

The workbook puts students in a smaller version of the same problem. Students and a partner are the lead carpenters hired to design cabinets for a client's new kitchen. The floor plan already has a sink, a stove, a refrigerator, and counters.

**Step 1: Explore the Kitchen Layout (4 min).** Partners study the floor plan and answer the workbook's three questions: where could cabinets go without blocking plumbing or appliances, how will people move through the room if cabinets go in certain spots, and could placing a cabinet in one area cause a problem somewhere else.

**Step 2: Plan the Cabinets (4 min).** Partners draw their cabinet layout on the workbook floor plan, adjusting placements to keep the room functional and easy to use.

**Step 3: Review with Another Group (2 min).** Pairs swap plans with another pair and answer one question out loud: which choice had the biggest effect on how the room works. Each pair marks one placement they would move after seeing the other plan.

**DOK 2:** Name one choice in your kitchen layout that forced you to change something else. What was the second change, and why was it necessary?

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Pairs that finish Step 2 in two minutes usually placed cabinets on empty wall space and never tested the walking path. Ask them to trace with a finger how someone carries a hot pan from the stove to the sink. That one question sends most pairs back to the plan.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-marked kitchen floor plan with the sink, stove, and refrigerator circled and the walking path drawn as a dotted line, so partners spend their time on placement instead of plan-reading. Give the research worksheet with one row filled in as an example.
- **Extension:** Research one real DFW infrastructure project (the High Five interchange, DART Silver Line, or the Trinity River Flood Control) on the TxDOT projects and studies page. What problem did civil engineers solve, and what else in the city had to change because of it?
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Infrastructure = Infraestructura, Flooding = Inundación, Cabinet = Gabinete, Layout = Distribución. The kitchen floor plan is a visual task, so ELL students can plan the layout before they need the vocabulary. Pair ELL students with bilingual peers for the Step 3 review.
