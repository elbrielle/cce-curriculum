# Week 1: Blueprint Builders — Architecture Careers

**5th Six Weeks** | Architecture & Construction Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore the Architecture & Construction career cluster through the Hats & Ladders app and the *Find Your Future* workbook, design an underwater construction safety plan in "Safety Supervisor," compare education and salary pathways for design careers, apply 3D modeling skills in TinkerCAD, and design and pitch a novelty landmark as an architectural firm in "Unexpected Architecture."

**Learning Target:** I can describe the Architecture and Construction career cluster, compare the education and salary pathways of at least three A&C careers, and design a 3D building model in TinkerCAD that meets the client specs.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when I can name three A&C careers and what each one does, when my research worksheet lists years of education, cost, and DFW salary for at least three careers, and when my TinkerCAD building has 4 walls, a roof, 1 door, and 2 windows and is exported as a PNG.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe the A&C career cluster, compare education requirements for at least three architecture careers, and design a 3D building model in TinkerCAD that meets client specifications."

## Career Connection

Architects design the buildings where people live, work, learn, and play, but the title also carries a long licensing path. Becoming a licensed architect requires a professional degree (5-7 years), an internship, and the Architect Registration Examination. Put that timeline next to the shorter certification routes students saw in the 4th Six Weeks so they can compare two ways into the same construction industry.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** **Architecture** at **MacArthur High School** (School of Architecture, Construction and Civil Engineering, or ACE) leads to the AutoDesk Revit Architecture certification. The workbook's district pages fill in the rest of the cluster (FYF pp. 196-197: "What is Happening at My District?"): the career exploration connection for A&C is **work readiness**, which is introducing yourself, communicating professionally, and preparing for opportunities. Three programs of study sit under the cluster. Architecture builds drafting, technical drawing, problem solving, and project planning. Construction Technology builds construction methods, safety practices, tool and equipment use, carpentry, and blueprint reading. Welding builds metal fabrication, welding technique, and industry safety. The industry certifications named are NCCER Core, Autodesk Revit, and the Structural Steel and Metal Welding Certification, the student organization is SkillsUSA, and the new Irving ISD CTE Center will open with state-of-the-art welding and construction labs. The district page closes with a student project worth naming out loud: a fully functioning tiny home built from scratch, with students responsible for design, structural framing, HVAC, plumbing, and electrical installation.

## Vocabulary

- **Architecture:** The art and science of designing buildings and other physical structures.
- **Blueprint:** A detailed technical drawing of a building, now created digitally using CAD software.
- **CAD (Computer-Aided Design):** Software used to create precision drawings. AutoDesk Revit and AutoCAD are industry standards.
- **Drafter:** A professional who creates detailed technical drawings based on an architect's designs. Requires less education than a licensed architect.
- **Licensed Architect:** An architect who has completed a professional degree, internship hours, and passed the ARE to practice independently.
- **Novelty Architecture:** A playful design style where a building is shaped like an everyday object, such as a library shaped like a stack of books. This is the design brief for the Day 4 firm activity.
- **Scale Model:** A smaller version of a real design, used to visualize how a building will look before construction.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Architecture & Construction printed pages 171-198. The chapter opener (FYF p. 171: "Exploring the World of ARCHITECTURE & CONSTRUCTION") frames the cluster as creating the spaces where people spend their lives, and it names the split students will see all week: some professionals focus on how a space looks and feels, others make sure it is safe and built to last. The opener puts a "Be the Decision Maker" prompt in front of students, an outdoor music venue the city wants blended into the landscape, and shows three Hats on the page: Carpenter, Cement Mason, and Construction Manager. The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet the A&C pathways in the Hats & Ladders app through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 198: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's own programs of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 196-197).

Two chapter activities carry this week:

- **Safety Supervisor** (Day 1): Career Climb activity. Students are the construction safety supervisor for an underwater research lab being built deep beneath the ocean surface. They study six named hazards of underwater construction, research specialized equipment and procedures, build a poster safety plan with the top 5 rules, the essential equipment, and a safety map showing emergency exits, oxygen stations, and safe zones, then share the poster in a small group (FYF pp. 172-173: "Safety Supervisor").
- **Unexpected Architecture** (Day 4): Career Climb activity. Firms of 3-4 students take a call from the city council asking for a landmark that will put the city on the map. They name their firm, review the city's goals from the teacher's Climber Notes, brainstorm on sticky notes past regular bricks and wood, draw a labeled blueprint with a front and a side view, then pitch and give one another feedback on creativity, connection to city goals, visual design, materials, and delivery (FYF pp. 182-184: "Unexpected Architecture").

A third activity, **Powerskill: Tolerance for Ambiguity** (FYF pp. 180-181), puts partners on a 3D-printed home with a half-finished client brief. It runs as the Day 3 extension. The chapter's remaining activities are spoken for by the other three A&C weeks in this six weeks, so this week leaves them alone: Systems Thinking (pp. 174-175) anchors civil engineering, Spot the Problem (pp. 176-179) and Creativity (pp. 191-193) anchor construction trades, and Written Communication (pp. 185-190) and Plumbing Under Pressure (pp. 194-195) anchor HVAC, electrical, and plumbing.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 1, the "Be the Decision Maker" prompt on the chapter opener (FYF p. 171), where an outdoor music venue must either build into the hillside or work around it. Distinguished: pose the prompt to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "I would build _____ because _____" and "A venue that looks good from outside can still feel _____ inside, so I would trade _____ for _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. While pairs talk, listen for which pairs name a trade-off rather than a preference, and pick your two share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 1, the two pauses during the A&C cluster tour video. Distinguished: name both stop points before the video starts, give 45 seconds per jot with the prompt on screen, and offer two stems: "One career I did not expect in this cluster is _____" and "One thing I still want to know about _____ is _____." Read jots over shoulders during both pauses and track which careers students name. If most jots say Architect only, steer the Hat browse that follows toward Drafter, Urban Planner, and Landscape Architect so Day 2 research is not four rows of the same career.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 4, Activity 1, the three timed TinkerCAD checkpoints. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times, one lap per checkpoint, each lap with a single target (minute 7 walls and roof grouped, minute 14 door and window holes cut, minute 20 one detail element added), marking the clipboard rubric as you go. Have the feedback ready for the two known misconceptions before class starts: shapes selected but not grouped, and a hole shape that never overlapped the wall. If more than a handful of students miss the minute 14 target, pivot and reproject the hole sequence to the whole room instead of repeating it desk by desk.
- **Chunking:** Day 3, Activity 2, the five core TinkerCAD skills. Distinguished: teach one skill at a time on the projector, say what each skill is for before modeling it (resize makes a wall, hole makes a door or a window), and close each chunk with the thumbs check already written into the plan before releasing the next one. Note which skill drew the most thumbs down. That skill becomes a 5 minute re-teach at the start of Day 4 Activity 1, and students who cleared all five chunks start their detail element early instead of waiting.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 5, Activity 1, the building presentations. Distinguished: post the three blocks before the first presenter, Voice 0 while each PNG loads, Voice 3 for the presenter, Voice 1 for the star and wish partner talk, and run a visible 2 minute countdown for every presenter. Define where presenters stand and where the star and wish slips go so transitions do not eat presentation time. Narrate the students who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If the rotation is running long by the third presenter, trim partner talk to one star only and announce the change before the next presenter begins.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- TinkerCAD starter template with a basic floor plan pre-built for students who need extra support
- Simplified salary worksheet with one career row pre-filled as an example
- Pair work option for TinkerCAD, one student designs, the other navigates/coaches
- Pre-printed safety poster layout with three labeled boxes (top 5 rules, essential equipment, safety map) so students who need support spend the Day 1 window on content rather than on layout

### Extensions
- Research the MacArthur HS Revit certification pathway and create a 4-year course plan
- **Powerskill: Tolerance for Ambiguity** (FYF pp. 180-181), the Day 3 extension: partners are the site foreman on a 3D-printed home with a half-finished client brief, sort what they know from what they do not, draw a rough floor plan on chart paper anyway, and log each assumption they had to make and why
- **60-second self-pitch (CCE-original):** students who finish early on Day 5 write and deliver a 60-second pitch about themselves, using their Building Blocks as evidence. There is no workbook page for this pitch, so the delivery standard comes from the checklist at (FYF p. 299: "Prepare & Present"): speak clearly at a steady pace, look up and engage the audience, show confidence even when nervous, and use notes to guide rather than reading word for word. The district page supplies the framing question (FYF p. 196): what makes a strong first impression?
- Compare Architect vs. Civil Engineer career trajectories using BLS data from both weeks

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Architecture = Arquitectura, Blueprint = Plano, Design = Diseño, Scale = Escala
- TinkerCAD is highly visual and accessible for all language levels. Provide bilingual command reference card (Drag = Arrastrar, Resize = Cambiar tamaño, Group = Agrupar, Hole = Agujero)
- Bilingual salary comparison worksheet with Spanish category labels
- Pair ESL students with bilingual peers during presentations
