# Day 1: Cluster Roles and Safety Supervisor

## Lesson Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 50 minutes |
| **Target** | Describe how Architecture & Construction roles work together and use supplied evidence to plan for a fictional hazard scenario. |
| **TEKS** | d(1)(B), d(1)(C) |
| **Evidence** | Individual Safety Supervisor plan + cluster-role exit response |

## Before Class

- Open the locked FYF pp. 171–173 visuals and the fixed hazard/equipment guide in Canvas.
- Prepare Canvas annotation and paper routes. The plan needs five rule lines, six short equipment fields, and at least half a page for the map.
- Review the answer guidance. Accept different priorities when the student cites the supplied scenario evidence.
- Do not use open-web searches or live H&L navigation as required evidence.

> **Boundary:** This is a fictional hazard-identification exercise, not a real commercial-diving plan. Student work is never operating guidance. Commercial diving requires trained personnel, qualified supervision, formal procedures, specialized systems, and emergency planning.

## 50-Minute Flow

### 1. Notice the system — 5 minutes

Students identify one design decision visible in the classroom. Clarify that architects organize spaces and coordinate with engineers and other specialists; one person does not perform every design or safety calculation.

### 2. Meet the cluster — 10 minutes

Use FYF p. 171 and a current MacArthur ACE card. Students sort Architecture, Construction, Engineering, and Welding into a shared project sequence and name one worker contribution. H&L cluster browsing is optional if the live student view has been verified.

### 3. Safety Supervisor evidence plan — 25 minutes

Students use only the supplied fictional scenario and evidence guide.

1. Match each scenario hazard to one relevant control or equipment category.
2. Select and justify the five highest-priority rules.
3. Label essential equipment or trained-personnel needs.
4. Draw a map with exits, safe zones, and neutral emergency-equipment locations.
5. Mark one limitation: what would require a qualified professional rather than a Grade 8 design decision?

### 4. Peer evidence check — 5 minutes

Partners check for five rules, evidence links, readable map labels, and the professional-boundary statement. Students revise their own work; partners do not assign grades.

### 5. Exit — 5 minutes

Name one Architecture & Construction career connected to this scenario, state what that worker would actually do, and explain how that role connects to one other cluster role.

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students distinguish a hazard, a control, and trained professional responsibility.
- The map has readable labels rather than decorative detail.
- Students do not present pure oxygen, improvised diving procedures, or their plan as real safety guidance.
- Career explanations name work, not only a job title.

## Supports and Absence Route

Use the same locked visuals, evidence guide, and plan in Canvas. Provide read-aloud, bilingual labels, speech-to-text, or paper. A student does not need H&L access, drawing skill, or a partner to complete the evidence.
