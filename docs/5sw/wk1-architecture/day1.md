## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Look around this room. Someone designed this building, the shape of the walls, the placement of the windows, the width of the hallways. Who made those decisions, and what did they need to know to make them?**

Discuss 2-3 responses, then connect what students noticed to the work of an architect: drawing a space, calculating whether it can stand, and planning how people will move through it.

## Activity 1: A&C Cluster Tour + Be the Decision Maker (20 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 171: "Exploring the World of ARCHITECTURE & CONSTRUCTION") + (FYF p. 198: "App Exploration")

Open the lesson by connecting this week to the trades careers students explored in the 4th Six Weeks. Architecture and construction are two sides of the same coin, architects design, and trades professionals build. Read the chapter opener together: this cluster is about creating the spaces where people spend their lives, and inside it some professionals focus on how a space looks and feels while others make sure it is safe and built to last. The three Hats printed on the opener page are Carpenter, Cement Mason, and Construction Manager.

1. **Watch the cluster tour video** (embedded in the H&L app cluster page). While watching, students use **Stop and Jot:** pause twice to write down one career that surprised them and one question they have.
2. **Review the Myth Busters** for the A&C cluster (common misconceptions about construction careers).
3. **Run the "Be the Decision Maker" prompt** from the chapter opener (FYF p. 171): *the city wants a brand new outdoor music venue that blends into the natural landscape around it. Do you build into the hillside or work around it? Do you prioritize how it looks from the outside or how it feels on the inside?* **Think-Pair-Share:** 30 seconds silent Think, then Partner A for 30 seconds, then Partner B for 30 seconds. Two pairs share with the whole class. Close with the opener's own question: what does someone in this field need to understand about how people actually use and move through spaces every day?

> [H&L PLATFORM] The App Exploration page walks students through the cluster in order (FYF p. 198): open the Hats & Ladders app, go to Clusters, click the Architecture and Construction Cluster, watch the "Cluster Tour" video, click "Game Time" and play the game, then write on the workbook page what one thing they learned. Next they find 1 Hat that matches their personality or interests and 1 Hat that feels like it is NOT a fit, writing why for each. Point the browse at Architect, Drafter, Interior Designer, Urban Planner, and Landscape Architect, and have students check education requirements, DFW salary, and demand level as they go. They open "Pathway Possibilities," answer the questions there, and rate at least 1 pathway, then rate at least 3 Hats and jot their thoughts on the cluster with emojis, pictures, or words.

The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet Irving ISD's own A&C programs of study on the district pages instead (FYF pp. 196-197): Architecture, Construction Technology, and Welding.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    If students have trouble finding the A&C cluster in the app, direct them: "Click on Career Clusters from your dashboard, then scroll to Architecture and Construction." The workbook's instruction on page 198 is: "Get out your laptop. Open the Hats & Ladders app. Go to Clusters. Click on the Architecture and Construction Cluster."

## Activity 2: Safety Supervisor — Underwater Research Lab (20 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 172-173: "Safety Supervisor"), a Career Climb activity

Introduce this activity by explaining that before architects design a building, construction safety comes first. A construction safety supervisor makes sure everyone on a site follows the rules so accidents and injuries do not happen. They check that workers wear their safety gear, like helmets and gloves, and that tools and machines are used properly.

The workbook scenario: students are the construction safety supervisor keeping workers safe while an underwater research lab is built deep beneath the ocean surface. Their job is to design a safety plan that keeps everyone following the rules that far below the surface.

**Step 1: Learn about Underwater Construction (2 min).** All construction can be dangerous, but underwater construction has six specific things to watch out for, printed on page 172:

- Lack of oxygen
- Equipment failure
- Water pressure and temperature
- Difficult communication environment
- Poor visibility
- Exhaustion

**Step 2: Research Online (5 min).** Students research specialized underwater equipment and procedures and capture what they find in the My Notes lines on page 173. This is what makes the plan specific instead of generic.

**Step 3: Create a Safety Plan (10 min).** Using chart paper or poster board, students design the safety plan for the underwater research lab. It must include:

- Top 5 safety rules that every worker must follow
- Essential safety equipment
- A safety map showing workers where the emergency exits, oxygen stations, and safe zones are located in case of an emergency

**Step 4: Get Feedback (3 min).** Students share the poster in a small group and compare similarities and differences. The comparison is the point: two supervisors reading the same six hazards rarely pick the same top 5 rules.

**Class Discussion (page 173):** How can planning ahead reduce problems and emergencies on a job site? Why do construction companies invest time and money into safety training? What kinds of people do you think would enjoy a career focused on safety and protection?

!!! note "Printed step numbers"
    Pages 172-173 print two steps labeled "Step 2" (Research Online, then Create a Safety Plan) and label Get Feedback as "Step 3." The sequence above is the workbook's actual order. Tell students to work down the page rather than by the printed numbers.

**DOK 2 (extension / homework):** How would you compare the safety challenges of building underwater versus building a 30-story skyscraper? What skills does a safety supervisor need for each? *(Complete after the exit ticket if time; otherwise submit as a 1-page reflection tomorrow. The 10-minute poster window focuses on the underwater scenario only.)*

## Differentiation

- **Support:** The six hazards are already printed on page 172, so the barrier is layout, not content. Hand out poster board pre-divided into three labeled boxes (top 5 rules, essential equipment, safety map) so students spend the window on solutions rather than on deciding where things go.
- **Extension:** Compare underwater construction safety to space station construction safety, what additional hazards exist?
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Safety = Seguridad, Hazard = Peligro, Equipment = Equipo. The safety map activity is visual and accessible for all language levels.
