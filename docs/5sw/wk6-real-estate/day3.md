## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: What does it mean to be your own boss? Would you want that? Write one reason yes, and one reason no.**

**Stop and Jot:** 90 seconds of writing before any talk, one reason yes and one reason no on the page. Post two stems: "Being my own boss would be good because _____" and "The part that would worry me is _____."

Collect 2-3 responses. Most students see the appeal (freedom, no boss, unlimited earnings) but also the anxiety (no paycheck if you have a bad month, no company health insurance, everything depends on you). Bridge: real estate is the most common way people become their own boss without starting a new company from scratch, and today they spend an investor's money to find out how those decisions actually get made.

## Activity 1: Flip This House — Renovation Budget (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 238-240: "Flip This House"), a Career Climb activity

Open the workbook to page 238 and read the introduction together. Real estate investors buy homes, improve them, and sell them for more, but they do not upgrade everything. Every choice comes down to return on investment, which is the question of whether an upgrade raises the home's value by more than it costs. Pairs are the investment team that just bought a fixer-upper in a growing town.

**Step 1: Understand the Need (2 min).** Read the four facts the workbook gives about buyers in this town: they want modern kitchens and updated bathrooms, outdoor living space is a big selling point, clean open layouts sell faster than cramped ones, and expensive features do not always raise the final sale price.

**Step 2: Choose Your Upgrades (6 min).** Pairs circle their upgrades in the table of eight options on page 239 and must stay inside a **$25,000** renovation budget. Every row carries a cost and an estimated value increase, and two of the eight lose money on paper: the luxury closet upgrade costs $5,000 and adds $3,000, and the smart home tech package costs $9,000 and adds $6,000.

**Step 3: Build Your Renovation Strategy (4 min).** Pairs lock the final plan into the workbook's four-column table (upgrade, cost, value add, reason) and explain why their upgrades fit this town's buyers, how they balanced cost against value, and what trade-offs they had to make.

**Active Monitoring:** Walk the room twice. Lap 1 target: the circled upgrades total $25,000 or less. Lap 2 target: every row's reason names a buyer preference from Step 1, not a personal preference. Two errors are predictable, a team that spends most of the budget on the luxury closet and the designer lighting because those sound impressive, and a team that adds up the value increases without subtracting the costs. If more than a handful of pairs miss the lap 1 target, rework one line of the table on the projector for the whole room.

**Bridge (3 min).** Investors are one kind of real estate entrepreneur. Agents are the other, and most of them are entrepreneurs too even though they work under a broker:

- **Independent contractor status:** agents are not employees of their brokerage. They receive 1099 income, not W-2, and pay their own taxes, health insurance, and retirement savings.
- **Personal brand:** their face is on business cards, yard signs, and listings, and many specialize in luxury homes, first-time buyers, or investment properties.
- **Marketing spend:** social media, listing photography, open houses, and mailers all come out of the agent's own pocket, the same way any small business owner pays for advertising.
- **Referrals:** repeat business comes from past clients recommending them, which takes years to build.
- **Owning a brokerage:** successful agents can become brokers and open their own firm, managing other agents.

**DOK 3:** Two upgrades in the table cost more than the value they add. Name a town where an investor might still choose one of them, and use the Step 1 buyer facts to explain what would have to be different about the buyers there.

## Activity 2: DFW Real Estate Market Analysis (18 min)

**Source:** BLS Real Estate Brokers page + Texas Real Estate Research Center ([recenter.tamu.edu](https://www.recenter.tamu.edu)) + optional DFW news articles

The simplest path: project the BLS Real Estate Brokers and Sales Agents page ([bls.gov/ooh/sales/real-estate-brokers-and-sales-agents.htm](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/real-estate-brokers-and-sales-agents.htm)) and the Texas Real Estate Research Center DFW data dashboard for the whole class to read together, no pre-curation needed. If you have time to pre-curate, 2-3 short news articles from Dallas Morning News, D Magazine, or BisNow add local texture, but they are optional enrichment, not required.

Students read and identify:

- **Population growth drivers:** Corporate relocations (Toyota, Charles Schwab, Goldman Sachs moving DFW HQs), job growth, lower cost of living vs. California
- **Home price trends:** Median DFW home price has grown significantly over the past 10 years
- **Inventory challenges:** Not enough homes for the people moving in, which drives prices and commissions up
- **Who is buying?:** Out-of-state transplants, young professionals, families priced out of Austin

Students fill in a DFW Market Analysis notes section with:

- **Three reasons DFW is a strong real estate market**
- **One factor that could slow the market down** (interest rates, oversupply, economic downturn)
- **One career opportunity this market creates**

**DOK 3:** What conclusions can you draw about why the DFW area is one of the best places in the country to start a real estate career right now?

## Activity 3: Entrepreneurship Reflection (10 min)

Students write a 1-page reflection on the Entrepreneurship Reflection template. Prompts:

1. **Define entrepreneurship in your own words.**
2. **Give two examples of how real estate agents function as entrepreneurs.**
3. **Compare a real estate agent to the ventures you built in 3rd Six Weeks during "Million Dollar Idea." What is similar? What is different?**
4. **Personal fit:** Do you have characteristics that would fit entrepreneurship (independence, self-motivation, comfort with risk, people skills)? Explain.

**DOK 4:** Based on everything you have learned about entrepreneurship this year, from your "Million Dollar Idea" venture in 3rd Six Weeks to the house flip this week, create a set of characteristics that define a successful entrepreneur. Which characteristics do you personally have?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a simpler reflection template with 3 short-answer boxes instead of the 1-page format. Students fill in the boxes rather than writing full paragraphs.
- **Extension:** Finish "Flip This House" Steps 4 and 5 (FYF p. 240): write the listing description that sells the renovated home to buyers in this town, then share the flip with a small group and decide whether any team chose appearance over return. Or research a real estate entrepreneur (Barbara Corcoran, Ryan Serhant): what did they build, and what can a new agent learn from it?
- **ELL:** Bilingual Entrepreneurship Reflection template. Students may write in Spanish or bilingual. Pre-teach: Entrepreneur = Emprendedor, Business = Negocio, Independent Contractor = Contratista independiente.
