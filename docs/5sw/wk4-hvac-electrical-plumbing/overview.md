# Week 4: Power, Water, and Heat — Essential Skilled Trades

**5th Six Weeks** | Architecture & Construction Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore HVAC, electrical, plumbing, and welding careers through Hats & Ladders and the *Find Your Future* workbook; build a Skilled Trades Comparison Matrix of certification timelines and earning potential; write HVAC field notes for four AC service tickets in the "Powerskill: Written Communication" lesson; analyze DFW labor market data and classify each trade as high-skill, high-wage, or high-demand; research post-high-school apprenticeship, community college, and trade school routes; and work as a plumbing crew on a downtown water line break in "Plumbing Under Pressure." HVAC, electrical, and plumbing are covered as career-exploration topics using BLS and local apprenticeship data, because Irving ISD's only current pathway in this space is Welding at MacArthur (School of Architecture, Construction and Civil Engineering).

**Learning Target:** I can name the training and license each of three skilled trades needs, compare their DFW pay and job demand, and say why these trades count as high-demand jobs.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my Skilled Trades Matrix has apprenticeship length, license, certification, DFW starting pay, DFW experienced pay, and BLS job outlook filled in for all four trades, when my Labor Market Analysis worksheet marks each trade high-skill, high-wage, or high-demand with a number as proof, when each of my four HVAC service tickets has an observation line, a diagnosed problem, and an action, and when my plumbing crew's sketch shows the broken pipe, the water, the safe work zone, and where each of us is working.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe the certification and training requirements for at least three skilled trades, compare their DFW salaries and job demand, and explain why these trades are classified as high-demand occupations."

## Career Connection

Every new home, school, hospital, or commercial building needs electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and welding work. DFW growth adds projects while experienced tradespeople are retiring faster than new workers enter the field. Students research that gap through local pay, licensing, apprenticeship, and job-posting data.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** **Welding** at **MacArthur High School** (School of Architecture, Construction and Civil Engineering) leads to the Structural Steel & Metal Welding Certification. The workbook's district pages name all three of Irving ISD's Architecture and Construction programs of study (FYF pp. 196-197: "What is Happening at My District?"): Architecture, Construction Technology, and Welding, with Autodesk Revit, NCCER Core, and the Structural Steel and Metal Welding Certification behind them, SkillsUSA as the student organization, and a new Irving ISD CTE Center planned with welding and construction labs. The page's "What Does Learning Look Like?" panel describes students who built a fully functioning tiny home from scratch and handled the design, structural framing, HVAC, plumbing, and electrical installation themselves.

HVAC, Electrical, and Plumbing have no Irving ISD pathway of their own. Students interested in those trades pursue them after high school through apprenticeship programs or technical schools, which is why this week covers them as career exploration through BLS, CareerOneStop, and local apprenticeship research. The tiny home is the one place where that work appears inside a current district program, and Day 4 carries a `[VERIFY with CTE coordinator]` note on whether a student can choose it as a focus.

## Vocabulary

- **HVAC:** Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning. HVAC technicians install and repair climate control systems.
- **Journeyman Electrician:** A fully trained electrician who has completed an apprenticeship and passed a licensing exam. Can work independently.
- **Master Plumber:** The highest level of plumbing licensure. Master plumbers can run their own businesses and pull permits.
- **Welder:** A professional who joins metal parts using heat. Welders work in construction, manufacturing, pipelines, and shipbuilding.
- **Skills Gap:** The difference between the skills employers need and the skills available workers have. Construction trades have one of the largest skills gaps in America.
- **Field Notes:** Short written notes a technician leaves behind so the next person on the job knows what was already checked. Written in shorthand to save time without losing detail. This is the artifact students produce on Day 2.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Architecture and Construction printed pages 171-198, and this week takes the back half of the chapter. The cluster's pathway list no longer prints in the book. Students meet the pathways in the Hats & Ladders app through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 198: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's own programs of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 196-197).

Two chapter activities carry this week:

- **Powerskill: Written Communication** (Day 2): Students are an HVAC technician with four service tickets from homeowners whose air conditioning has stopped working. They learn what six AC parts do and what goes wrong with each, study a worked example of a field note, then write Site, Observation, Diagnosed problems, and Action lines for all four tickets in technician shorthand (FYF pp. 185-190). The four ticket photos live in the teacher's Climber Notes deck.
- **Plumbing Under Pressure** (Day 5): Career Climb activity. A water line has broken downtown. Teams of 3 take the Master Plumber, Pipefitter, and Journeyman Plumber roles, learn an emergency protocol, set priorities for before, during, and after the repair, sketch and label the work zone on chart paper, react to a crowd and a pressure spike, then present the plan to another team (FYF pp. 194-195).

Welding sits in the Manufacturing chapter rather than this one, because welders work across both industries. Students already worked a welder's job there in 1SW: "Super Sports Manufacturing" (FYF pp. 204-206) had them design a bike rack, choose a metal, pick weld types, and stress-test a prototype. Day 2 opens by calling that back, then looks at Welder as a career instead of a task.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 5, Activity 2, the workbook's crew-role discussion question after each pair of teams presents (FYF p. 195): why is it important for everyone on a crew to have a clear role? Distinguished: pose it to the whole room, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "Our team worked well when _____" and "If two people had taken the same role, _____ would have happened." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who cites a specific moment from the crowd-and-pressure twist rather than a general answer, and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard instead of from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 3, Activity 2, the three classification cells on the Labor Market Analysis worksheet, where every high-skill, high-wage, or high-demand call has to carry a number. Distinguished: state the time box before students start, use the Electrician row you modeled in Activity 1 as the posted exemplar, and offer two stems: *"According to BLS data, the job outlook for _____ is projected to grow _____% over the next 10 years. In DFW, the average salary is $_____. This occupation is classified as high-demand because _____"* and the shorter *"This trade is high-demand because BLS says jobs will grow _____%."* Read cells over shoulders while students write. If most students are writing Yes with no figure attached, stop the room and re-model one cell rather than correcting them one at a time.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 4, Activity 2, the clipboard spot-check of the Skilled Trades Comparison Matrix. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway twice with one target per lap, lap 1 for cited numbers in place of blank cells and lap 2 for classifications that carry reasoning rather than a bare Y or N, marking the clipboard as you go. Have feedback ready before class for the two predictable errors: a national salary figure copied in where a DFW figure belongs, and an apprenticeship row filled in with "4 years" for every trade. If lap 1 shows more than a handful of matrices still missing the classification rows, pivot and put the Day 3 Labor Market Analysis worksheet back on the projector so the whole room can copy across from it at once.
- **Chunking:** Day 2, Activity 2, Step 1, the six AC parts. Distinguished: teach one part at a time from the workbook table, say what the part does before naming what goes wrong with it, and close each part with the thumbs check already written into the plan before releasing the next one. Note which part draws the most thumbs down. That part gets named again out loud while you model Service Ticket #1, and students who cleared all six start Ticket #2 while the model finishes.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 5, Activity 1, Steps 3 and 4, the priority list and the chart paper sketch. Distinguished: post the three blocks before chart paper goes out, Voice 2 for team planning and Voice 0 for the last minute of the sketch so teams check labels instead of talking, a visible countdown running through both steps, and a stated rule that markers return to the tray at the center of the table before the twist is announced. Narrate the teams meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones that are not. If a team is still arguing about roles when the Step 3 countdown starts, assign the remaining two roles for them so they lose planning time instead of the whole activity.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- Pre-populated BLS data sheets for each trade so students focus on analysis rather than data retrieval
- Simplified matrix with fewer comparison categories for students who need support
- Allow students to focus on just 2 of the 4 trades if the full matrix is overwhelming

### Extensions
- Calculate the lifetime earnings difference between entering a trade at 18 versus completing a 4-year degree and starting work at 22
- Research the growing demand for "smart HVAC" systems and how IoT technology is changing the trade
- Create a recruitment poster for one of the Irving ISD 2027 pathways targeting future 9th graders

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Electrician = Electricista, Plumber = Plomero, Welder = Soldador, HVAC = Climatización, Skills Gap = Brecha de habilidades
- Bilingual Skilled Trades Matrix with Spanish column headers
- Labor market data uses numbers and charts that are accessible across language levels
- Pair ELL students with bilingual peers for the Day 2 field notes and the Day 5 plumbing crew planning
