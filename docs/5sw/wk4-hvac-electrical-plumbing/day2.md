# Day 2: HVAC Evidence-First Field Notes
<!-- CCE DAILY CONTRACT START -->
## Daily Learning Contract

- **Topic:** Transferable Skills
- **Objective:** Students will identify skills that transfer among a variety of careers using evidence from Transferable Skills.
- **TEKS:** d(4)(B)
- **Demonstration of Learning:** four fictional service notes and one transfer sentence.
<!-- CCE DAILY CONTRACT END -->
**Time:** 50 minutes

**TEKS:** d(4)(B)

**Evidence:** four fictional service notes and one transfer sentence

## Before class

Embed Climber Notes *PowerSkill: Written Communication* tickets 1–4 in locked Canvas. Students use FYF pp. 185–190 as the default work surface. The enlarged CCE field-note form is only a no-workbook, absence, or scaffold route; students do not complete both. Never ask students to perform a real check or repair.

## 50-minute flow

1. **Warm-up, 5 min.** What makes a work note useful to the next person?
2. **Evidence language, 8 min.** Relabel the FYF fields before students write: keep **Observation**; treat **Diagnosed problems** as **Supported possibility**; treat **Action** as **Evidence limit + qualified next check**.
3. **Parts reference, 7 min.** Use the workbook reference to locate filter, indoor coil/evaporator, outdoor condenser, and airflow evidence. This is vocabulary, not technician authorization.
4. **Four tickets, 25 min.** Students complete FYF pp. 187–190 or the safe-label fallback. They may say “could be consistent with” but may not claim a complete diagnosis from one image.
5. **Exit, 5 min.** Rewrite one vague note into an evidence-first note and explain how the same written-communication skill transfers to another career.

## Teacher interpretation guide

- **Ticket 1:** visible ice buildup and weak cooling/airflow. This could be consistent with restricted airflow or another system issue. The image does not determine the root cause; a qualified HVAC professional checks airflow, filter, coil, and other system conditions.
- **Ticket 2:** visibly heavy dust/debris on the indoor coil with uneven airflow. A qualified professional evaluates cleaning and the complete airflow path.
- **Ticket 3:** visibly dirty filter plus supplied weak/dusty airflow. The evidence strongly supports a restricted filter, but students still route service to the authorized adult/professional rather than performing classroom repair directions.
- **Ticket 4:** visibly weathered/dirty outdoor unit with insufficient cooling. The photo does not prove the failed component. A qualified professional evaluates the condenser and full system.

Accept other careful interpretations tied to the supplied evidence. Do not reward confident guessing over a bounded claim.

## Access route

Every ticket is embedded with neutral alt text and adjacent complaint/extra-information text. Allow workbook, zoom, read-aloud, typed response, dictation, or the enlarged fallback. No personal home/HVAC story is required.
