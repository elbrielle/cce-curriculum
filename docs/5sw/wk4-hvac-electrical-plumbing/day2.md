## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Welding is used to build bridges, skyscrapers, ships, pipelines, and even spacecraft. Name one thing in this classroom that was probably welded.**

Quick share. Students will spot bolts on desks, metal chair frames, door hinges. Everything metal in a building has welded joints somewhere. This sets up that welding is everywhere, even when it is invisible.

## Activity 1: Welding Career Exploration (10 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 204-206: "Super Sports Manufacturing") + BLS OOH Welders

Students already did a welder's work once this year. In the Manufacturing cluster they were the expert welder at SuperSports Manufacturing, designed a bike rack for a 100-unit school district order, chose between aluminum, stainless steel, and carbon steel, picked spot, fillet, surfacing, and corner welds, labeled the joints on their sketch, and stress-tested a prototype (FYF pp. 204-206). Open with that: today they look at the same job as a career instead of a task.

Direct students to H&L. The Welder Hat sits under the Manufacturing cluster because welders work across construction, manufacturing, oil and gas, and aerospace. Students explore the Welder Hat and pull the same data categories they used for the other three trades:

- Apprenticeship length (often 3-4 years)
- Licensing and certification (AWS, American Welding Society, certifications)
- DFW starting salary and experienced salary
- BLS 10-year outlook
- One perk (variety of industries, travel opportunities)
- One quirk (physically demanding, hot work environments)

**Irving ISD connection:** MacArthur High School's Welding pathway already offers Structural Steel & Metal Welding certification, one of the few Irving ISD CTE pathways that leads directly to an industry-recognized credential right now.

Students add the Welder column to their Skilled Trades Comparison Matrix. The matrix is now complete with all 4 trades.

## Activity 2: HVAC Field Notes — Four Service Tickets (30 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 185-190: "Powerskill: Written Communication") + (Climber Notes: "PowerSkill Written Communication", slides 2-5)

Field notes are the short written notes a technician leaves behind so the next person on the call knows what was already checked. Technicians write them in shorthand to save time without losing detail. Today students are the HVAC technician holding four service tickets from homeowners whose air conditioning has stopped working properly.

**Step 1: Understand Key Parts (8 min).** Teach the six parts one at a time from the workbook table (FYF p. 185).

**Chunking:** name what each part does before naming what goes wrong with it, and close each part with a quick thumbs check before moving to the next. Whichever part draws the most thumbs down is the one to name again while modeling Ticket #1.

| Part (shorthand) | What it does | What can go wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Thermostat (Tstat) | Reads the room temperature and tells the system when to run | Misreads the temperature, so the building gets far too hot or cold |
| Evaporator coil (Evap coil) | Pulls heat out of the air inside the building | Ice builds up and blocks airflow, which can overheat the system or leak water |
| Air filter (Filter) | Screens dust and dirt out of the air entering the system | Clogs with dust, weakens airflow, and makes the system run constantly |
| Blower motor (Blower) | Pushes cooled air through the vents into rooms | Moves too little air, leaving rooms warm and freezing the coils |
| Refrigerant (Refrigerant) | Cooling fluid that carries heat away through the coils | Leaks or runs low, so the unit cannot cool the air |
| Condenser coil (Cond coil) | Outdoor unit that releases the trapped heat outside | Gets covered in dirt or leaves, traps the heat, and overheats the system |

Then project the workbook's example field note (FYF p. 186) so students see the shorthand before they write any of their own. Site: office building, 2nd floor. Observation: weak airflow, evap coil iced, filter dirty. Diagnosed problems: airflow low, possible low refrigerant. Action: replace filter, defrost coil, check refrigerant level. Every field note needs those three moves in order: what you see, what might be causing it, and what you would do about it.

**Step 2: Work the Service Tickets (22 min).** Project the Climber Notes deck one ticket at a time. Each slide carries the photo of that AC system, so hold on one photo while students write rather than clicking through. Students complete the Field Notes form for each ticket in the workbook (FYF pp. 187-190).

Work Ticket #1 as a whole-class model: read the photo together, take three observations from volunteers, and build the diagnosed-problems and action lines on the board. Then release Tickets #2, #3, and #4 for independent work at roughly five minutes each.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    The predictable weak note is an Action line that repeats the Observation line. "Filter dirty" is an observation; "replace filter, then recheck airflow" is an action. When you see a repeat, ask the student what a technician would actually pick up or turn on next. The second predictable error is a blank Diagnosed problems box, because students jump straight from what they see to what they would do. Both are worth a whole-room reminder after Ticket #2 rather than a desk-by-desk correction.

**DOK 3:** Two of your tickets show weak airflow, but the cause is not the same in both. What evidence in each photo told you which part to blame, and what would go wrong if the next technician acted on your notes and fixed the wrong part?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give the six-part table with the "what can go wrong" column already filled in, and set the target at Tickets #1 and #2 completed in full rather than all four. Point students at the example field note on p. 186 as a sentence bank.
- **Extension:** Write Ticket #4 entirely in technician shorthand, then trade forms with a partner who has to rewrite it in full sentences. If the partner cannot rebuild the meaning, the shorthand cut too much. Students who finish that take the workbook's own discussion question (FYF p. 190): which details matter most so another technician understands the problem?
- **ELL:** Bilingual AC parts card. Pre-teach: Thermostat = Termostato, Coil = Serpentín, Filter = Filtro, Blower = Ventilador, Refrigerant = Refrigerante, Leak = Fuga, Airflow = Flujo de aire. The Climber Notes photos carry the evidence visually, so students can observe before they write. Pair ELL students with bilingual peers for the Ticket #1 model.
