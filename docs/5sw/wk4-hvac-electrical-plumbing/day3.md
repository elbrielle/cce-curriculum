# Day 3: Labor Evidence and Classification

**Time:** 50 minutes

**TEKS:** d(5)(A), d(5)(B)

**Evidence:** four-career classification and limitation

## Fixed May 2024 U.S. evidence

| Occupation | Median | Growth 2024–34 | Annual openings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Electricians | $62,350 | 9% | about 81,000 |
| Plumbers, Pipefitters, and Steamfitters | $62,970 | 4% | about 44,000 |
| HVAC Mechanics and Installers | $59,810 | 8% | about 40,100 |
| Welders, Cutters, Solderers, and Brazers | $51,000 | 2% | about 45,600 |

## 50-minute flow

1. **Warm-up — 5 min.** Explain why growth rate and annual openings answer different questions.
2. **Publish the course rule — 8 min.** Review the high-skill, high-wage, and high-demand definitions from the overview. Label them as classroom comparisons.
3. **Model one occupation — 7 min.** Cite the number or preparation fact for every yes/no decision.
4. **Classify four occupations — 25 min.** Students use one page per occupation, then compare a growth trend and openings pattern.
5. **Exit — 5 min.** State one conclusion and one limitation of using national data for a local career choice.

## Teacher key

All four medians exceed $49,500. Electrician, plumber/pipefitter/steamfitter, and HVAC growth exceed 3.1%; welder growth does not. High-skill judgments must cite the published preparation/credential rule rather than the student's opinion. A high number of replacement openings does not erase a slower growth rate, and neither measure proves a DFW shortage or starting salary.

## Access route

All required data is fixed and labeled. Students do not search job boards or estimate missing values. Accept calculator, typed, enlarged, dictated, or paper responses and score the evidence chain rather than English mechanics.
