# Week 3: Built to Last — Construction Evidence

**5th Six Weeks** | Architecture and Construction Cluster | 5 class periods (50 minutes each)

## Lesson Objective

Students compare construction careers and preparation routes, investigate the steps for a Registered Apprenticeship and a Dallas College construction program, classify construction occupations with one published course rule, and turn visible clues from a fictional property into a careful evidence report and short professional briefing.

**Learning Target:** I can use dated evidence to compare construction careers and routes, explain what an assessment image supports and does not support, and communicate one finding clearly.

**Success Criteria:** My work keeps source labels with every number, records actual apply/enroll steps, classifies careers under the published rule, separates observation from inference, and names the qualified role that should evaluate a possible concern.

## TEKS Alignment

- **d(1)(C):** Identify construction career opportunities and the work products or decisions each role owns (Days 1, 4, 5).
- **d(2)(A):** Research and compare documented preparation requirements (Day 1).
- **d(3)(G):** Investigate and report the steps required to apply to a Registered Apprenticeship and enroll in a community or technical college route (Day 2).
- **d(3)(H):** Distinguish a union, professional/trade association, CTSO, and credential body; explain the value of one affiliated organization (Day 2).
- **d(5)(A), d(5)(B):** Analyze labor trends and classify occupations with a consistent, published evidence rule (Day 3).
- **d(4)(C):** Deliver an individual 30–45 second professional evidence briefing (Day 5).

## Evidence Boundary

The home-inspection sequence is a **fictional visual observation and report-writing exercise**. Students do not inspect their homes, touch wiring, climb roofs, handle mold, use tools, operate a thermal camera, diagnose a defect, estimate repair cost, recommend a purchase, or give repair instructions. A strong claim uses this structure:

1. **I observe:** only what is visible.
2. **This could indicate:** one cautious possibility.
3. **Evidence limit:** what the image cannot prove.
4. **Next qualified role:** who should evaluate the concern.

Texas Professional Real Estate Inspectors are licensed professionals. The Grade 8 activity does not imitate or replace a licensed inspection.

## Course Classification Rule

This is a transparent classroom comparison rule, not an official government designation. All scored rows use May 2024 U.S. BLS medians and 2024–34 national projections.

- **High-skill:** the BLS preparation field documents substantial occupation-specific preparation such as apprenticeship, moderate/long-term on-the-job training, a bachelor’s degree, or a regulated license/credential boundary.
- **High-wage:** May 2024 U.S. median is above the all-occupation median of **$49,500**.
- **High-demand for this comparison:** projected 2024–34 growth is above the all-occupation rate of **3.1%**.

Annual openings are analyzed separately. Openings include replacement needs and do not by themselves prove a local labor shortage.
