## Warm-Up (5 min)

What is the difference between “I see a brown ring” and “the roof is leaking”? Which statement is an observation?

## Activity 1: Role and Safety Boundary (8 min)

Read the fictional brief from FYF p. 176. Explain that a Texas Professional Real Estate Inspector is licensed and uses education, experience, examinations, insurance, and professional standards. Grade 8 students are practicing **visual evidence language**, not performing a licensed inspection.

Students do not inspect a real property, touch a panel, handle mold, climb, use tools, price repairs, or recommend whether someone should buy a home.

Model the four-part response:

> I observe ____. This could indicate ____. The image cannot prove ____. A qualified ____ should evaluate it next.

## Activity 2: Watch-For Categories (7 min)

Use the FYF categories—structure, roof, water/moisture, electrical, and insulation—but state that a photograph may show no obvious concern. “No obvious concern in this image” is valid; it is not proof that the entire system is safe.

## Activity 3: Five-Image Evidence Lab (25 min)

Embed the five licensed slides individually in Canvas. Hold each for about four minutes. Students draft Findings 1–5 directly in the same report they will revise on Day 5; they do not complete a second observation packet. Each finding records:

1. visible observation;
2. possible concern;
3. confidence/evidence limit;
4. qualified role for next evaluation.

### Teacher interpretation guide

- **Image 1:** decorative wall/window/radiator-cover area; no obvious defect is visible. Accept a neutral observation. The image does not clear the room or building.
- **Image 2:** visibly worn/damaged roof shingles. This could permit water entry; the image does not establish the extent or repair. Refer to a licensed inspector and qualified roofer.
- **Image 3:** ceiling/wall staining and discoloration. This could indicate current or past moisture; the image does not identify the source or prove mold. Refer to a licensed inspector and the appropriate qualified roofer/plumber after source evaluation.
- **Image 4:** open electrical service panel with visible conductors/cables. The photograph does not establish a code violation. Students do not touch or approach a real panel. Refer any concern to a licensed inspector/electrician.
- **Image 5:** damaged/swollen-looking cabinet base below a sink. This could indicate current or past moisture; the image does not locate a leak. Refer to a licensed inspector and qualified plumber.

## Activity 4: Thermal Image Boundary (5 min)

Use the regular/thermal comparison printed on FYF p. 178. Students describe the visible temperature pattern and state what additional evidence would be needed. A thermal difference can guide further evaluation; it does not by itself prove missing insulation, moisture, or air leakage.

## Exit Check (5 min, included in Activity 4)

Choose one image. State the observation, cautious possibility, evidence limit, and next qualified role.

## Supports and Fallback

Provide zoom, answer-neutral adjacent descriptions, teacher read-aloud, bilingual vocabulary, typing, dictation, and sentence frames. Low-vision students may use the description plus the same inference/limit task. Absent students use the locked image page independently.
