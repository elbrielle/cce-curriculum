# Week 2: Roots and Resources — Plant Science Careers

**3rd Six Weeks** | Agriculture, Food & Natural Resources Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students continue exploring the Agriculture, Food & Natural Resources cluster with a focus on Plant Science and Environmental & Natural Resources careers. They step into the hydroponics technician role in "Grow System Rescue" to diagnose a failing lettuce section, build a Farm to Table infographic for Sunny Fields Farm as an Agricultural Communications Specialist, research one emerging ag-tech career using BLS and USDA data, and connect the Plant Science program of study at Nimitz High School to local Irving career options.

**Learning Target:** I can name at least three plant science or environmental careers, find what is wrong in a hydroponic grow system and say what to fix first, design an infographic that shows how a crop gets from the farm to the table, and explain what the Plant Science program at Nimitz High School prepares students to do.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my career research worksheet lists the training, the pay, and one daily task for two plant science or environmental careers, when my Grow System Rescue page names the problem, the first fix, and my reason, when my Farm to Table infographic has a title, at least four steps, a picture for each step, and one fun fact about the farm, and when my emerging career brief names the job, the pay, and the trend that is making it grow.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe at least three plant science or environmental careers, diagnose a failing hydroponic grow system and justify which problem to fix first, design a Farm to Table infographic that explains how a crop reaches consumers, research one emerging agriculture career, and explain how the Plant Science program of study at Nimitz High School prepares students for agriculture and horticulture careers."

## Career Connection

Plant science workers grow food, manage soil and water, protect crops, and run nurseries, landscapes, conservation projects, and indoor growing systems. Students spend this week diagnosing a growing system and explaining how produce moves from a field to a customer, two jobs that require different kinds of plant knowledge.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** The **Plant Science** program of study at **Nimitz High School**, part of Nimitz's School of Agriculture Science, prepares students for careers in agriculture and horticulture with coursework in plant growth, soil science, greenhouse management, and floral design (FYF p. 100: "What is Happening at My District?"). The Agricultural Science program at Nimitz runs a greenhouse and an orchard on campus, and Future Farmers of America (FFA) is the student organization attached to it (FYF p. 101). The workbook's district pages also introduce the **CTE completer** idea, a student who finishes a full sequence of CTE courses in one career field rather than taking a single class, and the pathway's industry certification is BASF Plant Science.

## Vocabulary

- **Horticulture:** The science of growing plants, fruits, vegetables, flowers, and ornamental plants.
- **Precision Agriculture:** Using technology like GPS, drones, and sensors to optimize farming practices.
- **Conservation:** The careful management and protection of natural resources like water, soil, and forests.
- **Hydroponics:** Growing plants in nutrient-rich water instead of soil, usually indoors under lights. This is the system students repair in "Grow System Rescue."
- **Agricultural Communications Specialist:** A professional who combines ag knowledge with design and marketing skills to help farms tell their story.
- **Emerging Occupation:** A career that has grown rapidly because of new technology, new social needs, or new economic conditions.
- **Infographic:** A visual presentation of information using pictures, labels, charts, and short text to make complex ideas easy to understand.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Agriculture, Food, and Natural Resources printed pages 87-102. The chapter opener (FYF p. 87: "Exploring the World of AGRICULTURE, FOOD, AND NATURAL RESOURCES") frames the cluster as growing food, caring for animals, and managing the land and resources people depend on, and puts a "Be the Decision Maker" question in front of students: design a future farm that has to feed a busy city, underground, on rooftops, or in vertical towers. The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet the Plant Science and Environmental and Natural Resources pathways in the Hats & Ladders app instead, through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 102: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's own Plant Science program of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 100-101).

Two chapter activities carry this week:

- **Grow System Rescue** (Day 1): Career Climb activity. Students are the hydroponics technician on duty at a large indoor farm where a section of lettuce is failing. They learn the four parts of a hydroponic system, weigh four clues from the grow zone against four diagnostic tips, circle the problem and explain their reasoning, decide which problem to fix first, and compare their calls with a partner (FYF pp. 88-90: "Grow System Rescue").
- **Farm to Table** (Days 2-3): Career Climb activity. Students are the Agricultural Communications Specialist hired by Sunny Fields Farm, a family farm started in 1998 that grows strawberries, grapes, bell peppers, and cucumbers. They read the farm's email brief, brainstorm on sticky notes, build the infographic, and write two questions they would ask the farm team (FYF pp. 91-92: "Farm to Table").

The workbook's Step 5 (FYF p. 90), where students design an improved hydroponic system on chart paper and label how water, nutrients, and light reach every plant, runs as the Day 1 extension rather than in the 25 minute activity block.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 2, Activity 2, the partner share after the crop brainstorm. Distinguished: hold a silent 30 second Think on "what does a customer most want to know about this crop," then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "My crop is _____ and my first step is _____" and "A customer would trust Sunny Fields more if the infographic showed _____ because _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen for pairs who can name something beyond "it tastes good," and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands. If most pairs are stuck on taste and color, reread the farm's email facts (no harsh pesticides, farm tours, monitoring drones) before students start sketching.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 1, the two Hat questions on the App Exploration page (one Hat that matches you, one Hat that does not). Distinguished: name both stop points before students open the app, give 45 seconds per jot with the prompt on screen, and offer two stems: "The Hat that fits me is _____ because I like _____" and "The Hat that does not fit me is _____ because it needs _____." Read jots over shoulders during both pauses and track which careers get named. If most jots say Park Ranger only, steer the browse toward Soil Scientist, Horticulturist, and Water Treatment Specialist so the research worksheet rows are not four copies of the same career.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 3, Activity 2, the 30 minutes of Canva building. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times, one target per lap (minute 10 all four step headings typed, minute 20 an icon or image on every step, minute 25 the PNG exported and submitted), and have feedback ready before class for the two known misconceptions: a 12 section template chosen for a 4 step story, and font and color changes made before the text is in. If more than a handful of students miss the minute 20 target, pivot and project the Elements icon search to the whole room instead of fixing it screen by screen.
- **Chunking:** Day 1, Activity 2, the five steps of "Grow System Rescue." Distinguished: release one step at a time, say what each step is for before students start it (Step 1 gives you the four parts a system needs, Step 2 gives you the clues, Step 3 makes you commit), and close each step with the check already written into the plan before releasing the next one. Note which step draws the most blank pages. If Step 2 is where students stall, reteach the four diagnostic tips against one clue on the projector before releasing Step 3, and send students who cleared all five steps to the chart paper design early.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 4, Activity 2, the 25 minutes of BLS and USDA research, and Activity 3, the paired template trade. Distinguished: post the three blocks before students open a browser tab, Voice 0 for the research block and Voice 1 for the trade, a visible countdown for both, and a stated rule that finished templates go face up on the corner of the desk so the trade takes seconds instead of minutes. Narrate the students who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If the room is drifting off the BLS site by minute 10, cut the research block's remaining time into two shorter stretches with a 30 second stretch break between them and say so before the countdown restarts.

## Formative Assessment

- Career research worksheet rows for 2 careers + Grow System Rescue diagnosis (problem circled, reasoning written, first fix justified) + Comparison Matrix exit ticket (2 careers x pathway/training/task + outdoor-work pick). *Day 1, d(1)(B), d(1)(C)*
- Farm to Table brainstorm + sketch + Mini-Case exit ticket (elementary-school kindergarten audience; crop + design change + memorable fact). *Day 2, d(1)(C)*
- Canva infographic + Venn Diagram exit ticket (my infographic vs. classmate's same-crop design). *Day 3, d(1)(C)*
- Emerging Ag Career research + Ranked Justification exit ticket (4 emerging careers ranked by likelihood to pursue). *Day 4, d(1)(D)*
- Xello Work Experiences + Nimitz pathway notes + Concept Map exit ticket (favorited career + Work Experience skill + Nimitz pathway fit + 3SW indoor/outdoor pattern). *Day 5, d(1)(A), d(1)(C), d(2)(B)*

## Summative Assessment

**Farm to Table Infographic + Emerging Career Brief:** Students submit the completed Canva infographic for Sunny Fields Farm (FYF pp. 91-92: "Farm to Table") AND a one-paragraph brief on the emerging ag career they researched. The infographic is scored on accuracy (does it show real farm-to-table steps?), clarity (would a customer understand?), and design quality (uses pictures, labels, fun facts). The brief is scored on labor-market data accuracy and connection to a societal/technology trend (d(1)(C), d(1)(D)).
