## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Trace one meal you ate yesterday back to the farm. What plants were in it? Where did they grow? Who harvested them?**

Take 3-4 student responses. Use this to bridge from last week's Animal Systems focus to this week's Plant Systems focus. Both are part of agriculture; today the spotlight is on plants and the environment that grows them.

## Activity 1: Ag Cluster Tour + Two Pathways (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 102: "App Exploration"), Agriculture, Food, and Natural Resources chapter

Students work the workbook's App Exploration page, which gives the exact app path for this cluster.

> [H&L PLATFORM] Open the Hats & Ladders app. Go to Clusters and click the Agriculture, Food, and Natural Resources Cluster. Watch the "Cluster Tour" video, then click "Pathway Possibilities," answer the questions, and rate at least one pathway.

Name the two pathways this week lives inside while students browse:

- **Plant Science:** advances crop growth, production, and agricultural research
- **Environmental and Natural Resources:** conserves and manages natural resources like water and forests

Careers worth pointing out as students move through the cluster: Botanist (studies plants and how they grow), Horticulturist (grows and breeds plants for food, ornamental, and landscaping use), Soil Scientist (studies soil composition and what it can grow), Agricultural Engineer (designs farming equipment and systems), Park Ranger, Forester, and Environmental Scientist.

**Stop and Jot:** The App Exploration page asks two questions. Give 45 seconds for each. First, find one Hat that matches your personality or interests and write which Hat and why. Second, find one Hat that feels like it is NOT a fit and write which Hat and why.

Students then open both Hats and fill two rows of the career research worksheet:

> [H&L PLATFORM] Each Hat profile carries Education, Salary, and Daily Tasks sections. Students copy those three fields plus the career name onto the worksheet.

## Activity 2: Grow System Rescue — Diagnose the Grow Zone (25 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 88-90: "Grow System Rescue"), a Career Climb activity

Open the workbook to page 88. Students are the hydroponics technician on duty at a large indoor farm. A section of lettuce is in trouble: leaves are yellowing, roots are weak, and some plants have stopped growing. If nobody acts quickly, part of the harvest is lost. Release the steps one at a time and check each before the next.

**Step 1: Learn about Hydroponic Systems (3 min).** Read the four parts of a hydroponic system together (p. 88). Water flow moves water and nutrients to the roots, nutrients help plants grow strong, lights replace sunlight indoors, and a clean system prevents blockages. A good system moves water evenly to all plants, delivers the right amount of nutrients, provides consistent light, and keeps plants healthy. Check: every student can name the four parts before Step 2 opens.

**Step 2: Investigate the Grow Zone (6 min).** Students read the four observations from the affected section (p. 89): several leaves are yellow, some roots look dry or thin, water is moving slowly in part of the system, and lights in one area are dim. They weigh each observation against the workbook's four diagnostic tips and write what the clues might mean.

**Step 3: Identify the Problem (4 min).** Students circle one or more causes from the workbook's list (p. 89): water flow problem, lighting problem, nutrient problem, or system cleanliness problem. They write the reasoning that got them there. More than one answer can be defended here, and the reasoning is what gets scored, not the circle.

**Step 4: Decide What to Fix First (5 min).** A technician cannot fix everything at once (p. 90). Students pick the one problem to solve first and write why it is the priority and how fixing it helps the lettuce.

**Step 6: Compare Solutions (5 min).** Partners trade books and compare two things: which problem each one is fixing first, and the reason behind it. Step 5, the chart paper system design, runs as today's extension, so pairs compare diagnoses rather than drawings.

**Class Discussion (2 min).** Use the workbook's own prompts (p. 90): what other roles would a hydroponics technician work with, and what Powerskills does a hydroponics technician need to be successful?

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Students who circle all four causes on Step 3 have not committed to anything. Push them back to the four diagnostic tips on page 89 and ask which single tip their strongest clue matches. Dim lights and yellow leaves both point at light; slow water and thin roots both point at flow. Either is defensible when the reasoning names the clue.

**DOK 3:** Two technicians look at the same grow zone. One says the pump is the problem and the other says the lights are. What evidence in the grow zone would settle the argument, and which one would you check first?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pre-fill the career research worksheet with 2 specific careers (e.g., Botanist and Park Ranger) so students don't have to choose. For "Grow System Rescue," hand out a Step 2 with two of the four clues already matched to a diagnostic tip so students copy the pattern for the other two.
- **Extension:** Complete the workbook's Step 5 (FYF p. 90): design an improved hydroponic system on chart paper and label how water flows to all plants, how nutrients are delivered, how lights reach every plant, and where the plants sit. Students with extra time in the app play "Game Time" and rate three Hats (FYF p. 102).
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Plant = Planta, Soil = Suelo, Forest = Bosque, Water = Agua, Roots = Raíces, Nutrients = Nutrientes. Bilingual career research worksheet with Spanish field labels.
