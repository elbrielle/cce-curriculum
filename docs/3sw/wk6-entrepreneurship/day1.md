# Day 1: What Counts as an Entrepreneurship Opportunity?

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 50 minutes |
| **Objective** | Define entrepreneurship and identify opportunities in several fields, including one field of personal interest |
| **TEKS** | d(3)(I) |
| **Evidence** | Completed Entrepreneurship Opportunity Guide |
| **Sources** | FYF p. 221 and pp. 252-254; current Irving ISD High School CTE page |

## Before class

- Post the Entrepreneurship Opportunity Guide and licensed FYF opener.
- Open the current Irving ISD High School CTE page.
- If using H&L, test the Business cluster launch. The fixed guide remains the no-login route.

## 50-minute flow

### 1. Start with a decision — 5 minutes

Ask: “A local business can afford one move: advertise, hire, or buy equipment. Which move would you make, and what fact would you need first?”

Students think silently, then each partner gets 30 seconds. Listen for students who separate a preference from evidence.

### 2. Read the cluster opener — 10 minutes

Use FYF p. 221 to name the work of business analysts, financial examiners, e-commerce specialists, marketers, and entrepreneurs. Define entrepreneurship plainly: an owner finds a problem or unmet need, organizes a response, and takes responsibility for the result.

Do not present every self-employed person as successful or every idea as an opportunity. Customer need, cost, competition, rules, and delivery still matter.

### 3. Read the opportunity examples — 12 minutes

Students read the five fixed examples in the Opportunity Guide. For each, identify:

1. the problem or need;
2. the product or service; and
3. one owner responsibility.

Optional H&L route: students browse the Business cluster for one unfamiliar career. Do not make exact Hat titles or platform access load-bearing.

### 4. Build the cross-field list — 18 minutes

Students describe four opportunities in different fields, then select one field of personal interest. They write one fact they know and one question they must answer before spending money.

At minute 10, check for four copies of the same business. Prompt with: “What changes when the customer, rules, or work setting changes?”

### 5. Close — 5 minutes

Prompt: “Your cousin has $3,000 and wants to start a business. Name one field, one problem worth investigating, and the first question your cousin should answer.”

## Monitoring and key

Full evidence includes a problem or need, an offer, and an owner responsibility. “Restaurant,” “YouTube,” or “make money” alone is not an opportunity description.

Accept a wide range of fields. Do not score whether the teacher personally likes the business. Score the connection between problem, customer, offer, and responsibility.

## Support and absence route

Use the fixed examples, not memory from prior weeks. Students may complete the entire guide independently from Canvas. H&L is optional; no screenshot or favorite count is required.
