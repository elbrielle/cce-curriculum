## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: If you could start any business tomorrow with $0, what would it be? Be specific, name it, describe what it sells, and who buys it.**

Take 4-5 student responses. Capture them on the board. The class should see a wide range, food, art, tech, services, products. Bridge: every one of those is entrepreneurship, and entrepreneur is one of the four roles the workbook names on the opening page of this cluster.

---

## Activity 1: Exploring the World of Business, Marketing, and Finance (12 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 221: "Exploring the World of BUSINESS, MARKETING, AND FINANCE")

Open the workbook to page 221. Read the chapter opener together: this cluster is about helping organizations grow, connect with customers, and make smart financial decisions. Some roles focus on understanding what customers want; others manage money, build brands, or create new opportunities. Point to the three Hats printed on the page: **Business Analyst**, **Financial Examiner**, and **E-Commerce Specialist**. Ask the page's closing question: what skills do these roles need to make important decisions about money, customers, and business growth?

**Be the Decision Maker (FYF p. 221):** Read the boxed prompt aloud. A local business has enough money to do only one thing this year: launch a major advertising campaign, hire more employees, or invest in new equipment. Which option would you choose? How would you decide what gives the business the best chance to grow, and what information would you need before making the final call?

**Think-Pair-Share:** 30 seconds of silent Think, then Partner A for 30 seconds, then Partner B for 30 seconds. Post two stems so every pair has a way in: "I would pick _____ because _____" and "Hiring more people helps the business _____, but new equipment helps it _____." Call on two pairs to share, chosen from what you heard while circulating rather than from raised hands.

The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet the cluster's pathways in the Hats & Ladders app in Activity 2. Irving ISD's own programs of study sit on the workbook's district pages (FYF pp. 252-253: "What is Happening at My District?"): Business Management, Administrative Management, Retail Management, Real Estate, Entrepreneurship, and Marketing & Sales. Name the **Entrepreneurship** program of study specifically, since it is this week's topic, along with its Entrepreneurship and Small Business certification and the two student organizations behind it, DECA and Business Professionals of America.

## Activity 2: Cluster App Exploration + Defining Entrepreneurship (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 254: "App Exploration") and (FYF p. 252: "What is Happening at My District?")

> [H&L PLATFORM] The App Exploration page walks students through the cluster in order: open the Hats & Ladders app, go to Clusters, click the Business, Marketing, and Finance Cluster, watch the "Cluster Tour" video, play "Game Time," then find 1 Hat that matches their personality or interests and 1 Hat that does not fit them. Students record their answers on page 254. They finish the rest of the page, Pathway Possibilities and rating 3 Hats, on Day 5.

**Stop and Jot:** while the cluster tour video plays, students pause TWICE in their notebook (dated entry, labeled "3SW Wk6 Business") for 45 seconds each, with the prompt on screen:

- **Stop 1:** One business career you did not know existed
- **Stop 2:** One question you still have about starting a business

The district page describes Irving ISD's Entrepreneurship program of study as preparing students to start and manage businesses through innovation, business planning, financial management, and marketing strategies, while building critical thinking and leadership skills. Put that list on the board. Ask students which of those five words does the most work in a real business.

After the app exploration, each student writes their OWN definition of entrepreneurship in 1 sentence in their notebook. Examples:

- "An entrepreneur is someone who turns an idea into a real business that customers pay for."
- "Entrepreneurship is taking the risk of running your own company in exchange for the chance to build something you love."

Take 3-4 student definitions and write them on the board. Discuss: which definition is strongest? Why?

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Some students will define entrepreneurship as "rich people who own companies." Push back: entrepreneurs include lawn-mowing teenagers, food truck owners, Etsy sellers, and freelance graphic designers. Most are not rich. Most are people who saw a problem and built a solution.

## Activity 3: Cross-Cluster Entrepreneurship List (15 min)

**Source:** Connecting all 8 clusters explored this year

Students create a list of 5 entrepreneurial opportunities, one in each of FIVE different clusters they've explored this year. The clusters covered so far:

- Manufacturing (1SW)
- IT (1SW)
- Law & Public Service (2SW)
- Health Science (2SW)
- Agriculture (3SW Wks 1-3)
- Hospitality & Tourism (3SW Wk 4)
- Human Services (3SW Wk 5)
- Business (3SW Wk 6, current)

Each entry needs:

- **Cluster name**
- **Entrepreneurial opportunity** (specific business idea, not vague)
- **One business skill needed** (not a technical skill, a BUSINESS skill like marketing, accounting, customer service)

Example list:

| Cluster | Opportunity | Business Skill Needed |
|---|---|---|
| Health Science | Mobile dental hygienist serving rural areas | Scheduling and billing |
| Agriculture | Drone-based crop scouting service | Sales prospecting |
| Hospitality | Food truck for office workers downtown | Daily revenue tracking |
| Human Services | Mobile barber for elderly clients in care facilities | Insurance and licensing |
| Business | Marketing agency for local Irving small businesses | Client communication |

**DOK 4:** Look at your list. Which entrepreneurial opportunity is the EASIEST to start with little money? Which is the HARDEST? Defend your answer with at least one specific reason.

## Exit Ticket (3 min)

**EXIT TICKET** (Mini-Case / Scenario Application) · [Printable PDF](../../resources/exit-tickets/3sw-wk6-day1-business-cluster-tour-defining-entrepreneurship.pdf):

Scenario: My older cousin has $3,000 saved up and wants to start a small business in one of the clusters we've explored (Manufacturing, IT, Law, Health Science, Agriculture, Hospitality, Human Services, or Business).

1. Which cluster would I recommend for a low-budget ($3,000) startup, and why?

   My pick: _______________________

   Why: ____________________________________________________________

2. What is ONE specific entrepreneurial business my cousin could start in that cluster?

   Business idea: _______________________

3. What is ONE business skill (NOT technical skill) my cousin needs to succeed?

   Business skill: _______________________

My 1-sentence definition of entrepreneurship: "Entrepreneurship is _______________________________________________________."

*(d(3)(I))*

Submit your cross-cluster list with this ticket.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a partial list with 3 cluster names and 1 opportunity already filled in. Students fill in the remaining 2 clusters.
- **Extension:** Add a 6th cluster to your list and explain why it's the BEST cluster for entrepreneurship in Texas right now.
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Entrepreneur = Emprendedor/a, Opportunity = Oportunidad, Skill = Habilidad. Bilingual cross-cluster list template.
