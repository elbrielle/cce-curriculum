# Day 4: Venture Pitch Day

## Lesson Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 50 minutes |
| **Objectives** | Build a one-page venture brief for your group's chosen idea using the work already in your workbook; deliver a 2-minute pitch to a classmate investor panel; give and receive star and wish feedback; name the work ethic quality you brought to the pitch |
| **TEKS** | d(3)(I), d(4)(F) |
| **5E Phases** | Engage: Warm-Up · Explore: Venture brief build and practice · Extend: Pitches to the investor panel · Explain: Work ethic reflection · Evaluate: Exit Ticket |
| **Deliverable** | One-page venture brief + 2-minute pitch delivered + star and wish feedback from at least 2 other groups |
| **Materials** | Workbook pages 234-237 from Days 2-3, printed venture brief template, printed "Star + Wish" peer feedback forms, timer, projector |

---

## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: When you watch Shark Tank or a real business pitch, what makes some pitches work and others flop? List ONE thing each.**

Take 4 student responses. Capture the patterns: confidence, clear numbers, knowing the customer, having a real answer to "how do you make money," practiced delivery. These are the same things an investor panel listens for.

---

## Activity 1: Build the Venture Brief (12 min)

The idea each group chose at the end of Day 3 is the venture they pitch today. Every line of the brief comes from work already sitting in somebody's workbook, so no group starts on a blank page.

| Brief section | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| The problem | Step 1 problem statement (FYF p. 234) |
| The idea | Step 3 chosen idea (FYF p. 235) |
| Why us and not the other options | Step 4, question 2 (FYF p. 236) |
| Our biggest challenge | Step 4, question 3 (FYF p. 236) |
| Our call and why | Step 5 (FYF p. 237) |
| What we need to start | One new line the group writes: the people, money, or tools it would take to build the first version |

Groups fill the six sections on one page, assign one section to each member so everyone speaks, then practice the pitch once out loud against a timer. Pitches are 2 minutes maximum.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Some groups will spend all 12 minutes decorating the brief instead of rehearsing. Set a hard rule: by the 6-minute mark the brief is done and the rest of the time is practice out loud. A plain brief delivered well beats a colorful brief read off the page.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Groups who ask for a script are asking for the wrong tool today. The formal 30-second sales pitch structure, with its hook, benefits, and call to action, gets taught later in the year in the Sales and Presentations week (FYF pp. 241-243: "30 Seconds to Sell"). Today the target is a clear 2-minute explanation of a real problem and a real idea.

---

## Activity 2: Pitches to the Investor Panel (25 min)

Groups pitch in a random order, drawn from a hat so no group gains an advantage from going last. Each pitch is 2 minutes maximum. Three or four students sit as the investor panel and rotate between pitches so that most of the class serves on the panel at least once.

After each pitch the panel asks ONE question and the group gets 30 seconds to answer. Keep a short bank of questions on the board so panelists are never stuck:

- Who exactly has this problem?
- What stops someone else from copying this?
- What is the first thing you would build?

**Time, Voice, Body:** post the three blocks before the first group stands up. Voice 0 while a group sets up, Voice 3 for whoever is speaking, and Voice 1 for the star and wish writing between pitches. Run a visible 2-minute countdown for every pitch, and name one spot on the front desk where finished star and wish slips go so the handoff does not eat the next group's clock.

After all groups have pitched, hold a class vote on the most convincing venture by show of hands or sticky notes.

The peer feedback form ("Star + Wish") goes to each group after the vote:

- **Star:** What did you do well? (1 specific thing)
- **Wish:** What would I change to make the pitch stronger? (1 specific thing)

Each group should receive feedback from at least 2 other groups.

**DOK 3:** Looking at the pitches you heard today, what are 2 things that separated the strongest pitches from the weakest ones? Use specific examples.

## Activity 3: Work Ethic Reflection (5 min)

**Source:** TEKS d(4)(F): work ethic, integrity, dedication, perseverance

Connect the pitch experience to work ethic. The pitches that landed were the ones where the group was prepared, on time, and focused. The pitches that struggled often had a member who had not read the brief, had not practiced their section, or was not listening when the panel asked its question.

Quick whole-class discussion (3 minutes):

- What did you do to PREPARE for today's pitch?
- What does work ethic mean for an entrepreneur specifically?
- Why does perseverance matter MORE for entrepreneurs than for employees?

Then each student writes one sentence in their notebook:

- "The work ethic quality I demonstrated today was _____." (Examples: dedication, preparation, teamwork, perseverance, integrity)

## Exit Ticket (3 min)

**EXIT TICKET** (Trade-off / Dilemma Analysis) · [Printable PDF](../../resources/exit-tickets/3sw-wk6-day4-venture-pitch-day.pdf):

Your group has 12 minutes before pitching. You see TWO problems:

- **(A) The venture brief looks plain** (no title, no color, no sketch). Could polish for 8 minutes.
- **(B) One group member has not practiced their section out loud.** Could rehearse them for 8 minutes.

Pros of picking A (polish the brief): _______________________________________________________

Pros of picking B (practice the group member): _______________________________________________________

My group's choice (A or B): ________

Quality list: preparation / dedication / teamwork / perseverance / responsibility / reliability.

Which work-ethic quality matters MOST in this decision, and why is it the right one over the others? *(d(3)(I), d(4)(F))*

   My quality: _______________________

   Why: ____________________________________________________________

Submit your group's venture brief + at least 2 peer feedback forms with this ticket.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a venture brief template with the six section headers pre-printed and a sentence stem under each: "The problem is _____. Our idea is _____. People pick us over _____ because _____. Our biggest challenge is _____. Our call is _____ because _____. To start we need _____."
- **Extension:** Rewrite your group's brief as a 30-second version. Which two sections did you have to cut, and what did the pitch lose when you cut them?
- **ELL:** The pitch can be delivered in English, Spanish, or bilingual. Many real Texas business pitches are delivered bilingually. Pair with bilingual peers if needed for translation support, and allow the panel question to be answered in either language.
