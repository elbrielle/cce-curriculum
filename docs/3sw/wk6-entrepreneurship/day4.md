# Day 4: Venture Brief and Pitch

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 50 minutes |
| **Objective** | Build a clear venture brief, communicate the evidence in a short pitch, and connect a visible action to a professional characteristic |
| **TEKS** | d(3)(I), d(4)(F) |
| **Evidence** | Group brief plus each student’s speaking/written record, one peer note, and work-ethic response |
| **Sources** | Student work from FYF pp. 234-237; CCE Venture Brief and Individual Pitch Record |

## Before class

- Post the Venture Brief and rubric.
- Set a 90-second pitch timer and a 30-second question/transition timer.
- Count groups. Whole-class timing supports up to eight groups in 24 minutes. If there are more, use two supervised pitch stations or recorded submissions.
- Prepare a written or recorded route. Public speaking performance is not the grade.

## 50-minute flow

### 1. Launch — 5 minutes

Ask: “What makes an idea easy to understand in 90 seconds?” Capture three answers: clear problem, clear offer, and specific evidence.

### 2. Build and rehearse the brief — 15 minutes

Groups complete six sections:

1. problem and customer;
2. product or service;
3. reason a customer would choose it;
4. biggest challenge;
5. call and evidence; and
6. people, tools, time, or money needed for a first version.

At minute 9, stop decorating. Assign speaking jobs and rehearse once. A student may record, submit a written pitch, or present privately with the same evidence.

### 3. Pitch rotation — 24 minutes

For each group:

- 90 seconds: pitch;
- 30 seconds: one evidence question; and
- 30 seconds: transition while the audience writes.

Each student records one Star and one Wish for one pitch. The note must refer to evidence, not whether the student personally likes the business.

Suggested questions:

- Who exactly experiences this problem?
- What is the first version you could test?
- What evidence could change your call?

Skip the class vote. Popularity is not the learning target.

### 4. Individual work-ethic close — 6 minutes

Students choose integrity, preparation, dedication, perseverance, or reliability. They name one action they took and explain how that quality can matter for both a business owner and an employee.

Do not ask why perseverance matters “more” for owners. The work may look different, but both roles require professional responsibility.

## Monitoring and key

Strong responses name an observable action: arrived prepared, used accurate evidence, practiced, accepted a revision, completed a promised task, or answered honestly when evidence was missing.

Do not score confidence, accent, volume beyond audibility, design polish, or whether the group’s venture wins approval.

## Support and absence route

Students may speak live, record an approved 90-second response, present privately, or submit the written speaking record. A student without a group uses their own strongest idea and the same six-section brief. Private self-review is equal when peer feedback is unavailable.
