# Week 4: Kitchen Confidential — Culinary and Hospitality Careers

**3rd Six Weeks** | Hospitality & Tourism Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore the Hospitality and Tourism cluster through the Hats & Ladders app and the *Find Your Future* workbook, design a creative dish around a partner-chosen special ingredient in "Culinary Twist," solve three hotel crises in a staff role in "Hotel Rescue," design a full event experience as a catering business owner in "Cater and Create," build a competition plan for the "Powerskill: Motivation" lesson, compare salaries for three hospitality careers, complete the Xello Decision Making lesson, and connect to Singley Academy's School of Culinary Arts and Hospitality Services (ServSafe Manager certification).

**Learning Target:** I can name at least three hospitality careers, compare what three of them pay and how much training each one needs, solve a hotel problem in a staff role, and design a catering event a small-business owner could sell.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my menu mockup has a dish name, a description, a price, and a sketch, when my salary worksheet lists pay, education, outlook, and one daily task for three hospitality careers, when my team's Hotel Rescue sheet has a solution for all three problems and names what my role did, and when my catering plan has an event style, a themed menu, a table design, and one extra touch guests take home.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe at least three hospitality careers, explain entrepreneurial opportunities in the restaurant, hotel, and catering industries, design a creative dish with a special ingredient and present it on a menu, problem-solve as a hotel team, and design a catering event where the food, the table, and the extra touches all match one vibe."

## Career Connection

The hospitality industry is the world's largest employer. From Michelin-star restaurants to food trucks, from luxury hotels to event planning, this cluster offers careers for every personality type. Entrepreneurship is especially strong in hospitality, many people open their own restaurants, food trucks, catering businesses, or event-planning companies.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** **Singley Academy's School of Culinary Arts and Hospitality** offers both Culinary Arts and Hospitality Services pathways, the district's primary home for culinary training. Singley also operates the student-run **FireBird Cafe Catering** business as applied practice. **Lodging and Resort Management** is offered at Irving High School, MacArthur High School, and Nimitz High School. The workbook's district pages carry the local detail students respond to most (FYF pp. 124-125: "What is Happening at My District?"): the Culinary Arts and Lodging and Resort Management programs of study, students across the district preparing weekly lunches and catering events on campus and at venues around the city, the General Management and ServSafe Manager industry certifications, and FCCLA as the student organization. Those pages open on Executive Functioning, the staying-organized skill the Day 4 catering plan actually runs on.

## Vocabulary

- **Hospitality:** The business of providing food, drink, lodging, and entertainment to guests and travelers.
- **Entrepreneur:** A person who starts and runs their own business, taking on financial risk in exchange for potential profit.
- **ServSafe:** An industry-recognized food safety certification required for food service managers in most US states.
- **Motivation (Intrinsic):** Doing something because you enjoy it or find it personally meaningful, not for an external reward.
- **Motivation (Extrinsic):** Doing something to earn a reward (money, prize, recognition) or avoid a consequence.
- **Catering:** Planning and providing the food, the drinks, and often the whole guest experience for an event held somewhere other than a restaurant.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Hospitality and Tourism printed pages 111-126. The chapter opener (FYF p. 111: "Exploring the World of HOSPITALITY AND TOURISM") frames the cluster as creating experiences people remember, puts a "Be the Decision Maker" fall-festival dilemma in front of students, and introduces three Hats on the page: Food Critic, Chef, and Augmented Reality Tour Creator. The chapter prints no pathway list. Students meet the cluster's pathways in the Hats & Ladders app through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 126: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's own programs of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 124-125).

Four chapter sections carry this week:

- **Culinary Twist** (Day 1): students step into a chef's role, choose base ingredients, receive one special ingredient chosen by a partner, plan how to feature it, build a menu mockup with a name, description, price, and sketch, then present the dish to a group of three or four (FYF pp. 112-113: "Culinary Twist").
- **Powerskill: Motivation** (Day 2): students review six types of intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, design a high-stakes hyper-realistic cake competition for professional bakers with a name, goals, rules, prizes, and at least two named motivators, then compare plans with a partner (FYF pp. 121-123: "Powerskill: Motivation").
- **Hotel Rescue** (Day 3): teams of six each take a hotel staff role, read three crises (an overbooked hotel, sick kitchen staff before a business retreat, an unready VIP room), brainstorm role by role, then present one solution to the class (FYF pp. 117-118: "Hotel Rescue").
- **Cater and Create** (Day 4): students own a catering company that designs whole event experiences. They choose an event and a feeling, design a themed menu, design the table and floral side, add entertainment and one take-home touch, then compare designs with a partner (FYF pp. 119-120: "Cater and Create").

A fifth section, **Restaurant Rebrand** (FYF pp. 114-116), puts students in the head chef's chair at a restaurant whose old menu missed the mark. It runs as the Day 1 extension.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 1, the "Be the Decision Maker" prompt on the chapter opener (FYF p. 111), where the planner of a huge fall festival must choose between live entertainment and food experiences. Distinguished: pose it to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "I would spend more on _____ because _____" and "Guests remember _____, but guests come back for _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who names a guest need beyond fun (comfort, safety, cost), and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 5, Activity 1, the one-sentence decision-framework write that closes the Xello lesson. Distinguished: give 90 seconds with the prompt on screen and post two stems, the framework stem already in the plan ("If I had to decide between _____ and _____, I would gather information about _____") and an entrepreneurship stem for students who finish it fast ("An entrepreneurial opportunity in hospitality is _____, and it needs _____ skills beyond cooking"). Read jots over shoulders. If most students stall on the gather-information step, model one out loud with a chef versus lodging manager comparison before students start the exit ticket.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 3, Activity 2, the 25 minutes of Hotel Rescue brainstorming. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times, one target per lap (lap 1, every student has a role written down and one idea for Problem 1; lap 2, the quieter roles have contributed to Problems 2 and 3, not just the Hotel Director; lap 3, each team has picked its presentation problem and assigned one sentence per role). Have feedback ready before class for the two known stalls: a team that lets the Hotel Director answer everything, and a team that solves all three problems by saying "apologize and give a refund." If more than a handful of teams miss the lap 2 target, pause the room and reassign the speaking order role by role.
- **Chunking:** Day 1, Activities 2 and 3, the five Culinary Twist steps. Distinguished: release one step at a time, say what each step is for before students start it (base ingredients build the dish, the special ingredient forces the creative problem, the menu mockup sells it), and model the whole chain once on the projector with a single example dish before students choose anything. Close each step with a quick check: hands up when four to five base ingredients are circled, hands up when the special ingredient is written in. Note which step draws the longest silence. If most students stall at the menu description, project one strong description and one flat one and let the room name the difference before Step 4 continues.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 4, Activity 2, the three design frames on the Cater and Create sheet. Distinguished: post the three blocks before markers come out, Voice 1 for partner design talk and Voice 2 for the Share and Compare that follows, a visible countdown for each frame (menu, table, extra touches) so no pair spends 25 minutes on flowers, and a stated rule that markers live in the center tray and finished sheets go face up at the table edge. Narrate the pairs who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If the room is running long on the table frame, announce the shortened extra-touches block before the countdown restarts rather than after.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- Pre-printed Culinary Twist menu mockup template with a sample description and price
- Salary comparison worksheet with one career row pre-filled
- Hotel Rescue role cards with sentence stems for each role
- Cater and Create sheet with the event style and the feeling already circled, so students start at the menu frame

### Extensions
- "Restaurant Rebrand" (FYF pp. 114-116): as the new head chef, sort four customer complaints into what needs to improve, pick a restaurant theme, and lay out a full menu with two appetizers, two main dishes, a specialty drink, and a dessert
- Hotel Rescue: design a 4th hotel crisis your team would have to solve
- Cater and Create: build the event's invitation or menu card in Canva using the colors from your table design
- Research the Singley Academy School of Culinary Arts and Hospitality and write a 4-year course plan for the Culinary Arts or Hospitality Services pathway

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Restaurant = Restaurante, Chef = Chef, Hotel = Hotel, Catering = Servicio de banquetes, Customer = Cliente
- Bilingual menu mockup template option
- Hotel Rescue role cards color-coded so non-English speakers can identify their role visually
- Pair with bilingual peers during group activities
