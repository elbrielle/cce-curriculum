# Day 4: Salon Entrepreneurship + Stress Toolkit

## Lesson Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 50 minutes |
| **Objectives** | Identify entrepreneurial opportunities in the beauty industry; sketch a salon business concept with six required elements; complete the "Stress Toolkit" activity by designing 3 social media posts that teach stress management techniques |
| **TEKS** | d(3)(I) |
| **5E Phases** | Engage: Warm-Up · Explore: Salon concept sketch and Stress Toolkit posts · Explain: Partner review of the posts · Evaluate: Exit Ticket |
| **Deliverable** | Salon business concept sketch (6 elements) + 3 stress management social media posts |

## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Would you rather work IN someone else's salon or OWN your own salon? What's good and bad about each?**

Take 4-5 student responses. Surface both sides, employees have stable pay and no risk; owners have higher potential income but take on rent, payroll, and inventory costs.

---

## Activity 1: Salon Business Concept Sketch (15 min)

**Source:** TEKS d(3)(I): Define entrepreneurship and identify entrepreneurial opportunities. Builds on the "Be the Decision Maker" salon and wellness studio prompt from Day 1 (FYF p. 127).

Project an example salon business concept on the board:

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| Business Name | Reset Studio |
| Services Offered | Haircuts, color, scalp treatments, hot oil |
| Target Customer | Working professionals 25-45 looking for a relaxing 60-90 min experience |
| Location | Las Colinas (high foot traffic, mid-range income area) |
| Unique Selling Point | Calm music, plant-filled space, free 5-min scalp massage with every cut |
| One Business Skill Needed | Customer scheduling software / inventory management |

Students sketch their OWN salon (or barber shop, nail salon, spa, skin care studio, or SFX makeup studio) on the concept template. They must include all 6 elements above. Students who named a business during Day 1's Be the Decision Maker prompt start from that name.

The point: cosmetology is creative, but the BUSINESS side requires a different set of skills, accounting, marketing, scheduling, supply ordering. An entrepreneur in cosmetology has to learn both sides.

Irving ISD students get to practice exactly this. The Cosmetology program of study runs a **student-run salon** that provides hair-cutting services to real customers on select weekends during the school year, which means students handle scheduling, customer service, and supplies alongside the cutting (FYF pp. 136-137: "What is Happening at My District?").

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Students often pick the "Unique Selling Point" too generic ("good service" or "low prices"). Push them: what is something that NO other salon in your area is doing? That's the actual hook.

## Activity 2: Stress Toolkit, Learn and Create (25 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 132-133: "Stress Toolkit", Steps 1-2)

Read the workbook's opening (p. 132): stress is a normal part of life, but too much of it affects mood, health, and the ability to stay focused, and it shows up as headaches, body aches, and trouble sleeping. Stress management tools help people calm down, focus, and stay healthy. Students work for the city as the **Health and Wellness Coordinator**, and the mayor has assigned them to create a series of social media posts that teach people about stress management tools.

**Step 1: Learn about Stress Management Techniques (about 8 min).** Project the workbook's table (p. 132):

| Stress Management Tool | How It Helps |
|---|---|
| Deep breathing exercises | Helps slow your heart rate and calm your mind |
| Journaling | Allows you to express emotions and reduce anxiety |
| Listening to music | Can improve mood and help with relaxation |
| Talking to a friend or mentor | Release stress and improve overall health |
| Talk with a professional | Provides support and helps process emotions |
| Time management strategies | Reduces feelings of being overwhelmed |
| Creative activities (drawing, coloring, crafting) | Helps focus the mind and relieve tension |
| Practicing gratitude | Shifts focus to positive aspects of life |

Each student picks **3 techniques** from the table. They will design one post for each.

**Step 2: Create Social Media Posts (about 17 min).** Students design 3 posts on the workbook page (p. 133) or in Canva. The workbook requires four elements on every post:

- A catchy title or headline
- A brief explanation of the stress management tool
- A fun fact, tip, or personal encouragement
- An eye-catching element (logo, drawing, or graphic)

Walk around and check that students are designing 3 DIFFERENT posts, each teaching a different technique, not three versions of the same one.

**DOK 4:** This activity puts you in the role of a Health and Wellness Coordinator. How does that career connect to cosmetology and to the SFX work you did this week? Use one thing a client experiences in a salon chair in your answer.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Many students will pick the techniques they already use. Push them to include at least one that is NEW to them. Journaling, time management, and gratitude get dismissed fast by students who have not tried them, and they are some of the most effective on the list.

## Activity 3: Share with a Partner (3 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 133: "Stress Toolkit", Step 3)

Students pair up, review their partner's posts, and give at least one piece of constructive feedback. The workbook's three discussion questions run the share: which post is the most effective and why, what is one new stress management tool you learned from your partner, and how could you use one of these techniques in your own life.

If time remains, take the workbook's class discussion question out loud: how could your core personality type (Doer, Analyzer, Creator, Helper, Persuader, or Organizer) help you succeed in a Health and Wellness career?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pre-fill the salon concept template with 2 elements as examples. Provide pre-built Canva templates for the three posts, with the headline already styled and a box for each of the four required elements.
- **Extension:** Build a full salon business plan: startup costs, monthly expenses, projected revenue at year 1. Use real DFW-area rent and supply costs. Or rewrite one Stress Toolkit post twice, once as a text to a friend and once as an email to a school counselor, using the district page's professional communication prompt (FYF p. 136).
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: Salon = Salón, Customer = Cliente, Stress = Estrés, Wellness = Bienestar, Breathing = Respiración. The salon concept and the posts can be in English, Spanish, or bilingual, many community wellness campaigns in Texas are bilingual.
