# Day 3: SFX Quality Check + Texas TDLR Licensing

## Lesson Overview

| | |
|---|---|

## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Look at your design from yesterday. What is still holding, and what came loose overnight? Name the one part you would rebuild first.**

Take 4 responses without fixing the designs yet. Special effects artists test the work, rate what held up, and plan the repair before it reaches a screen. Students will do that first, then examine what Texas requires before someone can do cosmetology work for pay.

---

## Activity 1: SFX Quality Check and Improvement Plan (10 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 131: "Special Effects Makeup", DAY 3)

Students work the workbook's three closing steps on page 131.

**Step 1: Quality Check.** Students rate their own work on two scales, checking one box each: **materials stayed attached** (Excellent / Good / Needs Improvement) and **texture looks realistic** (Excellent / Good / Needs Improvement).

**Step 2: Problem Solving.** Students write the biggest challenge they hit during application and how they solved it, or tried to.

**Step 3: Final Improvement Plan.** Students write what they would change or add with more time or better materials.

**Stop and Jot:** Name all three writes before students pick up a pencil, give 3 minutes with the prompts on screen, and post two stems: "The biggest problem was _____ because _____" and "Next time I would _____ instead of _____." Read the jots over shoulders while students write.

**Class Discussion (2 min).** Use the workbook's own prompts (FYF p. 131): what made some transformations look more believable than others, how might an SFX artist adjust a design to balance realism with an actor's comfort, and what other professionals would an SFX artist have to work with?

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Students who rate themselves Excellent on everything are usually rating effort, not the design. Point them back at the two named criteria and ask them to show you the evidence: which piece is still attached, and which part of the texture reads from three feet away.

## Activity 2: TDLR Licensing Pathway Research (25 min)

**Source:** Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, [tdlr.texas.gov](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov), plus Irving ISD Cosmetology pathway information (FYF pp. 136-137: "What is Happening at My District?")

Open with the framing question: in Texas you need a license from the state to legally cut hair for money. Why would the state require that? Take two responses and land the class on safety (sanitation, chemicals, customer protection) and quality (skilled work, consumer trust).

**Navigation walkthrough (about 5 min).** Project the TDLR home page and walk students through finding the Cosmetology page:

1. From the TDLR home page, click **Programs** and then **Cosmetology**
2. The Cosmetology page lists license types (Operator, Esthetician, Manicurist, Salon, and others)
3. Click **Cosmetology Operator** and find the requirements section
4. Look for Required Hours, Exam Requirements, Application Fee, and Renewal Process

Demonstrate one of these sections live, then have students open the TDLR site on their own Chromebooks.

**Worksheet (about 20 min).** Distribute the TDLR Licensing Pathway worksheet. Students fill in the 3-column comparison:

| Route | Route 1: Ratteree HS Pathway | Route 2: Post-HS Cosmetology School | Route 3: Apprenticeship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where is the training? | | | |
| Hours required | | | |
| Approximate cost | | | |
| Length (months/years) | | | |
| Exam at the end? | | | |
| Earn money while learning? | | | |
| Pros | | | |
| Cons | | | |

For Route 1, students use the Irving ISD pathway information. Ratteree offers cosmetology hours in high school, students begin earning hours toward the license while still enrolled, the pathway's industry certification is the **Cosmetology Operator License**, its student organization is **SkillsUSA**, and the program runs a student salon that serves real customers on select weekends (FYF pp. 136-137).

For Route 2, students use TDLR data on standard cosmetology school requirements (typically 1,000 hours in Texas).

For Route 3, students research apprenticeship options on TDLR (Texas allows licensed cosmetologists to train apprentices under specific rules).

After filling in the table, students answer:

> "Which route would you choose, and why? Use at least one specific data point from your worksheet to defend your answer."

**DOK 3:** What conclusions can you draw about the advantages of starting the Cosmetology pathway in high school versus waiting until after graduation? Name 2 advantages and use specific data.

!!! warning "Common Issue"
    The TDLR website can be hard to navigate for middle schoolers. The breadcrumbs change frequently. If students get lost, direct them to use the search bar on tdlr.texas.gov and search "Cosmetology Operator", the top result should be the right page.

## Activity 3: Route Defense with a Partner (8 min)

In the last 8 minutes, students pair up and trade worksheets. Each partner reads the other's choice and asks one question: "Why didn't you pick [the other route]?" This forces both students to defend their reasoning out loud.

**Think-Pair-Share:** Hold a silent 30 second think on "which route gets me working fastest, and what does it cost me," then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds. Post two stems at different levels: "To become a licensed cosmetologist in Texas, you must _____, which takes about _____, and then you must pass _____" and "I did not pick _____ because the _____ column on my worksheet says _____." Two pairs share out, chosen from what you heard while circulating. Listen for who cites a number rather than a feeling.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pre-fill the worksheet with the route names and the "Where is the training?" row already filled in. Students focus on the data fields. For the Quality Check, provide the two rating scales with a one-line description under each level.
- **Extension:** Compare Texas cosmetology licensing to ANOTHER state (e.g., California requires 1,500 hours; Florida requires 1,200 hours). Why might states require different hours?
- **ELL:** Pre-teach: License = Licencia, Apprenticeship = Aprendizaje, Hours = Horas, Exam = Examen, Improve = Mejorar. Bilingual licensing pathway worksheet with Spanish column headers. Provide a visual TDLR navigation guide with screenshots.
