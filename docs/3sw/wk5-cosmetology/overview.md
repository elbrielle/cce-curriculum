# Week 5: Style and Service — Cosmetology Careers

**3rd Six Weeks** | Human Services Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore the Human Services cluster through the Hats & Ladders app and the *Find Your Future* workbook, design and build a special effects makeup transformation across three days using the workbook's texture Style Guide, evaluate their own design against a quality-check rating scale and write an improvement plan, research Texas TDLR cosmetology licensing and compare three routes to a license, sketch a salon business concept, and complete the "Stress Toolkit" social media activity in the role of a community Health and Wellness Coordinator.

**Learning Target:** I can name at least three Human Services careers and what each one does, build a special effects makeup design that uses a real texture and holds together, rate my own work and say how I would improve it, list the steps to a Texas cosmetology license, and explain one way to make money owning a beauty business.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my Concept Card names a character, a texture from the Style Guide, my materials, and a color palette, when my finished design has materials that stay attached and stack instead of lying flat, when my TDLR worksheet compares three routes on hours, cost, and length, when my salon sketch has all six elements, and when my three stress management posts each teach a different technique.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe at least three cosmetology and personal care careers, design and build a special effects makeup transformation that uses a chosen texture, rate my own work and name one improvement, identify the steps to become a licensed cosmetologist in Texas through TDLR, and explain one entrepreneurial opportunity in the beauty industry."

## Career Connection

The beauty industry is a $500+ billion global market that offers diverse career paths from hairstylists to estheticians to nail technicians to barbers to special effects makeup artists. In Texas, cosmetologists are licensed by the **Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR)** and must complete 1,000 hours of training, pass an exam, and renew the license periodically. This is a strong entrepreneurship cluster, many cosmetologists start as employees and eventually open their own salons or work as independent contractors.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** **Cosmetology** is offered at **Ratteree Career Development Center** as a travel-to career-center pathway. Students from any home high school can attend Ratteree for part of their day. The workbook's district pages carry the local detail students respond to most (FYF pp. 136-137: "What is Happening at My District?"): the Cosmetology program of study prepares students for careers in the beauty industry by building skills in hair, skin, and nail operations plus customer service and professional practices; students run a **student-run salon** that provides hair-cutting services to real customers on select weekends during the school year; the industry certification is the **Cosmetology Operator License** through TDLR; and the student organization is **SkillsUSA**. The same pages open with a Career Exploration Connection on **professional communication**, the difference between texting a friend and emailing an adult at school or work. That distinction matters in a salon, where the same student who books a client by text confirms the appointment by email, and it is a two-minute discussion worth having whenever a student asks how salon clients actually get booked.

## Vocabulary

- **Cosmetologist:** A licensed professional who provides hair, skin, and nail services. Requires state licensing.
- **Special Effects (SFX) Makeup:** Makeup used in film, TV, and theater to transform an actor, creating injuries, aging, creatures, or fantasy looks with textures and prosthetics.
- **Prosthetic:** A realistic add-on for the body, often sculpted from silicone or foam latex, that an SFX artist attaches to change how an actor looks.
- **TDLR:** Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, the state agency that issues cosmetology, barber, and other professional licenses in Texas.
- **Esthetician:** A skincare specialist who performs facials, chemical peels, waxing, and other skin treatments.
- **Salon Owner:** An entrepreneur who owns and operates a beauty salon, managing both creative work and business operations (rent, payroll, marketing, inventory).
- **Apprenticeship:** A training model where students learn by working alongside a licensed professional while earning hours toward their own license.
- **Stress Management:** Techniques to reduce stress and improve mental and physical wellbeing, important for both client-facing workers and clients.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Human Services printed pages 127-138. The chapter opener (FYF p. 127: "Exploring the World of HUMAN SERVICES") frames the cluster as helping people improve their daily lives and overall well-being, names counselors, cosmetologists, and community support workers, and prints three Hats on the page: Hair Stylist, Recreational Therapist, and Aesthetician. Its "Be the Decision Maker" box hands students a salon and wellness studio to name and stock with services. The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet Human Services pathways in the Hats & Ladders app through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 138: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's own Cosmetology program of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 136-137).

Two chapter activities carry this week:

- **Special Effects Makeup** (Days 1-3): the chapter's flagship and the only activity in the book the workbook itself splits across three days. Students join an SFX team on a fantasy film where a character touches an ancient artifact and their skin cracks and transforms. Day 1 is partner research plus a Concept Card built from a four-option texture Style Guide. Day 2 is the build: prep and color the materials, apply the adhesive base, then place and stack the pieces with tweezers. Day 3 is the professional close, a quality-check rating scale, a problem-solving write, and a final improvement plan (FYF pp. 128-131: "Special Effects Makeup").
- **Stress Toolkit** (Day 4): students work for the city as the Health and Wellness Coordinator, study a table of eight stress management tools, and design three social media posts, each with a headline, a short explanation, a tip, and an eye-catching element. They close by trading posts with a partner for constructive feedback (FYF pp. 132-133: "Stress Toolkit").

The chapter's embedded Powerskill lesson, **Advocacy** (FYF pp. 134-135), belongs to 2SW Wk5 and is not repeated here. If students bring it up, point back to the mobile farmers' market flyer they built that week.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 3, Activity 3, the worksheet trade where partners ask each other why they did not pick the other licensing route. Distinguished: hold a silent 30 second Think on the question "which route gets me working fastest, and what does it cost me," then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "To become a licensed cosmetologist in Texas, you must _____, which takes about _____, and then you must pass _____" and "I did not pick _____ because the _____ column on my worksheet says _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who cites a number rather than a feeling, and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 1, the four write boxes printed on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 138). Distinguished: name all four before students open the app (one thing learned from the cluster tour and game, one Hat that fits and why, one Hat that does not fit and why, a closing jot on the cluster), give 45 seconds per box with the prompt on screen, and offer two stems: "This Hat fits me because I like _____" and "This Hat is not a fit because it asks me to _____ all day." Read jots over shoulders while students work. If most students name only Hair Stylist, steer the Pathway Possibilities step toward Aesthetician, Recreational Therapist, and Nail Technician so the Day 1 exit ticket is not two versions of the same career.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 2, Activity 2, applying the adhesive base and placing materials. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway twice, one target per lap, lap 1 checking that every student waited the full 30 to 60 seconds so the adhesive is tacky rather than wet, lap 2 checking that pieces overlap and stack instead of sitting in one flat layer, and mark a station checklist as you go. Have the feedback ready before class for the two failures that show up every time: material dropped onto wet glue, and a design built flat with no depth. If more than a handful of students miss the lap 2 target, pause the room and demonstrate stacking on the document camera instead of fixing it station by station.
- **Chunking:** Day 2, Activities 1 through 3, the workbook's three build steps. Distinguished: release Prep, Base, and Design as three separate chunks, say what each one is for before students start it (paint sets the color story, the base sets the tack, the placement sets the depth), and close each chunk with the check already written into the plan: pieces dry to the touch, glue tacky and not wet, first row of material overlapping. Note which chunk stalls the most students. That chunk becomes the 2 minute re-teach before the detail pass in Activity 3, and students who cleared all three start combining two textures early instead of waiting.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 2, Activity 1, materials prep and station setup. Distinguished: post the three blocks before any glue is opened, Voice 1 for partner build talk and Voice 0 during the 30 to 60 second tack wait so students can actually count it out, a visible countdown on screen for the 15 minute prep and the 20 minute build, and a stated rule that painted pieces dry on the labeled plate, tweezers stay on the station mat, and loose seeds and pasta go in the scrap tray. Narrate the stations meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones that are not. If a station is losing build time to hunting for supplies, move that station's tray to the table before the next countdown starts.

## Summative Assessment

**Cosmetology Career Brief:** Students submit a one-page brief that includes (1) the SFX Concept Card plus a photo of the finished design and the final improvement plan, (2) the TDLR Licensing Pathway worksheet, (3) a paragraph defining one entrepreneurial opportunity in the beauty industry and the business skills required, (4) the three Stress Toolkit social media posts, and (5) a paragraph on whether the Irving ISD Cosmetology pathway and its student-run salon interest them. Scored on accuracy of TDLR licensing data (d(2)(A), d(3)(G)), entrepreneurial reasoning (d(3)(I)), and self-reflection on career fit.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- Concept Card with the Character Description line and one Style Guide texture already filled in, so students start at materials and color
- Pre-colored materials (dyed pasta, plain coarse salt) available at a side station for students who need the Day 2 prep time back
- TDLR Licensing Pathway worksheet with 1 row pre-filled
- Pre-built Canva templates for the Stress Toolkit social media posts

### Extensions
- Film the 10-second close-up transformation video the workbook suggests (FYF p. 131), showing the texture bending and moving
- Compare Texas cosmetology licensing requirements to another state (e.g., California, Florida)
- Build a full salon business plan with startup costs, monthly expenses, and 1-year break-even projection
- Research the Ratteree pathway and create a 4-year course plan including required electives
- Rewrite one Stress Toolkit post twice, once as a text to a friend and once as an email to a school counselor, using the district page's professional communication prompt (FYF p. 136)

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Cosmetology = Cosmetología, License = Licencia, Salon = Salón, Apprenticeship = Aprendizaje, Texture = Textura, Layer = Capa, Stress = Estrés
- Bilingual TDLR Licensing Pathway worksheet
- The Style Guide's four textures are picture-friendly, project a photo for each one (snake scales, dried mud, tree bark, crystal cave) so the vocabulary lands visually before students choose
- Visual TDLR navigation guide with screenshots
