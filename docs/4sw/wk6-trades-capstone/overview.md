# Week 6: Skills That Transfer: Evidence, Organizations, and Mid-Year Reflection

**4th Six Weeks** | Cross-Cluster Synthesis | 5 class periods (50 minutes each)

## Lesson Objective

Students use a fictional truck scenario to practice analytical reasoning without pretending to diagnose or repair a real vehicle, compare transferable skills across six careers, distinguish CTSOs and professional associations from credentialing and government organizations, apply work ethic and integrity to documented workplace decisions, and write a private mid-year reflection from fixed evidence.

**Learning Target:** I can separate a clue from a conclusion, prove that a skill transfers across careers, identify what different career organizations actually do, and use evidence to explain how my career thinking has changed.

**Success Criteria:** I will produce a bounded truck-evidence plan, a six-career skill comparison, an organization-type decision, an integrity response, and a four-part private reflection with enough evidence for another reader to follow my reasoning.

## TEKS Alignment

- **d(4)(B):** Identify skills transferable among a variety of careers (Day 1 cross-career analytical-reasoning exit, Day 2 comparisons, and Day 5 reflection).
- **d(3)(F):** Explain the importance of co-curricular and extracurricular activities to career exploration and development (Day 3 CTSO comparison).
- **d(3)(H):** Identify professional associations and the value of membership (Day 3 and the Day 5 reflection).
- **d(4)(F):** Define and identify work ethic, integrity, dedication, and perseverance in the workplace (Day 4 and supporting Day 5 evidence).

Community service may appear as one organization benefit, but this week does not claim primary d(4)(E) mastery. FAA, ASE, SkillsUSA, TSA, NSPE, and AOPA are not interchangeable organization types.

## Assessment and Grading

Days 1-4 are formative. Day 5 is a private recovery or replacement route and remains unpublished, ungraded, and worth zero points until a teacher explicitly assigns it for that purpose. The [4SW assessment map](../../resources/six-weeks-assessment-map.md) already places two majors (Week 1 Blueprint and Week 2 Individual Plan) and three minors (Weeks 3-5), so this reflection does not become an extra required grade.

The reflection rubric scores four 0-4 criteria: change supported by evidence; transferable-skill proof; professional-association reasoning; goals, support, and revision. Score reasoning, not career preference, platform history, public speaking, vehicle knowledge, handwriting, or English mechanics unless meaning is unclear.

## Teacher-Readiness Boundaries

- The truck scenario is fictional. Students identify **possible system concerns** and a trained-inspection route; they do not diagnose a real vehicle, choose repair settings, or tell someone it is safe to drive.
- Use the fixed career and organization cards. Do not require six dense websites, open search, personal memberships, or current chapter access.
- H&L is supplemental in this week. No screenshot of a private Career Plan is required.
- All required directions stay visible in Canvas. Optional vocabulary, models, and source notes may use disclosures.
- Day 1 uses the workbook first. Days 3-4 use Canvas practice Quizzes first. Paper fallbacks are complete access routes, not a default printing list.
- Every fallback gives a separate full-width response area for each multi-sentence job. No 48-cell grid asks students to squeeze examples into tiny boxes.

## Absence and Platform Route

Each paper fallback contains the fixed evidence needed for independent work. Canvas annotation, typed entry, upload, private media, and paper are equal where offered. Missing Week 0 or prior-week artifacts are replaced by a teacher-provided evidence strip; students are never told to reconstruct a private profile from memory or expose a screenshot.
