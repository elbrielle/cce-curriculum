## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: Think of someone who is great at something, an athlete, a musician, a YouTuber, a family member at their job. What do most people see them doing? What do most people NOT see?**

Take 2-3 quick responses. Draw a waterline through one answer: place the visible career result above it and the training, practice, setbacks, and help below it. Students will build their own version today.

## Activity 1: The Career Iceberg (20 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 6-8: "The Career Iceberg"), a Chapter 1 activity

!!! note "This activity lives in Chapter 1, not at the end of the book"
    The workbook opens with the iceberg because first impressions of a job form before any research does. CCE runs it here, at mid-year, when students have 18 weeks of favorites to test it against, and 6SW Wk6 returns to the same pages when the ladder is assembled. Nothing about the activity is a year-end wrap-up.

**Step 1: Look at an example (4 min).** Project page 6 and read the framing: job titles make careers look simple, but underneath the surface workers solve problems, communicate, make decisions, and handle challenges nobody sees from the outside. The book's worked example is an iceberg about winning a trophy in a sports competition. Partners compare what sits on top against what sits underneath and answer the book's question: what does that tell you about winning first place?

**Step 2: Build the career frames (5 min).** Pages 7 and 8 print two career icebergs, an **Electrician** and a **Nurse**, with the drawing frames already there. Do the Nurse frame with the whole class so the research move is modeled once. Above the waterline students write what they *think* the job does. Below it they research and add the deeper parts: skills, responsibilities, tools, training, and challenges. Push past "helps people" to items like anatomy coursework, 12-hour shifts, drawing blood under pressure, and explaining bad news to a stranger's family. The Electrician frame on page 7 is the extension for students who finish early.

**Class Discussion (p. 8), 2 min:** How did your thinking change after researching the career? Which responsibility underneath the iceberg was most surprising? Why is it important to learn more about careers before making decisions about the future?

**Step 3: Build your own iceberg (9 min).** Page 8 closes with an Extra Time prompt to choose a career and build a personal iceberg. That prompt is this week's artifact. Students pick a career from their Day 1 audit, ideally one of their top favorites, and draw it on the blank template (or in a digital canvas if they prefer):

- **Title** at the top of the iceberg
- **At least 3 words or images** on the top (visible) section, what people will see when they reach this career
- **At least 10 words or images** underneath the iceberg, the hidden work, skills, training, and small steps

Students who get stuck should browse the H&L app or flip back through their own workbook pages rather than inventing items. The underwater list should be things they have actually seen, read, or practiced this year.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Students may put only vague, positive ideas underwater, such as "hard work" or "studying." Ask what the work cost: "missed friend's birthday," "got a bad grade and had to retake," or "had to ask for help when I didn't want to." The iceberg should show a specific cost students could not see above the waterline.

## Activity 2: Rung 2 — Career Goal (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 283-284: "Rung 2: Career Goal"), the second rung of the Capstone ladder

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip. Verify Before Class"
    Before class, open H&L in a test or teacher account and confirm the Career Plan tool loads, shows pathway recommendations, and matches your district's configuration. If the tool is unavailable at your district, skip directly to Activity 3 (Pathway Cross-Reference) and use the printed Irving ISD CTE Pathways poster as your single source of truth, with the Rung 2 pages as the student artifact.

Now shift from the personal reflection to the rung. Rung 2 opens by telling students this is a starting point, not a forever decision, which is worth reading aloud to the room.

Students look at their top Hats and **choose one Hat as their career goal**, write the name on page 283, then answer **Why This Career?** in their own words, specifically, not "it pays well."

> [H&L PLATFORM] The rung's Career Snapshot instruction is to open the Hats & Ladders app, click **Career Plan**, and complete the steps before answering the workbook questions. If the Career Plan tool is not yet active for your district, students research the same questions from the Hat pages and BLS and answer them on pages 283-284 on paper.

The Career Snapshot questions on pages 283-284:

1. What does someone in this career do? (a short, plain summary)
2. Where might they work? (office, outdoors, hospital, school, and so on)
3. Do they work mostly with people, ideas, data, or tools?
4. What are the **perks** (things you are excited about) and **quirks** (things you are hesitant about)?
5. **Connection to Me:** how does this career connect to your interests, Powerskills, Building Blocks, and personality types from Rung 1?

Then students take a screenshot of their Career Plan home screen showing recommended pathways and save it to their student drive folder under "4SW Wk1 Career Plan."

**Active Monitoring:** Walk a fixed pathway three times, one target per lap. Lap 1: every student has located the Career Plan. Lap 2: every student has a named Hat on the career goal line. Lap 3: the people-ideas-data-tools question is answered. Have feedback ready for the two predictable stalls, an app that opened on the Profile tab instead of Career Plan, and a named career goal with Why This Career left blank. If more than a handful stall on the same lap, reproject that step to the whole room instead of fixing it screen by screen.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    If the H&L Career Plan tool is not configured for Irving ISD or shows no recommendations, have students manually compare their top 3 favorited Hats to the Irving ISD CTE Pathways poster on the wall. The cross-referencing, not the tool, is the actual learning.

Rung 3 is tomorrow, and Rungs 6 and 7 (FYF pp. 292-296) are next week's course-mapping work.

## Activity 3: Pathway Cross-Reference (7 min)

Students look at the H&L recommended pathways and the Irving ISD CTE Pathways poster side by side. They write on the back of their iceberg sheet:

- **H&L recommended pathway #1:** _____
- **Closest Irving ISD pathway available:** _____
- **Campus where it is offered:** _____ (Irving High, MacArthur, Nimitz, Singley Academy, Cardwell Career Prep, Ratteree Career Development Center, or multi-campus)

If H&L recommends something not offered locally (e.g., Marine Biology), students pick the closest analogue (Veterinary Science at Nimitz).

**DOK 3:** What conclusions can you draw about the overlap between H&L's recommended pathways and the actual pathways available at Irving ISD high schools? What would you do if your top recommendation is not offered locally?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pre-built iceberg template with the underwater section divided into 4 labeled zones. Skills, Tools, Training, Challenges, the same four the workbook names, so students know what to put where. Students who finish the basic 10 items can keep going. For Rung 2, give the five Career Snapshot questions on separate lines with the first one answered as a model.
- **Extension:** Fill the Electrician frame on page 7 as a second iceberg, then compare. What is different underwater between the Electrician and the student's own career? Which iceberg has more overlap with the student's current strengths?
- **ELL:** Visual iceberg template with image search permission. Students can paste images instead of writing words. Pre-teach: Iceberg = Iceberg, Hidden = Oculto, Visible = Visible, Pathway = Trayectoria, Perks = Ventajas, Quirks = Inconvenientes.
