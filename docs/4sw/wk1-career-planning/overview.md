# Week 1: My Career Blueprint — Mid-Year Review

**4th Six Weeks** | Cross-Cluster Planning | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students revisit their core personality type results from Week 0, audit every career they have favorited across the first three six-weeks blocks, work the first three rungs of the *Find Your Future* Capstone ladder (Who You Are, Career Goal, Career Deep Dive), build a career iceberg that surfaces the hidden work behind a visible job title, and select a top Irving ISD pathway for the second half of the year.

**Learning Target:** I can compare my Week 0 core personality type to the careers I have favorited this year, build a career iceberg for a job I am interested in, and pick one Irving ISD pathway with a reason.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my Core Types vs. Favorites worksheet lists my top 3 clusters by favorite count, when my personal career iceberg has at least 3 items on top and at least 10 items underneath, when Rungs 1, 2, and 3 in my workbook name a career goal with the training it needs and a starting salary, and when my Mid-Year Update names one Irving ISD pathway and the campus that offers it.

!!! note "Buffer week: adapt freely for state testing season"
    The original scope-and-sequence intentionally left this week light (Xello completions + eDynamic supplements, no new cluster) because it lands during STAAR / state testing season when periods get pulled, shortened, or canceled with little notice. Rungs 1 through 3 and the pathway selection below are load-bearing for 4SW Wk2 and 6SW Wk6 downstream, but the supporting activities (gallery walk, extended discussion, extension research) are deliberately cut-or-condense candidates when a testing day eats a period. Keep: the career iceberg, Rungs 1-3, and Pathway Ranking. Flex: everything else.

## Career Connection

Students have now spent 18 weeks exploring Manufacturing, IT, Law, Health Science, Agriculture, Hospitality, Human Services, and Business. Every favorite they tagged, every career they researched, every Hat they liked, all of that data lives in their H&L Climber Profile. Rung 1 of the Capstone ladder sends them back into that profile on purpose (FYF pp. 281-282: "Rung 1: Who You Are"), the H&L Career Plan tool pulls it into one personalized roadmap, and students cross-reference the result with the actual Irving ISD CTE pathways available at Irving High, MacArthur, Nimitz, Singley Academy, Cardwell Career Preparatory Center, and Ratteree Career Development Center.

**Irving ISD Pathways:** All pathways across the district. Students narrow to their top 1-2 pathways this week and commit to one in Week 2.

## Vocabulary

- **Core personality types:** The six Hats & Ladders types that connect what a person likes doing to the careers that fit: Doer, Analyzer, Creator, Helper, Persuader, Organizer. Students met these in Week 0 through the app task Discover Your Core.
- **Career Plan:** In H&L, the personalized roadmap that pulls core personality types, Work Values, Building Blocks, favorited Hats, and pathway recommendations into one place. Rung 2 sends students into it directly (FYF p. 283).
- **Career iceberg:** Visible career outcomes (the tip) sit on top of invisible work: skills practiced, tools learned, training completed, challenges handled.
- **Learning agility:** Being flexible and ready to adjust when a plan does not work, then trying new ideas instead of repeating the same steps (FYF p. 140).
- **Pathway Selection:** Choosing a sequence of HS courses and experiences that aligns with a career interest.
- **Endorsement:** In Texas, one of five HS specialization tracks (STEM, Business, Arts, Public Services, Multidisciplinary).

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook concentrates its planning work in the Capstone chapter (printed pp. 277-300), an eight-rung career ladder that students assemble across the rest of the year and present in 6SW. This week climbs the first three rungs:

- **Rung 1: Who You Are** (FYF pp. 281-282): Students open the Hats & Ladders app and review their own profile, their top Hats, Pathway Possibilities, Building Blocks, Powerskills, and favorite pathways and clusters. The rung lists six inputs to reflect on (interests, Building Blocks, personality types, work values, strengths, and skills), gives four starter questions, models the difference between a vague answer and a specific one, and closes with a Partner Activity where a partner adds one strength the student missed.
- **Rung 2: Career Goal** (FYF pp. 283-284): Students choose one top Hat as a career goal, explain why, then complete the app's Career Plan steps and answer a Career Snapshot: what does someone in this career do, where might they work, do they work mostly with people, ideas, data, or tools, and what are the perks and quirks of the job. A Connection to Me section ties the answer back to Rung 1.
- **Rung 3: Career Deep Dive** (FYF pp. 285-286): Education or training required as a checkbox list, special certifications or licenses, how long preparation takes, estimated starting salary against the national and local average, whether the field is growing, stable, or declining, and a three-part Skills Check (skills the career needs, skills I already have, skills I will need to build).

Two Chapter 1 sections carry the rest of the week. **"The Career Iceberg"** (FYF pp. 6-8) opens the book rather than closing it. Step 1 gives a worked example, an iceberg about winning a trophy in a sports competition, that partners compare top against underneath. Step 2 prints two career frames, Electrician (p. 7) and Nurse (p. 8), where students write what they think the job does above the waterline and research the deeper skills, responsibilities, tools, training, and challenges below it. A Class Discussion and an Extra Time prompt to build an iceberg for a career of the student's own choosing close the section. **"Being a Career Thinker and Doer"** (FYF p. 22) defines career thinkers as people who stay curious and take action, and asks one Discuss question about the gap between thinking about a goal and moving toward it. The same page carries **"Building a Career Community,"** a three-person support-system list that pairs well with the Day 5 reflection.

The H&L app's **Career Plan** tool reads from the student's Climber Profile and generates a personalized pathway recommendation. The **Coach Dashboard** lets the teacher see every student's plan and message students directly. **Rungs 6 and 7** (FYF pp. 292-296) are next week's work, and the full ladder, its 32-point rubric, and the final presentation land in 6SW.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 3, the growth discussion where partners compare their Week 0 core personality type against their top cluster by favorite count. Distinguished: hold a silent 30 second Think on the question "do these two agree," then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "My type is _____ and my top cluster is _____" and "My favorites moved toward _____, which tells me my _____ side got stronger this year." Take three share-outs, not one, and make sure at least one is a student whose data did not match. Listen while pairs talk for who can name a cause for the gap, and pick your share-out students from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 1, Activity 2, the favorites audit through the eight explored clusters. Distinguished: name the stop points before students open the Climber Profile, pause the tally every three minutes for a 45 second jot with the prompt on screen, and offer two stems: "The cluster with the most hearts is _____, which surprises me because _____" and "A cluster I expected to like more is _____." Read jots over shoulders during the pauses. If most jots name the same one or two clusters, steer the Rung 1 reflection that follows toward Building Blocks and Powerskills rather than cluster names so students have something besides a cluster label to say about themselves.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 2, Activity 2, the Rung 2 Career Snapshot and Career Plan navigation. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times with one target per lap, carrying the checklist already built for this week (the student located the Career Plan, named one Hat as the career goal, and answered the people-ideas-data-tools question). Have feedback ready before class for the two predictable stalls: a student who cannot find the Career Plan because the app opened on the Profile tab, and a student who names a career goal but leaves Why This Career blank. If more than a handful of students stall on the same lap, pivot and reproject that step to the whole room instead of fixing it screen by screen.
- **Chunking:** Day 2, Activity 1, the career iceberg in three steps. Distinguished: run the workbook's Step 1 trophy example as the first chunk and close it with a thumbs check that every pair can say one thing that lives underneath, then the Electrician and Nurse frames as the second chunk with the stems "On top of my iceberg, people see _____" and "Underneath my iceberg, the hidden work is _____" posted, then release the personal iceberg only after those stems have been used out loud. Note which chunk produces the thinnest underwater lists. If the Nurse frame stays shallow, model two more underwater items on the projector before students start their own iceberg, and let students who filled both frames deeply start their personal iceberg early.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 5, Activity 3, the pathway dot-vote gallery walk. Distinguished: post the three blocks before any student leaves a seat, Voice 1 while students walk and place dots, Voice 0 while the class reads the finished distribution, and Voice 2 for the discussion that follows. Run a visible 8 minute countdown for the walk itself, state that students move one direction around the room so posters do not bottleneck, and name where the leftover dots go when a student uses fewer than three. Narrate the students who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If a poster is drawing a crowd, redirect the next group to an empty poster and add that observation to the class-data discussion.

## Summative Assessment

**Mid-Year Reflection (Day 5):** Students submit their updated My Career Journey reflection sheet showing growth from Week 0 to mid-year. The reflection must include: (1) core personality type comparison (Week 0 vs. now), (2) top 3 favorited careers with rationale, (3) selected Irving ISD pathway with the campus where it is offered, (4) one specific insight from the career iceberg about the hidden work behind their goal. Scored on evidence of self-awareness growth (d(1)(A)), pathway selection with rationale (d(8)(A)), and connection between assessment data and career choice (d(8)(B)).

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- Pre-printed sentence stems for the mid-year reflection: "My core personality type is _____. The clusters I explored most are _____. My top pathway is _____ because _____."
- Simplified Career Plan navigation guide with screenshots
- Pre-built iceberg template with the underwater section divided into labeled zones (Skills, Tools, Training, Challenges) so students know what to put where
- Allow students to use Irving ISD pathways poster as a visual reference during pathway selection
- Reduced Real Game decision log: track 3 decisions instead of all 8

### Extensions
- Compare H&L Career Plan recommendations to MyNextMove Interest Profiler results. Do they agree?
- Build a 4-year HS course sketch using Texas OnCourse for the selected pathway (this previews Wk 2)
- Research dual-credit options at the chosen Irving ISD high school
- Run the "Powerskill: Learning Agility" zoo activity (FYF pp. 140-141) as a stand-alone flex block if a testing day frees a period. Students critique a reptile-exhibit opening plan that keeps drawing low turnout, brainstorm replacement steps on sticky notes while keeping what still works, and sketch a poster, display idea, or activity station for the new plan. It is the same move students make on Day 5 when their own plan changes.

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Career Plan = Plan de carrera, Iceberg = Iceberg, Pathway = Trayectoria profesional, Endorsement = Especialización
- Bilingual mid-year reflection template with Spanish sentence stems
- The career iceberg is highly visual. Students can draw or paste images instead of writing
- Pair ESL students with bilingual peers during the Gallery Walk discussion
- Xello Quick Sims include visual decision interfaces accessible across language levels
- The workbook reads well above grade level, so read the Rung 1 and Rung 2 prompt paragraphs aloud and encourage browser translation for the Career Snapshot research
