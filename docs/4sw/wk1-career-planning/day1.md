## Warm-Up (5 min)

!!! note "Before the Warm-Up. Return Week 0 Reflections"
    Have students pull out their **Week 0 My Career Journey reflection** from the class folder you saved them in per the 1SW Wk0 Day 5 Persistent Portfolio admonition. If a student was absent during Week 0 or is new to the class this semester, have them run the H&L **Discover Your Core** task today as their personal baseline, treat this week as their Wk0 equivalent. Also have every student open their Climber Profile in H&L now (before the warm-up prompt) to surface any login or data-access issues before the activity begins.

**WARM-UP: Look back at your Week 0 My Career Journey sheet (or pull up your Climber Profile in H&L). Do you think you would get the same core personality type if you took Discover Your Core again today? Why or why not?**

Collect 2-3 student responses. If students have gone hazy on the six types, reproject the type table from the Climber Notes deck (Climber Notes: "Learning Your Core Personality Types", slide 3) for 30 seconds rather than re-teaching it. Bridge to the idea that career exploration is supposed to change you. The point of the first 18 weeks was not to lock students into a career, it was to give them enough data to make a real choice.

## Activity 1: Rung 1 — Who You Are (12 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 281-282: "Rung 1: Who You Are"), the first rung of the Capstone ladder

Open the workbook to page 281 and read the framing together. Rung 1 is the foundation of the eight-rung career ladder students will finish and present at the end of the year, and it is entirely about the student.

> [H&L PLATFORM] The rung's own instruction is to open the Hats & Ladders app and review the profile: **top Hats, Pathway Possibilities, Building Blocks, Powerskills, and favorite pathways and clusters**. Students have been feeding that profile since Week 0, so this is a review pass, not a new assessment.

Students then write or sketch on page 282, reflecting on the six inputs the rung names: interests, Building Blocks, personality types, work values, strengths, and skills. The workbook supplies four starter questions:

1. What do I like to do in my free time?
2. What school subjects do I enjoy most?
3. What are some things I feel confident doing?
4. What do people say I am good at?

The rung also models the specificity bar with its own example: "I like video games" is too thin, while "I like playing strategy video games because I enjoy challenges, solving problems, and thinking ahead" names the skill underneath the interest. Project that pair before students start writing. It is the difference between a Rung 1 that carries the rest of the ladder and one that has to be redone in 6SW.

**Partner Activity (p. 282):** In the last 3 minutes, students share their page with a partner and the partner adds **one strength or skill the writer did not think of**. The addition goes on the writer's page, in the partner's handwriting, so it stays visible as outside evidence.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Watch for students who write nothing because "I'm not good at anything." That is usually self-doubt rather than reality. Point them at one thing they did well earlier this year, name the skill it took, and have them write that line first. The Partner Activity is the second safety net; make sure those students get a partner who will actually name something.

## Activity 2: Favorites Audit (20 min)

**Source:** H&L Climber Profile + scope-and-sequence Ch 1-13 history

Students open the H&L app and navigate to their **Climber Profile**. They scroll to the favorited Hats section and count how many careers they have favorited in each of the eight clusters explored so far:

1. Manufacturing (1SW Wk1)
2. IT (1SW Wk2-5)
3. Law & Public Service (2SW Wk1-2)
4. Health Science (2SW Wk3-4, Wk6)
5. Agriculture (3SW Wk1-3)
6. Hospitality (3SW Wk4)
7. Human Services (3SW Wk5)
8. Business (3SW Wk6)

> [H&L PLATFORM] In H&L, students go to **Climber Profile → Favorited Hats** to see every career they have liked across all clusters. If the app sorts by date rather than cluster, students will need to manually tally. That tally itself is the activity. The core personality type they are comparing against comes from the **Discover Your Core** task in the Profile section, which students completed in Week 0.

**Stop and Jot:** Name the stop points before students start tallying. Every three minutes, pause the room for a 45 second jot using one of two stems: "The cluster with the most hearts is _____, which surprises me because _____" or "A cluster I expected to like more is _____."

On the printed Core Types vs. Favorites worksheet, students fill in:
- **Column A, core personality type from Week 0** (Doer, Analyzer, Creator, Helper, Persuader, or Organizer)
- **Column B, Number of favorites in each cluster** (8 rows)
- **Column C, The 3 clusters with the most favorites**, ranked
- **Column D, Does my Week 0 core type match my current top cluster?** (Yes / No / Sort of)

Students who have not favorited many careers spend the first 5 minutes browsing their Climber Profile history and adding 3-5 favorites they remember liking but never tagged.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Some students will have zero favorites, usually because they forgot to tap the heart icon during earlier weeks. Let them go back through clusters now and add favorites based on memory. The point of the audit is the reflection, not the historical accuracy of when the favorite was added.

## Activity 3: Think-Pair-Share — Growth Discussion (10 min)

In pairs, students share:

1. Their core personality type from Week 0
2. Their top cluster by favorite count today
3. Whether the two match, and if not, what explains the gap

Hold a silent 30 second Think on question 3 first, then time Partner A and Partner B for 30 seconds each before opening the rest of the pair talk. After 4 minutes of partner talk, collect 3 responses from across the room. Look for both "my type matches perfectly" and "my favorites went a totally different direction" stories, both are valid mid-year outcomes, and make sure at least one of the three is a student whose data did not match.

**DOK 2:** How would you explain the difference between "interests" and "favorites" using your data from today? Which one do you trust more, and why?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** For students who cannot find their Week 0 results, project the H&L **Profile → Discover Your Core** navigation steps. If they still cannot find it, give them the option to run the task today and treat that as their baseline. For Rung 1, give the four starter questions on a half sheet so students answer one box at a time instead of facing a blank page.
- **Extension:** Compare core personality type to favorited Hats by counting how many of the favorites match the recommended Hats for that type in H&L. Calculate a "match percentage."
- **ELL:** Bilingual core personality type card (Doer = Hacedor, Analyzer = Analizador, Creator = Creador, Helper = Ayudante, Persuader = Persuasor, Organizer = Organizador). Rung 1 accepts sketches as well as writing, so students can draw their answers. Allow students to write the growth statement in Spanish.
