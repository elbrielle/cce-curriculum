# Week 5: Automotive Evidence and Training Routes

**4th Six Weeks** | Transportation, Distribution & Logistics | 5 class periods (50 minutes each)

## Learning target

I can use visible evidence to describe a fictional collision problem, compare three automotive occupations on one data basis, and evaluate training routes without confusing an entry-level certificate, professional ASE certification, college, and registered apprenticeship.

## Success criteria

Students can:

- record visible collision evidence without claiming a complete safety diagnosis;
- connect three automotive occupations to daily work and common preparation;
- compare salaries for three careers using May 2024 U.S. national medians;
- explain the difference between ASE Entry-Level credentials and professional ASE certification;
- compare a public technical-college example, employer training, and registered apprenticeship using current source labels; and
- write a private evidence brief with one limitation and one verification question.

## Governing evidence and claim boundaries

- **Automotive Service Technicians and Mechanics:** May 2024 U.S. national median $49,670; 4% projected growth, 2024-34; about 70,000 annual openings. Typical preparation varies: many complete a postsecondary nondegree program, while some enter with a high school diploma and learn on the job. [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/automotive-service-technicians-and-mechanics.htm)
- **Diesel Service Technicians and Mechanics:** May 2024 U.S. national median $60,640; 2% projected growth; about 26,500 annual openings. High school plus on-the-job learning is common; employers may prefer postsecondary diesel training. [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/diesel-service-technicians-and-mechanics.htm)
- **Automotive Body and Related Repairers:** May 2024 U.S. national median $51,680; 2% projected growth for automotive body and glass repairers; about 16,000 annual openings for the combined group. [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/automotive-body-and-glass-repairers.htm)
- **ASE:** Entry-Level certification has no work-experience requirement. Professional ASE certification requires the relevant test and approved hands-on work experience or a qualifying training substitution. Do not tell students that passing one school test makes them a professional ASE-certified technician. [ASE Entry-Level](https://www.ase.com/entry-level/) · [ASE work experience](https://ase.com/tests/work-experience/)
- **Registered Apprenticeship:** A registered program is a paid job with structured on-the-job learning, related instruction, mentorship, and progressive wages. Availability and entry requirements depend on the sponsoring employer or program. Do not present every dealership job as a registered apprenticeship. [Texas Workforce Commission](https://www.twc.texas.gov/services/job-training/find-apprenticeships)
- **Tarrant County College example:** The published 2026-27 in-county tuition and fee estimates range from $888 for the 12-credit General Service Technician award to $4,440 for the 60-credit Automotive Service Technology AAS, excluding books and materials. Students must retain the year, residency assumption, and exclusions. [TCC Automotive Service Technology](https://www.tccd.edu/academics/courses-and-programs/programs-a-z/credit/automotive-automotive-service-technology/)
- **Irving program context:** FYF pp. 168-169 are the district-customized HQIM source for **Automotive Technology**, **Collision Repair**, and **Diesel & Heavy Equipment Technology**. The current Irving High School CTE page confirms the broader **Automotive, Collision Repair and Diesel** offering at Cardwell, Irving, MacArthur, and Nimitz. Use the workbook names with students; use the current coursebook, counselor, or CTE program to verify enrollment logistics the workbook does not state.
