# Week 5: Under the Hood — Automotive Careers

**4th Six Weeks** | Transportation, Distribution & Logistics Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

## Lesson Objective

Students explore the Automotive & Collision Repair pathway through the Hats & Ladders app and the *Find Your Future* workbook, complete the "Crash Crew" activity by inspecting a damaged vehicle, writing a Collision Report, and building a step-by-step repair plan, research ASE certification and apprenticeship pathways, compare salaries across automotive careers in the DFW area, and examine how the Ratteree pathway lets students start automotive training in high school.

**Learning Target:** I can name three automotive careers and the training each one needs, write a repair plan for a damaged car, and compare what automotive careers pay to a career from another cluster.

**Success Criteria:** I will know I am successful when my Collision Report lists at least three damaged parts and a probable cause, when my repair plan gives a repair-or-replace call and a reason for every part, when my salary worksheet shows DFW pay, education time, and education cost for three automotive careers, and when my Ratteree vs. trade school notes show the time and the cost of both options.

## Demonstration of Learning

> "I can describe at least three automotive careers with their certification requirements, inspect a damaged vehicle and write a collision repair plan, explain the apprenticeship and ASE certification model, and compare salaries between automotive careers and a career from another cluster."

## Career Connection

The automotive industry is one of the largest employers in America, and the DFW metroplex is a major hub for dealerships, fleet maintenance, collision centers, and diesel repair. What makes automotive careers different from most others students have explored is the **apprenticeship model:** many technicians enter the field through paid on-the-job training combined with technical school, earning money while they learn. ASE (Automotive Service Excellence) certification is the industry standard, and Irving ISD's **Ratteree Career Development Center** offers the Automotive pathway for students to begin ASE preparation during high school.

**What is Happening at Irving ISD?** **Automotive** is offered at **Ratteree Career Development Center** as a travel-to career-center pathway. Students from any home high school can attend Ratteree for part of their day and prepare for **Automotive Service Excellence (ASE)** certification. The workbook's district pages add the local detail students respond to most (FYF pp. 168-169: "What is Happening at My District?"): **Automotive Enterprise Days**, community service events where students in the Automotive Technology and Collision Repair programs provide oil changes and tire rotations to the public at heavily discounted rates; three Transportation programs of study, Automotive Technology, Collision Repair, and Diesel & Heavy Equipment Technology; the ASE industry certification; and SkillsUSA as the student organization. The workbook does not name a host campus for each program, so confirm placement with the district CTE coordinator before telling students where to enroll.

## Vocabulary

- **ASE (Automotive Service Excellence):** The nationally recognized certification for automotive professionals. Tests cover engine repair, brakes, electrical, HVAC, and many more specialties.
- **Apprenticeship:** A training model where a worker learns a trade by working alongside experienced professionals while also taking classroom instruction. Usually paid.
- **Diesel Technician:** A professional who maintains and repairs diesel engines used in trucks, buses, construction equipment, and generators.
- **Collision Repair:** Restoring vehicles damaged in accidents, bodywork, painting, frame straightening.
- **Trade School:** A postsecondary institution focused on hands-on technical training, usually 6 months to 2 years.
- **Collision Report:** The written record a technician fills out after inspecting a damaged vehicle: what the vehicle is, where the damage is, which parts are damaged, and what probably caused it. It is the Day 1 deliverable.
- **Repair or Replace:** The call a collision technician makes on every damaged part, based on how bad the damage is, what the part costs, and whether a repaired part will still be safe.

## Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

The *Find Your Future* workbook gives Transportation, Distribution, and Logistics printed pages 149-170. The chapter opener (FYF p. 149: "Exploring the World of TRANSPORTATION, DISTRIBUTION, AND LOGISTICS") frames the cluster as moving people and products from place to place efficiently and safely, and puts three Hats on the page: Automotive Engineering Technician, Autobody Technician, and Heavy Truck Driver. Students meet the cluster's pathways in the Hats & Ladders app through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 170: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's own programs of study on the district pages (FYF pp. 168-169).

One chapter activity carries Day 1:

- **Crash Crew** (Day 1): Career Climb activity. Students are the collision repair technician at a shop where a damaged vehicle has arrived. They study the damage images without writing anything, complete a Collision Report (vehicle description, area of damage, at least 3 damaged parts, visible damage description, probable cause), then work with a partner on a repair plan that makes a repair-or-replace call for every part, sequences the work, and estimates how long the job takes (FYF pp. 150-152: "Crash Crew").

A second chapter activity, **Safety Squad** (FYF pp. 156-159), puts pairs in the role of an automotive safety engineer investigating two crashed vehicles for a pattern of airbag failures. Students review crash evidence and mechanic field reports, complete a Safety Investigation Report, classify the problem as cosmetic, mechanical, or safety-critical, make and justify a recall decision, and present to another pair. It runs across roughly two class periods and needs the teacher's Climber Notes deck for the crash images (Climber Notes: "Safety Squad", slides 2-5). It is offered as this week's enrichment block rather than a scheduled day, because Days 3 and 4 carry the salary comparison and pathway research the week is built on.

The chapter's remaining activities belong to neighboring weeks: aircraft maintenance and the transportation survey project run in Wk 3, and the Analytical Reasoning Powerskill lesson (printed pp. 153-155) runs in Wk 6.

## IISD Instructional Strategies

- **Think-Pair-Share:** Day 1, Activity 2, the Crash Crew Class Discussion (FYF p. 152). Distinguished: take the last two prompts to pairs, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "An older car changes the plan because _____" and "A collision repair technician needs _____ most because _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who names a Powerskill beyond "being careful," and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
- **Stop and Jot:** Day 2, Warm-Up, the apprenticeship-versus-college choice students write before any data is on the board. Distinguished: give a 60 second time box with the prompt on screen and offer two stems, "I would rather _____ because _____" and "The risk of my choice is _____." Read jots over shoulders and count how many students name a risk at all. If almost none do, front-load the Net Financial Position row when the comparison chart goes up so the tradeoff lands before students commit to a side.
- **Active Monitoring:** Day 3, Activity 1, the three-career salary worksheet. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times, one lap per career row, each lap checking a single target (at least 2 of the 3 sources used for that career, and each number written in the column it belongs in). Post the comparison stem so students have the language ready at the ratio row: "An Auto Technician with ASE certification earns about $_____ per year. Compared to _____, that is higher or lower by $_____. The education takes _____ years and costs about $_____." Have feedback ready before class for the two predictable errors: a national BLS median copied into the DFW column, and an hourly wage recorded as if it were annual. If the hourly-versus-annual error shows up on more than a handful of sheets, pause and convert one wage on the board for the whole room instead of fixing it desk by desk.
- **Chunking:** Day 1, Activity 1, the two Crash Crew steps. Distinguished: run Step 1 as look-only with nothing written, close it with a verbal check that every student can name one damaged area out loud, and only then release Step 2 and the Collision Report boxes. Note which students started writing during Step 1, because those are the students who record the first dent and miss the rest of the car, and give them a second look at the projected image before they finish the report. The same two-chunk shape runs on Day 4, one column of the comparison template per chunk, with the pathway timeline graphic comparing free in-district training to paid post-graduation tuition as the exemplar students fill against.
- **Time, Voice, Body (TVB):** Day 5, Activity 2, the cross-cluster presentations. Distinguished: post the three blocks before the first presenter, Voice 0 while the note card is handed off, Voice 3 for the presenter, Voice 1 for the listening-grid partner check, and run a visible countdown for every presenter at whichever time cap the class picked. Define where presenters stand and where finished note cards go so transitions do not eat presentation time, and narrate the students who are already meeting the expectation. If the rotation is running long by the fifth presenter, announce the tighter cap before the next presenter begins so nobody is cut short without warning.

## Differentiation

### Scaffolded Learning
- Pre-filled salary data for one of the three automotive careers on the comparison worksheet
- Simplified apprenticeship vs. college chart with visual timeline graphics already drawn
- Allow students to use CareerOneStop's Compare Occupations tool for side-by-side data instead of manual research
- Pre-filled Collision Report with the Vehicle Description box and one damaged part already entered, plus a labeled car diagram (hood, fender, bumper cover, headlight assembly, quarter panel) so students can name parts without hunting for vocabulary

### Extensions
- Run the **Safety Squad** investigation (FYF pp. 156-159) as an enrichment block for students who finish early or as a two-period extension for the whole class. Pairs review the crash images and mechanic field reports from the Climber Notes deck, complete the Safety Investigation Report, classify the airbag problem as cosmetic, mechanical, or safety-critical, decide whether the vehicle model should be recalled, and present the decision to another pair.
- Build a detailed cost analysis comparing the free Ratteree pathway to attending UTI after graduation (~$30-50K tuition)
- Research the transition from gas vehicles to electric vehicles and how it is creating new automotive careers (EV technician, battery diagnostics)
- Interview a local automotive professional in person or via video about their career path and bring back 3 quotes for the Day 5 presentation

### ELL Language Support
- Pre-teach: Apprenticeship = Aprendizaje, Certification = Certificación, Mechanic = Mecánico, Collision Repair = Reparación de colisiones, Trade School = Escuela técnica
- Bilingual salary comparison worksheet with Spanish headers and sentence stems
- CareerOneStop has Spanish-language career videos for many automotive careers, confirm and assign as needed
- Visual timeline comparing apprenticeship vs. college pathways is accessible across language levels
