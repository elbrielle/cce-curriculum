## Warm-Up (5 min)

**WARM-UP: How many people do you think are involved in getting a single airplane from Dallas to New York? Take a guess. Be specific, count pilots, mechanics, baggage, fuel, security, food prep, and dispatchers.**

Take 4-5 student guesses. Most students underestimate dramatically. Reveal: a typical commercial flight involves 100+ people behind the scenes, 2 pilots, 4-6 flight attendants, mechanics, fuelers, baggage handlers, gate agents, dispatchers, ATC, weather forecasters, and maintenance crew. Bridge: this entire week is about the people you do not see who keep transportation running.

## Activity 1: Transportation Cluster Tour (15 min)

**Source:** (FYF p. 149: "Exploring the World of TRANSPORTATION, DISTRIBUTION, AND LOGISTICS") + (FYF p. 170: "App Exploration")

Open the workbook to page 149 and read the chapter opener together. This cluster is about moving people and products from one place to another efficiently and safely. Some roles plan the most efficient routes. Others make sure the right product reaches the right place at exactly the right time. Three Hats sit on the opener page: Automotive Engineering Technician, Autobody Technician, and Heavy Truck Driver. Read the "Be the Decision Maker" dilemma next: a major retailer needs shipments delivered across the country within 48 hours, so do you split the shipment across several transportation methods to lower the risk, or choose one method and streamline it? Take two or three responses before students open their laptops.

> [H&L PLATFORM] Students start the App Exploration page (FYF p. 170). Open the Hats & Ladders app, go to **Clusters**, and click the **Transportation, Distribution, and Logistics Cluster**. Students watch the "Cluster Tour" video and answer the page's first question, what is one thing you learned. Students use **Stop and Jot** during the cluster tour video, pause twice to write one career that surprised them and one question they have.

The chapter does not print a pathway list. Students meet the cluster's pathways in the app by clicking **Pathway Possibilities** on the App Exploration page. Irving ISD's own programs of study in this cluster are named on the district pages (FYF pp. 168-169): Automotive Technology, Collision Repair, Diesel and Heavy Equipment Technology, and Aviation Maintenance. Aviation lives inside this cluster, and it is where the rest of the week goes.

After the cluster tour, students browse Hats briefly to see which Transportation Hats are available. They research an aviation Hat on Day 2 and finish the App Exploration page on Day 5.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    Some students will get stuck scrolling endlessly through Hats. Set a 5-minute timer for the browse and stop the room when it goes off. The cluster tour is the warm-up, not the main event today.

## Activity 2: Transportation Survey Project (25 min)

**Source:** (FYF pp. 166-167: "Transportation Survey Project"), a Career Climb activity

Open by reading the workbook framing aloud (paraphrased): students are the new Transportation Needs Analyst for their local community. People at recent town meetings have complained about public transportation. It is unreliable, expensive, or unavailable in some areas. Before recommending changes, the student needs a community survey to figure out what is really going on.

!!! warning "Print errata on page 166"
    Step 2 is printed above Step 1 on page 166. The real order is Step 1 discuss the challenges, then Step 2 create the survey. Say so before students open the page, or groups start writing questions before they have talked about the problem.

**Step 1: Discuss Transportation Challenges (5 min)**

Students partner up or join a small group of 3-4 and use sticky notes to jot ideas against the workbook's prompts (p. 166):
- What transportation challenges might people face in our community (Irving)?
- Do people have a hard time getting to work or school?
- Is public transportation available? How reliable is it?
- What types of transportation could help people (DART buses, vans, bikes, rideshares)?

**Think-Pair-Share:** 30 seconds of silent Think on the first prompt, then Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, then the group pools its sticky notes into 5-6 challenges it expects to find in Irving. Two pairs share out.

**Step 2: Create the Transportation Survey (15 min)**

The workbook (p. 166) has small groups develop the survey with a digital tool. Groups draft together, then each student records their own 10 questions on the printed Transportation Survey template so every student has an artifact. The workbook requires:
- **7 multiple-choice questions**
- **3 short-answer questions**

Examples to project on the board:

- Multiple choice: "How do you usually get to work or school? (a) Drive my own car (b) Get a ride (c) DART bus (d) Walk or bike"
- Multiple choice: "How often do you have trouble getting where you need to go? (a) Daily (b) Weekly (c) Rarely (d) Never"
- Short answer: "What is one thing you would change about transportation in our community?"

Students who finish early start the workbook's Step 4, Build a Campaign (p. 167). They pick one way to recruit people to take the survey and sketch it: a social media post, a television or radio announcement, a printed flyer, or a page on the city's website.

**Step 3: Develop an Incentive (5 min)**

The workbook (p. 167) defines an incentive as something that motivates people to do something, like a small prize or a raffle entry, and explains that incentives raise the number of people who finish a survey. Examples: $5 gift card raffle, free movie ticket, donut from a local shop, free DART day pass. Students write their incentive at the bottom of the survey template and explain in one sentence why it would work.

!!! tip "Facilitation Tip"
    The incentive step is fast but important. It teaches students that surveys do not just appear in mailboxes. Real research requires thinking about how to motivate participation.

**DOK 2:** How would you compare the kinds of information you get from multiple-choice questions versus short-answer questions? Why does a real survey need both?

## Activity 3: Class Discussion (3 min)

The workbook (p. 167) closes the activity with a Class Discussion. In their original groups of 3-4, students answer:
- What kinds of problems do you think people might report if they took your survey?
- Which survey question do you think is the most important, and why?
- How could a city use the answers from your survey to make better transportation plans?
- What core personality types would be most likely to enjoy this type of work?

Take 1-2 group responses to share with the class.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pre-printed survey template with 4 of the 10 questions already drafted as examples. Students adapt the examples and write 6 of their own. Pair with a peer for the group brainstorm.
- **Extension:** Build the survey in Google Forms instead of paper. Email the link to family members and bring back 3 real responses to Day 2 for analysis.
- **ELL:** Bilingual survey template with Spanish question stems. Pre-teach: Survey = Encuesta, Transportation = Transporte, Community = Comunidad, Incentive = Incentivo, Question = Pregunta.
