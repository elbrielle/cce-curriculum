    Week 2: Building Strong — Civil Engineering Careers

    5th Six Weeks | Architecture & Construction / STEM Cluster | 5 class periods (50 min each)

    Lesson Objective

    Students explore civil and structural engineering careers through the Hats & Ladders app and the Find Your Future workbook, practice systems thinking on a kitchen layout where one choice changes the whole room, investigate emerging occupations in green building and smart infrastructure, research the impact of standardized tests (PSAT/SAT/ACT) on engineering college admissions, and apply the Engineering Design Process to a weight-bearing bridge challenge and to a Mars Rover design brief.

    Learning Target: I can name at least three civil engineering careers and the training each one needs, explain how PSAT, SAT, and ACT scores affect getting into an engineering program, and design, build, and test a bridge that holds weight across a 12-inch gap.

    Success Criteria: I will know I am successful when my research worksheet lists the job, the training, the pay, and the tools for one engineering career, when my cabinet layout shows one placement I changed because it affected something else in the room, when my test worksheet names the one test that matters most for my pathway and why, when my bridge sketch labels at least three structural elements, and when my bridge holds weight in the class test and my redesign statement names what failed first.

    Demonstration of Learning

    "I can describe at least three civil engineering careers, explain how PSAT/SAT/ACT scores affect college admission for engineering programs, and apply engineering design principles to build a weight-bearing bridge."

    TEKS Alignment

    • d(1)(B): Explore and describe the CTE career clusters (Day 1).
    • d(1)(C): Identify career opportunities within the Engineering pathway (Days 1, 3, 4, 5).
    • d(1)(D): Research and evaluate emerging occupations (green building, smart infrastructure) (Day 2).
    • d(2)(A): Research and describe academic and training requirements for engineering careers (Day 1 career research worksheet).
    • d(3)(E): Explain the impact of standardized testing (PSAT/SAT/ACT/ASVAB/TSI) on postsecondary opportunities (Day 2).

    Materials Needed

    • Chromebooks with internet access (1 per student)
    • Hats & Ladders student accounts + Find Your Future workbook, Engineering chapter (printed pp. 103-110) and the Powerskill: Systems Thinking pages (printed pp. 174-175)
    • Bridge-building materials: plastic drinking straws (40 per team), masking tape, index cards, scissors, small weights (pennies, washers, or a digital scale and textbooks)
    • 12-inch gap station (two desks or books spaced apart)
    • Chart paper or sticky notes for the Day 5 rover brainstorm (FYF p. 106: "Mission to Mars")
    • PBS Design Squad: pbskids.org/designsquad
    • BLS, Civil Engineers: bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/civil-engineers.htm
    • TxDOT projects and studies, Dallas district: txdot.gov/projects/projects-studies.html
    • College Board PSAT/SAT: collegeboard.org
    • Printed Engineering career research worksheet (CCE artifact; the research format is taught in Wk0)
    • Printed PSAT/SAT/ACT Impact worksheet
    • Printed Emerging Engineering Careers research template
    • Printed Bridge Design sketch page

    Career Connection

    Civil engineers design and maintain roads, bridges, water systems, airports, and buildings. DFW population growth keeps that work visible: students can look at TxDOT's interchange and frontage-road projects, the Trinity River flood-control system, and the DART Silver Line corridor through Irving. Because the workbook prints no civil engineering activity, this week is CCE-original and uses the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook and TxDOT's Dallas district project and study listings.

    This week also introduces standardized testing. Four-year engineering programs may use PSAT/SAT/ACT scores for admission and scholarships, and current 7th graders will take the PSAT 8/9 in 9th grade. The workbook's Engineering district pages place that planning inside Post-Secondary Options: four-year colleges, community colleges, technical and trade schools, apprenticeships, military service, training programs, and going straight to work (FYF pp. 108-109: "What is Happening at My District?").

    What is Happening at Irving ISD? The same district pages name Autodesk Fusion 360, Autodesk CAD Inventor, and FAA Part 107 as the cluster's industry-based certifications, and SkillsUSA, VEX Robotics, and the Technology Student Association as its student organizations. They also carry a Work-Based Learning box and one Irving story worth reading aloud: Irving ISD Engineering students have designed and built prosthetic limbs so classmates could play in middle school band and orchestra. The Irving ISD CTE offerings page lists Engineering as one of four pathways in MacArthur High School's School of Architecture, Construction and Civil Engineering (ACE), alongside Architecture, Construction, and Welding.

    [VERIFY with CTE coordinator] Irving ISD's program certifications poster labels the MacArthur ACE engineering pathway Civil Engineering and pairs it with Autodesk Fusion 360, while the MacArthur CTE page lists the pathway as Engineering and names no certification. The workbook lists Fusion 360 for the cluster but attaches it to no campus. Confirm the current pathway name and the certification it leads to before telling a student where to enroll.

    Vocabulary

    • Civil Engineer: An engineer who designs, builds, and maintains infrastructure like roads, bridges, and water systems.
    • Structural Engineer: A civil engineer who specializes in the load-bearing elements of buildings and bridges.
    • Systems Thinking: Seeing how the pieces of a project fit together, so you can tell how a change in one place will affect everything else.
    • PSAT/SAT: College Board standardized tests used for college admissions and National Merit Scholarship qualification.
    • ACT: Alternative college admissions test measuring English, math, reading, and science reasoning.
    • Smart Infrastructure: Modern infrastructure that uses sensors and data to manage systems efficiently (e.g., adaptive traffic lights, smart water grids).
    • Green Building: Construction practices that reduce environmental impact. LEED is the industry certification standard.
    • Truss: A structural framework of triangles used to support bridges and roofs because triangles distribute load efficiently.

    Bridge to Theory (Hats & Ladders)

    The Find Your Future workbook gives Engineering printed pages 103-110, the thinnest cluster chapter in the book, and it prints no civil engineering activity anywhere. The chapter opener (FYF p. 103: "Exploring the World of ENGINEERING") frames the cluster as designing, building, and improving the world around us, puts a "Be the Decision Maker" prompt in front of students about designing the airplane of the future, and introduces three Hats on the page: Aerospace Engineer, Biomedical Engineer, and CAD Technician. The chapter prints no pathway list. Students meet the engineering pathways in the app instead, through the Pathway Possibilities step on the App Exploration page (FYF p. 110: "App Exploration"), and they meet Irving ISD's programs of study, certifications, and student organizations on the district pages (FYF pp. 108-109).

    Two workbook activities anchor the week, and only one of them comes from the Engineering chapter:

    • Powerskill: Systems Thinking (Day 1): Students are the lead carpenters designing cabinets for a client's new kitchen. They read the floor plan, talk through with a partner where a cabinet can go without blocking plumbing, an appliance, or the path people walk, plan the layout, then review it with another pair and name which choices changed the room the most (FYF pp. 174-175: "Powerskill: Systems Thinking"). The lesson is borrowed from the Architecture and Construction chapter because it is the book's clearest statement of the idea this week runs on: one decision changes everything else.
    • Mission to Mars (Day 5): Career Climb activity. Students are an aerospace engineer at NASA designing the rover that replaces the one now retiring. They read the conditions on Mars (70 to -225 degrees F, rocky canyons and volcanoes, high winds and dust storms), brainstorm components on chart paper or sticky notes, sketch and label a prototype, then present it to a group of 3 or 4 and discuss which Martian condition was hardest to design around and what tests the rover would need before launch (FYF pp. 106-107: "Mission to Mars").

    The chapter's other activity, Protecting Wildlife (FYF pp. 104-105), is a robot blueprint brief for a wildlife conservationist. It belongs to the 4SW drone engineering week and this week does not run it.

    Everything else here is CCE-original by necessity. The workbook prints no bridge, no highway, no water system, and no civil engineering career, so the straw bridge challenge, the DFW infrastructure framing, and the emerging-careers research run on BLS, TxDOT, and the Hats & Ladders app rather than on a workbook page. That gap is named here so no one goes looking for a page that does not exist.

    IISD Instructional Strategies

    • Think-Pair-Share: Day 1, Activity 1, the "Be the Decision Maker" prompt on the chapter opener (FYF p. 103), where a company asks students to design the airplane of the future. Distinguished: pose it to the whole group, hold a silent 30 second Think, then time Partner A for 30 seconds and Partner B for 30 seconds, and post two stems at different levels: "One feature I would add is _ because " and "That feature would improve the trip for __, but it would cost the airline _____." Ask two pairs to share, not one. Listen while pairs talk for who names a tradeoff rather than a wish list, and pick your share-out pairs from what you heard rather than from raised hands.
    • Stop and Jot: Day 2, Activity 3, the "Why is it emerging?" line on the Emerging Engineering Careers template. Distinguished: box it at 90 seconds with the prompt on screen and post two stems at different levels: "A Smart Infrastructure Specialist is an EMERGING career because _. The technology or need that made it grow is " and "This career did not exist 10 years ago because __." Read the lines over shoulders while students write. If most jots say only "new technology," pull the class back for 60 seconds and name the three drivers on the board (aging infrastructure, climate and water, population growth) before students finish the rest of the template.
    • Active Monitoring: Day 4, Activity 1, the three timed build checkpoints. Distinguished: walk a fixed pathway three times, one lap per checkpoint, each lap with a single target (minute 8 base rails spanning 12 inches, minute 15 diagonal members forming visible triangles, minute 22 deck complete and the bridge standing on its own), marking the three-phase clipboard as you go. Have feedback ready before class for the two known build failures: straws taped end to end instead of bundled three or four together, and a deck laid across rails that were never triangulated. If more than a handful of teams miss the minute 15 target, pivot and reproject the truss geometry to the whole room, a triangle holding its shape under load and a square folding into a rhombus, instead of repeating it team by team.
    • Chunking: Day 3, Activity 2, the three bridge types. Distinguished: project one type at a time, say what each one is for before showing it (a beam spans, a truss spreads the load across many small pieces, an arch pushes the weight into the ground), demonstrate the triangle-versus-square load test on the projector inside the truss chunk, and close each type with the same three questions the plan already gives teams: does it fit a 12-inch straw span, what is its key structural element, and where are its triangles. Note which type draws the blankest answers. That type becomes a 2 minute re-teach before Activity 3 sketching starts, and teams who cleared all three chunks start their second sketch early instead of waiting.
    • Time, Voice, Body (TVB): Day 2, Activity 2, the six-station test comparison Gallery Walk. Distinguished: post the three blocks before the first rotation, a visible 2 minute countdown per station, Voice 1 for partner talk at a card and Voice 0 while walking between cards, and a stated rotation direction so pairs do not cross the room. Say where the Impact worksheet travels (in hand, not left on a desk) and which four cards are the priority if time runs short. Narrate the pairs who are meeting the expectation rather than correcting the ones who are not. If the rotation is running long by the third station, announce the cut to four stations before the next rotation rather than after it.

    Summative Assessment

    Emerging Career Research + Bridge Challenge Report (Day 5): Students submit their Engineering career research worksheet from Day 1, their emerging engineering career research template, their bridge sketch, their test results, and a redesign reflection. Scored on: emerging occupation research quality (d(1)(D)), engineering education requirements accuracy (d(2)(A)), standardized test awareness (d(3)(E)), and engineering design process documentation (d(1)(C)).

    Differentiation

    Scaffolded Learning

    • Bridge design templates showing truss, arch, and beam geometries with labeled parts
    • Simplified PSAT/SAT worksheet with key facts pre-filled so students focus on how scores apply to their pathway
    • PBS Design Squad guided instructions for students who need step-by-step scaffolding
    • Pair teams of 2 for the bridge challenge so students can divide sketching and building
    • A pre-marked kitchen floor plan for the Systems Thinking activity, with the sink, stove, and refrigerator already circled so partners spend their time on placement instead of plan-reading

    Extensions

    • Research LEED certification requirements and list the top 5 strategies in a LEED-certified building
    • Redesign the bridge based on test data and build a second prototype
    • Create a personal PSAT study plan for 9th grade (subjects to strengthen, prep resources)
    • Pick one real project from the TxDOT Dallas district list and write three sentences on the problem it solves, who solves it, and what would happen if no one did
    • Answer the workbook's Class Discussion question on the Mars Rover pages: what other professionals could an aerospace engineer collaborate with to design a rover, and what would each one contribute (FYF p. 107)

    ELL Language Support

    • Pre-teach: Civil Engineer = Ingeniero civil, Bridge = Puente, Truss = Armadura, Standardized Test = Examen estandarizado
    • Bilingual PSAT/SAT/ACT comparison handout with Spanish column labels
    • Bridge-building is hands-on and accessible across language levels
    • The kitchen floor plan and the rover sketch are both visual tasks, so ELL students can do the thinking before they need the vocabulary. The workbook reads well above grade level, so read the scenario paragraphs aloud
