    Day 5: Catch-Up and Your Choice (Flex Day)

    Lesson Overview
    Time 50 minutes
    Objectives (Teacher-chosen) Complete any Core CCE work that slipped from earlier in the week; learn the career research format the rest of the year reuses; run any optional World of Work chapter activities not yet covered; absorb end-of-week campus events
    TEKS d(1)(B) (Option B); flex day otherwise, core TEKS demonstrated on Days 2-4
    5E Phases Explore: Catch-up work and menu options · Extend: Perks and Quirks career research on two new Hats · Evaluate: Exit Ticket
    Deliverable Default: career research worksheet on 2 Hats (Option B). If any student missed a Core day, their completed catch-up work is the deliverable.
    Materials (as needed for the option chosen) Find Your Future workbook pp. 4-5 and pp. 12-16, printed career research worksheet, 2 per student (Printable PDF), Chromebooks, printed cluster posters, sticky dots, Xello accounts (optional)

    This is a flex day. Catch up or choose.

    Day 5 is the buffer day for Week 0. If Core Days A, B, or C were disrupted by campus events, SSO issues, or absences, use today to complete missed work. Otherwise, run Option B, which is the one flex item later weeks depend on, or pick from the rest of the menu. The core CCE content (core personality type, work values, Building Blocks, My Career Journey reflection) is already complete if your week ran clean.

    Option A: Catch-Up on Missed Core Work (Priority)

    If any student missed Core Day A (core personality type), Core Day B (work values or Building Blocks), or Core Day C (My Career Journey reflection), today is the day to catch them up. Prioritize:

    1. Missed Discover Your Core. The student completes the activity in the H&L app under Profile at the start of the period. Their top type is the baseline every downstream week references.
    2. Missed work values or Building Blocks. The student completes the H&L app entries, writes the results in the engineering notebook, and fills in the workbook Building Blocks inventory (FYF pp. 9-11).
    3. Missed My Career Journey reflection. The student uses the full period for the reflection handout (Core Day C Activity 2).

    Students who completed all Core Days run one of the options below in parallel while you work 1-on-1 with catch-up students.

    Time, Voice, Body: this parallel structure is the day's hardest management problem, so post the three blocks before the period starts. Voice 0 for the first 15 minutes while catch-up students take an assessment, Voice 2 after that for the partner options, a visible countdown so the room turns over together, and a stated drop point for finished worksheets so nobody queues at your table while you are working 1-on-1.

    Option B: Perks and Quirks and the Career Research Format (Recommended Default)

    Source: (FYF pp. 4-5: "Perks and Quirks")

    This option carries a downstream dependency.

    This is where CCE teaches the career research format for the whole year. Starting in 1SW Wk1 Day 2, every cluster week hands out a career research worksheet and names it as a format students already know. If Friday gets eaten, run this option in your first clean period of 1SW Wk1, before that worksheet goes out.

    Open the workbook to page 4. Read the definitions together: every career (the workbook calls them Hats) has its own responsibilities, skills, work environments, perks, and quirks. A perk is a part of a job someone may enjoy. A quirk is something unusual, unexpected, or challenging about it. Walk the printed Pest Control Technician example and its four details (travels often, works outdoors, works in difficult weather, close contact with bugs and critters). One student marked some as perks and others as quirks. Ask the room: would everyone mark those four the same way?

    Step 1: Choose Two Hats (p. 4). Students open the Hats & Ladders app, click the Hat Finder, and choose 2 Hats they want to explore more deeply. Encourage one Hat from a recommended cluster and one from a cluster they know nothing about.

    Step 2: Research the Reality of the Hats (pp. 4-5). The instruction and the perk, neutral, and quirk key are on page 4; the two Hat tables are on page 5. Students fill a table for each Hat, one row per detail, and mark each detail as a perk, a quirk, or neutral:

    • Salary or pay range
    • Education or training needed
    • Powerskills needed
    • Other skills needed
    • Work environment
    • One additional fact

    [H&L PLATFORM] Students research each Hat using the Hats & Ladders app and at least one additional source. Point them at the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook (bls.gov/ooh) for the second source, and require the pay and training figures to be confirmed there rather than taken from a single screen.

    Step 3: Share and Compare (p. 5). Partners compare their two Hats. Which career details felt most important to different people? Did anyone react very differently to the same job detail? Close with the workbook's discussion question: why is it important to look at many parts of a career instead of only one detail like salary?

    Then teach the CCE worksheet. Pass out the printed career research worksheet (Printable PDF), the artifact every cluster week reuses. Its six fields are not the page 5 list reordered, so name the change out loud rather than letting students assume they are copying the same table twice. Two fields carry over from page 5: education or training needed, and pay. Two fields are new and are not on page 5 at all: What Interests You? and Brief Job Description. Four page 5 rows do not appear on this sheet at all: Powerskills Needed, Other Skills Needed, Work Environment, and One Additional Fact. Those four stay in the workbook, where the perk and quirk reactions live.

    • Name of Career
    • What Interests You?
    • Brief Job Description
    • Education / Training Needed
    • Average Salary
    • What Tools, Equipment, or Skills are Needed for This Career?

    Students transfer Hat #1 onto the worksheet with you modeling on the projector, then complete Hat #2 on their own copy. Tell them plainly that this sheet returns every week for the rest of the year, and that the perk and quirk reactions they just wrote are what turns a list of facts into a decision about fit.

    DOK 3: Pick one detail you marked as a quirk. Explain how that same detail could be a perk for a different person. What would have to be true about that person?

    This option fits in about 30 minutes and is the recommended use of a clean Day 5.

    Option C: Powerskills and Design Thinking

    Source: (FYF pp. 12-14: "Introduction to Powerskills") and (FYF pp. 15-16: "Powerskill: Design Thinking")

    Run either part or both.

    Part 1: Introduction to Powerskills (about 20 minutes, pp. 12-14). Open the workbook to page 12. Powerskills are people skills that travel with you into any career. Walk the 10-row chart together (Communication, Teamwork, Problem-Solving, Adaptability, Leadership, Time Management, Creativity, Work Ethic, Empathy, Confidence), then run the workbook's three steps:

    1. Learn About Powerskills (p. 12). As a class, discuss one real example of when a student, or someone they know, used a Powerskill at school, at home, or in the community.
    2. Make Connections (p. 13). In pairs or small groups, students pick the top 2 Powerskills for each of the seven printed careers (Doctor, Chef, Plumber, Photographer, Video Game Designer, Principal, Athletic Coach) and write why. There are no wrong answers, but every pick must be backed up.
    3. Look at Yourself (p. 13). Students check off the Powerskills they already use, then answer which one they use without thinking, which one they wish were stronger, and whether they have a Powerskill the chart missed.

    Finish with the Powerskills Trading Card (p. 14): name, top two Powerskills, one real example of each, one Powerskill to grow this year and why, and a title that captures the student's strongest skill ("The Problem-Solving Pro," "The Calm in the Storm," "The Idea Generator").

    Part 2: Design Thinking, the Worst Idea activity (about 15 minutes, pp. 15-16). Open the workbook to page 15. Design Thinking here means deliberately generating the most ridiculous, impractical ideas you can, to break out of traditional thinking.

    1. The Bad Idea Briefing (p. 15). Read the prompt, then use the workbook box.
    2. My Worst Ideas (5 min, p. 16). Prompt: terrible ideas for a smartphone. The workbook example is a smartphone that can only be charged at the bottom of the ocean. The crazier, the better.
    3. Sharing with a Partner (5 min, p. 16). Pairs decide which idea is the absolute worst and write "Our worst idea is ____."
    4. Class Vote (p. 16). Each pair shares and the class votes on the worst of all. Then discuss the workbook prompts: what did you learn from using design thinking, how could the class's terrible ideas lead to a better smartphone, and how might doing this at work help people come up with innovative ideas?

    Facilitation Tip

    Students love Part 2 because there are no wrong answers. The dumber the idea, the better. Encourage absurdity. Then connect it back to Part 1: Design Thinking is the Creativity row of the Powerskills chart in action.

    Option D: Career Cluster Curiosity Gallery Walk

    Post the 14 H&L cluster posters (or printed cluster cards) around the room, one cluster per poster. Give each student 2 to 3 sticky dots. Students place dots under the 2 to 3 clusters they are most curious about. After everyone has placed their dots, lead a quick whole-class discussion:

    • Which clusters got the most dots?
    • Which clusters got the fewest?
    • Does that mean the unpopular clusters are less valuable? No. Many of the highest-paying, most stable careers are in clusters that do not sound exciting at first.

    Logistics Tip

    Release one table group at a time to avoid crowding. Students place dots at their own pace over 4 minutes rather than all walking together.

    This option fits in about 10 minutes.

    Option E: Xello Access and First Goal

    Source: Bowie Grade 8 Xello Completion Standards, "Log in to Xello" and "After high school goal."

    Students launch Xello through district SSO and confirm that the dashboard loads. After access is confirmed, students open About Me and select one after-high-school goal: Not sure yet, More school or training, or Alternate route. Tell students that the choice can change as they learn more this year.

    This 25-minute pair of tasks prepares students for Matchmaker. Do not add Matchmaker, Personality Style, Learning Style, Skills Lab, or Mission Complete to this block. Matchmaker alone is a 30-minute instructional task, and the live Grade 8 configuration does not assign Skills Lab or Mission Complete.

    Common Issue

    Xello and H&L logins are usually different SSO providers. If a student cannot log into Xello, check their roster status with the campus tech coordinator before troubleshooting on your own.

    This option fills about 25 minutes.

    Option F: Campus Event or Early Release

    If your campus has Friday events, early release, or any first-week end-of-week disruption, this is the day to absorb it.

    Exit Ticket (Optional)

    Run this ticket if Option B ran. Catch-up students turn in the core work they completed instead.

    EXIT TICKET (Comparison Matrix) · Printable PDF:

    Use the two Hats you researched today. Fill in the matrix from your worksheets.

    Hat 1: ___ Hat 2: ___
    Pay range
    Education or training needed
    One detail I marked as a QUIRK

    Bottom line: which Hat would you research more next week, and why? Use one cell from the matrix in your answer. Then name the one fact you would still need to look up before you could decide.

    (d(1)(B))

    Differentiation

    • Support: For catch-up students, sit 1-on-1 during the first 15 minutes and walk them through the H&L activity they missed. Do not let them skip ahead. For Option B, hand these students the worked-example version of the worksheet (Printable PDF), which prints a completed Veterinarian model above a blank set of the same six fields, so they copy a model before generating their own.
    • Extension: Students who finish any option early explore the H&L Hat Finder and favorite their first 3 Hats from any cluster. These become part of their Climber Profile portfolio and feed into the 4SW Wk1 Favorites Audit at mid-year.
    • ELL: Bilingual field labels for the Option B worksheet (Printable PDF): Salario = Salary, Educación = Education, Herramientas = Tools, Ambiente de trabajo = Work environment. Bilingual cluster names for Option D (Agricultura, Arquitectura, Artes, Negocios, Educación, Energía, Ingeniería, Ciencias de la Salud, Hospitalidad, Servicios Humanos, Tecnología de la Información, Ley y Servicio Público, Manufactura, Transporte). Pair ELL students with bilingual peers for discussion-heavy options.
