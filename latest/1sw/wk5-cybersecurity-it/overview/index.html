    Week 5: Cybersecurity, Favorite Clusters, and Capstone

    1st Six Weeks | Information Technology Cluster | 5 class periods (50 minutes each)

    Week Purpose

    Students close the Information Technology block by studying one emerging cybersecurity occupation, making careful phishing decisions, and applying work ethic and integrity to a community Cybersecurity Bootcamp plan. They also complete the required Grade 8 Xello Favorite clusters task and finish the 1st Six Weeks capstone.

    The capstone is one compact evidence packet:

    • Cybersecurity Bootcamp plan;
    • flyer prototype with a safe sign-up route;
    • original symbol for a postsecondary goal, with a middle-school-to-high-school and high-school-to-postsecondary step; and
    • Career Journey update using specific evidence from the six weeks.

    Paper, Canva, and Adobe Express are equal routes. A teacher may fabricate selected examples on a campus laser cutter after class, but laser output is not required, scored, or promised to every student.

    Week Learning Target

    I can use evidence to explain why cybersecurity work is changing, identify work ethic and integrity in cybersecurity decisions, choose a favorite career cluster, and describe two steps toward a goal after high school.

    Week Demonstration of Learning

    Students submit the Cybersecurity Capstone Evidence Portfolio. This is Major 2 in the 1SW assessment map and is scored with the 16-point 1st Six Weeks Capstone Evidence Rubric. The teacher scores the durable packet, not platform clicks, public speaking, artistic polish, or fabrication method.

    TEKS Alignment

    • d(1)(D): Research and evaluate emerging occupations related to career interests. Students use dated BLS evidence to evaluate Information Security Analyst growth, preparation, and one limitation of the source.
    • d(4)(F): Define and identify workplace examples of work ethic, integrity, dedication, and perseverance. Students analyze professional phishing responses and document work ethic and integrity choices in the bootcamp plan and flyer.
    • d(3)(A): Describe academic requirements for moving from middle school to high school and from high school to a career or postsecondary education. Students name one high-school action and one postsecondary preparation step for the goal represented by their original symbol.
    • d(1)(C), supporting: Identify career opportunities within one or more career clusters. The Xello Favorite clusters connection names a cluster and one career to investigate.

    Source Grounding

    District HQIM

    • FYF pp. 24-25: Safe or Spoofed? scenario, five red flags, seven-email investigation, and practice-message extension.
    • FYF pp. 34-35: Powerskill: Work Ethic Cybersecurity Bootcamp scenario, six planning questions, flyer direction, and discussion prompt.
    • FYF pp. 36-37: district-customized Information Technology programs, IBCs, and CTSOs. Present these pages as Irving ISD HQIM. A difference on a public website becomes a teacher verification note, not a competing student answer.
    • FYF p. 38: Information Technology App Exploration. H&L is supplemental this week; no grade depends on favorites, exact Hat titles, or a live feature.

    The statistics printed on FYF p. 34 are part of the workbook scenario. Do not relabel them as current national statistics without a dated source.

    Required Xello Grade 8 Completion Standard

    • Favorite clusters: 40 minutes; save at least one cluster.

    The live Completion Standards report controls the minimum. Xello's licensed My career clusters lesson is an expanded 90-minute option that assumes Matchmaker and saved careers and asks for two clusters. It is teacher background, not the required minimum for this lesson. Save careers belongs in 2SW Wk3 and does not get squeezed into Day 5.

    Current Career and Safety Cross-Checks

    • BLS Information Security Analysts: $124,910 May 2024 U.S. median, bachelor's degree plus related experience typical, and 29% projected employment growth from 2024 to 2034. These are national figures and projections, not starting pay or a job guarantee.
    • CISA Secure Our World: recognize and report phishing, use strong passwords, turn on multifactor authentication, and update software. For a suspicious message, pause, verify through a trusted route, report, and delete.
    • The current Irving ISD Singley Academy page names Cybersecurity in the School of Innovative Technology. The workbook remains the source for its printed certification and CTSO details.

    Teacher Readiness Checklist

    Before the week

    • Open FYF pp. 24-25 and 34-38 in Canvas.
    • Open the seven locked Safe or Spoofed email images and the teacher-only answer guidance.
    • Check Xello rosters and the Favorite clusters Completion Standards report.
    • Open the locked Xello My career clusters guide as optional background; do not impose its 90-minute prerequisites or two-cluster target.
    • Decide whether students will make the flyer and original symbol on paper, Canva, or Adobe Express. Paper is ready from the start.
    • Return the Week 0 Career Journey reflection before Day 5.
    • Post the 16-point capstone rubric before students begin the Day 3 evidence.
    • If demonstrating a laser cutter, follow the current campus SOP and model-specific manufacturer guidance. Only an authorized trained operator runs the machine. Use known compatible material, working exhaust/filtering, a test piece, constant line-of-sight supervision, and the campus emergency routine.

    Print/post in teaching order

    1. Cybersecurity Career Route Guide
    2. Phishing Red-Flag Checklist
    3. Cybersecurity Bootcamp Plan
    4. Completed Bootcamp Model
    5. Xello Favorite Cluster Connection
    6. Career Journey Update and English/Spanish support
    7. 1st Six Weeks Capstone Evidence Rubric

    Week at a Glance

    Day Topic Core experience Required evidence
    1 Emerging Cybersecurity Work Three-role route guide + BLS evaluation Possible route + emerging-occupation evaluation
    2 Phishing and Integrity FYF Safe or Spoofed + seven locked email images Seven decisions, hardest-call reason, safe response, integrity connection
    3 Work Ethic FYF Cybersecurity Bootcamp plan + flyer prototype Plan, prototype, and quality/integrity check
    4 Favorite Clusters Required 40-minute Xello task At least one saved cluster + career connection
    5 Transition Planning Original symbol + Career Journey update Four-piece Major 2 packet

    District Instructional Moves

    • Stop and Jot: Day 1, students mark one preparation fact and one source limitation before discussing whether the occupation is emerging.
    • Chunking: Day 2, teach one phishing clue at a time, then investigate one email at a time. Students record evidence before naming a decision.
    • Think-Pair-Share: Day 2, students compare the hardest email call. Each partner must name a clue and a safe next action.
    • QSSSA: Day 3 work-ethic prompt. Question: What would a sponsor notice your team doing behind the scenes? Stem: “They would notice us ___ because ___.” Signal: written task card. Share: partner, then selected class responses. Assess: listen for preparation, checking accuracy, privacy, or revision—not vague claims about working hard.
    • Active Monitoring: Day 3, track one audience, two actionable learning goals, accurate advice, safe sign-up, and one integrity choice.
    • Turn and Talk: Day 4, students explain one cluster-career connection before saving.
    • Stop and Jot: Day 5, students write the two transition steps before opening a design tool.

    Emergent Bilingual and Accessibility Supports

    • Put vocabulary, stems, and examples beside the step where students use them. Required directions stay visible; disclosures hold optional help only.
    • Use English/Spanish field labels, oral rehearsal, speech-to-text, read-aloud, enlarged print, and chunked presentation. A full translation is not required for every artifact.
    • Score evidence and reasoning, not accent, performance confidence, drawing skill, or English mechanics unless meaning is unclear.
    • Keep individual writing spaces proportional to the prompt. A two- or three-sentence explanation receives several ruled lines, not a single table cell.
    • Offer private written responses instead of public performance. The Xello reflection stays private.
    • Do not require students to disclose personal cybersecurity experiences, family financial details, real contact information, or actual passwords.

    Absence and Platform Rules

    • H&L or CyberSeek unavailable: use the fixed route guide; no required evidence is lost.
    • Xello unavailable: record the access issue, complete the paper connection, and schedule the required save during a supervised catch-up block.
    • Canva or Adobe unavailable: use paper with the same checklist and rubric.
    • Student absent Day 2: use the seven locked Canvas images and checklist; no partner is required.
    • Student absent Day 3: complete the same plan and paper flyer; use the model and self-check instead of peer feedback.
    • Missing Week 0 reflection: use the recovery prompts on the Career Journey update rather than assigning a zero.

    Optional Whole-Group Slide Deck

    When produced, the deck must teach the whole lesson from bellringer through submission and cleanup. Build a course-specific 1280×720 HTML/CSS storyboard, render exact high-resolution PNGs, assemble them as full-slide images in PowerPoint, add concise teaching moves and a [Sources] section in speaker notes, and inspect the exported PowerPoint. The paired Canvas guides and evidence artifacts remain the source of truth.
