# AVID Focused Notes Placement Audit

**Status:** source-grounded candidate for a later implementation slice; not part of the Week 0 launch and not yet a Canvas change.

## Decision

The first strong CCE destination for Jenna Hainlen's complete focused-note-taking structure is **3SW Wk3 Day 2-3, Pest Patrol Field Reports and Constraints**.

Do not add the full lesson to the H&L/FYF launch week. The launch needs short, private result interpretations, not a second note-taking unit. Do not relabel those short entries as AVID.

Do not import the literal paper-crane product into Pest Patrol. Preserve the handcrafted instructional function: students take notes for a stated purpose, revise and question those notes, summarize the important evidence, and then prove the notes are usable without reopening the source. In CCE, the Day 3 drone design is the authentic use test.

## Original source truth

- Slides 13-26 teach all five phases explicitly: Taking Notes, Processing Notes, Connecting Thinking, Summarizing and Reflecting on Learning, and Applying Learning.
- Slide 15 establishes a purpose and Essential Question, asks the teacher to choose a useful note format, and tells students to select/paraphrase rather than copy word for word.
- Slides 18-22 require students to revise, organize, question, connect, and move beyond gathering facts.
- Slide 24 separates summary from reflection: capture the most important learning, answer the Essential Question, name how the learning is useful, and plan the next move.

- Slides 31-38 turn the teaching into a concrete CORNELL sequence: Create the format and Essential Question, Organize notes, Review and revise, Note key ideas/questions, Exchange ideas to fill gaps, and Link learning with a summary.

## Why Pest Patrol is the fit

The existing CCE lesson already contains the full learning problem:

1. Students read three different worker reports: Agricultural Engineer, Farmer, and Plant Scientist.
2. They select facts that would change a drone design.
3. They process each fact into a functional `drone must` statement.
4. They connect the three sources, identify a work product, and rank constraints.
5. On Day 3, they must use those notes to create a labeled drone design and explain a trade-off.

That sequence is a genuine career application, not a study-skills detour. It also gives the teacher a visible answer to the key question: **Could the student use the notes to make a defensible design without rereading every report?**

## Preservation-first adaptation contract

### Keep

- Jenna's five named phases and the purpose-first logic.
- One Essential Question that controls what students record.
- Selection and paraphrase rather than copying every sentence.
- A visible revise/process move after initial note collection.
- At least one student-generated question, connection, or point of confusion.
- A summary/reflection that answers the Essential Question and names what the notes make possible.
- An application test followed by a specific note revision.
- Jenna's recurring slide rhythm: one move per projected slide, visible work time, explicit transition, and reflection after the use test.

### Change only because CCE requires it

- Replace the paper-crane source and product with FYF pp. 93-95 and the Pest Patrol design task.
- Use Grade 7 language and one completed agricultural-engineer model.
- Keep the existing two-page Pest Patrol Field Notes and Constraints sheet as the response home. Revise it minimally rather than adding a Cornell packet.
- Treat bilingual notes, speech-to-text, teacher read-aloud, annotation, and paper as equivalent access routes.
- Keep the note application private/individual even when a 60-second partner evidence check is used.

### Do not add

- No planner or binder-compliance check.
- No note-decoration, handwriting, color-coding, or supply grade.
- No literal paper-crane activity, race, prize, team points, or mandatory public share.
- No second worksheet, learning log, exit ticket, or Canvas submission for the same notes.
- No claim that Cornell format is the only valid note format.
- No reduction of the five phases to an invented three- or four-word CCE mnemonic.

## Feasible 50-minute Day 2 map

This is a replacement map for the current Day 2 moves, not additional work:

| Minutes | Existing Pest Patrol job | Preserved AVID job |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-5 | Product request versus defined problem | Establish purpose and Essential Question |
| 5-13 | Preview the three reports and note format | Select format; organize source sections |
| 13-31 | Read and record one or two useful facts per source | Take/select/paraphrase notes |
| 31-36 | Underline, clarify, and convert facts into `drone must` statements | Process/revise notes |
| 36-40 | Add one source question, connection, or point of confusion | Connect thinking |
| 40-48 | Write and rank three constraints; answer the Essential Question | Summarize and reflect |
| 48-50 | Close sources and preview the Day 3 use test | Prepare to apply learning |

Day 3 becomes the application test: students use the Day 2 notes to draft the drone. After the first design check, they identify one note that helped and one missing/unclear note they would revise. That closes the loop without a second submission.
