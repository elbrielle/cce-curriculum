Continue developing Irving ISD's Grade 7 Career and College Explorations course as a coherent, source-grounded, teacher-runnable curriculum. The immediate release priority is a flawless, low-prep launch package for the week beginning Monday, August 17, 2026. After that launch package is stable, continue the preservation-first reconciliation across the full 36-week course.

Work autonomously through the complete loop of source audit, preservation decision, implementation, rendering, independent review, parity verification, and owner-ready release preparation. Do not stop after producing an inventory, recommendations, sample lesson, or partial deck. Use natural pause points and a durable working buffer so progress survives interruption. You are authorized to use bounded parallel agents for non-overlapping source audits, artifact review, visual inspection, or independent gates; assign one writer per file/slice and never allow concurrent Canvas writers.

Do not redesign the course from scratch. Do not substitute generic AI-created routines, worksheets, slide decks, or visual systems for strong teacher-crafted or district-assigned materials that already exist. Work from the actual sources first, preserve what is good, and change only what a specific Grade 7, CCE, TEKS, access, privacy, currency, or classroom-operability requirement makes necessary.

The official student-facing course name is **Career and College Explorations (CCE)**. The owner may say CCR conversationally, but final curriculum artifacts should use the official course name unless a district source requires different wording.

## Owner intent and correction that governs this work

The prior AVID reconciliation moved too quickly from source ideas to newly generated CCE-branded artifacts. That was the wrong default. The Singley AVID teacher gave the owner permission to use the teacher-created materials she shared. Those original materials are therefore primary instructional sources for this reconciliation, not merely inspiration for generic replacements.

For every AVID-derived artifact or routine:

1. Open and inspect the actual original file and its linked weekly teacher slides.
2. Identify the exact teacher-crafted structure, classroom move, example, response design, and visual treatment that make it useful.
3. Preserve the original wording, sequence, layout, examples, and teacher moves wherever they already work.
4. Make only the minimum changes required for Grade 7, AVID-to-CCE framing, CCE TEKS, current facts, privacy, accessibility, equivalent response routes, local logistics, or the existing Hats & Ladders/Find Your Future/Xello sequence.
5. Record every material change and why it was necessary.
6. Label the result truthfully as one of:
   - **Original preserved**
   - **Original with minimal CCE/Grade 7 reframing**
   - **Substantially adapted from named source**
   - **New CCE artifact created only because no adequate source existed**
   - **Excluded**, with the exact reason
7. Never call a newly generated artifact an AVID resource or imply that an invented framework came from the teacher.

Permission from the teacher does not automatically clear third-party images, commercial packets, AVID-proprietary materials, student information, or embedded external content for public redistribution. Verify ownership and embedded rights artifact by artifact. Preserve teacher-created materials when permission covers them; redact private data; keep district/vendor-licensed items authenticated when their licenses require it; and do not use licensing as a blanket excuse to replace teacher-created work.

## Current course architecture and source hierarchy

Keep the assigned curriculum spine intact:

1. **Hats & Ladders / Find Your Future** is the core career-exploration curriculum and vocabulary source. Use the exact assigned workbook pages and platform tasks in intentional sequence. Do not jump to a distant workbook page merely because it contains a convenient activity.
2. **The authoritative CCE Scope and Sequence** controls weekly order, daily instructional purpose, TEKS, platform roles, and assessment placement.
3. **Xello Grade 8 completion standards** remain the required yearlong profile and planning extension where assigned. Xello does not replace the core career-exploration lesson and should not be added as generic enrichment.
4. **Technology and other platforms** are used only when the scope assigns them or when they perform an authentic task better than the workbook, notebook, Canvas, or a teacher-led routine.
5. **AVID teacher-created materials** provide strong classroom routines, focused note-taking, planning, reflection, self-advocacy, study skills, postsecondary comparison, and career-readiness structures when they close a verified CCE gap.
6. **Current official sources** support facts that the core curriculum does not supply or that require current verification.

Before changing a lesson, write down:

- the exact existing CCE lesson and evidence job;
- the exact core source page, platform task, or completion standard;
- the verified gap;
- the named teacher-created AVID source that addresses the gap;
- what will remain intact;
- the minimum required adaptation;
- why the student needs the artifact or interaction at all.

If the assigned workbook, H&L task, Xello task, OneNote page, Canvas-native interaction, or existing teacher-crafted handout already holds the evidence well, do not create another worksheet.

### Notebook onboarding

The owner will use **OneNote Class Notebook** as the default classroom route because it supports page distribution and fits the owner's workflow. The facilitator guides must not mandate OneNote or assume every teacher has the same platform setup.

Before making OneNote features load-bearing, verify the current official Microsoft Class Notebook workflow and the features enabled in the district environment. Separate confirmed behavior from an owner preference or unverified assumption.

Build one content-equivalent notebook system with two delivery routes:

- **Digital route:** OneNote Class Notebook or a teacher-designated digital notebook/folder.
- **Physical route:** a spiral notebook, composition notebook, binder section, or teacher-designated paper folder.

Both routes must support the same student actions and evidence. Neither route may be graded for purchased supplies, decoration, handwriting, tab count, color coding, or aesthetic neatness.

Do not invent the final notebook organization before studying the actual AVID Skills Check, focused-note-taking lessons, weekly slide routines, existing CCE evidence sequence, and the owner's need to push pages efficiently. Determine the smallest stable structure that teachers can sustain. The launch package must include:

- a teacher decision guide comparing digital and physical routes;
- exact setup steps for the owner's OneNote route;
- parallel physical-notebook setup steps;
- student-facing explanation of what belongs in the notebook and what remains in Find Your Future, Hats & Ladders, Xello, or Canvas;
- a naming and retrieval convention;
- absence, device, sync, and missing-notebook recovery;
- a low-stakes locate-and-use check;
- a plan for distributing or inserting the original/minimally adapted AVID pages;

### Launch-week slide decks

Produce complete, optional whole-group lesson decks for the launch sequence wherever projection materially improves onboarding, modeling, transitions, or student independence. Use the binding slide workflow.

For each deck:

- study the actual AVID weekly slides and the strongest owner-created/teacher-created decks for rhythm, pacing, examples, and classroom moves;
- preserve useful teacher-crafted slides or compositions when rights and course fit permit;
- use the stable Teacher/Student lesson pair as the instructional contract;
- storyboard the full class period from bellringer through Demonstration of Learning, submission, cleanup, and recovery;
- when a Jenna source slide supplies the right composition and instructional job, clone and minimally edit that actual 16:9 source frame through the approved presentation artifact workflow; do not flatten it into an AI summary slide;
- when the CCE/H&L lesson needs a genuinely new frame, build a fixed 16:9 HTML/CSS composition at 1280 x 720, render it to PNG, and assemble it through the approved raster-first workflow so new material remains stable and testable;
- add speaker notes with timing, facilitation, monitoring, misconceptions, supports, trim points, and sources;
- include visible timers or transitions only where they help the class act;
- include models and non-models before asking students to produce unfamiliar evidence;
- put word banks, sentence frames, labeled examples, and visual cues on the slide where students use them;
- retain the Canvas Student Guide as the accessible and absence route.

Use real, relevant images in preference to generic icon grids, decorative badges, emoji, AI-generated scenes, or stock-like filler. Google Images may be used only for discovery. Follow every image to its original source and record creator, title/description, direct URL, license or usage basis, and access date. Prefer district-assigned source imagery, government/museum/university/official organization media, Creative Commons/public-domain assets, or reputable licensed stock. Do not use search-result thumbnails, watermarked images, or imagery with unclear reuse rights.

Slides must look like a capable teacher designed them for this particular lesson, not like a templated corporate presentation or an AI card grid. One slide should do one instructional job. Use meaningful variation in composition, strong photographs or source images, readable type from the back of a classroom, and visible student actions. Do not force every slide into identical rounded rectangles.

Render and inspect every HTML slide and every final PPTX slide at full size. Reject clipping, blur, awkward wraps, tiny text, unexplained imagery, source omissions, repetitive layouts, or a deck that requires the teacher to reconstruct the lesson.

## Worksheet and student-artifact reset

Treat every existing CCE worksheet as unproven until it passes a lesson-by-lesson review. A technically clean PDF is not automatically engaging, usable, necessary, or teacher-ready.

Before keeping or creating a worksheet, ask in this order:

1. Does the assigned Find Your Future/Hats & Ladders page already provide the response surface?
2. Does the H&L or Xello platform already capture the evidence?
3. Does an original teacher-crafted AVID handout already perform the job well?
4. Would OneNote, a physical notebook, or a Canvas-native response be simpler?
5. Does the student need to annotate, sort, sketch, compare, rehearse, or retain something that genuinely justifies a separate printable?

If no separate worksheet is needed, remove it from the default route. If an original teacher-created handout works, preserve it and minimally reframe it. If a new artifact is unavoidable, begin from the closest strong human-authored example and the actual thinking job—not from the existing generic worksheet template.

Every retained or rebuilt artifact must:

- be explainable to students in under one minute after modeling;
- have a clear purpose and `Done when` condition;
- include a completed model and useful non-model when the structure is unfamiliar;
- use real/source-grounded visuals when they support understanding;
- avoid decoration-first tasks and repetitive box grids;
- prefill stable facts instead of requiring transcription;
- provide response space proportional to the requested thinking;
- place vocabulary, complete sentence frames, bilingual labels where intentionally used, and visual cues at the point of use;
- work in the default route and have an equal fallback;
- fit the realistic class period without hidden teacher prep;
- be rendered and inspected page by page at original detail.

Do not create a worksheet solely because an activity needs “something to turn in.” Use the least complicated evidence surface that fits the learning job.

## Teacher facilitator-guide standard

- the exact Topic, Objective, TEKS, and Demonstration of Learning;

- device, print, notebook, workbook, and platform routes;
- grouping and individual-accountability expectations;
- a complete model and useful non-model;
- a realistic 50-minute flow including transitions;
- minute checkpoints with visible look-fors;

- protected trim points that preserve the DOL;
- collection, return, reset, and cleanup;
- absence, late-arrival, no-device, no-workbook, platform-outage, and incomplete-work recovery;
- privacy, accessibility, and safety boundaries;
- slide-deck cues and speaker-note alignment;
- source and attribution records;

Every Student Guide must state, in plain Grade 7 language, what students are learning, doing, producing, saving, submitting, and doing next. It must preserve equivalent physical/digital, speech-to-text, dictation, AAC, and private routes where appropriate without creating a second easier curriculum.

## Quality and anti-slop gate

Reject a package when any of the following is true:

- the lesson cannot be traced to the Scope and Sequence and assigned sources;
- an AI-generated substitute displaced a stronger teacher-crafted artifact without a written necessity;
- the teacher must invent an example, answer, transition, material quantity, recovery route, or submission procedure;
- the worksheet is mostly empty boxes, repeated prompts, or cramped tables with little instructional value;
- the slide deck is a repetitive card grid, generic icon set, decorative template, or text dump;
- images are generic filler, unattributed, low resolution, watermarked, or disconnected from the task;
- student writing space is not proportional to the response;
- directions require copying information that could be supplied;
- notebook, workbook, H&L, Xello, Canvas, and worksheet jobs overlap or contradict one another;
- the lesson adds a second response home or duplicate submission;
- supports are hidden at the bottom instead of placed where students use them;
- a digital route is assumed with no physical/equivalent alternative, or a physical routine creates a supply-based penalty;
- timing excludes transitions, setup, login, material distribution, cleanup, or closure;
- a current factual claim lacks a direct source/date/measure/geography;
- a public artifact violates a rights or privacy boundary;
- canonical source, Canvas, IPC, and slides do not agree.

The fact that a file compiles, a PDF renders, or a Canvas builder succeeds is not evidence that the lesson is good. Require human visual inspection and a teacher-run mental walkthrough.

## Decision boundaries

Continue autonomously through ordinary source audit, preservation, minimal adaptation, local authoring, rendering, and QA. Stop and ask the owner when:

- a choice materially changes the Scope and Sequence, TEKS, assessment identity, grading, or required student evidence;
- an original teacher-created artifact and the current CCE lesson point in materially different instructional directions;
- public redistribution rights remain unclear after a reasonable source check;
- the H&L/Xello student workflow cannot be verified and alternatives would change the task;

- Canvas targets are not unique, an object is unexpectedly published, or a write would delete/broadly move/publish content;
- the final broad Canvas/GitHub publication is ready and requires owner approval.

Do not stop for minor copyediting, file naming, layout cleanup, or source-supported Grade 7 wording decisions. Record the decision and keep moving.
