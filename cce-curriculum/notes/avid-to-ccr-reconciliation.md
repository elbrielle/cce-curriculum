## Why this source is useful

The AVID pacing calendar is not a replacement curriculum for Career and College Exploration. Its strongest contribution is the classroom operating system around the curriculum:

- predictable weekly routines;
- short planning and reflection cycles;
- focused note-taking that ends in an application;
- self-advocacy practice;
- reusable discussion and movement structures;
- artifacts that help students name what they learned and what they will do next.

CCE already has a much stronger career-sector spine, current-source boundaries, paired Teacher and Student Guides, private/equivalent response routes, and a full 36-week evidence sequence. The right move is to borrow selected AVID routines and rebuild a few assignments for seventh grade. It would be a mistake to graft the AVID six-weeks units or weekly compliance system onto CCE wholesale.

## The most valuable idea: source-grounded routines at the point of need

Jenna's pitch is correct about one thing that CCE can use more deliberately: a few dependable, teacher-tested routines can lower teacher prep and help students see their growth. The routine should not become the course, and a newly named CCE framework should not replace the original merely because it is easier to summarize.

### TEKS fit of planners, journals, binders, and study routines

These routines are not outside CCE. Several are legitimate ways to teach or demonstrate the personal-success strand:

- **d(4)(A):** demonstrate effective time-management and goal-setting strategies;
- **d(4)(B):** identify skills transferable among careers;
- **d(4)(D):** apply core academic skills to meet personal, academic, and career goals;
- **d(4)(F):** define and identify workplace examples of work ethic, integrity, dedication, and perseverance.

The important distinction is what the teacher scores.

- A **planner check** is strong TEKS evidence when a student prioritizes a real CCE task, schedules a next action, notices a missed checkpoint, and revises the plan. Counting 24 boxes or grading whether every other teacher assigned homework is not strong CCE evidence.
- A **journal or learning log** is strong when it names course evidence, a transferable skill, a barrier or revision, and a next action. A generic “what did you do this week?” paragraph is much weaker.
- A **binder or digital-folder check** is useful as a low-stakes organization routine. If it is assessed, score whether the student can locate and use required CCE evidence—not binder style, purchased supplies, color coding, handwriting, or adult help at home.
- **Focused notes and study skills** support d(4)(D) when students use the notes to make a career comparison, defend a decision, revise a design, prepare an oral/AAC response, or recover missed work. Merely copying notes does not demonstrate the standard.

This means CCE can intentionally teach planner, journal, organization, and focused-note routines. They should be authentic tools used to complete career work, not separate AVID-style compliance grades.

### 1. First-week goal cycle

Preserve the useful field order from Jenna's actual First Week Goal-Setting Sheet: one CCE goal, why it matters, one specific action, a scheduled checkpoint, confidence, and accountability/support. Remove cross-class grade reporting, planner-entry counts, supply compliance, signatures, and other high-school/all-classes assumptions. Week 0 uses this source-grounded sequence without renaming it `Plan -> Do -> Recover -> Reflect`.

Best fit: establish one authentic CCE goal during 1SW Wk0, then revisit the same goal only when the curriculum provides a real checkpoint or revision job.

### 2. AVID focused note-taking

The five-phase AVID focused-note process has a sound central idea: establish a purpose, collect notes, process them, connect the thinking, and summarize/apply the learning. Jenna's paper-crane arc then tests whether the notes are actually usable. Preserve that complete instructional logic when CCE reaches a genuine note-taking need; do not relabel the four-part `Capture -> Label -> Question -> Use` shorthand as AVID or substitute a generic worksheet.

The Week 0 H&L launch uses only small, point-of-use notebook entries because H&L/FYF remain the core curriculum. A later focused-notes lesson should be built from the original slides and speaker notes, with Grade 7 scaffolds and one authentic CCE application.

### 3. Six-Weeks Evidence Log: hold for review

The locally created log could help students retrieve examples for a later resume, interview, portfolio, or capstone. It could also become a second response surface that teachers must maintain and students must reconstruct. It is therefore not a launch requirement or settled yearlong routine.

Before any later release, verify every downstream consumer, decide whether the actual notebook can perform the same retrieval job, test the teacher maintenance cost, and obtain owner approval. If retained, it must reference existing work rather than require resubmission of earlier graded artifacts.

### 4. Self-advocacy scenario practice

The source deck has 36 scenarios. The format is useful; the full deck is not. Select and rewrite approximately eight to twelve age-appropriate scenarios:

- asking for clarification on a confusing task;
- recovering work after an absence;
- addressing an overloaded group role;
- requesting an accessible response route;
- responding when family preferences conflict with a career interest;
- asking for help with an unfamiliar workplace task;
- responding to an uncomfortable or unsafe comment;
- clarifying a deadline or expectation.

Each scenario should use one common response frame:

> I need ___. The evidence or reason is ___. My proposed next step is ___.

Include an explicit trusted-adult and safety escalation route. Do not ask students to disclose private health, family, or discipline information.

Best fit: 2SW Wk5 PowerSkills, then reuse one scenario at a time where a group task or recovery route warrants it.

## Material to leave out

- AVID tutorials, TRFs, and AVID compliance routines.
- Weekly grade graphs or public grade discussions.
- Counting planner entries as a recurring CCE grade.
- Team-point systems that turn behavior into an assessment grade.
- Student names, seating charts, school-specific logistics, or dated announcements from the source decks.
- The named Stacey Wassif/Teachers Pay Teachers mock-interview packet: the supplied single-user license does not authorize copying, reworking, merging, uploading, or distributing its protected content as CCE curriculum.
- Dated career, college, military, financial, or program claims copied from the source materials without current verification.
- Activities whose main product is decoration, craft quality, or presentation polish.
- “Post first, then view peers” discussions when a private route is more appropriate.
- Any routine that requires students to reveal family finances, grades in other classes, health information, disability information, or personal future plans.

## Grade 10 to grade 7 conversion rules

1. **Explore, do not commit.** Seventh graders compare possibilities and identify a next question; they do not select a permanent pathway.
2. **Use fictional cases before personal disclosure.** Personal reflection can be optional and private.
3. **One decision at a time.** Reduce eight-to-ten-step projects to two or three bounded actions.
4. **Supply the evidence.** Avoid open searches, job-board accounts, or time-sensitive research unless a current fixed fallback is present.
5. **Make skill evidence concrete.** “I am responsible” is weaker than “I returned the shared tools and documented the test result even when it contradicted my idea.”
6. **Protect equivalent routes.** Paper, private Canvas text/upload, AAC, speech-to-text, and teacher conference routes must collect the same evidence.
7. **Do not reward polish over thinking.** Artwork, color, eye contact, physical handshakes, and public speaking should not determine the content score.
8. **Reference prior work in place.** A spiral asks students to use an earlier fact or skill example; it does not require another upload of the same graded artifact.
